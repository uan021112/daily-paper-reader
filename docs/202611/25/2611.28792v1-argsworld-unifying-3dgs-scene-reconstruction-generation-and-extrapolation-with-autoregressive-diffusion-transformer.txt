Extrapolation with Autoregressive Diffusion Transformer

Abstract
Autoregressive diffusion world models deliver strong video
generation performance, yet they typically output only RGB
frames rather than explicit 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)
scene representations, creating a gap between learned vi-
sual world dynamics and usable 3D assets for downstream
robotics, simulation and rendering.
To bridge this gap,
we introduce ArgsWorld, an autoregressive diffusion model
that also produces an explicit 3DGS scene. For each gen-
eration chunk, the diffusion transformer jointly processes
clean historical image latents and noisy latents for new
views. It keeps the historical image latents fixed and re-
freshes the scene features so that the Gaussian representa-
tion can be revised as new regions come into view. Our 3D
Self-Forcing Distillation trains the model on its own gen-
erated histories using a mixture of single-view and multi-
view data. Distribution matching supervises direct image
latents and latents of Gaussian renderings at both genera-
tion and additional cameras. ArgsWorld unifies 3D scene
reconstruction from posed images, scene generation from
image or text prompts, and scene extrapolation across cam-
era segments with disconnected endpoints. Experimental
results demonstrate that ArgsWorld achieves state-of-the-
art performance across multiple 3D scene modeling tasks.

We introduce ArgsWorld, an Autoregressive 3DGS
World Model for reconstruction, generation, and spatial ex-
trapolation. In Fig. 1, ArgsWorld reconstructs a 3D Gaus-
sian scene from three posed images, generates views along
the initial trajectory, and extends the scene along verti-
cal and aerial camera paths.
We build on the camera-
conditioned, 3D-adapted Wan backbone of FlashWorld [19,
32]. At each chunk, its diffusion transformer attends jointly
to clean historical image latents and noisy new-view latents.
The historical image latents anchor the appearance, and
the model refreshes scene features across the active views
to decode an updated set of Gaussians (Fig. 2). This lets
ArgsWorld continue the same scene on a new camera orbit
without generating bridging views.
Our 3D Self-Forcing Distillation trains the model in
the same autoregressive setting used for inference (Fig. 3).
Completing all four denoising steps supplies the clean his-
tory for the next chunk. During training, we replay a ran-
domly selected denoising step with gradients and apply
distribution-matching distillation [49, 50] to the direct im-
age predictions and to renderings of the updated Gaussian
scene at both generation and additional cameras. The train-
ing mixture includes single-view images and posed multi-
view sequences.
Our contributions are threefold:
• A unified 3DGS world model.
ArgsWorld uses
one camera-conditioned autoregressive diffusion 3DGS
model to reconstruct scenes from posed images, gener-
ate scenes from image or text prompts, and extrapolate
scenes along an extended trajectory.
• Consistent autoregressive scene expansion. ArgsWorld
grows a Gaussian world one chunk at a time, using com-
pleted generations as fixed visual context. Joint scene de-
coding and rendering consistency supervision encourage
consistent renderings at historical viewpoints while al-
lowing the scene representation to evolve as new regions
are generated.
• 3D Self-Forcing Distillation. Training on self-generated
histories and matching distributions in both the image and
rendering branches yields a four-step autoregressive sam-
pler with explicit 3DGS output.

2. Related Work
Video generation and autoregressive world models.
Video foundation models such as Wan [32] supply vi-
sual priors for controllable world generation. Existing ap-
proaches condition generation on actions [1] or camera tra-
jectories [9, 39, 40]. Training autoregressive video diffu-
sion on ground-truth histories can lead to error accumula-
tion on generated histories at inference. Self Forcing [15]
addresses this mismatch by training on self-generated roll-
outs with sequence-level supervision. BiWM [29] retains
bidirectional attention and combines camera-control adap-
tation with few-step distillation.
CausVid and LongLive
accelerate causal rollouts [47, 51], while WorldMem re-
trieves past views for consistency [44].
SolarWM [13]
scales long-horizon video training, and InSpatio-World [30]
uses state-anchored autoregression for interactive 4D simu-
lation. ArgsWorld uses self-generated histories to condition
a bidirectional generator that jointly predicts images and up-
dates an explicit Gaussian scene.

NeRF [25] and 3D Gaussian Splatting [18] enable differen-
tiable rendering, with 3DGS providing explicit primitives.
Feed-forward models estimate multiview geometry [34, 37]
or synthesize novel views [17, 36]. pixelSplat, MVSplat,
and DepthSplat reconstruct Gaussians from posed im-
ages [2, 4, 46]. AnySplat [16] handles uncalibrated image
collections by predicting Gaussian primitives and camera
parameters jointly.
Diffusion priors support novel-view
synthesis [7, 24, 54] and text-driven 3D generation [12, 27].
FlashWorld [19] integrates Gaussian decoding into video
diffusion for image- or text-conditioned generation.
For
extrapolation,
WonderWorld [53] constructs Gaussian
surfels from outpainted views, and LucidDreamer [5]
uses diffusion-guided completion and alignment.
Won-
derTurbo [26] combines image inpainting and depth
completion with incremental fusion of newly predicted
3D Gaussians. ArgsWorld revises the scene jointly across
historical and new views within a single autoregressive
generator.
Distribution-matching distillation.
Distribution Match-
ing Distillation (DMD) [50] trains a fast generator using the
difference between the scores of a pretrained teacher and
an online model of the generated distribution. DMD2 [49]
improves score estimation through a two-time-scale update
rule and adds adversarial supervision. FlashWorld [19] uses
distribution matching to transfer its multi-view image prior
to Gaussian renderings. BiWM [29] distills bidirectional
video generation from self-generated histories. ArgsWorld
applies distribution matching to the direct image sequence
and to renderings of the updated scene at two sets of cam-
eras. This supplies training signals for both image synthesis
and geometry through the same rollout.
3. Method
ArgsWorld takes an ordered sequence of calibrated cam-
eras, optional posed reference images, and an optional text
prompt.
It produces an explicit 3D Gaussian scene that
can be rendered at additional query cameras. We initial-
ize the model from a 3D adaptation of a video diffusion
transformer (Sec. 3.1), then extend it to generate views in
successive chunks (Sec. 3.2). We train this autoregressive
model by distilling its own rollouts in both image and ren-
dering space (Sec. 3.3).
3.1. 3D Adaptation
The backbone is a camera-conditioned Wan2.2-TI2V-5B
diffusion transformer (DiT) [32]. We encode each RGB
frame independently with the same frozen variational au-
toencoder (VAE), obtaining one normalized spatial latent
grid per camera pose. Compression is spatial within each
frame, so four input frames yield four latent grids. This pre-
serves view-specific structure, such as contours and occlu-
sion boundaries, and keeps it aligned with the correspond-
ing camera rays for 3D decoding. For a camera ray with
origin o and unit direction d, the six-channel ray map con-
catenates d and o −(o⊤d)d. The DiT takes noisy RGB
latents, ray maps, and reference latents concatenated along
the channel axis, with text supplied through cross-attention.
Reference latents occupy the slots of their calibrated cam-
eras, and unobserved slots are zero-filled.
Image and Gaussian outputs.
We form the noisy latent
xσ = (1 −σ)z + σϵ from a clean latent z and Gaussian
noise ϵ ∼N(0, I). The DiT predicts a 48-channel velocity
vθ and a 1024-channel feature map per view. With the flow-
matching velocity target ϵ −z [22], the clean prediction is
ˆz = xσ −σvθ.
(1)
A separate Gaussian decoder takes the predicted features,
the detached clean latents, and the cameras. It uses a train-
able copy of the Wan VAE decoder with feature injection
and a pixel-aligned head that predicts two Gaussians per
grid location. Each center is obtained from a subpixel ray
offset and positive depth. The remaining outputs are opac-
ity, anisotropic scale, rotation, and spherical harmonic ap-
pearance coefficients [18].
Initialization.
We use the pretrained checkpoints from
FlashWorld [19]. The few-step checkpoint initializes the
student DiT and Gaussian decoder, and the 3D-adapted dif-
fusion checkpoint provides the frozen teacher.

3.2. Chunk-wise Autoregressive Inference
We split the trajectory into ordered camera chunks
C1, . . . , CB. At chunk b, the state contains the cameras
C<b, the final direct RGB latents Hb−1, and the most re-
cently computed features for those cameras. For the first
chunk the history is empty, and any reference images en-
ter through the reference channels at their matching camera
slots. The model initializes the noisy RGB stream for the
current chunk from Gaussian noise and the current feedback
channels from zeros. This shared input format supports re-
construction with several posed references, generation with
optional image or text prompts, and extrapolation by con-
tinuing a saved rollout with new cameras.
Bidirectional attention and scene update.
At step j, we
prepend Hb−1 to the noisy current latents xb,j. Historical
tokens receive timestep zero, and current tokens receive the
noise level σj. The stored RGB latents and features also en-
ter their conditioning channels in Fig. 2. With bidirectional
attention over C≤b, the DiT predicts clean latents ˆzb,j for
the current views via Eq. (1) and refreshes features Fb,j for
all views. The decoder DGS rebuilds the scene using the
features and the combined sequence Zb,j = [Hb−1; ˆzb,j],
Gb,j = DGS(Fb,j, sg(Zb,j), C≤b) ,
(2)
where sg denotes stop-gradient. For ray construction and
decoding, we center the camera prefix at the first camera
and normalize by its spatial extent. We then map Gaus-
sian centers and scales back to the fixed scene frame. The
clean RGB history anchors appearance during each update.
Newly generated views can refine geometry in overlapping
regions through the refreshed historical features. The de-
coder uses the current-view features to add geometry for
regions revealed by the new cameras.
Rendering feedback and history.
We write ¯z(G, C) =
E(R(G, C)) for the latent obtained by rendering a scene
G at cameras C and encoding the resulting views with
the frozen VAE encoder E. Between denoising steps, the
current-view render latents ¯z(Gb,j, Cb) and features feed
back into the DiT. We re-noise those render latents for the
next step,
xb,j+1 = (1 −σj+1) ¯z(Gb,j, Cb) + σj+1ϵ,
(3)
with fresh Gaussian noise ϵ. After S = 4 steps, we carry
Hb = Zb,S and Fb,S into the next chunk and use Gb,S as
the scene for rendering.

4. Experiments
Training details.
We initialize Wan2.2-TI2V-5B with
FlashWorld weights [19].
We alternate mixed-data and
multi-view-only training stages. We use a curated, high-
quality subset of DL3DV-10K [21], RealEstate10K [56],
ACID [23], and SpatialVID [35] with sharp, high-fidelity
clips, substantial camera motion, and high aesthetic qual-
ity [8].
For single-view examples, we pair Echo-4o-
Image [48] with camera trajectories sampled randomly from
WildRGB-D [43]. In the final stage, we draw posed se-
quences and single-view images at a 2:1 ratio.
To train
for both generation and reconstruction, the first chunk of
a multi-view rollout receives one to eight posed reference
images from the same sequence.
We train on eight GPUs at 480×704, sampling two to
five chunks of eight views per rollout. We weight the three
DMD branches equally for both data sources. We evaluate
the checkpoint at 4,200 global steps. Appendix Appendix A
gives the full training configuration. Appendix Appendix C
shows additional qualitative results for reconstruction, gen-
eration, and extrapolation.

5. Conclusion
We present ArgsWorld, a unified autoregressive diffu-
sion transformer for explicit 3D Gaussian scene model-
ing. Our framework integrates multi-view reconstruction,
image- and text-conditioned scene generation, and camera-
controlled extrapolation within a single generative architec-
8


ture. Bidirectional attention refreshes scene features while
retaining clean RGB history, and 3D Self-Forcing Distilla-
tion supervises RGB predictions and Gaussian renderings
on self-generated rollouts. This strategy improves visual fi-
delity and cross-view consistency while enabling efficient
four-step inference.
Extensive experiments demonstrate
state-of-the-art performance across multiple 3D scene mod-
eling benchmarks, validating the effectiveness of our uni-
fied approach. These results establish autoregressive diffu-
sion as a promising paradigm for constructing, generating,
and continuously expanding explicit 3D worlds.

Appendix
A. IMPLEMENTATION DETAILS
Architecture.
The Wan2.2-TI2V-5B backbone has 30
transformer blocks and 24 attention heads of dimension
128. We remove temporal padding from the frozen VAE
and sample a normalized 48-channel posterior latent inde-
pendently for each view. The encoder downsamples by a
factor of 16 along each image axis. At 480×704, the 30×44
latent grid becomes 330 tokens per view after 2×2 patchi-
fication. The student concatenates 48 noisy latent channels,
six ray channels, 48 reference channels, 1024 feedback fea-
ture channels, and 48 feedback latent channels, for 1174
input channels. The teacher sees the first three groups, and
the critic adds one channel carrying the branch identifier.
We encode text with the frozen UMT5 encoder at a maxi-
mum length of 512 tokens.
Gaussian decoding and rendering.
The reconstruction
decoder adds a convolutional projection of the DiT features
to the first decoder activation of the unnormalized clean la-
tent. Its prediction head produces two Gaussians per spa-
tial output location. A truncated exponential gives posi-
tive depth, and a sigmoid bounds opacity and the pixel-
size-scaled anisotropic scales. Each Gaussian uses a unit
quaternion and degree-two spherical harmonics. We raster-
ize RGB and depth in FP32. Multiscale depth regularization
uses average pooling with kernel sizes 4, 2, and 1, weighted
by 4/7, 2/7, and 1/7, respectively.
Data and conditioning.
We sample posed sequences and
single-view images at a 2:1 ratio. Each rollout uses a uni-
formly sampled length of two to five eight-view chunks.
Within the multi-view stream, the sampling weights for
DL3DV-10K, RealEstate10K, ACID, and SpatialVID are
4:2:1:1.
The nominal input frame strides are 3–4 for
DL3DV-10K and ACID, 5–12 for RealEstate10K, 3–5 for
SpatialVID, and 5–7 for WildRGB-D. Each posed clip
shares a center crop with a crop-scale factor drawn uni-
formly from [0.8, 1.0]. We update the intrinsics for the crop
and resize. For the novel-view branch, we sample a second
trajectory of equal length at a smaller frame stride within the
span of the rollout. Its camera slots carry no reference la-
tents. Image conditioning is enabled with probability 0.7,
with prompt dropout of 0.1 on image-conditioned exam-
ples. We sample the number of posed references uniformly
from one to eight and select distinct slots uniformly in the
first chunk. A single-view example uses the first slot for its
reference image when image conditioning is enabled.
Distillation and optimization.
The online critic starts
from the same adapted FlashWorld checkpoint as the frozen
teacher. The teacher uses text classifier-free guidance with
scale 3. We sample one of the four denoising steps uni-
formly for replay and share it across chunks. We average the
three DMD losses and set the normalizer floor in Eq. (5) to
10−6. We sample DMD noise from scheduler entries within
the nominal range σ ∈[0.02, 0.98], with bounds tied to
the replayed denoising step. The adversarial head projects
the 1024-channel critic features to 128 channels and applies
three stride-two residual blocks of widths 128, 256, and 512
to obtain per-view patch logits. For multi-view samples, we
regularize these logits with an MSE between real predic-
tions before and after adding Gaussian noise of standard
deviation 0.05 to the noisy real latents. The penalty has
weight 10 in the critic objective. The logistic adversarial
terms for the student and critic each have weight 0.005 per
branch. The depth and opacity penalties each have weight
0.01. The latent consistency term has weight 0.1 through
step 2,200, 0.4 through step 3,000, 0.6 through step 3,350,
1.0 through step 4,000, and 0.4 for the final 200 steps.
We use AdamW with peak learning rates of 10−6 for
the student and Gaussian decoder and 5×10−7 for the critic
and adversarial head, with (β1, β2) = (0.9, 0.95), weight
decay 10−6, and gradient clipping at 1. Learning rates warm
up over 1,000 global steps. After 100 critic-only steps, we
update the student and decoder once every four global steps
and the critic at every step. The global batch contains eight
examples, one per GPU, with no gradient accumulation. We
use BF16 autocasting, fully shard the DiTs, and checkpoint
activations in the trainable networks and rendering pipeline.
