Long-horizon robotic manipulation demands reliable execution across interdependent stages, yet
standalone World-Action Models (WAMs) suffer from compounding physical errors, while stateless
Vision-Language Model (VLM) supervision incurs prohibitive chunkwise latency and lacks 3D spatial
persistence. We present EmboEagle, a closed-loop agentic framework that unifies subtask-conditioned
WAM control, latent-space adaptive supervision, and memory-guided geometric recovery. First, we
adapt sequential WAMs via decoupled two-stage flow matching that preserves cross-stage visual
history while calibrating truncated action supervision at subtask boundaries. Second, without an
auxiliary vision encoder, we distill teacher intervention decisions into a lightweight routing gate driven
solely by the WAM Action Expert’s pre- and post-chunk latent transitions and subtask embeddings.
Third, upon invocation, the VLM decouples diagnostic status from discrete routing actions and
executes parameterized recovery primitives grounded in structured memory (initial references, active
grasp bindings, and tool traces), followed by mandatory post-repair verification. Across RMBench
and LIBERO-Mem, EmboEagle achieves 91.3% and 90.6% average success, surpassing the strongest
baselines by 8.0 and 9.8 percentage points, while boosting real-world success over VoLoAgent under
identical executor weights. Moreover, adaptive gating cuts VLM calls by 74.2% versus chunkwise
monitoring (91.3% vs. 93.7% success).

Introduction
Long-horizon robotic manipulation requires a robot to preserve task intent across changing object configurations
and grasp states, deciding when a subtask completes, retaining spatial cues needed by subsequent stages, and
correcting physical errors before they compound. World-Action Models (WAMs) establish a strong foundation
for this execution layer by coupling visual dynamics prediction with continuous action generation. Recent
advances extend this paradigm through history-conditioned control in Long-WAM (Huang et al., 2026) and
episodic visual guidance in MaP-WAM (Zhao et al., 2026a). Nevertheless, turning a capable visuomotor
executor into a reliable long-horizon agent raises a complementary control question: how should a system
allocate high-level supervision and translate its decisions into physical corrections during an ongoing task?
VLM-based orchestration bridges learned policies with explicit planning, monitoring, and manipulation
tools (Chen et al., 2026a; Gu et al., 2026b), yet the timing of this supervision imposes a sharp efficiency–
responsiveness trade-off. As contrasted in Figure 1(a–b), open-loop execution lacks error recovery, whereas
querying a VLM after every action chunk repeatedly returns routine continuation commands and incurs
unnecessary overhead; conversely, lengthening a fixed checking interval delays the detection of subtask
completions, auxiliary tool opportunities, and failed grasps. While progress-based and event-driven triggers
mitigate parts of this cost (Gu et al., 2026b), existing triggers often rely on heuristic rules or auxiliary

To address these challenges, we propose EmboEagle, an agentic framework that integrates subtask-conditioned
WAM execution with adaptive VLM invocation and memory-guided geometric recovery (Figure 1(c)). Crucially,
its design separates the decision to request supervision from the supervisory decision itself, uniting three tightly
coupled components: ✿Boundary-aware subtask WAM adaptation. Starting from pretrained Long-WAM
checkpoints, we perform decoupled two-stage flow-matching adaptation (Video DiT LoRA followed by full
Action DiT fine-tuning) on subtask-conditioned demonstrations, preserving cross-stage visual history while
truncating action supervision at subtask boundaries via a calibrated fixed-horizon loss. ✿Latent-delta adaptive
VLM invocation. Rather than duplicating visual encoding with an external perception network, we train a
lightweight routing gate distilled from teacher decisions using the WAM Action Expert’s pre- and post-chunk
token transitions and subtask embeddings, keeping post-intervention verification outside optional gating.
✿Decoupled orchestration and memory-guided recovery. When invoked, the VLM separates diagnostic status
from discrete routing actions. Assist operations can establish gripper–object bindings for subsequent recovery
or directly support execution. Recovery draws on these bindings, initial references, and recent tool feedback
to select tool primitives, with immediate verification after each intervention. Our main contributions are
threefold:
➊Closed-loop agentic architecture. We present EmboEagle, which unifies subtask-conditioned WAM execution,
decoupled VLM task orchestration, and persistent geometric memory with mandatory post-repair verification.
➋Latent-space adaptive VLM invocation. We formulate selective monitoring as teacher-decision distillation
over WAM action-token transitions and subtask semantics, enabling lightweight runtime gating without an
auxiliary vision backbone.
➌Boundary-aware WAM adaptation and empirical validation. We develop a two-stage subtask adaptation
scheme to adapt to the long-horizon settings. We validate our method alongside closed-loop evaluations on
RMBench and LIBERO-Mem.

Related Work
World Action Models for Robotic Manipulation. World action models (WAMs) couple visual dynamics prediction
with action generation to bring video pretraining into robot control. LingBot-VA (Li et al., 2026a) jointly learns
frame prediction and policy execution through an autoregressive diffusion framework, while DreamZero (Ye
2


et al., 2026) adapts a pretrained video diffusion backbone to joint video–action modeling. Further, Fast-
WAM (Yuan et al., 2026) retains video co-training while omitting explicit future prediction at inference,
reducing action-generation latency.
Long-WAM (Huang et al., 2026) studies how autoregressive video
pretraining supports the use of extended observation histories and combines causal world-action adaptation
with efficient execution. MaP-WAM (Zhao et al., 2026a) organizes historical observations into episodic records
and generates language and visual plans from memory. Then it conditions action and progress prediction
on those plans. These models provide predictive and temporal representations that support higher-level
orchestration.
Agentic Embodied Frameworks. Agentic embodied frameworks connect high-level reasoning to executable
skills and use feedback to coordinate multi-stage tasks (Liu et al., 2023b; Huang et al., 2022). SayCan (Ahn
et al., 2022) grounds language-based skill selection in learned affordance estimates. VoLo (Chen et al.,
2026a) extends physical orchestration to interruptible learned policies, perception models, and manipulation
primitives, allowing the controller to switch tools when execution requires correction. HarnessWAM (Gu et al.,
2026b) maintains scene beliefs and a task graph, constrains plans to the executor’s capabilities, and combines
outcome verification with local recovery. State maintenance provides another axis of design: HiMe (Ji et al.,
2026) separates execution, working memory, and longer-term planning, whereas LAW (Cao et al., 2026)
couples planning, status assessment, and action evaluation with hierarchical visual memory.
VLM supervision. The cost of VLM supervision motivates separating frequent observation from expensive
reasoning (Gu et al., 2026a). Checking every action chunk can repeat unchanged decisions, whereas fixed
intervals cannot adapt to when transitions or failures occur. Existing systems use different signals to schedule
deliberation. HarnessWAM (Gu et al., 2026b) uses learned progress and completion cues to trigger its task
manager. Spotter (Li et al., 2026b) employs a local Qwen screener to flag potential errors for a VLM judge,
with bounded asynchronous supervision and checks following repair. TUD (Yang et al., 2026) triggers generalist
reasoning from the cross-step dispersion of action re-predictions, using computations already performed by its
dual-system architecture.

Problem Definition
We consider language-conditioned robotic manipulation in a partially observable environment, where ac-
complishing a global instruction G requires completing a sequence of interdependent subtasks. At control
step t, the robot receives multi-view RGB observations ot and proprioceptive state qt, with calibrated depth
available for geometric perception. Let ht denote the interaction history of observations, robot states, and
executed actions up to t; early observations often establish object identities or spatial relations required later
in the task (Zhao et al., 2026a). We assume access to a pretrained World-Action Model and target-task
demonstrations annotated with subtask instructions from a supported vocabulary G.
Let gk(t) ∈G denote the active subtask instruction, whose index k(t) may span multiple control chunks.

Executing long-horizon tasks purely in open loop leads to compounding errors and unrecoverable failures,
whereas querying a Vision-Language Model (VLM) at every chunk boundary incurs substantial latency on
routine motion. Our problem therefore requires coordinating low-level chunk execution with a selective VLM
invocation decision bt ∈{0, 1} and structured physical recovery: bypassing supervision (bt = 0) during routine
progress while invoking high-level orchestration (bt = 1) at stage transitions or failures to reliably fulfill G
with minimal redundant VLM queries.

Methodology
Overview. Figure 2 presents the overall closed-loop workflow of EmboEagle. At runtime, a subtask-conditioned
WAM executes chunkwise control while a structured memory anchors geometric invariants and a VLM
orchestrates subtask transitions and physical recovery (▷Section 4.1). To eliminate redundant supervision
during routine execution, a lightweight routing gate distilled from teacher decisions dynamically schedules VLM
invocations from internal WAM transitions (▷Section 4.2). Finally, to support accurate stage execution and
latent gating, we adapt the WAM through a boundary-aware two-stage flow-matching scheme (▷Section 4.3).

Subtask-Conditioned WAM Adaptation
Subtask data curation. To enable stage-aligned chunk execution, we adapt a pretrained sequential WAM (Huang
et al., 2026) to follow subtask instructions from the controller vocabulary G (optionally paired with global
task or progress cues). Demonstrations are partitioned by episode into contiguous subtask intervals [ts, te)
prior to sampling training windows. For any start step t ∈[ts, te), the input visual history hW
t
spans across
the subtask onset ts to preserve physical continuity across transitions (replicating the initial frame with a
padding flag when history is insufficient at episode start), whereas future action supervision is truncated at
te via the valid-action mask µt,j = I[t + j < te] for j ∈{0, . . . , H −1}. Future visual supervision retains
observations up to the completion state at te (repeating and masking later frames), and boundary windows
with fewer than H valid actions are preserved to ensure responsive behavior near subtask endings.
Two-stage adaptation. We adapt the backbone through two decoupled flow-matching stages (Lipman et al.,
2022) while keeping the proprioceptive, VAE, and text encoders frozen. First, we equip the Video DiT’s
attention projections with LoRA adapters (Hu et al., 2021) and optimize a future-latent flow-matching
objective. Second, we freeze the adapted video branch and fine-tune the full Action DiT under action flow
matching: during training, the frozen video branch processes noised ground-truth future latents to supply
visual key–value conditioning to the Action DiT, whereas at inference it synthesizes these future conditions
directly from visual history.

Experiments
5.1
Experimental Settings
Datasets. We evaluate long-horizon, subtask-wise robotic manipulation on RMBench (Chen et al., 2026b) and
LIBERO-Mem (Chung et al., 2026) using full-task success rate. RMBench comprises nine bimanual tasks,
including five M(1) tasks requiring a single task-relevant past observation and four M(n) tasks requiring
multiple observations. LIBERO-Mem comprises ten single-arm tasks involving single placement, counting,
and sequencing/spatial reasoning. We further design two real-world tasks: garbage sorting, which requires
sorting colored objects into their corresponding containers, and bread toasting, which involves processing two
slices of bread through a toaster-and-plate sequence. Both tasks require coordinating multiple manipulation
stages while tracking which objects have already been processed.
Baselines. We compare EmboEagle against four families of representative methods aligned with each eval-
uation suite. ✤VLA-based policies include π0.5 (Intelligence et al., 2025), X-VLA (Zheng et al., 2026),
MemoryVLA (Shi et al., 2026), and ActMem-VLA (Zhao et al., 2026b). ✤WAM-based policies include
HiMem-WAM (Sun et al., 2026), AR-WAM (Jiang et al., 2026a), and MemoryVAM (Jiang et al., 2026b). ✤
Agentic frameworks include 2AM (Hu et al., 2026), MaP-WAM (Zhao et al., 2026a), and VoLoAgent (Chen
et al., 2026a). ✤Other policy baselines include DP (Chi et al., 2025), SlotSSM (Chung et al., 2026), and
Mem-0 (Chen et al., 2026b) as visuomotor and memory-based policy references. See Appendix A for detailed
descriptions.
Implementation details. We segment demonstration episodes into subtasks via the VLM planner and initialize
executors from Long-WAM (Huang et al., 2026) RoboTwin2.0 (Chen et al., 2025) and LIBERO (Liu et al.,
2023a) checkpoints (the same architecture for real-world tasks). Policies condition on the active subtask for
RMBench and additionally incorporate the global task and stage index for LIBERO-Mem, observing 48 steps
of visual history to predict H = 32 action chunks. We train Video DiT LoRA adapters for 10k steps before

5.2
Main Results
RMBench. Across the nine memory-dependent bimanual tasks in Table 1, EmboEagle achieves 91.3% mean
success, outperforming the strongest baseline MaP-WAM (83.3%) by 8.0 points and AR-WAM (67.8%) by
23.5 points. Crucially, EmboEagle trains a single unified model across all tasks, whereas MaP-WAM trains a
separate model per task. While flat visuomotor and VLA policies (DP, π0.5, X-VLA) stay below 11%, and
memory-augmented baselines suffer sharp task-specific drops (e.g., MaP-WAM 19% on Observe and Pick Up;
HiMem-WAM 8% on Press Button; AR-WAM 16% on Swap T), EmboEagle ranks top-two on all nine tasks
(first on three) and reaches at least 89% on eight. Across memory regimes, EmboEagle attains 95.2% on M(1)
tasks, substantially ahead of MaP-WAM (75.6%) and AR-WAM (71.2%), while maintaining 86.5% on M(n)
tasks (versus 63.5% for AR-WAM and 19.8% for HiMem-WAM). On early-observation and stage-handoff
tasks such as Observe and Pick Up (89% vs. 19% for MaP-WAM) and Rearrange Blocks (100% vs. 66%),
closed-loop orchestration and 3D-memory grounding prevent early drift from compounding (Figure 3, left).
Even on contact-sensitive Battery Try, EmboEagle scores 66% (second to MaP-WAM at 82%, and 14 points
above AR-WAM), confirming consistent cross-task reliability.

gate against fixed 32-step (chunkwise) and 64-step monitoring schedules across the nine RMBench tasks.

Conclusion
We presented EmboEagle, a closed-loop agentic framework for long-horizon robotic manipulation. Using
decoupled two-stage flow matching, EmboEagle retains cross-stage visual history while calibrating boundary-
truncated supervision for subtask-aligned control. A lightweight gate distilled from teacher decisions triggers
VLM calls solely from the Action Expert’s latent transitions and subtask embeddings without auxiliary
encoders. When invoked, the VLM decouples diagnostic status from routing actions and grounds recovery
primitives in persistent geometric memory with post-repair verification. Jointly, these mechanisms unify
execution-driven supervision timing with anchored physical repair across multi-stage tasks.

A
Detailed Experimental Settings
A.1
Datasets and Preprocessing
RMBench. RMBench (Chen et al., 2026b) comprises nine bimanual manipulation tasks: five single-observation
tasks, M(1) (Observe and Pick Up, Rearrange Blocks, Put Back Block, Swap Blocks, and Swap T), and
four multi-observation tasks, M(n) (Battery Try, Blocks Ranking Try, Cover Blocks, and Press Button).
For WAM adaptation, we split the demonstrations of each task by episode at a 9:1 training-to-validation
ratio, segmenting each episode into contiguous subtask intervals conditioned on the corresponding subtask
instruction. We combine the head and wrist camera views as visual inputs and standardize joint states,
gripper states, and actions using training-split statistics.
LIBERO-Mem. LIBERO-Mem (Chung et al., 2026) comprises ten single-arm manipulation tasks spanning
three categories: two single-placement tasks, P (Bowl lift-and-return and Bottle lift-and-return), four counting
tasks, C (Bowl lift-and-return ×3, Bottle lift-and-return ×3, Bowl lift-and-return ×5, and Bowl lift-and-return
×7), and four sequencing and spatial tasks, S (Swap two bowls, Rotate three bowls, Center occupied basket,
and Center empty basket). Within each task, successful demonstrations are split by episode at a 9:1 training-
to-validation ratio and partitioned into sequential manipulation stages. Each stage pairs the scene and wrist
camera views with the global instruction, active subtask, and stage index k/n to disambiguate repeated cycles.
Real-world tasks. We collect demonstrations on a Franka robot arm equipped with a scene camera and a wrist
camera across two multi-stage tasks: garbage sorting (sequentially placing green, blue, and red blocks into
color-matched bins) and bread toasting (inserting bread slices into separate toaster slots, transferring them
sequentially onto a plate stack, and pressing the red button). Complete trajectories are split by episode at
a 9:1 training-to-validation ratio prior to extracting contiguous subtask segments. Each segment pairs the
global instruction and active subtask with both camera views, 7-DoF joint positions, and gripper opening.
A.2
Details of Baselines
We compare EmboEagle against four families of representative methods aligned with each evaluation suite,
including (i) VLA-based policies, (ii) WAM-based policies, (iii) Agentic frameworks, (iv) Other policy baselines.
➠VLA-based policies include:
• π0.5 (Intelligence et al., 2025) co-trains on diverse robot demonstrations and web data to jointly predict
semantic subtasks and continuous actions from visual-language inputs.
• X-VLA (Zheng et al., 2026) introduces learnable source-specific soft prompts within a shared transformer
backbone for scalable cross-embodiment action generation.
13


• MemoryVLA (Shi et al., 2026) retrieves visual details and semantic cues from perceptual-cognitive memory,
fusing them with live observations for diffusion decoding.
• ActMem-VLA (Zhao et al., 2026b) encodes executed action history via Mamba to guide early denoising
steps before refining action sequences with a frozen action expert.
➠WAM-based policies include:
• HiMem-WAM (Sun et al., 2026) learns hierarchical motion-skill latents and updates task memory at skill
transitions.
• AR-WAM (Jiang et al., 2026a) conditions scene dynamics and action generation on target boxes and skill
tokens.
• MemoryVAM (Jiang et al., 2026b) compresses past visual observations into compact memory tokens that
jointly condition video prediction and action decoding.
➠Agentic frameworks include:
• 2AM (Hu et al., 2026) employs a multimodal agent to track episode history and steer a low-level policy via
subtask instructions and optional 2D spatial cues.
• MaP-WAM (Zhao et al., 2026a) uses completed subtask records to generate language and visual plans that
condition next-stage action and progress prediction.
• VoLoAgent (Chen et al., 2026a) uses a VLM orchestrator to monitor execution and switch between learned
policies and manipulation tools for error recovery, sharing identical WAM weights with EmboEagle in
real-world tests.
➠Other policy baselines include:
• DP (Chi et al., 2025) generates action chunks via visual-conditioned denoising under receding-horizon
control.
• SlotSSM (Chung et al., 2026) tracks objects across frames with persistent object-centric slots and retains
per-slot temporal histories via a state-space model for action prediction.
• Mem-0 (Chen et al., 2026b) conditions actions on a subtask-start visual anchor and recent sliding-window
frames.
A.3
Details of Implementations
WAM adaptation. We initialize the RMBench and LIBERO-Mem executors from the RoboTwin2.0-IDM and
LIBERO-IDM checkpoints of Long-WAM (Huang et al., 2026), respectively. Stage 1 trains rank-32 LoRA
adapters (α = 32) on the query, key, value, and output projections of the Video DiT self- and cross-attention
layers for 10k steps at a peak learning rate of 5×10−5. Stage 2 freezes the adapted video branch and fine-tunes
all Action DiT parameters for 30k steps at a peak learning rate of 10−5, while keeping the VAE, text, and
proprioceptive encoders frozen throughout. Both stages use AdamW (β1 = 0.9, β2 = 0.95, weight decay 0.01)
with a global batch size of 32 across four 80 GB NVIDIA H100 GPUs, BF16 mixed-precision compute, and
FP32 master weights. Each stage starts an independent cosine schedule with 500 warmup steps (1,500 steps
for LIBERO-Mem action fine-tuning). Evaluating the training sampling distributions yields fixed-horizon
calibration factors (Eq. (7) of the main paper) of C = 1.14 on RMBench and C = 1.23 on LIBERO-Mem.
Gate training and calibration. We curate the distillation dataset by rolling out chunkwise teacher supervision
across successful RMBench episodes to collect labeled chunk boundaries (Skip vs. Call), grouping episodes
that share a task-seed pair or duplicate feature transitions into the same split to prevent leakage. At each
boundary, the gate pairs the 1,024-dimensional action-token transition ∆ft = f +
t −f −
t with the 512-dimensional
active subtask embedding et. Each block is standardized and ℓ2-normalized before concatenation and joint
standardization via N(·), fitting all statistics on unique training transitions. The routing gate fϕ is a single-
hidden-layer MLP (128 ReLU units, 0.2 dropout) trained for 100 full-batch epochs with AdamW (learning
14


rate 10−3, weight decay 0.01) under Eq. (6) of the main paper. On the validation split, we select the highest
logit threshold η retaining at least 97% Call recall under both uniform and inverse-frequency weighting,
yielding η = −4.71 (bt = I[rt ≥η]).
Closed-loop supervision. At each scheduled RMBench check, GPT-6-Astra receives the structured context Xt
comprising initial and current RGB views from the head and both wrist cameras, up to five temporally ordered
recent head-camera frames, the global goal G and active plan Pt, end-effector poses and gripper openings qt,
and the memory record mt (refs, held, and last_tool). Episode initialization and post-intervention verification
always enforce mandatory VLM checks (bt = 1). For fixed-interval comparisons, the WAM retains chunk
horizon H = 32 while querying the VLM every chunk (32 steps) or every second chunk (64 steps).
Real-world execution. On each physical Franka task, EmboEagle and VoLoAgent share identical task-specific
WAM executor weights for controlled comparison. Conditioned on live scene and wrist RGB views, 7-DoF
joint positions, gripper opening, and global plus subtask instructions, the executor predicts H = 32 action
steps specifying absolute 7-DoF joint targets and gripper commands.

B.2
Ablation Study on Memory
To evaluate the contribution of structured recovery memory beyond VLM visual context, we ablate the
structured memory mt across four RMBench tasks (Table 5). In w/o Structured Memory, the VLM still
observes the initial and recent frames in Vt, but assist and recovery skills execute without cached initial poses
(refs), active gripper–object bindings (held), or prior tool feedback (last_tool). Removing mt reduces mean
success across the four tasks from 97.0% to 56.8% (−40.2 points), demonstrating the importance of structured

C
Limitations and Outlook
We note two main limitations of the current system. First, although our latent-delta routing gate eliminates
74.2% of routine VLM checks, each triggered orchestration or post-intervention verification step still incurs
non-trivial communication and inference latency when querying large VLM services, constraining deployment in
strictly real-time or bandwidth-limited settings. Second, closed-loop reliability remains tied to the underlying
VLM’s embodied scene understanding and spatial reasoning capacity. While EmboEagle achieves substantial
gains over prior orchestrators under a capable backbone, substituting smaller models degrades progress
diagnosis and tool parameterization across multi-stage tasks. Together, these factors motivate post-training
or distilling embodied diagnostic and spatial grounding capabilities from frontier supervisors into compact,
on-device vision-language models, paired with asynchronous deliberation overlapped with low-level WAM
execution to narrow the lightweight-backbone gap and advance real-time responsiveness.
