Abstract
Occlusions and limited sensor range make ego-only 3D per-
ception insufficient for safe driving. Collaborative percep-
tion addresses this by enabling connected vehicles to share
complementary observations, yet it remains constrained by
tight vehicular communication budgets. While spatial se-
lection is more flexible than fixed-rate feature compression,
existing selectors remain largely sender-driven and waste
scarce bandwidth on ego-visible targets. Furthermore, ap-
plying convolutions after masking spreads isolated tokens
into their neighborhoods, silently violating low-bandwidth
constraints. To address this, we propose Spatial Elastic Con-
sensus (SEC), a bandwidth-adaptive framework centered
on bilateral demand-supply consensus. Instead of unilateral
token selection, the ego vehicle formulates a content-aware
demand prior while each collaborator evaluates its local
supply. This single-round consensus allocates the token bud-
get to critical ego-missed evidence rather than ego-visible
redundancy, without transmitting dense maps. To guarantee
exact budget compliance, an anti-dilation refine-before-mask
pipeline refines features prior to discrete token selection,
preventing receptive-field leakage. A lightweight deformable
sampler then aggregates these sparse messages with learned
local offsets, compensating for the spatial context lost to to-
ken selection and mitigating inter-agent pose drift. Evaluated
at a stringent 64 KiB budget, SEC consistently achieves the
best AP@0.7 on the simulated OPV2V and V2XSet datasets
and the real-world DAIR-V2X dataset. These results demon-
strate that combining bilateral consensus with budget-exact
transmission is key to efficient low-bandwidth collaborative
perception.
1. Introduction
Accurate 3D perception is a cornerstone of autonomous
driving. While recent LiDAR and BEV detectors have sub-
stantially advanced single-agent 3D perception [10, 23, 25,

26, 39, 43, 47], ego-only sensing remains constrained by
physical limitations such as severe occlusions and limited
sensor ranges. These limitations create extensive blind spots
that hide safety-critical objects, as illustrated in Fig. 1(a–b).
Collaborative perception (CP) [3, 11, 16] addresses this bot-
tleneck by allowing connected agents to share intermediate
bird’s-eye-view (BEV) features over vehicle-to-everything
(V2X) networks [30, 34–36]. However, broadcasting high-
dimensional BEV tensors incurs prohibitive wireless band-
width overhead. A critical challenge for real-world CP is
achieving high performance under strict per-agent commu-
nication budgets of only tens of kilobytes, which demands
highly precise token selection. Unlike fixed-rate compres-
sion, token selection must decide which spatial cells carry
the evidence the ego lacks; a small error in this decision
wastes the entire budget. At 64 KiB per collaborator, only
0.76% of the 8,448 BEV cells fit in the budget.

To reduce payloads, feature compression methods en-
code BEV maps into compact representations [8, 30], but
static encoders struggle with dynamic traffic and fluctuating
channel capacities. Alternatively, spatial selection methods
transmit only salient spatial tokens [7, 19–21]. While of-
fering greater flexibility, existing sparse selection pipelines
suffer from two coupled structural limitations. First, most se-
lectors evaluate feature confidence unilaterally [7] or via de-
coupled scores. This sender-driven design inevitably wastes
scarce bandwidth on transmitting targets the ego vehicle
has already perceived, leaving critical occluded blind zones
unassisted (Fig. 1c). Second, a pervasive architectural flaw
undermines strict budget control: applying spatial convolu-
tions after discrete token masking dilates isolated tokens into
neighboring cells. This “mask-before-refine” process silently
inflates the transmitted payload and corrupts downstream
fusion, severely limiting the efficacy of ultra-low bandwidth
transmission.
Reducing the payload alone does not resolve these issues:
supply–demand-aware fusion [33], multi-scale deformable
communication [41], and channel-wise selection [2] still
rely on largely sender-driven or separately computed spa-
tial selection, and none enforces an exact k-token support
after feature refinement. To overcome these structural limi-
tations, effective low-bandwidth collaboration requires two
coupled mechanisms: a bilateral supply–demand consensus
that precisely isolates ego-missed targets, and a refine-before-
mask pipeline that prevents budget inflation. Controlled diag-
nostics support both choices: a multiplicative demand prior
shifts tokens toward ego-missed targets, and refine-before-
mask keeps the support at exactly k cells under identical
weights.
We propose Spatial Elastic Consensus (SEC), a
communication-efficient and budget-exact collaborative per-
ception framework. As illustrated in Fig. 2, SEC relies on
two core designs: (1) Bilateral Consensus and Anti-Dilation
Selection (Engine B). The ego agent constructs a content-
aware demand map while each collaborator purifies its lo-
cal features. Their single-step Hadamard product (Fig. 1d)
isolates complementary regions and enforces exact token
sparsity via a straight-through estimator with zero dilation
leakage. (2) Stable Sparse Training and Aggregation (En-
gines C & D). We introduce foreground warm-up and gate
supervision during training to stabilize discrete selection,
and employ a lightweight 8-point deformable sampler to
aggregate the retained sparse tokens, effectively mitigating
inter-agent pose drift.
Our main contributions are:
• We propose SEC, a novel bandwidth-adaptive frame-
work that introduces a single-round bilateral supply-
demand consensus mechanism. It effectively suppresses
ego-visible redundancy and prioritizes occluded blind
spots without transmitting dense demand or supply maps.
• We design an anti-dilation refine-before-mask pipeline
that strictly locks the transmitted payload to target token
counts, eliminating post-mask receptive-field leakage and
ensuring exact budget compliance at inference.
• Extensive experiments demonstrate that SEC achieves su-
perior accuracy under stringent communication constraints.
Notably, at a 64 KiB operating knee, SEC retains near-
saturated unconstrained accuracy with only a tiny fraction
of the payload.
2. Related Work
Collaborative Perception and Ego-Side Demand Priors.
Multi-agent collaborative perception (CP) overcomes single-
vehicle line-of-sight limits via intermediate bird’s-eye-view
(BEV) feature sharing, optimally balancing representation
richness and bandwidth [30, 35–37, 44, 45]. To address se-
vere occlusions, prior studies quantify spatial ambiguity via
Bayesian deep learning [13], scene completion [1], or con-
fidence maps [7]. However, these uncertainty metrics are
predominantly exploited retrospectively at the receiver for
feature re-weighting [24, 32], failing to prevent the upstream
transmission of ego-visible redundancy. In contrast, we for-
mulate a proactive ego-side saliency map (demand) to gate
transmission, explicitly focusing scarce bandwidth on ego-
missed regions.
Communication-Efficient Collaboration. To satisfy strict
bandwidth constraints, early CP methods use coarse link-
level handshakes [20, 21]. Fine-grained methods instead
prune spatial content via sender-side top-k selection [7],
receiver-guided queries [5, 32], channel filtering [2], robust
aggregation [46], codebook-based filling [8], pragmatic com-
munication [9], or demand-supply fusion [33]. Despite this
progress, two conventions persist. Selection is usually scored
by one party, either the sender’s own confidence or a receiver-
side query, so cells the ego already observes can still con-
sume budget and the decision never becomes joint. Refine-
ment, in turn, typically follows masking, so the transmitted
support exceeds the selected cells and no longer matches
the nominal budget. SEC reverses both, forming a single
agreement between demand and supply before any token
is committed and moving refinement ahead of masking, so
the support is exactly k cells by construction. Recent work
explores temporal and spatial awareness [42], cooperative re-
construction [29], and contrastive view learning [28]. These
methods also differ in scope. Collaborator selection chooses
whom to communicate with [19–21], whereas token selec-
tion chooses what to send. They are complementary, and
SEC addresses the latter.
Sparse Deformable Alignment and Discrete Optimiza-
tion. Aggregating multi-agent features requires efficiency
and resilience to pose drift. Dense Transformers capture
global dependencies [4, 17, 18, 22, 35, 38, 40] but incur
severe complexity, while rigid grids fail under localization

Framework Overview. Our pipeline comprises four engines,
illustrated in Fig. 2. (1) Engine A, BEV encoding: It encodes
each agent’s LiDAR into a BEV feature Fj with a weight-
shared backbone and regroups the shared features into a
multi-agent tensor together with an agent-validity mask. (2)
Engine B, SEC: It identifies critical information via supply–
demand interaction and packages it for low-bandwidth trans-
mission. (3) Engine C, lightweight deformable aggregation:
It aggregates the retained tokens with an ego-conditioned de-
formable sampler. (4) Engine D, detection and optimization:
It decodes 3D boxes.

4. Experiments
4.1. Experimental Settings
Datasets and Evaluation Metrics. We evaluate on
OPV2V [36], V2XSet [35], and DAIR-V2X [44] for LiDAR-
based 3D object detection, covering simulated and real-world
scenarios. We use the official test splits for OPV2V and
V2XSet and the validation split for DAIR-V2X, for which
test annotations are not publicly released. Detection perfor-
mance is measured by per-frame Average Precision (AP) at
IoU thresholds 0.3, 0.5, and 0.7, without global re-sorting
across frames. Communication cost is counted from the
transmitted feature values and token coordinates. The main
comparison fixes the feature payload at 64 KiB per collabo-
rator. OPV2V and V2XSet scenes contain one ego vehicle
and up to four collaborators within a 70 m communication
range; DAIR-V2X contains one vehicle and one roadside
unit.
Implementation Details. We implement SEC on the Open-
COOD framework with a PointPillars backbone, a 0.4 m
BEV voxel size, at most 32 points per voxel, and C=256
feature channels. The BEV feature grid is 48 × 176 for
OPV2V and V2XSet. SEC is trained end-to-end with Adam
(learning rate 5 × 10−4, weight decay 10−4), four warm-up
epochs, and a cosine schedule. Random flip, yaw rotation,
and scaling are applied consistently to all agents. All ex-
periments run on NVIDIA RTX 4080 GPUs. At 64 KiB,
SEC retains up to 99.4% of the best observed AP using only
6.25% of the maximum payload (Fig. 6a); we use Vcomm=16
as the Pareto-optimal knee for all main results.

Ablation Studies on Consensus Design. Table 4 compares
our multiplicative gate with a residual alternative, G(i)
res =
M (i)
supply ⊙(1 + Mdemand). The two differ in what the ego can
express. In the residual, demand only scales a collaborator
token between 1× and 2×, so a cell the ego already observes
stays in the candidate set. The residual also sums the two
signals, so a strong supply can outweigh a weak demand
without either side agreeing. Multiplication is a conjunction:
a cell survives only if the ego needs it and the collaborator
supplies it, so ego-visible evidence is removed before top-k
runs and every transmitted token adds what the ego lacks.
The two designs therefore agree at the operating point but
diverge as the budget grows, since the residual spends a
rising share on redundancy it cannot veto.
Ablation Studies on Anti-Dilation Refinement. The order
of refinement and masking decides whether the budget is
enforceable. Refinement is not pointwise: a 3×3 convolution
spreads each active cell into its neighbourhood. Applied after
masking, it replaces the discrete support with that receptive
field, so the network rather than k sets the active count, and
later layers make the map dense. Refinement before masking
makes the budget an invariant, since the mask is the last
operation and exactly k cells leave the collaborator at any
depth or width. The reversed order is not a bandwidth cost
but a loss of what makes the budget binding, which is why
it stays dense after retraining and loses accuracy at every
budget (Fig. 5, Fig. 6(e)).

4.4. Qualitative Results
Visualization of Token Selection. Fig. 4 traces a V2XSet
frame with 30 GT objects and four collaborators at k=64.
Multiplication makes transmission a conjunction: a cell sur-
vives only if the ego needs it (b) and a collaborator can
supply it (c), so the product (d) drops ego-visible cells before
top-k selection and the 64 tokens (e) land on the ego-missed
targets. In this frame, detection (f) recovers 10 of the 13
objects that the ego-only model misses, and matches the
unconstrained 1024 KiB output at 1/16 the payload.
Visualization of Sparsity and Method Comparison. Fig. 5
isolates the ordering under fixed weights. Since the mask
comes last, the support stays at exactly 64 cells after every
layer; refinement first lets the convolution widen it to 8,448,
so the budget no longer bounds the payload. Fig. 7 shows
the same effect at 64 KiB on two scenes. Tokens spent on
cells the ego already sees or on background leave distant
vehicles undetected, whereas SEC places its tokens on the
right targets.
5. Conclusion
This paper has introduced SEC, a novel bandwidth-adaptive
framework that replaces sender-driven pruning with bilat-
8


eral demand-supply consensus. By systematically coupling
a single-round multiplicative gate with an anti-dilation se-
lection pipeline, SEC strictly enforces exact token budgets
while precisely isolating critical ego-missed information.
Comprehensive evaluations demonstrate that SEC substan-
tially outperforms existing methods under stringent commu-
nication constraints.
