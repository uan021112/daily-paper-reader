Introduction
Recent years have seen rapid progress in 3D shape generation,
driven by advances in generative modeling and large-scale 3D
datasets. This progress has opened up a wide range of applications,
from game and film asset creation to AR/VR content authoring and
robotic simulation. In many of these settings, the practical goal is
to recover a high-fidelity 3D mesh from reference images while
preserving faithful geometry, fine surface details, and alignment to
the observed view.
The dominant recipe for this problem is latent diffusion [Rom-
bach et al. 2022]. A variational autoencoder (VAE) is first trained
to compress 3D shapes into a latent space, and a diffusion model is
then trained to generate within that space. Following this recipe,
recent work has largely focused on how to parameterize the VAE
latent space. Existing designs can be broadly organized into vec-
set representations [Zhang et al. 2023, 2024], triplane [Wu et al.
2024], and structured sparse-voxel latents [Li et al. 2026a; Wu et al.
2026; Xiang et al. 2025]. Although these designs encode geometry
differently, they share the same premise: geometry is first com-
pressed into a learned latent representation and only then modeled
by the generator. This latent paradigm carries two limitations for
high-fidelity geometry generation. First, VAE reconstruction acts
as an information bottleneck: fine details lost during encoding can-
not be recovered by the downstream diffusion model. Second, the
two-stage pipeline introduces an objective mismatch, because the
VAE is optimized for reconstruction whereas the diffusion model is
optimized to match the learned latent distribution. The generator
is therefore constrained by both the fidelity of the learned repre-
sentation and the gap between independently trained objectives.
These observations suggest that further refinement of latent design
is unlikely to remove either limitation, and motivate us to revisit
the premise of latent compression itself.
We therefore take a different route and propose Direct3D-V3, a
latent-free diffusion transformer (DiT) framework that operates in
the native voxel domain by directly modeling raw SDF voxel grids
during both training and sampling. This places the generator in
the same signal space where geometry is ultimately evaluated, but
also makes scalability the central challenge: a dense SDF volume
grows cubically with resolution. To address this, we adopt a dual-
granularity architecture: a patch-level DiT reasons about coarse
semantics and global layout on a sparse sequence of patch tokens,
while a lightweight voxel-level DiT restores fine geometric detail at
voxel granularity. This coarse-to-fine organization reflects a broader
principle in high-resolution pixel-space diffusion [Wang et al. 2025;
Yu et al. 2025]: global structure and high-frequency detail are easier
to model when they are handled at compatible granularities. For 3D
geometry, surface sparsity makes this principle particularly effective,
since the most informative signed-distance variation lies near the
zero-level set. Concretely, the patch-level model operates on surface
patches, while the voxel-level model predicts high-frequency details
within selected patches. Together, these modules make native SDF
diffusion tractable at 15363.
Operating directly on SDF voxels introduces a new optimization
challenge, but also opens up a new form of geometric supervision.
On the one hand, even small perturbations in predicted SDF val-
ues can cause Marching Cubes to extract non-watertight surfaces
with open boundaries, a failure mode that VAE decoders tend to
suppress implicitly by learning the manifold structure of meshes.
We address this issue with an implicit manifold regularization loss
that penalizes abrupt SDF discontinuities between neighboring vox-
els, substantially reducing non-watertight artifacts in practice. On
the other hand, once the DiT predicts raw SDFs, each grid can be
converted directly into a mesh via Marching Cubes, allowing ren-
dered normal losses to supervise the SDF voxel output through
the extracted mesh. A related idea was explored by Hi3DGen [Ye
et al. 2025], but its reliance on VAE decoding for normal rendering
incurs substantial memory overhead, limiting its applicable reso-
lution to 2563. By contrast, our approach operates without such a
bottleneck, scaling to resolutions as high as 15363, and the resulting
normal supervision yields measurable improvements in both the
visual quality and geometric fidelity of the generated meshes. To
improve alignment between the conditioning image and generated
geometry, Direct3D-V3 also predicts the camera parameters of the
conditioning image as an auxiliary task, encouraging the DiT to
learn the mapping from image observations to 3D shape.
With the predicted camera and native SDF voxel output, Direct3D-
V3 can be extended to generative reconstruction. The camera sup-
plies the input view for test-time refinement, while the raw SDF
grid provides geometry that can be optimized without repeatedly
passing through a heavy VAE decoder. Building on this interface,
we first use a pre-trained normal prediction model to estimate the
normal map of the input image. We then extract a mesh from the
predicted SDF via Marching Cubes, render the mesh normal map
under the predicted camera, and optimize the discrepancy between
the rendered normal and the predicted normal. We interleave this

In summary, the main contributions of this paper are:
• We propose Direct3D-V3, a latent-free DiT framework that
trains and samples directly on raw SDF voxel grids in the na-
tive voxel domain and scales to a resolution of 15363 through
a dual-granularity architecture.
• We equip the native voxel formulation with three comple-
mentary signals: implicit manifold regularization for SDF
consistency, rendered normal supervision for surface detail,
and an auxiliary camera branch for alignment between the
conditioning image and generated geometry.
• We further show that the latent-free design extends natu-
rally to generative reconstruction, enabling effective normal-
guided test-time refinement while preserving the geometric
prior learned by the generator.
• Extensive experiments on image-to-3D generation show that
Direct3D-V3 achieves competitive or superior performance
to state-of-the-art latent-based methods, while producing
meshes with finer geometric detail and better alignment with
the input image.

Related Work
2.1
Native 3D Generation
Recent progress in 3D generation has been dominated by latent dif-
fusion pipelines, in which a variational autoencoder first compresses
geometry into a compact latent space and a diffusion model is then
trained to generate in that space [Rombach et al. 2022]. Within this
paradigm, one important line of work builds on vecset [Zhang et al.
2023] representations, which provide an implicit latent description
of 3D shape through a fixed-length set of tokens that can be decoded
into neural fields. CLAY [Zhang et al. 2024] scales this represen-
tation to controllable large-scale 3D asset generation. TripoSG [Li
et al. 2025] further combines vecset latents with large-scale rectified
flow training to improve synthesis fidelity. Dora [Chen et al. 2025a]
and Hunyuan3D 2.0 [Zhao et al. 2025] study this line from the VAE
perspective, showing that sharp edge sampling and stronger cross-
attention improve the preservation of fine geometric structures
during shape autoencoding.
Another representative family uses explicit triplane features. Di-
rect3D [Wu et al. 2024] factorizes the latent into three orthogonal
feature planes, injecting explicit positional structure into the latent
representation and making diffusion modeling easier to scale than
purely implicit token sets. Hyper3D [Guo et al. 2025] further aug-
ments the triplane latent with a low-resolution 3D grid latent, which
supplements explicit 3D information and supports higher resolution
representation with more efficient training.
A third and rapidly growing family uses sparse voxel structures
as the latent representation. TRELLIS [Xiang et al. 2025] proposes
the unified structured latent representation, which can be decoded
into multiple downstream 3D formats, including NeRF [Milden-
hall et al. 2021], FlexiCubes [Shen et al. 2023], and 3D Gaussian
Splatting [Kerbl et al. 2023]. Direct3D-S2 [Wu et al. 2026] intro-
duces spatial sparse attention, which significantly improves both
training and inference efficiency for high resolution generation.
SparseFlex [He et al. 2025b] focuses on high resolution shape model-
ing with arbitrary topology, extending sparse structured generation
to more flexible geometric outputs. Sparc3D [Li et al. 2026a] in-
troduces a sparse deformable marching cubes representation that
can efficiently convert raw meshes to high resolution watertight
ones. TRELLIS2 [Xiang et al. 2026] introduces a new sparse voxel
structure, O-Voxel, that can jointly encode geometry and appear-
ance in a compact native representation. Despite their differences
in architecture and engineering, all of these methods still rely on
compressing geometry into a learned latent space before generation.
Our work departs from this common assumption and asks whether
the latent stage can be removed entirely.
2.2
Latent-Free Diffusion Models
The idea of training diffusion models directly in data space has deep
roots in image generation. Early models such as DDPM [Ho et al.
2020], Improved DDPM [Nichol and Dhariwal 2021], ADM [Dhari-
wal and Nichol 2021], and EDM [Karras et al. 2022] all operate
directly in pixel space and establish the effectiveness of end-to-
end denoising in the original signal domain. As model scale in-
creased, however, many works shifted to latent space for efficiency.
LDM [Rombach et al. 2022] made latent compression the dominant
recipe for high-resolution diffusion, and transformer-based genera-
tors such as DiT [Peebles and Xie 2023] and U-ViT [Bao et al. 2023]
further strengthened this trend by showing that large transformer
backbones remain highly effective when operating on compact la-
tent tokens.
Recent work has returned to the question of whether latent com-
pression is necessary at all. JiT [Li and He 2025] argues that diffusion
models should directly predict clean data rather than noise-like quan-
tities, and shows that simple large patch transformers can already
become strong pixel-space generators without tokenizers or auxil-
iary losses. DiP [Chen et al. 2025b] studies how to make pixel-space
diffusion practical at higher resolution by combining a DiT backbone
with a lightweight patch detailer head, balancing global structure
modeling with local detail synthesis. DeCo [Ma et al. 2025] ob-
serves that modeling low-frequency semantics and high-frequency
details with a single transformer is inefficient, and therefore decou-
ples them using a semantic DiT, a lightweight pixel decoder, and a
frequency-aware flow matching objective. PixelDiT [Yu et al. 2025]
instead adopts a fully transformer-based dual-level design, where
a patch-level DiT captures global semantics and a pixel-level DiT
restores texture details directly in pixel space. These methods show
that latent-free diffusion can be competitive for image generation,
but they cannot be directly transferred to 3D generation, where
SDF voxel grids are sparse, memory-intensive, and coupled to mesh
extraction. SDF-Diffusion [Shim et al. 2023] provides an early 3D
counterpart by avoiding VAE compression, yet its generation is
limited to 1283 resolution, which restricts the geometric precision
of the resulting shapes. Our Direct3D-V3 extends the latent-free
principle to high resolution native 3D generation by operating on
surface SDF voxel grids and scaling to resolutions such as 15363.

2.3
Generative Reconstruction
One important line of work uses image diffusion priors to improve
image-to-3D reconstruction. Zero-1-to-3 [Liu et al. 2023] learns
viewpoint-conditioned novel view synthesis from a single image
and shows that a diffusion prior with explicit relative camera control
can already support zero-shot 3D reconstruction. SyncDreamer [Liu
et al. 2024] improves this direction with a synchronized multi-view
diffusion model that generates all target views jointly and corre-
lates them through 3D-aware feature attention, substantially im-
proving cross-view consistency. Wonder3D [Long et al. 2024] pre-
dicts both multi-view color images and multi-view normal maps
through cross-domain diffusion, then fuses these geometric cues
into textured mesh reconstruction. InstantMesh [Xu et al. 2024]
combines an off-the-shelf multi-view diffusion model with a sparse-
view large reconstruction model and a differentiable iso-surface
extraction module, enabling high-quality mesh generation in only a
few seconds. These methods significantly improve coarse structure
recovery, novel view consistency, and surface appearance, but their
optimization is still driven primarily by image-space consistency
rather than direct alignment of fine geometry with the observed
input view.
Another related line exploits video diffusion priors for view syn-
thesis and reconstruction. Stable Virtual Camera [Zhou et al. 2025]
formulates generative view synthesis as a general diffusion model
conditioned on arbitrary input views and target camera trajectories,
producing temporally smooth novel views under large viewpoint
changes. GEN3C [Ren et al. 2025] further introduces a 3D cache
built from depth predicted on seed images or previous frames, and
conditions video generation on renderings of this cache under user-
specified camera trajectories. This design yields more precise camera
control and stronger temporal 3D consistency than prior video diffu-
sion models. These methods offer richer camera-aware priors than
single-image diffusion, but their supervision still lives mainly in
image or video space and is not directly tied to mesh-level geometric
detail.
Closest to our setting are methods that place reconstruction con-
straints inside generation itself. ReconViaGen [Chang et al. 2025]
designs global geometry conditions for coarse structure, local per-
view conditions for fine geometry and texture, and rendering-aware
velocity compensation to correct the denoising trajectory toward
the input views. CUPID [Huang et al. 2025] uses a two-stage flow
pipeline: it first predicts an occupancy cube and a UV cube to es-
timate the object-centric camera, then uses a pose-aligned condi-
tioner to inject pixel-aligned image features for detailed geometry
and appearance. Pixal3D [Li et al. 2026b] generates 3D assets in a
view-aligned space to better preserve image-to-3D correspondence
and input details. Our method follows this direction, but benefits
from a simpler native interface: the latent-free DiT directly outputs

Latent-Free Diffusion Transformers for 3D Generation
Existing latent-based methods compress 3D shapes into an interme-
diate latent space, introducing an unavoidable fidelity gap between
the original geometry and what the diffusion model ultimately learns
to generate. To circumvent this fundamental limitation, Direct3D-V3
performs training and sampling directly on raw SDF voxel grids,
eliminating the VAE entirely, as detailed in Section 3.1. To balance
geometric expressiveness with computational tractability at high
resolutions, Direct3D-V3 employs two complementary DiT mod-
ules operating at patch and voxel granularity respectively, decou-
pling coarse shape semantics from fine-grained surface detail, as
described in Section 3.2. To ensure the geometric validity of gen-
erated shapes, we introduce an implicit manifold regularization
that enforces smooth SDF transitions between neighboring voxels,
detailed in Section 3.3. Since the model predicts SDF voxel grids
directly, we can also extract meshes with Marching Cubes and apply
rendered normal supervision in the geometric space, as detailed in
Section 3.4. Finally, the framework additionally predicts the camera
parameters of the conditioning image, which pushes the generated
mesh to align more closely with the input view, as elaborated in
Section 3.5. An overview of the framework is shown in Figure 2.
3.1
Latent-Free Diffusion on SDF Voxel Grids
Latent diffusion has become a practical way to reduce the cost of
high-dimensional generation, but in 3D shape generation the com-
pression stage also constrains the geometry that the diffusion model
can learn. A 3D VAE must encode detailed surface structures into
a compact representation and later decode them back to geometry,
which can discard fine local detail and introduce the reconstruction
bottleneck discussed above. The latent space is also optimized for
reconstruction fidelity rather than for the distributional objective
used by diffusion, leading to a mismatch between the two training
stages. We therefore discard the latent space and train the diffusion
model directly in the voxel domain, using raw SDF voxel grids as
the generative state.

3.2
Dual-Granularity DiT Architecture
While sparse patch tokenization substantially reduces the sequence
length for attention computation, it introduces a tension between
efficiency and geometric fidelity. A large patch size keeps the num-
ber of tokens manageable, but results in patch embeddings of high
dimensionality 𝑝3, making it difficult for a single DiT to simultane-
ously model global shape semantics and recover fine-grained surface
detail. Conversely, operating directly at voxel granularity preserves
geometric detail but renders attention computation intractable. In-
spired by the frequency decoupling strategy of DDT [Wang et al.
2025] and PixelDiT [Yu et al. 2025], which separate low-frequency
semantic content from high-frequency detail, we propose a Dual-
Granularity DiT architecture that decomposes this challenge across
two complementary modules: a patch-level DiT that models coarse
shape semantics and global layout at patch granularity, and a light-
weight Voxel-level DiT that restores fine-grained geometric detail
at voxel granularity, conditioned on the output of the patch-level
DiT. The architecture is illustrated in Figure 3.

4
Application: Generative Reconstruction
The latent-free DiT can be used not only as a generator, but also as a
native 3D prior for reconstruction. Given a single input image, stan-
dard image-to-3D generation samples a plausible complete shape
conditioned on the image, but the generated surface may still devi-
ate from the observed view in fine geometric detail. Our framework
exposes two quantities that make test-time reconstruction natural:
the DiT predicts the camera parameters of the conditioning image,
and its clean prediction is the SDF voxel grid itself. We therefore
refine the sampling trajectory directly in the SDF domain by com-
paring normals rendered from the predicted mesh with normals
estimated from the input image. This turns the generator into a
reconstruction prior: the diffusion trajectory maintains plausible
unseen geometry, while the visible surface is corrected by an explicit
geometric signal from the input view. The generative reconstruction
pipeline is illustrated in Figure 5.
4.1
Native Geometry as a Reconstruction Prior
The central advantage of our latent-free formulation is that every
clean DiT prediction can be immediately treated as geometry. Let I be
the input image, MI be its foreground mask, and Φn be a pretrained
normal prediction model such as Lotus-2 [He et al. 2025a]. We
estimate an input view normal map NI = Φn(I), which provides
a dense geometric target over the visible object region. During
sampling, the DiT predicts a clean SDF voxel grid ˆx0 together with
an initial camera estimate ˆ𝜿:
(ˆx0, ˆ𝜿) = 𝑓𝜽(x𝑡,𝑡, I).
(14)
Because ˆx0 is already a raw SDF grid, we can extract a mesh ˆM =
MC(ˆx0) and render it under ˆ𝜿without invoking a VAE decoder.
This native geometry path is what makes the reconstruction pro-
cedure practical. In a latent-based pipeline, each inner optimization
step must decode an intermediate latent representation into geom-
etry before mesh extraction and rendering, which makes iterative
test-time refinement expensive at high resolution. In our framework,
Marching Cubes and differentiable rendering are applied directly to
the DiT output, and gradients can be propagated back to the SDF
grid itself. The normal predictor supplies a view-aligned geometric
observation, the camera branch supplies the approximate image-to-
shape pose, and the latent-free SDF output supplies the optimizable
geometry. Together, these components form a closed reconstruction
loop in the native geometry domain.

the geometry in the wrong direction. We therefore first refine the

local normal ambiguity.

5.4
Generative Reconstruction Results
We further evaluate our Direct3D-V3 in the single-view generative
reconstruction setting. We compare against TRELLIS [Xiang et al.
2025], TRELLIS2 [Xiang et al. 2026], CUPID [Huang et al. 2025], and
ReconViaGen [Chang et al. 2025]. Given a single image, Direct3D-
V3 first samples a complete shape from the learned prior, uses the
predicted camera parameters to establish the input view, and refines
the native SDF prediction with normal-guided test-time optimiza-
tion. Since the refinement is applied directly to the SDF grid, the
observed surface can be corrected in the geometry domain while
the diffusion prior continues to regularize unobserved regions.
Table 3 reports reconstruction accuracy on GSO using CD, F1-
score at 0.95 and 0.99, and Volume IoU. Direct3D-V3 achieves the
best CD and F1 scores among all compared methods, while obtaining
a competitive Volume IoU slightly below TRELLIS2. Although these
results validate our reconstruction framework on a standard bench-
mark, GSO contains relatively simple scanned objects and does not
fully reveal how different methods handle complex geometry. We
therefore further compare on AI images and community images,
which contain denser local structures, richer visual styles, and more
challenging fine detail. As shown in Figure 8, CUPID, ReconVia-
Gen, TRELLIS, and TRELLIS2 recover plausible complete shapes, but
their surfaces are often over-smoothed or locally inconsistent with
the input image on thin structures, concave parts, and fine details.
Direct3D-V3 reconstructs more faithful visible surface geometry,
with stronger alignment to the input image, sharper local details,
and coherent global completion.
We also extend our framework to multi-view inputs with minimal
changes. We concatenate the DINOv2 image tokens extracted from
all views and inject them into the latent-free DiT through the same
cross-attention layers, fine-tuning the model for 20K steps. At infer-
ence time, we use the mesh generated by the TRELLIS MultiDiffusion
pipeline to obtain the surface patch indices. We evaluate this setting
on scenes from the standard Tanks and Temples dataset [Knapitsch
et al. 2017]; the qualitative results are shown in Figure 9, where
our method reconstructs meshes with sharper and more complete
surface details than competing approaches.

Conclusion and Limitations
We presented Direct3D-V3, a latent-free DiT framework that revis-
its the need for VAE-based compression in high-fidelity 3D gener-
ation. Instead of modeling compressed 3D latent representations,
Direct3D-V3 performs diffusion in the native voxel domain by di-
rectly denoising raw SDF voxel grids. To make this formulation

scalable, the model uses sparse surface patch modeling and a dual-
granularity DiT, where a patch-level transformer captures global
shape structure and a lightweight voxel-level transformer restores
local SDF variation. Beyond scalability, Direct3D-V3 makes the na-
tive SDF output geometrically reliable by constraining neighboring
SDF values with implicit manifold regularization, supervising the
extracted surface through rendered normals, and estimating the
conditioning camera with an auxiliary branch for better view align-
ment. The same native SDF voxel output also enables generative
reconstruction, where test-time normal guidance refines the ob-
served surface while the learned prior completes unseen regions.
Experiments show competitive or superior performance against
recent image-to-3D methods, together with finer local geometry.
Limitations. The current framework has two main limitations.
First, generative reconstruction depends on accurate first-stage sur-
face indices, which define the sparse SDF support used by the latent-
free DiT. If this support misses image-observed structures or places
patches in inconsistent regions, test-time normal guidance may
distort the mesh, especially around thin structures or ambiguous
silhouettes. Figure 13 visualizes this failure mode, where inaccurate
first-stage surface indices cause normal-guided refinement to over-
update the native SDF and produce distorted geometry. Improving
robustness to inaccurate sparse support remains an important di-
rection, potentially through stronger camera estimation, guidance
with uncertainty estimates, or more constrained geometry refine-
ment. Second, Direct3D-V3 currently focuses on geometry only. In
principle, the latent-free formulation can be extended beyond SDF
values by adding channels for texture, material, or PBR attributes to
the voxel grid. However, jointly modeling geometry and appearance
in this native space will require additional design to keep training
stable and to preserve consistency between surface structure and
material signals. We leave this unified latent-free geometry and
appearance generation setting for future work.
