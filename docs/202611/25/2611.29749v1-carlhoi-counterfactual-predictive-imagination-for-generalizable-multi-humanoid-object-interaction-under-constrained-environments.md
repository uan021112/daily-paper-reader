## Abstract
Recent advances in physics-based humanoid control enable teams of varying sizes to coordinate their movements and cooperatively transport shared objects. However, adapting learned cooperative behaviors to unseen environmental constraints remains challenging, particularly when obstacles require coordinated detours and changes in team configuration. We propose CarlHOI, a counterfactual predictive imagination framework built upon a counterfactual-anchored relational latent world model (CarlWM) for human-object interaction (HOI), enabling multi-humanoid teams to adapt to unseen environmental constraints without retraining their underlying cooperative control policies. CarlHOI explicitly represents the shared object, individual humanoids, and environmental obstacles as interacting entities. Trained offline using counterfactual simulator rollouts, the model imagines alternative collective futures under different shared waypoint commands, predicting how the coupled humanoid team and object would respond. By evaluating these imagined outcomes through predicted progress, failure, and contact risks, and long-horizon value estimates, CarlHOI selects adaptive guidance commands while preserving the pretrained cooperative controller. We introduce a benchmark comprising three challenging obstacle-constrained transport tasks to evaluate adaptation and generalization. Despite being trained exclusively with four humanoids, our model generalizes without retraining to teams of two to ten humanoids, unseen obstacle geometries and placements, unseen objects, and novel combinations of environmental constraints. Extensive experiments demonstrate improved cooperative transport success over evaluated baselines across diverse configurations. Results highlight the potential of counterfactual predictive imagination to extend pretrained multi-humanoid cooperative skills beyond their original training conditions.
