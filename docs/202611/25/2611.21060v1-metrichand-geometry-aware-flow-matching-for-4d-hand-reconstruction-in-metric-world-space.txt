ABSTRACT
Egocentric hand motion provides physical motion supervision for dexterous manipulation and vision-language-
action pretraining. Reliable use of this supervision requires accurate and temporally coherent 4D hand recon-
struction in metric world space. Existing methods typically lift framewise estimates produced under weak
perspective into world coordinates. By ignoring joint-dependent depth variation at close range, this approxima-
tion couples errors in translation, orientation, and articulation that persist after world lifting and worsen under
occlusion. We present METRICHAND, a geometry-aware flow matching framework for 4D hand reconstruction
in metric world space. METRICHAND encodes depth priors from hand–object interaction regions into joint-
associated representations that preserve local spatial structure and metric scale. Fused with visual features, this
geometry guides coarse metric estimation and conditions every step of temporal generation. The model jointly
recovers world-space translation, global orientation, MANO articulation, and shape, while structural priors and
temporal context support estimation under partial occlusion. METRICHAND reduces camera-space MPJPE by
49.3% and 52.9%, absolute world-space MPJPE by 28.7% and 21.7%, and wrist localization error by 48.5%
and 37.9% relative to HaWoR, respectively. World-space acceleration error decreases by 44.2% and 64.3%,
demonstrating improved temporal fidelity alongside more accurate absolute localization. Our project page is
available at https://bieber1205.github.io/MetricHand/.

INTRODUCTION
Hand motion lies at the center of how humans interact with the physical world. In egocentric video,
wrist position and orientation specify end-effector targets, while local hand pose defines the artic-
ulated configuration that can be transferred to a robot hand through retargeting. These states turn
human manipulation videos into scalable physical motion supervision for dexterous manipulation
learning and vision-language-action (VLA) pretraining (Kareer et al., 2025; Hoque et al., 2026;
Yang et al., 2025). Localization errors can shift hand–object interaction targets, and temporal jitter
can propagate into generated trajectories. These applications therefore require accurate and tempo-
rally coherent 4D hand motion in a common world coordinate system with metric scale.
Existing egocentric 4D hand reconstruction commonly combines two stages. Image-based methods
such as HaMeR and WiLoR recover detailed hand observations and visual features from individual
frames (Pavlakos et al., 2024; Potamias et al., 2025). Temporal methods such as HaWoR and Hand-
Flow combine these estimates with camera motion to reconstruct world-space trajectories (Zhang
et al., 2025; Xu et al., 2026). These approaches improve temporal consistency under moving cam-
eras, but they still depend on the image frontend to establish the geometric state of the hand in each
frame. When that estimate is biased or the hand is occluded, temporal processing must recover
motion from incomplete and potentially distorted evidence.
Metric hand reconstruction requires resolving the depth ambiguity left by weak-perspective esti-
mates and camera-normalized rays. HandFlow (Xu et al., 2026) conditions flow matching on vi-
sual features and skeletal rays, with detection confidence controlling observation strength. Stable-
Hand (Zeng et al., 2026) uses predicted observation quality to adapt the flow path and initialization,
alongside hand, camera, and scene-geometry conditions. Our focus is the spatial organization of ge-
ometric evidence: associating metric depth and nearby hand–object surfaces with individual joints
so that the generator can use these cues for both placement and articulation.
Our key insight is that a topology-aware representation of hand–object interaction (HOI) geometry
can serve as a shared condition throughout 4D hand reconstruction. Local surface structure provides
cues for recovering hand articulation under occlusion, while metric depth guides hand localization
in world coordinates. Integrating this geometric evidence with temporal memory further supports
motion consistency across frames and trajectory continuity across windows. Based on this insight,
METRICHAND fuses RGB and estimated depth for coarse metric hand estimation, then samples
appearance and surface geometry around 21 projected joint anchors. Quality weighting and tem-
poral memory organize these features across windows, and the resulting conditions guide every
flow update to jointly recover world-space translation, global orientation, articulation, and shape.
Our geometry-conditioning ablations show that maintaining this evidence throughout generation
improves reconstruction over using depth only for coarse estimation or supplying geometry only at
the first flow step (Table 3).
Under a shared protocol on HOT3D and ARCTIC, the evaluated configuration achieves the lowest
wrist localization error and absolute camera- and world-space joint errors among the reevaluated
methods. Relative to HaWoR, absolute world-space MPJPE decreases by 28.7% and 21.7%, and
world-space acceleration error by 44.2% and 64.3%, respectively. These results demonstrate im-
provements in absolute localization and motion fidelity (Fig. 1). Component and condition ablations
assess geometric representation, flow generation, and temporal memory.
Our contributions are:
• We propose METRICHAND, which associates the spatial structure and metric scale of HOI
depth priors with hand topology, providing shared geometric conditions for metric local-
ization and occlusion-robust pose estimation.
• We formulate geometry-aware temporal flow matching that combines quality-aware condi-
tions with overlapping memory to jointly generate translation, global orientation, articula-
tion, and shape.
• We achieve state-of-the-art hand reconstruction on HOT3D, ARCTIC, DexYCB, and
HO3D, as well as on unseen real-world HOI4D videos under zero-shot evaluation.

RELATED WORK
Image-based Hand Reconstruction.
MANO represents hand shape and articulation with a dif-
ferentiable parametric mesh (Romero et al., 2017). Transformer-based models such as HaMeR and
WiLoR recover detailed hands from images (Pavlakos et al., 2024; Potamias et al., 2025); Hamba
and HandOS explore alternative spatial reasoning and localization architectures (Dong et al., 2024;
Chen et al., 2025). These models provide visual features and articulation priors. Recovering metric
motion additionally requires geometric evidence that connects the reconstructed pose to its physical
placement.
Hand Motion Estimation and Generation.
Temporal methods combine image evidence with
motion priors to reconstruct hand trajectories. HMP learns a hand-motion prior (Duran et al., 2024),
while Dyn-HaMR and HaWoR recover motion under moving cameras (Yu et al., 2025; Zhang et al.,
2025). ViDiHand adapts video-diffusion representations for hand reconstruction (Wang et al., 2026).
ACE-Ego-Hand uses a deterministic clean-latent encoder and a bidirectional spatiotemporal decoder
to recover bimanual trajectories under occlusion and out-of-sight gaps (Liu et al., 2026). MINT
jointly predicts camera motion, field of view, camera-frame hand states, and observability from a
shared video representation, using large-scale pipeline supervision (Zhu et al., 2026). HandFlow
formulates hand recovery with flow matching and confidence-aware observation conditioning (Xu
et al., 2026; Lipman et al., 2023). Our focus is the explicit geometric evidence supplied to temporal
generation: topology-aware sampling associates HOI appearance, metric depth, and local surface
structure with hand joints, and the resulting conditions remain available throughout the flow process.
Metric Depth Estimation.
Monocular geometry models such as Metric3D, Metric3D v2, and
MoGe-2 recover depth and metric-scale cues from a single image (Yin et al., 2023; Hu et al., 2024;
Wang et al., 2025a). Their spatial predictions complement the appearance features of a normalized
hand crop. BLADE uses depth to address close-range perspective distortion in body reconstruc-
tion (Wang et al., 2025b). For articulated hand motion, depth provides both absolute placement cues
and the local layout of the interaction region. We fuse this evidence with RGB features, sample it
around hand joints, and preserve it across time as a shared condition for localization and temporally
coherent pose estimation. Camera-motion estimation supplies the reference needed to express this
geometry in world coordinates (Teed & Deng, 2021; Zhang et al., 2025).

METHOD
Overview.
METRICHAND uses interaction geometry to connect metric hand placement with tem-
poral pose recovery (Fig. 2). RGB features and depth priors first produce coarse hand estimates,
which are expressed in a metric world frame using the estimated camera trajectory (Sec. 3.2).
Topology-aware sampling then gathers appearance and surface geometry around the projected hand
joints. Observation quality and cross-window memory organize this evidence into joint-associated
conditions (Sec. 3.3). A conditional flow model reads these conditions at every integration step to
jointly recover world-space translation, global orientation, articulation, and shape (Sec. 3.4).

3.2
GEOMETRY-AWARE HAND REPRESENTATION
Multi-Encoders.
Following image-based hand reconstruction (Pavlakos et al., 2024; Potamias
et al., 2025), a trainable WiLoR-initialized encoder extracts hand–object appearance features F I. A
frozen monocular geometry model supplies full-frame metric depth (Hu et al., 2024; Wang et al.,
2025a), and frozen DROID-SLAM estimates relative camera motion (Teed & Deng, 2021). Depth
is aligned to each hand crop with the RGB transform. RGB–D fusion, Temporal MANO, and the
Metric Head are trained jointly, while the depth and SLAM networks remain fixed.
RGB–D Fusion.
The representation combines dense depth injection with joint-associated geomet-
ric aggregation. The depth encoder processes normalized log depth, validity, and spatial gradients
to preserve local surface layout, while absolute-depth statistics retain metric context. Depth features
are aligned with the RGB feature map and injected through a spatially gated residual:

3.3
GEOMETRY CONDITION GENERATION
The condition generator turns crop features into structured evidence. Joint anchors align RGB–D
features with hand topology, quality prediction suppresses unreliable content, and a spatiotemporal
queue links joint identities across windows. The output preserves local hand–object geometry, global
context, and temporal correspondence for the flow model.
Topology-Aware Sampling.
We construct an HOI representation indexed by anatomical joints.

Condition Encoding.
The encoder combines camera-ray cues, 2D skeletal anchors, topology-
aware HOI features, and observation quality into Cn, projecting current and queued evidence to a
shared width while retaining source, time, hand, and joint identities. Cn supplies cross-attention,
while its pooled representation ¯Cn modulates the flow blocks.
3.4
GEOMETRY-AWARE FLOW MATCHING
Direct framewise regression lacks a unified temporal update when local evidence is biased or par-
tially occluded. Building on flow matching and its use for generative hand recovery (Lipman et al.,
2023; Xu et al., 2026), we map a two-hand window to motion tokens with component, time, and
hand identities. Rotations use continuous 6D coordinates (Zhou et al., 2019), translation is normal-
ized, and shape is shared within the window. The velocity field vϕ(xτ, τ, Cn) transports Gaussian
noise toward the joint state distribution, with the coarse estimate supplied through Cn rather than
used as the initial sample.
Condition-Guided Generation.
DiT-style blocks (Peebles & Xie, 2023; Xu et al., 2026) couple
motion tokens across state components, time, and hands through self-attention, and retrieve Cn
through cross-attention. Flow time τ and pooled context ¯Cn modulate normalization and residual
gates. Every velocity evaluation reads Cn: depth guides placement, while joint-associated HOI and
memory features guide articulation and temporal consistency.

Metrics.
We report camera MPJPE and absolute world MPJPE (ABS-W-MPJPE) for joint local-
ization. WristErr is the Euclidean error of the single physical wrist point in camera coordinates.
RA-MPJPE subtracts the wrist, whereas PA-MPJPE fits a per-frame similarity transform. World
W-MPJPE and WA-MPJPE use blockwise similarity alignment fitted to the first two frames or the
full block, respectively (Zhang et al., 2025). Position errors are in millimetres. World acceleration
error, in m/s2, measures disagreement with ground-truth joint dynamics on the original timeline.
The world reference is established by rigid camera alignment, with no further hand alignment for
ABS-W-MPJPE.
4.2
COMPARISON WITH STATE OF THE ART
Baselines.
We compare against SLAM-lifted HaMeR, WiLoR, and HMP (Pavlakos et al., 2024;
Potamias et al., 2025; Duran et al., 2024), and video-based methods including Dyn-HaMR, HaWoR,
HandFlow, ACE-Ego-Hand, and MINT (Yu et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2025; Xu et al., 2026; Liu
et al., 2026; Zhu et al., 2026). Dyn-HaMR retains its native optimized world trajectory.
Quantitative Results.
Table 1 shows that METRICHAND achieves the lowest absolute joint and
wrist localization errors among the reevaluated methods on both datasets. We analyze the results in
terms of metric localization, pose accuracy, and temporal consistency.
For metric localization, ABS-W-MPJPE reaches 20.58 and 120.86 mm on HOT3D and ARCTIC,
reducing HaWoR’s errors by 28.7% and 21.7%, respectively. Wrist localization error also decreases
by 48.5% and 37.9%, indicating more accurate physical placement of the reconstructed hands.
For pose accuracy, PA-MPJPE is 3.18 and 5.18 mm on the two datasets, the lowest values in the
tables. Camera-space MPJPE reaches 8.60 and 17.54 mm, showing improvements in both similarity-
aligned pose and absolute hand geometry.

CONCLUSIONS AND LIMITATIONS
Conclusion. We presented METRICHAND, a geometry-aware flow matching framework for recon-
structing 4D hand motion in metric world space. Our key insight is to use hand–object interaction
geometry as a shared condition for metric localization and occlusion-robust pose estimation. HOI
depth and visual features guide coarse metric estimation and form joint-associated conditions that
retain spatial structure and metric scale. Quality-aware conditioning and temporal memory maintain
this evidence throughout flow generation, jointly recovering world-space translation, global orienta-
tion, articulation, and shape. Experiments on HOT3D and ARCTIC demonstrate improved absolute
localization and temporal fidelity. These results highlight the value of persistent geometric condi-
tioning for accurate and temporally coherent hand reconstruction from egocentric video.
Limitations. World-space accuracy remains dependent on the estimated camera trajectory and
monocular depth prior. Errors in DROID-SLAM rotation, translation, or recovered metric scale
propagate to the reconstructed hands and can produce substantial world-space offsets even when
camera-space pose is accurate. On ARCTIC, camera MPJPE is 17.54 mm, whereas absolute world
MPJPE remains 120.86 mm. METRICHAND achieves lower absolute world error than the compared
9


baselines, including those using the shared metric-scaled DROID trajectory. Improving camera mo-
tion and depth estimation remains necessary for more accurate global hand placement.
