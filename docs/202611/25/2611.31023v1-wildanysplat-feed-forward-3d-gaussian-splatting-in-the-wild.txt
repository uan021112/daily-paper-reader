We present WildAnySplat, a feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting framework
that enables robust novel-view synthesis from in-the-wild multi-view im-
ages. Unlike controlled captures, real-world images often contain multi-view
inconsistencies caused by transient occluders, illumination changes, and
camera ISP, which degrade the quality of existing feed-forward reconstruc-
tion methods.
To address this challenge, we introduce a student–teacher framework
that learns transient-robust and appearance-consistent 3D representations.

Introduction
Feed-forward 3D reconstruction methods have recently made sig-
nificant progress in enabling fast novel-view synthesis from multi-
view images [Jiang et al. 2025; Wang et al. 2025a]. In particular,
3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) [Kerbl et al. 2023] has emerged as
a powerful representation due to its rendering efficiency and high
visual fidelity, and various follow-ups have adapted foundational 3D
models to decode Gaussian primitives [Jiang et al. 2025; Moreau et al.
2026]. However, existing feed-forward approaches largely assume
consistent multi-view observations, limiting their applicability to
controlled capture settings.
In contrast, real-world image collections, such as those found in
internet photo datasets, exhibit substantial multi-view inconsisten-
cies. These include transient occluders (e.g., pedestrians, vehicles),
illumination changes across time, and camera-dependent color pro-
cessing. Such effects violate the assumptions of multi-view consis-
tency and lead to degraded geometry, color artifacts, and ghosting
in reconstructed scenes.
While optimization-based methods such as NeRF in the
Wild [Martin-Brualla et al. 2021] and WildGaussians [Kulhanek
et al. 2024] address these challenges through per-scene fitting and
explicit robustness mechanisms, they incur high computational
cost and are unsuitable for real-time or large-scale deployment.
Feed-forward approaches, on the other hand, remain brittle under
these conditions due to their inability to disentangle transient and
persistent scene components.
In this work, we propose WildAnySplat, a feed-forward frame-
work that explicitly models and mitigates multi-view inconsistencies
in in-the-wild images. Our method builds upon the AnySplat ar-
chitecture and introduces three key components. First, we predict
per-frame transient masks that identify unreliable regions, which
are used both to reweight training losses and to guide voxelization
fusion of Gaussians. Second, we model per-image color transforma-
tions via learned bilateral grids, enabling recovery of a consistent
canonical appearance across views. Third, we use a student–teacher
training framework with hybrid supervision, where a teacher net-
work operating on clean observations provides stable geometric
supervision to a student trained on in-the-wild inputs.
A central challenge in this setting is the absence of ground-truth
supervision for clean, transient-free observations. To address this,
we combine large-scale synthetic augmentation with pseudo su-
pervision derived from optimization-based methods. Specifically,
we generate synthetic transient artifacts and color perturbations
during training, and further leverage outputs from methods such
as WildGaussians to provide pseudo-clean images and uncertainty
maps. This hybrid supervision strategy enables our model to gener-
alize effectively to real-world in-the-wild data.
Our results demonstrate that WildAnySplat achieves improved
robustness relative to the AnySplat backbone and competitive per-
formance among current feed-forward in-the-wild methods, with
the largest quantitative gains occurring on dynamic RealX3D cap-
tures.
Our contributions are:
(1) Unified handling of in-the-wild inconsistencies in
feed-forward 3DGS. A student–teacher framework learns
transient-robust and appearance-consistent canonical 3D
representations from inconsistent multi-view images.
(2) Transient-aware Gaussian fusion via learned masks. Per-
view transient masks enable robust photometric supervision
and suppress unreliable Gaussians during voxel fusion.
(3) Spatially-varying appearance modeling with bilateral
grids. Per-view bilateral grids model cross-view appearance
variation, enabling recovery of a canonical appearance.
(4) Hybrid supervision with synthetic augmentation and
pseudo labels. Combining transient and ISP augmentation
with pseudo labels from optimization methods addresses lim-
ited paired supervision and the synthetic-to-real domain gap.
1.1
Related Work
1.1.1
Optimization-based Novel View Synthesis in the Wild. Opt-
imization-based methods have been the dominant approach for
handling in-the-wild image collections due to their flexibility in
modeling inconsistencies. NeRF in the Wild [Martin-Brualla et al.
2021] introduced per-image appearance embeddings to account
for illumination and color variation across images, with transient
regions handled by uncertainty estimation. Subsequent methods
further improved reconstruction quality, efficiency, and sparse in-
puts [Barron et al. 2021, 2023; Lin et al. 2024; Niemeyer et al. 2022;
Ren et al. 2024; Verbin et al. 2022].
Recent methods based on 3DGS explore similar approaches. Meth-
ods such as GS-W [Zhang et al. 2024], Wild-GS [Xu et al. 2024],
SWAG [Dahmani et al. 2024], and WildGaussians [Kulhanek et al.
2024] incorporate robust loss functions, visibility modeling, or uncer-
tainty estimation to handle occlusions and inconsistent observations.
SpotlessSplats [Sabour et al. 2025] and DeGauss [Wang et al. 2025c]
explicitly address distractors by separating static and dynamic com-
ponents or filtering transient artifacts during optimization. While
these methods achieve strong performance, they rely on compu-
tationally expensive per-scene optimization and are therefore not
suitable for real-time or large-scale applications.
1.1.2
Feed-forward Novel View Synthesis. Recent feed-forward ap-
proaches enable faster, generalizable reconstruction by learning
mappings from input images to scene representations. Early works
such as pixelNeRF [Yu et al. 2021] and MVSNeRF [Chen et al. 2021]
learn generalizable radiance field predictors from sparse views.
More recently, feed-forward approaches have been extended to
3DGS [Charatan et al. 2024; Chen et al. 2024; Hong et al. 2025; Liu
et al. 2025b; Moreau et al. 2026; Tian et al. 2025; Wang et al. 2025b;
Zhang et al. 2025], enabling efficient inference with high rendering
quality. Recent pose-free methods such as AnySplat [Jiang et al.
2025] and DA3 [Lin et al. 2025] demonstrate that transformer-based
architectures can jointly predict camera poses, depths and Gaussian

1.1.3
Feed-forward Novel View Synthesis in the Wild. Several re-
cent works extend feed-forward NVS methods to unconstrained
settings. GenWildSplat [Gupta et al. 2026] and Sparse-View 3DGS in
the Wild [Li et al. 2025a] address the challenge of generalization to
real-world image collections with varying capture conditions, but
focus on sparse views. WildSplatter [Fujimura et al. 2026] introduces
appearance control mechanisms for feed-forward 3DGS, while Wil-
dRayZer [Chen et al. 2026] explores self-supervised learning for
large-scale dynamic environments. Wild3A [Li et al. 2025b] further
demonstrates fast feed-forward reconstruction in dynamic scenes.
Despite this progress, existing methods typically address only sub-
sets of the challenges present in in-the-wild data. Handling transient
occluders and color inconsistencies are often treated independently,
and supervision remains limited due to the lack of ground-truth
clean observations. Notably, GenWildSplat uses a pretrained seg-
mentation network [Jocher et al. 2023] to predict transient masks,
rather than detecting occluders that are truly multi-view inconsis-
tent. WildRayZer predicts motion masks and masks input tokens
prior to entering the reconstruction network, rather than learning
unified, robust 3D reconstruction at the representational level.
Our work differs from these approaches in three key aspects. First,
our clean-teacher/in-the-wild-student training strategy maintains
more robust reconstruction and pose estimation in the presence
of inconsistent inputs. Second, we jointly model transient occlud-
ers and spatially-varying appearance from multi-view inputs via
learned masks and bilateral grids, influencing both training and
Gaussian fusion. Third, we introduce a hybrid supervision strategy
that combines synthetic augmentation with pseudo ground-truth
signals derived from optimization-based methods, bridging the gap
between synthetic and real-world data.
1.1.4
Appearance Modeling and Color Consistency. Modeling ap-
pearance variation across views is critical for in-the-wild recon-
struction. While optimization-based methods [Kulhanek et al. 2024;
Martin-Brualla et al. 2021; Wang et al. 2024] typically learn per-
image latent variables or affine color corrections jointly during
reconstruction, recent work explores harmonizing images before
or after reconstruction. PPISP [Deutsch et al. 2026] uses a learned
post-processing module for radiance field rendering that models
common photometric variations, e.g. exposure, white-balance, CRF
etc. Difix3D+ [Wu et al. 2025] enhances NVS renderings through
single-step diffusion. [Alzayer et al. 2025] employ a diffusion-based
relighting model to match images with illumination variations to
a given reference image prior to NeRF reconstruction. Similarly,
CHROMA [Shin et al. 2026] proposes feed-forward bilateral grid pre-
diction for consistent multi-view appearance harmonization prior to
scene-specific 3DGS. We build on this idea by integrating bilateral
grid prediction into a fully feed-forward 3DGS framework, allowing
us to recover canonical Gaussian colors while compensating for
per-image appearance variation.

2.1
Overview
Given a set of in-the-wild multi-view images {𝐼𝑖} exhibiting signifi-
cant cross-view inconsistencies, our goal is to predict a 3D Gaussian
representation that enables high-quality novel-view synthesis.
To address this challenge, we propose WildAnySplat, a feed-
forward 3DGS framework trained using a student–teacher paradigm.
The student network operates on in-the-wild inputs, while a frozen
teacher network processes corresponding clean observations

form of camera poses and depth predictions, guiding the student
toward more robust geometry under real-world inconsistencies.
Our framework consists of three key components: 1) feed-forward
Gaussian prediction, 2) transient-aware masking and Gaussian voxel
fusion, and 3) per-view appearance modeling via learned bilateral
grids, all trained under the student–teacher framework. An overview

2.3
Training Data
We train on a diverse mixture of synthetic and real-world datasets
following recent feed-forward methods [Jiang et al. 2025; Wang
et al. 2025a]. We use scenes from 13 public datasets: DL3DV [Ling
et al. 2024], Hypersim [Roberts et al. 2021], ARKitScenes [Baruch
et al. 2021], RealEstate10K [Zhou et al. 2018], BlendedMVS [Yao
et al. 2020], ScanNet++ [Yeshwanth et al. 2023], Co3D [Reizen-
stein et al. 2021], Unreal4K [Tosi et al. 2021], KITTI-360 [Liao et al.
2021], DTU [Aanæs et al. 2016], ETH3D [Schöps et al. 2017], Wil-
dRGBD [Xia et al. 2024], and Dynamic-RE10K [Chen et al. 2026], as
well as hold-out scenes from RealX3D [Liu et al. 2025a] and Pho-
totourism [Snavely 2006]. These span indoor and outdoor scenes,
object-centric and large-scale captures, as well as a range of geomet-
ric and appearance variations. This diversity is important for robust
in-the-wild reconstruction, as it exposes the model to varying scene
layouts, illumination conditions, and camera characteristics.
A major challenge in training robust in-the-wild reconstruction
models is the lack of paired supervision consisting of corrupted
input images and corresponding clean observations. To address this,
we generate clean and in-the-wild pairs using synthetic augmenta-
tion (transient object and camera ISP variation) and pseudo-clean
supervision from optimization-based reconstruction as follows.
2.3.1
Synthetic Transient & ISP Augmentation. To simulate tran-
sient occluders commonly observed in in-the-wild images, we aug-
ment clean training images by randomly inserting 2D objects sam-
pled from the COCO dataset [Lin et al. 2014]. Specifically, we ran-
domly select segmented foreground objects and alpha-composite
them into the training views with randomized scale, position, and
appearance perturbations. For each augmented image, we addition-
ally generate ground-truth transient masks corresponding to the
inserted objects, which are used to supervise the transient mask
prediction (Sec. 2.2.2).
In practice, we found that naively pasting segmented objects into
images produced unrealistic artifacts that were too easy for the
network to detect. To improve realism and better approximate real-
world transient occluders, we additionally apply several appearance
adaptation operations to the pasted objects, including boundary
blurring, noise injection, and affine color matching to the local
background region. These augmentations encourage the model to
learn semantic transient reasoning rather than relying on low-level
copy-paste cues.
To further improve robustness to appearance inconsistencies, we
apply randomized ISP-style color augmentations, including expo-
sure shifts, white balance changes, saturation variation, contrast
perturbations, vignetting, and local color transforms. Together, these
augmentations simulate the lighting and camera-processing varia-
tions commonly encountered in casually captured imagery. Exam-
ples of our synthetic paired training data are shown in Fig. 3.
2.3.2
Pseudo Clean Image Supervision. To help close the domain
gap between real and synthetic data, we construct an additional
dataset of paired in-the-wild and pseudo-clean images using hold-
out scenes from the Phototourism [Snavely 2006] and Dynamic-
RE10K [Chen et al. 2026] datasets. Specifically, we employ WildGaus-
sians [Kulhanek et al. 2024] and render transient-suppressed pseudo-
clean images from the resulting reconstructions. For each training
image 𝐼𝑤
𝑖, we obtain 1) a pseudo-clean target image 𝐼𝑐
𝑖rendered from
the optimized WildGaussians reconstruction, and 2) uncertainty
maps indicating unreliable or poorly reconstructed regions, e.g.
transient regions. These pseudo-clean renderings serve as teacher
supervision within our student–teacher framework, enabling the
student network to learn transient-invariant canonical scene repre-
sentations while remaining exposed to realistic in-the-wild degra-
dations. Importantly, the scenes used for pseudo-clean supervision
are disjoint from the evaluation scenes used in our experiments.
Examples of our pseudo-clean paired data are shown in Fig. 3.

Implementation Details. We train on four GPUs with 140 GB
of VRAM for 35k iterations, using AdamW optimizer and cosine
learning rate scheduler, with a peak learning rate of 2e-5 and a
warmup of 2k iterations. As in-the-wild data and heavy augmenta-
tion can destabilize training, we accumulate gradients over every 4
batches. Training datasets are sampled at random every iteration
and we use a dynamic batch-size sampler that randomly samples be-
tween 2 and 16 frames, with a maximum input resolution of 448×448
px. For the training objective, we set 𝜆𝑝ℎ𝑜𝑡𝑜= 1.0, 𝜆𝑑𝑖𝑠𝑡𝑖𝑙𝑙= 0.1, and
𝜆𝑏𝑔= 0.0001. We predict bilateral grids at a resolution of 16 × 16 × 8.
The teacher network uses pretrained VGGT weights and remains
frozen throughout training. At inference time, only the student
network is used, enabling efficient feed-forward reconstruction. Ad-
ditional implementation details, component ablations, and measure-
ments of inference runtime, peak GPU memory, and preprocessing
cost are provided in the supplementary material.
3
Experiments
Datasets. We evaluate WildAnySplat on four real-world in-the-
wild NVS benchmarks:
• RealX3D [Liu et al. 2025a]: High-quality real-world paired
multi-view captures with dynamic occluders.
• Phototourism [Snavely 2006]: Internet photo collections
with severe appearance variation and transient occluders.
• NeRF On-the-Go [Ren et al. 2024]: Casually captured scenes
containing dynamic objects and lighting changes.
• RobustNeRF [Sabour et al. 2023]: Challenging real-world
scenes with strong multi-view inconsistencies.
Following prior work, we evaluate NVS using 3, 6, 9 and 12 input
views. Performance is measured using PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS.
Baselines. We compare to recent feed-forward 3DGS methods:
• AnySplat [Jiang et al. 2025]: The backbone on which our
method is built. Extends VGGT [Wang et al. 2025a] for feed-
forward 3DGS and aggregates pixel-aligned Gaussians in a
voxel grid.
• Off The Grid [Moreau et al. 2026]: Replaces pixel-aligned
Gaussians with adaptive sub-pixel placement. Usually out-
performs AnySplat, but uses colors from source images for
Gaussian colors, making it weak under appearance variation.
• WildSplatter [Fujimura et al. 2026]: Jointly predicts Gaus-
sians and embeddings for unconstrained images, enabling
flexible modulation of Gaussian colors for appearance control.
• GenWildSplat [Gupta et al. 2026]: Predicts depth, cameras,
and Gaussians in a canonical space from sparse, unposed
images. Incorporates learned appearance and semantic seg-
mentation to handle illumination changes and transients.

Quantitative results are presented in Table 1. WildAnySplat achieves
competitive performance across the evaluated datasets and input-

on Phototourism and in sparse-view settings. Qualitative compar-

Robustness to Real-World Variation. WildAnySplat achieves its
largest quantitative gains on the RealX3D dataset. At 9 views, it
achieves a PSNR of 19.83 dB, approximately 2 dB above the next-best
method, WildSplatter, while obtaining an LPIPS of 0.270.
Backbone Improvement. Compared with its AnySplat backbone,
WildAnySplat improves most evaluated metrics and settings. For
instance, on NeRF On-the-Go with 12 input views, WildAnySplat
improves PSNR from 14.14 dB to 15.24 dB relative to AnySplat. The
component ablation in the supplementary material further examines
the individual contributions of transient-aware fusion and bilateral-
grid appearance modeling.

number of input views. Off-the-Grid is competitive in sparse-view
settings, such as the 3-view RobustNeRF evaluation. WildAnySplat
benefits from additional input views and becomes more competitive
on RobustNeRF and NeRF On-the-Go, consistent with the use of
cross-view evidence for transient suppression.
Challenging Illumination. On the Phototourism benchmark,
which is characterized by extreme appearance changes, WildAnyS-

comparable PSNR (e.g. 12.57 dB at 3 views).

4
Limitations
Despite strong performance, WildAnySplat has several limitations.
First, although our model improves robustness to distractors, it
can still struggle in scenes containing large or persistent dynamic
regions. When moving objects dominate the scene, pose and recon-
struction quality can degrade due to insufficient static observations.
Second, our bilateral-grid appearance model primarily captures
low-frequency spatially-varying color transformations. Extreme
illumination changes, strong view-dependent effects, or highly non-
linear camera processing may not be fully modeled, leading to resid-
ual appearance inconsistencies.
Third, we rely on optimization-based methods to generate teacher
supervision for real-world scenes. Although this supervision is com-
puted offline, generating pseudo-clean ground truth introduces ad-
ditional preprocessing cost and the student may inherit biases or
artifacts from the teacher.
Finally, performance remains limited on highly challenging scenes
with severe multi-view inconsistencies. Addressing these cases may
require stronger geometric reasoning or explicit dynamic-scene
modeling.

5
Conclusion
We have presented WildAnySplat, a feed-forward framework for ro-
bust 3DGS from in-the-wild images. Our method addresses two key
challenges in unconstrained multi-view reconstruction: transient
occlusions and cross-view appearance variation.
To achieve this, we introduced transient-aware masking for robust
Gaussian aggregation and supervision, bilateral-grid appearance
modeling for canonical color reconstruction, and a student–teacher
training framework that leverages clean supervisory signals derived
from synthetic augmentation and optimization-based methods.
Experiments on challenging in-the-wild datasets show that the
proposed components improve the robustness of the AnySplat back-
bone and provide competitive performance among feed-forward
3DGS methods across varied capture conditions.
