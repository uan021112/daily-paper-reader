Abstract
Active 3D reconstruction aims to allocate a sparse view
budget to recover complete geometry and high-fidelity ren-
derings. However, existing methods degrade sharply in multi-
room homes: myopic next-best-view scoring gives transit no
credit for distant rooms and over-polishes irreducible nearby
residuals, whereas dense mappers reach far rooms only by

1. Introduction
Active 3D reconstruction lets an embodied agent build geo-
metric and visual models of an unfamiliar scene by choosing
where to observe [9, 14, 18, 26, 42]. In multi-room homes
connected by doorways and corridors, the agent must allo-
cate a sparse view budget online across the entire house.
Existing methods select views by coverage, uncertainty,
or information gain [4, 14, 15, 18, 28, 40–42, 50], learn
policies from prior scenes [3, 5, 12, 25], plan hierarchi-
cally [26, 43], or optimize continuous trajectories [51]. In
multi-room homes, they face a dilemma between view spar-
sity and house-scale coverage (Fig. 1). Sparse view planners
are efficient in single rooms but stall across houses, leaving
distant rooms unreconstructed. Dense mappers reach those
rooms only by streaming frames along the entire trajectory,
at the cost of tens of thousands of images.
Sparse planners misallocate views in multi-room homes
for two reasons. First, myopic step-wise scoring gives cor-
ridor transit no credit for the value of its distant destination
and replans at every step, so a robot that has rebuilt its living
room keeps polishing the sofa and leaves the next room un-
visited (Fig. 1, top). Second, residual magnitude overstates
what new views can fix, as glossy or finely detailed surfaces
keep high rendering errors long after optimization stops im-
proving them. As a result, distant rooms are abandoned too
early and nearby defects are pursued too long. How can an
agent reconstruct all rooms of a home under a sparse view
budget?
We propose ROAM-GS, an active reconstruction frame-
work that decouples where the agent heads from where it
captures (Fig. 2). Each decision selects a goal, i.e., a target
region with a viewpoint that observes it, and a prefix of the
path toward it; the agent traverses the prefix without sensing
and captures only at its endpoint. A 3D Surface Recon-
struction Memory records post-update defects and viewing
directions on surface cells, so that endpoints, routes, and
goals are scored by geometric projection without rendering.
Persistent goals resolve both causes of misallocation. The
selected goal stays active across decisions and its transit
prefixes inherit the value it still offers beyond the route, so
distant rooms are reached; the goal is released only when an
arrival measurably improves its target region, so irreducible
residuals stop attracting views. Selective views avoid dense
streaming: long prefixes cross corridors for a single capture,
short prefixes inspect locally, and surfaces passed along the
route are credited without being captured.
Our contributions are threefold:
• Persistent goals with selective views. We propose a
training-free multi-room reconstruction framework that
jointly selects a goal and a variable-horizon path prefix un-
der one score, holds the goal until measured improvement
releases it, and captures only at prefix endpoints.
• Surface Reconstruction Memory. We introduce a 3D
memory of post-update defects and viewing directions that
scores endpoints, virtual routes, and goals by geometric
projection, without Gaussian rendering.
• State-of-the-art multi-room reconstruction. Without
per-scene tuning, ROAM-GS raises mean coverage from
74% to 93% on HSSD [22] and from 47% to 96%
(+11.7 dB PSNR) on HM3D [33], matches 90-minute
dense mapping using 173× fewer views, and preserves
single-room Replica fidelity [37].
2. Related Work
View Utility for Active Reconstruction. Differentiable
radiance fields [30, 31] and Gaussian representations [8, 10,
13, 21] support online dense mapping [20, 29, 38, 39, 45,
53]. Active reconstruction builds on such maps and scores
candidate views by geometric coverage [7, 48], volumetric
information gain [14], uncertainty [9, 16, 27, 32, 34, 46,
49, 50], Gaussian confidence or information gain [4, 15, 18,
28, 40, 41], or learned and semantic priors [5, 11, 12, 17,
42]. Some methods also account for viewing directions [18]
or redundancy between views [28, 51]. However, these
methods value individual candidate views, whereas a sparse
multi-room planner must also value the route toward a view
and the distant goal it serves. ROAM-GS records post-update
defects and viewing directions in 3D cells, scoring endpoints,
routes, and goals from one Surface Reconstruction Memory
by geometric projection without Gaussian rendering.
Long-Horizon Exploration and Planning. Frontier ex-
ploration [44], receding-horizon and sampling-based plan-
ners [1, 23, 36], and hierarchical frameworks [2, 52] plan
beyond individual views. Active neural and Gaussian map-
ping systems adopt graph-, sampling-, or hierarchy-based
exploration [19, 24, 26, 43, 47], and TRACE [51] optimizes
continuous ergodic trajectories. Learned planners predict
long-term goals [3, 6], and NextBestPath [25] predicts the
surface coverage accumulated along paths, trained on prior
scenes. Under a sparse view budget, a planner must decide
where along the route to capture and when to abandon a
goal. ROAM-GS holds goals until measured improvement
releases them and captures only at variable-horizon prefix
endpoints, without offline policy training.

3.2. Surface Reconstruction Memory
Both terms of S must predict which unresolved surfaces
an unvisited pose would see and from which direction. An
uncertainty map rendered in a single image plane cannot an-
swer this, since it is tied to the pose it was rendered from and
records no viewing directions. ROAM-GS therefore stores
reconstruction quality on the surfaces themselves, in a mem-
ory Mt of 3D surface cells into which the backprojected
depth points of each capture are indexed (Fig. 2).

decisions, and releases it only when measured feedback
shows that further captures stop helping.

3.4. Selective Views
Along the route to a fixed goal, consecutive frames ob-
serve heavily overlapping content, much of it already re-
constructed, so capturing continuously or after every short
motion spends the view budget on redundant frames. Se-
lective views instead choose how far to travel before each
capture and credit uncaptured content along the route.
Variable-horizon prefixes.
Let Pg be the collision-free
waypoint path from Tt to qg planned on Vt. Truncating it
at a set of horizons H gives the prefixes of Eq. (2), Π(g) =
{Pg[1: min(h, |Pg|)]}h∈H. A long prefix crosses an entire
corridor or room for the cost of one capture, whereas a short
prefix keeps the agent close by for local inspection. Since
the prefixes of every goal in CΓ
t compete under the same
score, capture spacing adapts to the scene geometry without
a hand-crafted switch between exploration and inspection.

4. Experiments
4.1. Implementation Details
Platform and sensor.
We implement ROAM-GS in the
Habitat simulator [35]. The agent controls its 3D position,
yaw, and pitch with fixed roll, and each capture uses one
pitch angle selected from {−60◦, 0◦, 60◦}. The RGB-D
sensor has a 60◦× 60◦field of view and retains depth mea-
surements in D ∈[0.1, 5] m. Following ActiveGS [18], the
mapper runs 10 optimizer updates per capture, and ROAM-
GS integrates every capture into Gt. All experiments run on
a single NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU.

5. Conclusion
We presented ROAM-GS, a training-free active reconstruc-
tion framework for multi-room homes that decouples where
the agent heads from where it captures. A Surface Recon-
struction Memory records post-update defects and viewing
directions on 3D surface cells and scores endpoints, routes,
and goals by geometric projection without rendering. Persis-
tent goals credit transit with the value of distant destinations
and are released only when arrivals measurably improve their
target regions, while selective views capture only at the end-
points of variable-horizon path prefixes. Without per-scene
tuning, ROAM-GS achieves state-of-the-art multi-room com-
pleteness and rendering quality on HSSD and HM3D, pre-
serves single-room Replica fidelity, and surpasses 90-minute
dense mapping within 20 minutes using 165× fewer images.

6. Limitations
All evaluated scenes lie on a single floor. ROAM-GS plans
paths in three dimensions, but our experiments leave open
how well it can maintain a goal on another floor while navi-
gating stairs.
Our experiments also assume a static layout. If a door
closes after the agent passes through it, the occupancy grid
may still mark the doorway as traversable. A moved object
can likewise leave outdated geometry and viewing history
in the map. Handling these changes would require detecting
the affected regions and updating the Gaussian map, the
occupancy grid, and the Surface Reconstruction Memory
together.
