## Abstract
Even in multi-camera setups, occlusions can leave individual body joints with only a single usable 2D observation. Such an observation defines a viewing ray but leaves the joint's depth undetermined. We introduce Body of Evidence (BoE), a multi-view 3D human pose estimator that predicts per-camera joint depths alongside 2D joint locations. To reduce monocular depth ambiguity, we propose geometry-conditioned depth prediction, which combines image features with previously reconstructed 3D body geometry as contextual depth cues. We further introduce learnable depth-aware triangulation (LDAT), which augments differentiable algebraic triangulation with predicted depth constraints, enabling reconstruction even from a single contributing camera while preserving the benefits of geometric triangulation when multiple views are available. The reconstructed 3D poses subsequently guide depth prediction in the next iteration, allowing depth estimates and 3D geometry to progressively refine each other. Experiments on CMU Panoptic demonstrate state-of-the-art in-domain performance and superior average performance across evaluated camera subsets and unseen camera arrangements. BoE is particularly effective under sparse camera coverage, outperforming the previous state of the art by 10.6 AP25 points in the challenging three-camera setting.
