Abstract
Text-conditioned 3D indoor scene generation requires lay-
outs satisfying geometric, functional, and semantic con-
straints. However, existing diffusion methods rely primarily
on static textual conditions, limiting their ability to assess
evolving scenes and translate semantic discrepancies into
executable object-level corrections. MLLM-based post-hoc
refinement provides semantic feedback but remains decou-
pled from denoising, potentially disrupting scene coher-
ence. We propose ReflectScene, an MLLM-guided closed-
loop diffusion framework. First, we introduce Hierarchi-
cal Prior-Grounded Scene Understanding that aligns struc-
tured layouts with multi-view visual evidence, to interpret
object-specific context and scene global semantics via a
frozen multi-modal large language model (MLLM). Sec-
ond, we propose MLLM-Guided Semantic Reward Eval-
uation to distill MLLM’s diagnoses and assessments on
possible candidate corrections into a lightweight Reward
Predictor, enabling correction decision making with re-
duced computation burden. Finally, Decision-Conditioned
Trajectory-Consistent Diffusion is proposed to integrate se-
lected corrections through transient conditioning and per-
sistent clean-estimate projection, correcting object layout in
the generated scene while preserving non-target arrange-
ments throughout denoising. Experiments on 3D-FRONT
demonstrate improved functional consistency and semantic
alignment while maintaining competitive generation quality
and diversity.
1. Introduction
Text-conditioned 3D indoor scene generation aims to syn-
thesize realistic furniture layouts from natural language,
with applications in virtual reality, game development, and
embodied AI [9, 18, 30]. Beyond geometric plausibility, re-
alistic scenes must satisfy functional and semantic require-
ments, including meaningful spatial relationships and con-

textual dependencies [18, 39].
As illustrated in Fig. 1, recent learning-based approaches
have achieved promising results in indoor scene synthe-
sis. DiffuScene [28] employs denoising diffusion to jointly
model furniture attributes, while InstructScene [19] incor-
porates semantic graph priors for controllable generation.
These methods primarily rely on static textual conditions or
predefined structural priors, and present limited ability to
reassess intermediate denoising states and correct emerging
semantic discrepancies. Consequently, generated layouts
may remain geometrically plausible while failing to satisfy
the functional and contextual intent of the input description.
Meanwhile, multimodal foundation models provide rich
semantic and functional knowledge for scene understanding
and reasoning [3, 15, 40]. Function2Scene [29] combines
geometric measurements, LLM reasoning, and VLM as-
sessment, while SceneReVis [38] employs vision-grounded
diagnosis and executable actions for iterative scene refine-
ment.
Despite these advances, semantic reasoning and
diffusion-based generation remain largely decoupled: static
conditions cannot respond to evolving semantic discrepan-
cies, while post-hoc corrections are not inherently propa-
gated through subsequent denoising. Bridging this gap re-
quires reliable intermediate scene understanding, context-
aware evaluation of candidate corrections, and persistent in-
tegration of accepted object-level decisions into the gener-
ative trajectory, without unnecessarily disrupting satisfac-
tory scene content.
Moreover, repeatedly invoking large
MLLMs to evaluate multiple candidates introduces substan-
tial computational overhead.
In this paper, we present ReflectScene, an MLLM-
guided closed-loop diffusion framework that integrates
semantic understanding, reward evaluation, object-level
decision-making, and corrective generation into a single
reverse-diffusion trajectory.
To achieve this goal, it re-
quires faithfully representing the evolving layout, efficiently
evaluating context-dependent candidate corrections, and
integrating selected corrections into the reverse-diffusion
trajectory without disrupting satisfactory scene content.
To represent the intermediate layout, we develop Hierar-
chical Prior-Grounded Scene Understanding, which com-
bines structural-semantic alignment, multi-view visual su-
pervision, and geometry-aware metric learning to produce
object-specific context and global scene representations. To
make candidate evaluation tractable, we introduce MLLM-
guided Semantic Reward Evaluation and Object-Level De-
cision, which distills a frozen MLLM evaluator into a
lightweight Reward Predictor that estimates prompt align-
ment, functional coherence, and semantic consistency while
retaining candidate-specific scene understanding. To main-
tain the effect of selected corrections during generation, we
develop Decision-conditioned Trajectory-consistent Diffu-
sion, which feeds accepted decisions back into the reverse-
diffusion trajectory through transient guidance and persis-
tent correction constraints, while preserving non-target at-
tributes.
These components are interleaved at denoising
steps, and corrections accumulated within each buffering in-
terval are then executed sequentially, enabling progressive
refinement along a single reverse-diffusion trajectory.
Our main contributions are summarized as follows:
• We introduce ReflectScene that integrates semantic as-
sessment and executable object-level corrections into a
single reverse-diffusion trajectory, bridging the gap be-
tween static conditional generation and decoupled post-
hoc refinement.
• We

2. Related Work
2.1. 3D Indoor Scene Synthesis
3D indoor scene synthesis aims to generate plausible fur-
niture arrangements with semantic coherence and spatial
consistency. Early approaches primarily focus on learning
object-centric scene distributions. For example, ATISS [22]
models indoor scenes as unordered object sets using au-
toregressive transformers, while DiffuScene [28] employs
denoising diffusion to jointly generate object semantics,
spatial attributes, and geometric features. Such generative
representations[13, 36] enable flexible modeling of diverse
furniture configurations and scene compositions.
Recent studies[10, 20, 37] further incorporate struc-
tural priors to improve layout plausibility and control-
lability.
For instance, InstructScene [19] introduces se-
mantic graph priors to capture object relationships, while
MMGDreamer [35] employs mixed-modality graphs for
geometry-controllable generation. SemLayoutDiff [26] fur-
ther incorporates architectural constraints into semantic lay-
out diffusion. These advances [6, 7, 24, 34] enhance the
modeling of object interactions and spatial dependencies.
Nevertheless, these approaches largely rely on learned dis-
tributions or predefined priors, with limited explicit feed-
back on evolving scene configurations.
2.2. Feedback-Guided Scene Refinement
Improving spatial and functional plausibility has motivated
various guidance and refinement strategies.
One direc-
tion incorporates explicit physical constraints into gener-

2.3. Multimodal Priors for 3D Understanding and
Generation
Pretrained multimodal models offer rich semantic knowl-
edge, motivating efforts[14, 23, 27, 32] to connect 3D repre-
sentations with language models. For example, PointLLM
[31] aligns point-cloud features with language representa-
tions for 3D object understanding, while 3D-LLaVA [5]
introduces a dedicated 3D connector for scene-level multi-
modal reasoning. These approaches[1, 2, 4] facilitate the in-
tegration of geometric information with pretrained semantic
knowledge.
Beyond understanding, pretrained multimodal priors
have also been explored for generative modeling. For in-
stance, RepFusion [21] incorporates MLLM priors into
diffusion-based image generation through dynamic condi-
tioning on evolving visual representations, highlighting the
potential of state-dependent multimodal guidance. How-
ever, translating multimodal semantic assessments into pre-
cise and executable geometric corrections remains underex-
plored.
3. Method
ReflectScene formulates text-conditioned 3D scene gen-
eration as a closed-loop process, in which intermediate
layouts are interpreted, corrected and fed back into the
same reverse-diffusion trajectory.
To achieve this goal,
ReflectScene is composed of three interconnected compo-
nents: Hierarchical Prior-Grounded Scene Understanding,
MLLM-guided Semantic Reward Evaluation and Object-
Level Decision,
and Decision-Conditioned Trajectory-
Consistent Diffusion.
As illustrated in Fig. 2, given a text prompt and an ini-
tial noise state, the diffusion model progressively gener-
ates a scene layout. At each denoising step checkpoint, the
Hierarchical Prior-Grounded Scene Understanding module
constructs structured object-specific context representations
and global scene representation of the intermediate lay-
out. These hierarchical representations are then passed to
MLLM-guided Semantic Reward Evaluation and Object-
Level Decision to evaluate candidate corrections and se-
lect an object-level correction. The Decision-conditioned
Trajectory-consistent Diffusion module then incorporates
the selected decision into subsequent denoising while pre-
serving accepted corrections along the reverse-diffusion tra-
jectory.

standing

intermediate layout before selecting an object-level correc-
tion, requiring a representation that jointly captures object
semantics and geometry and the semantic priors needed for
functional evaluation. To this end, we introduce a Hierar-
chical Prior-Grounded Scene Understanding module, which
first constructs a set of hierarchical representations of the
intermediate layout and then adapts it to a frozen MLLM
through complementary semantic and metric feature paths.
The resulting representation supports candidate-specific re-
ward evaluation and object-level decision making. More-
over, a multi-view alignment pretraining scheme is intro-
duced for facilitating the adaptation of scene representations
to MLLM’s hidden space.

3.2. MLLM-guided Semantic Reward Evaluation
and Object-level Decision

Object-Level Decision module, which distills the MLLM’s
candidate-level evaluations into a lightweight Reward Pre-

subsequent denoising.

4.4. Models with Different Capabilities
Tab. 6 compares different reasoning strategies and MLLM
backbones to investigate their impact on scene generation.
Adapter-only and Geometric Algorithm achieve compara-
ble performance to Qwen2-VL-2B, suggesting that bypass-
ing contextual MLLM reasoning or relying on geometric
heuristics provides limited benefits. Increasing backbone
capability to Qwen2.5-VL-3B and 7B yields substantial im-
provements across all metrics, highlighting the importance
of effective semantic and functional reasoning. However,
scaling further to Qwen3-VL-32B brings only marginal
gains over 7B, indicating diminishing returns as stronger
reasoning capabilities approach the demands of structured
indoor scene generation. This suggests that effective se-
mantic reasoning, rather than model scale alone, is crucial
for improving scene generation.

We presented ReflectScene, an MLLM-guided closed-
loop diffusion framework integrating hierarchical scene

serving corrections. Experiments on 3D-FRONT show im-
proved functional consistency and semantic alignment with
competitive generation quality and diversity.
