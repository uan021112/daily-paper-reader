DeepSoMo: Deformable Object Motion Estimation under
Proprioceptive Partial Observability via Predictive Latent Dynamics
Hanwen Zhao, Weicheng Dai, Luoyao Chen, Chen Feng*
https://ai4ce.github.io/DeepSoMo
Abstract—Estimating the motion of deformable bodies in soft
robotics from proprioceptive sensing is challenging due to partial
observability, as internal observations provide only indirect access
to global motion, contact, and shape evolution. In such settings,
instantaneous observations are insufficient, and accurate estima-
tion requires temporally consistent latent representations. This
paper studies deformable motion estimation under proprioceptive
partial observability from a latent dynamics perspective. We
argue that a key requirement for long-horizon estimation is
latent temporal consistency, where latent states remain predictive
of future observations. To this end, we present DeepSoMo,
a learning-based framework that enforces predictive structure
in latent dynamics through a self-supervised latent prediction
constraint. We evaluate the approach in a canonical deformable
dynamics setting using high-fidelity finite-element simulation as a
controlled source of ground truth. Results demonstrate consistent
improvements in trajectory estimation, orientation recovery,
shape reconstruction, and contact inference over architecture-
matched recurrent baselines.
Index Terms—Modeling, Control, and Learning for Soft
Robots, Deep Learning Methods, and Representation Learning.
I. INTRODUCTION
State estimation is fundamental for robot control, planning,
and interaction, but it is especially challenging for soft and
deformable robots. Unlike rigid robots, whose states can often
be approximated by low-dimensional pose and joint variables,
deformable robotic bodies require estimating coupled motion,
contact, and shape evolution under nonlinear deformation. This
challenge is amplified under proprioceptive sensing, where
embedded sensors observe the body only indirectly and may
provide limited access to global pose or external contacts.
Such sensing is attractive in planetary, subterranean, cluttered,
or visually degraded environments, where external cameras,
markers, and global localization may be unreliable. The re-
sulting problem is deformable-body motion estimation under
proprioceptive partial observability: global motion, orientation,
contact, and deformation history must be inferred from incom-
plete internal observations.
Soft or compliant spherical robots provide a motivating
embodiment for this problem. Spherical robots have long been
studied as robust mobility platforms for harsh and unstructured
environments because their sealed bodies provide omnidi-
rectional mobility, impact tolerance, environmental isolation,
and resistance to overturning [1]–[3]. Most existing spherical
robots rely on rigid or hard shells, which simplify modeling
and control but limit passive adaptation to uneven terrain.
Recent inflatable or pressurized spherical designs suggest
that compliance can improve impact absorption and terrain

In robotics and machine learning, significant progress has
been made in inferring physical dynamics from exteroceptive
visual observations, particularly for rigid-body systems [6]–
[8]. Extending such approaches to deformable systems un-
der proprioceptive sensing remains challenging due to high-
dimensional state spaces, nonlinear material behavior, and
complex contact dynamics.
Recent work in soft robotics has explored learning-based
modeling of deformable systems, including particle-based
simulators [8] and vision-based proprioception for recovering
high-fidelity static deformable shapes using embedded cam-
eras [9]. While these approaches demonstrate that internal
observations can encode rich geometric information, they
focus on static or near-static configurations and do not ad-
dress dynamic motion estimation, where translation, rotation,
contact, and shape evolution are tightly coupled over time.
A central difficulty in dynamic deformable motion analysis
is partial observability. Proprioceptive visual observations
reflect only local deformations and provide no direct reference

to global pose or external contacts. As a result, instantaneous
observations are insufficient to recover the underlying physical
state, and accurate estimation requires aggregating information
over time. Although recurrent models are commonly used
for temporal aggregation [6], unconstrained latent dynamics
in deformable systems often suffer from error accumulation
and latent drift. Prior work in visual dynamics suggests
that latent representations optimized only to explain current
observations are insufficient for capturing long-term temporal
evolution [7], [10]. These observations motivate the need for
latent representations that are explicitly constrained to remain
predictive of future observations, which we refer to as latent
temporal consistency.
This paper studies deformable motion state estimation under
proprioceptive partial observability through latent temporal
consistency. We argue that enforcing predictive structure in
latent dynamics is a key modeling principle for improving ob-
servability and robustness in deformable systems, such as soft
spherical robots operating in rough or uncertain environments.
As illustrated in Fig. 1, we evaluate this principle in a
canonical deformable dynamics setting inspired by soft spher-
ical robots, where global motion, contact, and shape evolution
must be inferred solely from internal visual deformation. The
simulated body is used as a controlled testbed for isolating the
estimation problem before deployment on physical soft robotic
hardware. High-fidelity finite-element simulation generates
ground-truth motion and shape data for evaluation. Across
this setting, we demonstrate that enforcing latent temporal
consistency yields consistent improvements in trajectory esti-
mation, orientation recovery, shape reconstruction, and contact
inference compared to architecture-matched baselines.
To address the challenges of deformable motion estimation
under proprioceptive partial observability, we present Deep-
SoMo as a concrete instantiation of the modeling principles
outlined above. Our contributions are as follows:
(1) We identify latent temporal consistency as a key require-
ment for estimating the motion states of deformable bodies
from proprioceptive visual observations. We show that rich
contact and deformation cues captured by internally mounted
cameras can support accurate inference of global motion,
shape evolution, and contact states when temporal coherence
is properly enforced.
(2) We formulate deformable motion analysis as a sequential
latent state estimation problem under partial observability,
where instantaneous observations are insufficient to recover
the underlying physical state. By explicitly modeling temporal
dependencies in the latent space, our approach enables long-
horizon estimation of coupled translation, rotation, shape, and
contact dynamics from proprioceptive inputs.
(3) We introduce a self-supervised latent prediction con-
straint that regularizes the evolution of latent dynamics by
enforcing consistency between current latent states and future
observations. This constraint encourages the latent representa-
tion to function as a predictive internal state without requiring
explicit physical supervision. As a result, the learned latent
dynamics exhibit reduced drift and improved long-horizon
stability, leading to more robust deformable motion estimation
compared to unconstrained recurrent baselines.
II. RELATED WORKS
Visual Motion Estimation and Physical Reasoning. Prior
work on visual motion understanding largely falls into two
categories: visual prediction and learning physical dynamics
from observations. Early data-driven approaches focused on
predicting motion events or outcomes from visual inputs, such
as nearest-neighbor retrieval for event prediction [11] and
recurrent models for sequence modeling and visual forecasting
[6], [12], [13]. While these methods demonstrate that tempo-
ral visual observations can encode motion information, they
typically emphasize appearance-level prediction rather than
explicit state estimation.
A complementary line of work studies learning physical
reasoning and dynamics from visual observations. Battaglia
et al. [7] introduced intuitive physics models that reason
about object stability and interactions, while subsequent works
explored estimating physical parameters or latent states un-
derlying Newtonian dynamics from images or videos [14]–
[16]. Closest to our setting, Fragkiadaki et al. [17] proposed
learning predictive models of physical dynamics directly from
raw visual input in rigid-body environments, and Ehrhardt et
al. [18] demonstrated that neural networks can implicitly re-
cover physical states from visual data. These approaches focus
on rigid-body systems observed through exteroceptive vision
and do not address deformable dynamics or proprioceptive
partial observability.
Soft Robot Proprioception. Proprioceptive sensing is
widely used in soft robotics to estimate deformation, contact,
and interaction forces. Prior work has embedded stretchable
or compliant sensors into soft robots to measure bending
curvature, force, or tactile information [19]. Examples include
ionogel-based soft sensors integrated via 3D printing [20] and
internally illuminated elastomer foams for measuring bending
and twisting [21]. While effective for specific designs, these
approaches typically rely on low-dimensional representations
and are constrained to predefined workspaces.
Deformable Object Modeling. Modeling deformable ob-
jects has long been a challenge in computer graphics and
robotics due to their high degrees of freedom and nonlinear
material behavior. Classical approaches rely on explicit geo-
metric or physical representations, including implicit surfaces
and curves [22]–[24] and parametric spline-based models [25],
[26]. Surveys such as [19] provide comprehensive overviews
of these techniques in robotic manipulation.
With the emergence of learning-based methods, discrete
representations such as meshes and point clouds have become
increasingly popular. Neural architectures including PointNet
[27], PointNet++ [28], DGCNN [29], and FoldingNet [30]
enable learning directly from point cloud data and have been
applied to various 3D perception tasks. Several works have
explored learning-based modeling of deformable dynamics,
including particle-based interaction networks for deformable
simulation and control [8]. Zhang et al. [31] learn locally
linear latent dynamics for action-conditioned deformable lin-
ear object prediction, while Van der Merwe et al. [32]
combine learning and model-based optimization to estimate
deformable–rigid contact interactions for manipulation. Par-

Predictive Latent Representation Learning. Recent ad-
vances in self-supervised representation learning have em-
phasized learning latent states that are predictive of future
observations, rather than reconstructive of the current input.
Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA) and related
approaches propose learning representations by predicting
future latent embeddings, thereby enforcing temporal con-
sistency while avoiding pixel-level reconstruction [33]. Such
methods have demonstrated strong performance in learning
temporally coherent and transferable representations from
high-dimensional sensory data.
While predictive latent representation learning highlights
the importance of temporal consistency, these approaches are
developed in the context of generic visual perception and are
agnostic to embodiment, contact interactions, and physical
state estimation. In contrast, our work applies the principle of
predictive latent consistency to deformable motion estimation
under proprioceptive sensing, where latent states must encode
physically meaningful information about motion, shape, and
contact evolution under severe partial observability.
III. METHODS
A. Preliminaries
Problem Formulation. We study deformable motion state
estimation under proprioceptive partial observability. Let st
denote the deformable object state at time t, consisting of
body-frame incremental translation xt, body-frame orientation
change rt, surface shape pt, and contact state ct. These
quantities describe local motion and deformation inferred
from internal observations; global pose trajectories can be
reconstructed by composing the estimated body-frame motion
increments over time.
Due to the proprioceptive sensing setup, the underlying
physical state of the system is not directly observable. Instead,
the agent receives a sequence of high-dimensional internal vi-
sual observations It that reflect local deformation and contact.
We therefore introduce a latent state lt that aggregates past
observations and serves as an internal representation of the
deformable system. Crucially, lt is not assumed to correspond
to a physical state; rather, it is interpreted as a predictive
latent representation that must remain informative of future
observations and motion under partial observability.

Framework Overview. As illustrated in Fig. 2, DeepSoMo
instantiates three modeling components: (1) a proprioceptive
visual encoder that maps internal observations to latent fea-
tures, (2) a latent dynamics model that aggregates information
over time under partial observability, and (3) task-specific
decoders that map predictive latent states to local deformable
motion variables, which can be composed to recover global
motion for evaluation.

B. Dataset Generation
Simulation Platform. We use finite-element simulation to
generate high-fidelity ground-truth data for supervised training
and evaluation. Unlike rigid-body systems, where efficient
simulators such as MuJoCo [34] or Bullet [35] are available,
deformable dynamics require more computationally intensive
methods. In this work, PolyFEM [36] is employed as a
data generator due to its robustness under large deformation
and computational efficiency. FEM simulation is not used at
inference time.
Simulation Setup. The main dataset consists of a de-
formable sphere with diameter 0.5m rolling over generated
terrains. Terrains are created by adding Gaussian noise to
20×20m mesh grids, inducing varied contact conditions and
temporal deformation-change patterns for learning motion pre-
diction under different object–terrain interactions. To quantify
terrain diversity, we compute height-field statistics over the
top contact surface of each generated STL mesh. Table I
summarizes the resulting elevation and slope statistics across
the 100 terrain instances. These metrics show that the terrain
set contains controlled but meaningful variation in elevation
and localized surface orientation. For each of the 100 terrain
instances, as illustrated in Fig. 3, 10 simulation configurations
are generated by varying initial pose, terrain scale, and external
acceleration fields. Each simulation runs for 100 time steps
at 0.1s per step, yielding 1000 simulations in total. We
reserve 200 simulations from held-out terrain environments
for testing and split the remaining 800 into 640 training and
160 validation simulations.
For the object-variation experiments, we generate two addi-
tional datasets: shape variation and shape–material variation.
Each dataset contains 500 simulations, with 100 reserved for
testing and the remaining 400 split into 320 training and 80
validation simulations.
Proprioceptive Observation Rendering. PolyFEM outputs
deformable meshes at each time step. Vertex positions are used
to form point clouds, from which proprioceptive visual obser-
vations are rendered using Blender. Two grayscale cameras are
mounted internally, oriented back-to-back, and rigidly attached

C. Model Architecture
Proprioceptive Visual Encoder. Each proprioceptive im-
age is processed independently by a shared-weight convolu-
tional encoder based on ResNet-18 [37], producing a 512-
dimensional feature vector. Features from the two camera
views are concatenated to form the observation embedding
ft ∈R1×512.
Latent Dynamics Modeling with Self-Supervised Latent
Prediction. To aggregate information over time, we employ
a recurrent latent dynamics model implemented using Long
Short-Term Memory (LSTM) units [12]. LSTMs are chosen
for their stability, simplicity, and well-understood behavior
under limited training data. While recurrent aggregation cap-
tures temporal dependencies, unconstrained latent dynamics
in nonlinear deformable systems often suffer from error ac-
cumulation and drift over long horizons [6]. The proposed
predictive latent consistency constraint is agnostic to the
specific recurrent architecture and can be applied to alternative
sequence models.
To mitigate this issue, we introduce a self-supervised latent
prediction constraint that enforces latent temporal consistency
as shown in Fig. 4. Specifically, the latent state at time t is
encouraged to remain predictive of future observations in the
latent feature space. Let lt denote the LSTM output at time
t. We impose a prediction objective that aligns lt with the
encoded feature at the next time step ft+1. This prediction
can be further cascaded to produce a higher-order predictive
latent ht, similar in spirit to predictive latent representation
learning approaches [33].
This constraint regularizes the latent dynamics by anchoring
them to future observability, rather than relying solely on
instantaneous reconstruction or regression losses. Thus, lt is
best understood as a learned predictive state, not an explicit
analytical physical state. By aggregating deformation history
under partial observability, it is encouraged to retain tempo-
rally relevant factors such as rolling direction, accumulated
orientation change, contact-induced deformation, and defor-
mation evolution.

latent representation is decoded into task-specific local motion
and deformation outputs using four separate decoders:

Body-Frame Orientation Change. The orientation decoder
estimates the relative orientation change between consecutive
time steps, expressed in the body frame. We adopt the con-
tinuous 6D rotation representation proposed in [38], which
avoids discontinuities present in Euler-angle parameterizations
and is well suited for gradient-based learning. The decoder
outputs this representation, which is converted to a valid
rotation matrix via Gram–Schmidt orthogonalization. Global
orientation can be recovered by composing the predicted body-
frame rotations over time.
Shape Reconstruction. Surface shape is reconstructed using
a point-cloud decoder adapted from FoldingNet [30]. A fixed
prototype point cloud is deformed via point-wise transforma-
tions conditioned on the latent state, producing a reconstructed
shape in the body frame. Global coordinates can be obtained
by transforming the shape using the composed translation and
orientation estimates, following approaches presented in [9].
Contact Point Classification. A contact classifier predicts
whether each point in the reconstructed point cloud is in
contact with the environment, enabling explicit modeling of
object–environment interaction.

IV. EXPERIMENTS
A. Baseline Methods
To evaluate the proposed principle of enforcing predictive
structure in latent dynamics, we compare DeepSoMo against
baselines designed to isolate the effect of the latent prediction
constraint under the same deformable-state estimation task.
The main baselines share the same proprioceptive visual
encoder, LSTM dynamics module, and task decoders whenever
possible, so performance differences can be attributed primar-
ily to the prediction objective rather than model capacity or
output design.
ResLSTM (NoPred). This is the primary architecture-
matched baseline. It feeds encoded proprioceptive features
directly into an LSTM for temporal aggregation, followed by
the same decoders for translation, rotation, shape, and contact
estimation. No explicit predictive constraint is imposed on
the latent dynamics. This baseline tests whether predictive
latent consistency improves long-horizon estimation beyond
standard recurrent aggregation under partial observability.
ObsPred (Image). This baseline tests an alternative form of
predictive supervision in which future prediction is enforced
at the observation level rather than in latent space. Encoded
features are decoded back into image space using transposed
convolutions and bilinear upsampling, and prediction losses
are computed between reconstructed and ground-truth images.
This comparison evaluates whether image-level prediction
provides the same benefit as latent prediction. However,

Normalized Trajectory Error (NTE) measures trajectory
drift relative to the total ground-truth trajectory length. For
a sequence of length T, we compute

because small body-frame rotation errors can accumulate into
large absolute trajectory deviations over long sequences.
Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) measures the average
Euclidean distance between reconstructed and ground-truth
global trajectories. Since all sequences share the same initial
pose, no trajectory alignment is performed.
Absolute Rotation Error (ARE) reports the angular error
between reconstructed global orientations and ground truth.
Chamfer Distance (CD) evaluates deformable shape recon-
struction accuracy by computing the bidirectional distance
between predicted and ground-truth point clouds.
Contact Point Classification Accuracy (CPC) reports the
percentage of points correctly classified as being in contact
or non-contact with the environment.
C. Implementation Details
All models are implemented in PyTorch [40]. Experiments
are conducted on a single NVIDIA A100 GPU. Each model
is trained for 200 epochs with a batch size of 5 using the
Adam optimizer with an initial learning rate of 10−3 and a
ReduceLROnPlateau scheduler. Each experiment is repeated
three times with different random seeds to assess robustness.
Average training time per model is approximately 5 hours.

D. Performance
Quantitative Results. Main results are summarized in
Table II. Across the reported metrics, DeepSoMo consis-
tently outperforms both baselines. DeepSoMo reduces ATE
by 32.9% relative to ResLSTM and by 23.6% relative to
ObsPred, while also achieving the lowest NTE. These results
indicate that predictive latent consistency reduces long-horizon
trajectory drift relative to both unconstrained recurrent aggre-
gation and image-level prediction. Because ATE is computed
after composing predicted body-frame translation and rotation
increments over 100 time steps, it reflects accumulated long-
horizon drift rather than frame-wise shape-sensing accuracy.

underperform the ResNet–LSTM backbone despite their larger
capacity. We found that their errors are dominated by less
stable rotation estimation, which compounds into large global
trajectory errors after long-horizon composition. This suggests
that, for proprioceptive deformable motion estimation with
limited simulation data, locality-preserving visual features and
gated recurrent dynamics provide more suitable inductive
biases than patch-based visual tokens or full self-attention
dynamics.
Qualitative Results. Fig. 5 shows representative qualita-
tive results. Compared to baseline methods, DeepSoMo more
accurately tracks global motion, deformation, and contact
evolution over time. Additional visualizations and animations
are provided in the supplementary material.
E. Ablation Studies and Design Choices
Backbone Ablations. Table III reports the impact of vary-
ing the vision encoder and latent dynamics backbone. Among
vision backbones, ResNet18 significantly outperforms VGG
and ViT variants, suggesting that residual architectures provide
more stable features for long-horizon latent aggregation. For
temporal modeling, LSTM-based dynamics outperform dilated
causal convolutions (DCC), vanilla RNNs, and transformers,
indicating that gated recurrence remains effective under severe
partial observability.
Prediction Strategy Ablations. Table IV analyzes the effect
of different prediction strategies. Enforcing prediction in latent
space consistently outperforms image-level prediction while
requiring far fewer parameters. Increasing prediction horizon
beyond one step yields marginal gains, while alternative pre-
diction targets improve over the no-prediction baseline but
underperform the proposed design. Overall, aligning latent
states with future encoded observations provides the most
stable and accurate long-horizon dynamics.

V. CONCLUSION
We presented DeepSoMo, a learning-based framework for
deformable motion estimation under proprioceptive partial
observability. By leveraging internally observed deformation
as a rich proprioceptive signal, DeepSoMo infers coupled
motion, shape, and contact states without access to external
sensing. The central contribution of this work is the identi-
fication and enforcement of latent temporal consistency as a
key requirement for long-horizon deformable state estimation.
We instantiate this principle through a self-supervised latent
prediction constraint that regularizes latent dynamics to remain
predictive of future observations. Extensive experiments using
high-fidelity FEM-generated ground truth demonstrate that
this predictive structure substantially reduces latent drift and
improves trajectory estimation, orientation recovery, shape
reconstruction, and contact inference compared to architecture-
matched recurrent baselines.
Limitation and Future Work. This work is limited by the
inherent constraints of proprioceptive visual sensing, which
encodes motion only through deformation and contact and
therefore cannot observe deformation-sparse motions such as
free fall or frictionless sliding. In addition, our experiments
focus on simulated single-object interactions with static terrain
and do not yet include real-world validation, multi-object
interactions, or deformable–deformable collisions. Real-world
deployment will require addressing soft-body fabrication, em-
bedded sensing, calibration, and sim-to-real transfer. While the
incremental body-frame formulation improves local observ-
ability and latent temporal consistency, long-horizon predic-
tions may still accumulate error. Future work will investigate
complementary sensing modalities, such as IMUs, tactile or
pressure sensors, strain sensors, and actuator feedback, to
improve observability, while also extending the framework
to physical soft robotic systems and downstream control or
planning.
