Abstract
Online 3D Gaussian Splatting reconstructs a scene during
capture: each keyframe spawns primitives once and refines
them for only a few dozen iterations, leaving little chance to
fix poor initialization or missing detail. Gaussians are also
inherently soft, needing many overlaps for solid surfaces.
We present Hierarchical Incremental Beta Splatting
(HIBS), which brings beta kernels to incremental recon-
struction. Their shape parameter interpolates between a
soft, Gaussian-like profile and a flat-topped, near-opaque
surfel, acting as a confidence dial for geometry.
Our
confidence-aware spawning uses geometric observability
and multi-view depth agreement to initialize both opacity
and shape, producing solid primitives where geometry is
verifiable and soft, correctable primitives where it is not. As
online densification, error-driven relocation recycles stale
primitives into photometric-error hotspots. Bounded beta
kernel support enables exact tile culling and faster raster-
ization, and a closed-form, shape-preserving merging for-
mulation provides a level-of-detail (LOD) hierarchy for fast
optimization and real-time rendering. On Mip-NeRF 360 in
the on-the-fly protocol, HIBS improves PSNR by up to 0.8
dB while reducing optimization time by up to 40%.

1. Introduction
3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) [26] has made high-quality
radiance-field capture practical, but the standard pipeline
is offline: images are collected, poses are recovered by
structure-from-motion [51], and the scene is optimized for
tens of thousands of iterations.
Recent work instead re-
constructs during capture [25, 39, 41], providing immediate
feedback and reducing the delay from capture to observable
reconstruction to seconds. We target this online regime by
reconsidering the primitive itself.
Online reconstruction inverts the assumptions of offline
3DGS. Each incoming image is registered as a keyframe,
spawns new primitives once, and is optimized only for a
short burst before the next keyframe arrives. This leaves lit-
tle room for gradient-driven clone/split densification, opac-
ity resets, or long optimization schedules to repair poorly
initialized primitives. In practice, what spawning produces
is largely what the model keeps. The critical decisions—
what to place and how much to trust it—must therefore be
made at initialization from measurable evidence.
We argue that under these constraints the primitive itself
can be improved, and that the Gaussian is suboptimal. A
Gaussian is fully opaque only at its center and translucent
elsewhere; representing a solid surface therefore requires
overlapping many Gaussians, together with the densifica-
tion and long optimization that offline 3DGS can afford.

Without this machinery, online reconstruction must spawn
translucent Gaussians and hope a short optimization burst
can arrange them correctly.
We instead use the beta kernel [35], whose bounded pro-
file has a learnable shape parameter b that continuously
interpolates between a Gaussian-like volume and a flat-
topped, near-opaque surfel. A single beta primitive can thus
be a flat-topped surface patch while retaining a soft repre-
sentation for uncertain geometry, thin structures, or volume.
While Deformable Beta Splatting [35] uses this parame-
ter offline as a compactness degree of freedom learned over
long optimization schedules, we use it from geometric evi-
dence as a confidence axis. The key question is therefore not
only how to optimize the shape, but whether the available
evidence justifies treating a primitive as a solid surface.
That evidence must be geometric, not merely photomet-
ric. Naively spawning solid kernels is harmful: a compact,
near-opaque primitive at the wrong depth blocks transmit-
tance and suppresses gradients behind it.
Conversely, a
low photometric error does not guarantee correct geometry.
When the triangulation baseline is small relative to depth,
many depths explain the observed views similarly well, yet
only a narrow range remains consistent under parallax.
We therefore use the beta shape parameter as a confi-
dence dial. Confidence-aware spawning combines two in-
dependent geometric cues, Multi-view stereo (MVS) agree-
ment with an aligned monocular prior and depth observ-
ability, to initialize each primitive along this soft-to-solid
axis: well-constrained candidates are spawned solid, while
ambiguous ones remain soft and correctable. As additional
views arrive, a lifetime controller updates the same param-
eter from accumulated multi-view evidence, progressively
turning confirmed geometric confidence into solidity.
To compensate for the absence of offline densification,
we introduce error-driven relocation.
Near-dead primi-
tives are recycled into photometric-error hotspots, where
render-preserving copies provide additional capacity pre-
cisely where the current model is deficient.
The beta representation is also performant. Its bounded
support yields an exact tile-culling bound and faster ras-
terization, while its closed-form moments enable a shape-
aware level-of-detail hierarchy for large captures.
Together, these components yield an online reconstruc-
tion system in which the primitive is adapted to the short
optimization horizon rather than relying on offline densi-
fication to compensate for it. Our experiments show im-
proved reconstruction quality and efficiency across online,
benchmark, and large-scale captures.
In summary, our contributions are:
• We identify the Gaussian primitive as suboptimal in the
spawn-once, optimize-briefly regime and adopt the beta
kernel as a confidence-adaptive online primitive whose
shape ranges from soft to solid.
• We introduce confidence-aware spawning, which initial-
izes opacity and shape from independent depth agreement
and geometric observability.
• We introduce error-driven relocation, a render-preserving
online alternative to clone/split densification that recycles
near-dead primitives into photometric-error hotspots.
• We derive a closed-form merge for beta primitives, pre-
serving their shape statistics and enabling a level-of-detail
hierarchy for large-scale reconstruction.
• We develop a fast beta rasterizer with a shape-correct
culling bound that exploits the kernel’s compact support.

2. Related Work
Novel View Synthesis.
Neural Radiance Fields [3, 43]
synthesize photorealistic novel views from posed images,
but volumetric rendering remains computationally expen-
sive. 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) [26] instead enables
efficient rasterization and fast optimization.
Subsequent
work improves compositing and rendering fidelity [12, 47,
56] and scales to large scenes with hierarchical representa-
tions such as Hierarchical 3DGS [27] and Octree-GS [48].
These methods are predominantly offline, relying on pre-
computed poses and long optimization schedules, whereas
we target reconstruction during capture.
A range of systems reconstruct 3DGS maps incremen-
tally from video, including SLAM-based methods [4, 19,
21, 25, 39, 45, 58] and joint pose-and-reconstruction ap-
proaches [10, 32].
SLAM methods spend far more per
keyframe, e.g., hundreds of mapping iterations, densifica-
tion, and long final refinement [39, 58]. Feed-forward and
hybrid methods [23, 30, 31, 33] trade scene-specific opti-
mization for speed, typically at reduced fidelity. Closest
to our setting, On-the-Fly NVS [41] and i3DGS [42] re-
construct image streams with short optimization bursts and
no densification, using learned matching and hierarchical
structures.
However, these methods retain the Gaussian
and spawn it once per view. Our work instead revisits the
primitive itself, introducing a controllable notion of solidity
specifically for this short-burst regime.
Primitive Kernels and Densification.
Alternative splat-
ting primitives have been proposed to improve compact-
ness, surface accuracy, or geometric fidelity, including gen-
eralized and radial kernels [14, 22], planar Gaussians [20],
point sprites [2, 16, 50], linear and convex primitives [18,
53], volumetric ellipsoids [37], and trilinear splats [8]. De-
formable radial kernels [22] are flexible, but planar and
without closed-form moments, which our spawning and
merge rely on.

Splatting (DBS) [35, 36] introduces a bounded beta ker-

3DGS densification has been refined through error-

In particular,
3DGS-MCMC [28] reuses low-opacity primitives by relo-

and periodic densification, whereas our online setting has
neither. We adapt relocation to this regime by recycling
near-dead primitives and targeting them using attributed
photometric error.
Efficient Rasterization.
Recent work accelerates 3DGS
through improved backward passes, gradient reduction,
tighter tile bounds, and fused optimization [6, 13, 15, 38,
47]. We build on these ideas for the online burst, but de-
rive the rasterization bounds for the bounded beta kernel. In
particular, the Gaussian cutoff is not valid for low-opacity
solid kernels, making the beta-specific bound a correctness
requirement in addition to an efficiency improvement.
Depth Priors and Uncertainty.
Monocular and multi-
view depth predictors are commonly used to initialize or
regularize radiance-field reconstruction [33, 54, 55]; for
example, Meuleman et al. [41] combine aligned monocu-
lar depth with guided MVS. Uncertainty is often estimated
post-hoc or used for view selection [11, 24]. We instead in-
corporate uncertainty directly into the online primitive life-
cycle: agreement between independent depth estimates and
geometric observability determine the primitive’s opacity
and shape, while the evolving shape field provides a live
confidence representation.
3. Preliminaries: Online 3DGS
Online 3DGS systems [30, 41] reconstruct an RGB stream
incrementally: each incoming frame is registered, used to
spawn new primitives, and followed by a short optimization
burst before the next keyframe. In i3DGS [42], registration
matches each frame against previously registered keyframes
using place recognition and learned feature matching [1, 34,
46], initializes the pose with PnP/RANSAC, and performs
local bundle adjustment over a covisibility graph, all run-
ning in tens of milliseconds per frame.
After registration, pixels with local image detail are sam-
pled and lifted to 3D using available multi-view and monoc-
ular depth cues [30, 41, 42]. The samples are instantiated as
low-opacity, translucent isotropic Gaussians, followed by a
short optimization burst of only a few dozen iterations. Cru-
cially, these online systems do not perform the clone/split
densification or opacity resets used by offline 3DGS, nor the
long per-keyframe mapping and final refinement of Gaus-
sian SLAM [39]: primitives are spawned once and then re-
tained, making their initialization critical.
4. Method
Our method reconstructs a primitive-based radiance field
online from an unordered stream of RGB images. We adopt
the pose-estimation backbone of Meuleman et al. [42], but
redesign the primitive and its lifecycle. In the spawn-per-
view, short-burst regime, positions receive too little opti-
mization to recover from poor initialization, while longer
optimization overfits already-seen keyframes.
We there-
fore make confidence explicit in the primitive representa-
tion: each primitive can be either a soft probe or a self-
supporting surface patch, and primitives are later relocated
from unused capacity to image-space error hotspots. An
overview of our method can be found in Fig. 2.

4.4. Error-Driven Incremental Relocation
The overall amount of new detail that can be added per
keyframe is limited, and offline clone/split heuristics re-
quire longer optimization.
Instead, inspired by the relo-
cation mechanism of 3DGS-MCMC [28], we introduce an
error-targeted split: near-dead primitives are recycled by
teleporting them to poorly reconstructed live primitives.
Once per keyframe, dead primitives, being below the
recycling-opacity threshold, outside the hierarchy, and past
the spawn-protection window, serve as sources. Live des-
tinations are sampled according to their per-primitive error
attribution, weighted by error consistency and restricted to
the top 10%, so high-error regions receive more capacity.
Each selected source is teleported to be a copy of the desti-
nation’s position, shape, and color, producing stacks of re-
located primitives whose size grows with residual error.
Before optimization, copy stacks are made render pre-
serving, by adjusting their combined opacity and scale [28].
Small positional jitter and optimizer resets allow the subse-
quent burst to separate the copies and represent more detail.

4.6. Fast Beta Rasterization
The bounded support of the beta kernel permits tighter ras-
terization bounds than the Gaussian. For opacity o, the nor-
malized squared radius at the rasterizer’s 1/255 cutoff is
xcut = max(0, 1 −(255o)−1/β(b)). We use this bound
for tile culling.
The standard 3DGS Gaussian threshold
is insufficient for solid, low-opacity beta kernels and can
discard tiles containing nonzero contributions; the beta-
specific bound is therefore both a correctness fix and a
speedup.
The optimization burst is further accelerated with a
FasterGS-style fused rasterizer [13]: backward recomputes
forward quantities per primitive, keeps intermediate gradi-
ents in registers, and applies sparse Adam directly to the
optimizer state. Primitive gradients therefore never mate-
rialize in memory. The regularizers are also applied ana-
lytically inside the fused backward/optimizer update. This
rasterizer substantially reduces computation time.

Closed-form merging by moment matching.
Each
primitive carries an opacity o, center µ, shape matrix Σ,
and scalar shape parameter b, with β = 4eb. Its compactly
supported kernel is ρ(x) = o (1 −∥w∥2/9)β, where w is
the whitened offset.
Two properties make closed-form merging possible.
First, the mass Z, covariance Cov = ξ(β)Σ with shrink
factor ξ(β) = 4.5/(β + 2.5), and Mardia kurtosis κ(β) =
15(β + 2.5)/(β + 3.5) all have closed forms (Sec. A).
Here Σ parameterizes the support ellipsoid rather than the
spread, since peakier profiles concentrate mass near the cen-
ter. Second, κ is strictly monotone in β with 15 −κ(β) =
15/(β + 3.5), so on the band (75/7, 15) it has the closed-
form inverse β = 15/(15−κ)−3.5 and uniquely identifies
the profile within the family.
We merge primitives by moment matching rather than
KL minimization. For compactly supported kernels, KL is
unsuitable because any nonzero mass outside the parent’s
support makes the divergence infinite.

Large-scale training.
The hierarchy is rebuilt periodi-
cally rather than per keyframe, with dynamic-object and
sky masks applied when available. To prevent redundant
spawning, views observing primitives below one-quarter of
their finest observed resolution are excluded from spawning
and receive reduced update weights. Finally, a targeted re-
pair pass allocates 1000 iterations to the most deteriorated
training views, identified by photometric errors exceeding
(1.5×) the median, mitigating recency bias.
6. Results and Experiments
We evaluate using the online protocols of Meuleman et
al. [41] on three MipNeRF360 scenes (garden, counter,
bonsai, marked with otf) [3], three TUM scenes [52],
and three StaticHikes captures [40], with the same held-
out views for evaluation. We additionally evaluate on the
3DGS benchmark [3, 17, 26, 29] and two large-scale cap-
tures, SMALLCITY [27] and CITYWALK [41]. We com-
pare to methods including MonoGS [39], S3PO-GS [4],
LongSplat [32], On-The-Fly NVS [41], i3DGS [42],
DROID-Splat [19], CF-3DGS [10], DepthAnything3 [33],
ARTDECO [30], H3DGS [27] and Octree-GS [48], as well
as GLOMAP-based [44, 51] offline references using Beta
Splatting [35] and Faster-GS [13]. We report PSNR, SSIM,
LPIPS, and total time, averaging ours over five runs.
Unless noted otherwise, experiments use an RTX 5090
(32 GB) and i9-14900KS with 64GB DDR4 RAM on Win-
dows 11. For methods requiring reduced input resolution,
we follow [41, 42]. These results are reported in Sec. D.

7. Conclusion
Limitations and Future Work.
On the 3DGS bench-
mark, experiments show a remaining quality gap to fully
optimized offline methods, suggesting that further improve-
ments in online pose refinement [42]—orthogonal to our
work—are a promising direction for closing the remaining
gap. The merge approximates out-of-band groups and the
rendered contribution, and shuffled input can reduce qual-
ity. Further promising directions include integration with
VR [9] and using the confidence map for refinement [11].
Conclusion.
We present an online reconstruction frame-
work that redesigns the primitive lifecycle around the
short optimization horizon of incremental capture.
Our
bounded beta kernel encodes a continuous notion of solid-
ity, observability-aware spawning initializes this confidence
from geometric evidence, and error-driven relocation reallo-
cates unused capacity to poorly explained regions. Closed-
form moment matching enables a shape-aware hierarchy,
while bounded-support rasterization and fused optimiza-
tion make the representation efficient for large-scale on-
line reconstruction. Together, these components yield faster
and higher-quality reconstruction throughout capture, while
turning the primitive shape field into a live confidence visu-
alization.

A mixture of beta kernels can fall

scale-invariant, shrinking a group to sub-pixel size does not
change this. We restrict the parent to b⋆∈[−4, 4] and clip
κmix to ˆκ in the corresponding band [κ(4e−4), κ(4e4)]. Be-

profile and keeps β⋆finite.

B. Implementation Details & Startup
The system is implemented in PyTorch 2.11/CUDA 12.8,
with performance-critical stages implemented as custom
GPU kernels. Feature extraction uses XFeat [46], matching
combines mutual nearest neighbors with LighterGlue [34],
retrieval uses MixVPR [1], and monocular depth priors use
Depth Anything V2 [55]. The pose-estimation and map-
ping stages follow the backbone of i3DGS [42], including
P3P/RANSAC, triangulation, and local bundle adjustment.
Rasterization uses our beta-kernel implementation of a
FasterGS-style fused rasterizer [13]. Forward quantities are
recomputed during the backward pass, and the sparse Adam
update and analytic regularizer gradients are applied di-
rectly to the optimizer state without materializing primitive
gradients. Spherical-beta colour parameters are stored com-
pactly for the fused implementation, while exported models
retain the standard SH-width layout for compatibility.
Each keyframe performs one 30-iteration optimization
burst.
The bootstrap uses 8 keyframes, local BA oper-
ates on the 5 most similar keyframes, and the LoD hier-
archy is rebuilt every 10 keyframes. Inactive fine levels and
keyframes beyond the active window are offloaded to host
memory. All reported timings include the online pose esti-
mation, spawning, optimization, and hierarchy stages.
