ZARA: Zero-shot Animatable and Relightable 3D
Avatar reconstruction from a single image
Yixiao Chen, Dongdong Weng, Chengfeng Zhao, Tao Gong, Zhihe Zhao, Zhe Zhu, Yue Liu, and Yuan Liu
Abstract—Recent large human reconstruction models create
remarkable animatable 3D avatars from a single image, but
their appearance remains entangled with the illumination and
cannot be relighted. In contrast, existing 3D relightable-avatar
reconstruction methods typically require person-specific opti-
mization. We present ZARA, a zero-shot feed-forward framework
that reconstructs an animatable 3D Gaussian avatar together
with its intrinsic attributes from a single image. ZARA couples
a pretrained large-scale human reconstruction model with a
Material Transformer to inherit geometry priors while learning
material-aware Gaussian representations. To preserve localized
details, we introduce Gaussian-aligned material feature fusion
strategy, which injects multi-scale material features onto indi-
vidual Gaussians and combines them with geometry tokens. As
most human-avatar datasets lack ground-truth material maps, we
use pseudo-material labels as conditioning inputs and supervision
targets during training. We further introduce MatFace regular-
ization to preserve facial structure and color in albedo prediction
under unreliable facial pseudo-material supervision. Extensive
experiments demonstrate that ZARA reconstructs animatable
and relightable humans within tens of seconds without person-
specific optimization or post-processing, outperforming existing
baselines in both relighting quality and materials generalization
ability. As the ability of recovering the physically-based-rendering
attributes of the human, ZARA also enables direct material
editing of the avatar’s appearance.

I. INTRODUCTION

ECONSTRUCTING high-fidelity and animatable 3D hu-
man avatars with relighting capability is crucial for many
applications, such as games, immersive AR/VR, and film pro-
duction. Most existing methods for reconstructing animatable
and relightable avatars are person-specific, that is, a trained
model can only be applied to a particular individual wearing a
specific outfit. Although these methods have achieved promis-
ing rendering quality with the significant development of
NeRF [1]–[3] and 3DGS [4]–[6] techniques, they require time-
consuming reconstruction or optimization for each subject
and require at least monocular video as input, which limits
their broader applicability. A series of methods rely on ultra-
high-quality OLAT (one-light-at-a-time) data captured using
Light Stage systems [7]–[9], and employ generative models to
learn accurate avatar appearance, material, and motion models.
However, they still model specific subject, while the costs of
data acquisition and storage are prohibitively high.

A straightforward strategy for material learning relies on
the inverse-rendering framework: the model predicts the PBR
attributes, and yields RGB outputs through the rendering
equation for self-supervision. Nevertheless, this approach is
plagued by the ill-posed nature of inverse-rendering, which
impedes training. We therefore resort to direct material su-
pervision. Given most existing human-avatar datasets lack
ground-truth material maps, inspired by MvInverse [17] and
UniRelight [18], we construct pseudo-material maps through
a frozen inverse-rendering model [19], with the resulting
maps serving as conditioning priors and supervision targets.
Furthermore, because the facial regions of pseudo-label may
be misaligned, we introduce MatFace Regularization for facial
albedo supervision. Instead of relying exclusively on facial
pseudo-albedo-label pixels that may contain identity shifts,
MatFace Regularization uses the RGB image as structural
guidance and the pseudo-label albedo as the color source,
which preserves recognizable facial features while learning
intrinsic facial materials.
Together, these components enable ZARA to reconstruct
relightable and animatable avatars impressively in tens of
seconds from a single image. Extensive experiments demon-
strate that our framework improves the generalization and
realism of zero-shot 3D animatable and relightable avatar
generation, bridging the gap between reconstruction under
fixed lighting conditions and relighting. Since our method
explicitly generates PBR materials for 3D Gaussian avatars,
it also supports appearance editing via material modification.
Our main contributions are summarized as follows:
• We propose ZARA, a zero-shot feed-forward framework
that reconstructs an animatable and relightable 3D Gaus-
sian avatar with explicit per-Gaussian PBR attributes from
a single image.
• We introduce a cascaded geometry-material architecture
that couples a pretrained Geometry Transformer with
a Material Transformer, thereby integrating geometric
tokens, global material context, and per-gaussian aligned
local material features for each Gaussian.
• We introduce MatFace Regularization, which combines
structural cues from the input RGB image with color
cues from pseudo-label albedo to preserve facial structure
and intrinsic color under unreliable pseudo-label material
supervision.
II. RELATED WORK
A. PBR-Based Relighting and Inverse Rendering
Relighting aims to synthesize the appearance of a subject
under a target illumination. Image-based relighting methods
[18], [22] directly transform the observed appearance accord-
ing to the target illumination, without necessarily recovering
explicit 3D geometry or material attributes. However, nearly all
these methods perform relighting in image space and therefore
struggle to maintain consistency across viewpoints. On the
other hand, inverse rendering [17], [19], [23]–[27] addresses
the inverse problem of decomposing observed images into
geometry, materials, and illumination, thereby providing the
intrinsic properties required by the physically based rendering.
Recent 3D relightable generation methods [28]–[33] further
recover or assign physically based material attributes to objects
or scenes, enabling them to be rendered under novel environ-
ment illumination with the multi-view consistency. However,
these methods mainly focus on static objects or scenes and
do not explicitly address the reconstruction of animatable and
relightable 3D full-body avatars from a single image.
B. Relightable and Animatable Avatar Reconstruction
Relightable avatar methods [34]–[37] model geometry, ma-
terials, and illumination to animate avatars and render them
under novel lighting conditions. RANA [38] reconstructs re-
lightable articulated neural avatars from monocular videos by
estimating geometry, albedo, and lighting. ARG [39] extends
Gaussian-based avatar modeling with PBR attributes using a
Style-UNet architecture. SG-IA [21] introduces surfel-based
Gaussian inverse rendering to estimate PBR properties for
dynamic clothed humans from monocular videos. DNF-Avatar
[20] distills neural fields into an explicit Gaussian represen-
tation for real-time animatable avatar relighting. More recent
methods [9], [40]–[46] significantly improve relighting real-
ism by combining generative-model based controllable avatar
representations with intrinsic or albedo-like appearance cues
and learned PBR-like or illumination-conditioned rendering
from controlled captures. However, all of above approaches are
person-specific and require monocular or multi-view videos,
controlled capture setups, OLAT data, or costly per-subject
optimization.
C. Feed-Forward single image Animatable Avatar Reconstruc-
tion
Recent feed-forward LRM-based methods [11], [12], [47],
[48] have substantially improved the quality and reconstruction
speed of animatable avatars from a single image. Instead of
optimizing a separate representation for each subject, these
methods learn strong human priors from large-scale datasets

seconds using MM-Transformer-based large human recon-

with large generated multi-view human data. DynaAvatar [13]

supervision to improve geometric detail, identity preservation,
and pose-driven animation quality. Despite their advances,
above feed-forward 3D gaussian avatar methods focus on
reconstructing geometry, deformation, and appearance under
the observed illumination. The recovered appearance of these
methods therefore often remains entangled with the lighting
condition of the input image, and these methods do not
explicitly estimate intrinsic properties or equip with relighting
module. As a result, the reconstructed avatars cannot be
directly relighted under novel illumination.
Among the few feed-forward alternatives with relighting
capability, LCA [14] extends its LHRM-based pretrained
avatar model to relighting by replacing its decoder heads
and performing relighting-specific post-training on controlled
multi-view captures with OLAT data using learnable radiance
transfer. S3F [10] reconstructs animatable implicit avatars with
structured body-aligned 3D features and performs relighting
with albedo and shading, which represents material-light in-
teractions jointly within illumination code rather than through
independently parameterized reflectance attributes. Changing
the lighting requires re-running the network to obtain the code,
which limiting the ability of material-illumination interaction.
ZARA instead estimate pbr materials to canonical Gaussians
from a single image and enable relighted appearance through
deferred PBR for each target pose and view, providing explicit
material control and physically grounded, view-consistent re-
lighting without person-specific optimization or post-training.

B. 3D Gaussian Splatting
We adopt the 3D Gaussian Splatting framework [4] as
the 3D representation for avatars. Each Gaussian primitive
is parameterized by a position p, scaling factors s, rotations
q, opacity o, and spherical harmonic color coefficients c.
Different from vanilla 3DGS, each Gaussian primitive in
ZARA is further equipped with albedo a, roughness r, and
normal n parameters to support physically based rendering.
Given these properties, the rendering process is given by
I = SplattingRasterizer (p, x, s, o, q) ,
(3)
where I is the rendered image, and x can be c, a, r, and n.
At inference time, we additionally perform physically based
rendering to obtain relighting results via deferred shading [53],
[54].
IV. METHOD
As shown in Fig. 1, given a single image, ZARA recon-
structs a canonical 3D Gaussian avatar that can be animated
and relighted under novel illumination. A pretrained human re-
construction backbone predicts canonical Gaussian geometry,
while the proposed material encoder and material transformer
estimate per-Gaussian albedo and roughness. Gaussian-aligned
feature fusion injects local material evidence from image space
to the corresponding Gaussian primitives, and the proposed
MatFace Regularization strategy improves facial albedo learn-
ing. Given a target motion sequence, the canonical avatar is
deformed and rendered to each target pose and illumination.
The entire reconstruction process is feed-forward and requires
neither person-specific optimization nor test-time fine-tuning.

B. Material Learning, Encoding, and Prediction
Material Priors and Supervision. A straightforward ap-
proach for material learning follows the inverse-rendering
paradigm adopted in prior works [20], [21], [27]–[29]: the
model reconstructs or predicts the material properties of the
avatar, produces RGB images via the rendering equation, and
performs self-supervision against the ground-truth RGB im-
ages. Nevertheless, this scheme requires simultaneous predic-
tion of geometry, materials and lighting, which suffers from the
classic ill-posed problem of inverse-rendering. An alternative
strategy is to supervise model learning directly using ground-
truth materials, which provides richer direct material informa-
tion for the model. However, existing human-avatar datasets

A. Datasets collection
For training, we use Human4DiT-3D [60], DNA-Rendering
[62] and in-the-wild human videos from YouTube and BiliBili
which is collected by Human4DiT [60]. Total training data
comprise approximately 1.8 million frames. Human4DiT-3D
provides scanned assets, yet only meshes and textures captured
under uniform illumination are available. We feed each asset
into the PBR pipeline and render it with a specified envi-
ronment map as the input and reference to our model. For
quantitative evaluation, we render the original uniform-lighting
textures of Human4DiT-3D as the albedo ground truth and
the geometric normals as the normal ground truth. We also
perform test experiments on RANA [38] datasets, which is
a synthetic dataset that includes ground-truth albedo, nor-
mal, and relighting results at novel pose for evaluation. For
Human4DiT-3D and DNA-Rendering, we split the dataset so
that training and testing subjects do not overlap, obtaining 50
and 10 test subjects, respectively. RANA dataset are randomly
sampled 10 subjects for testing. We also collect additional 50
in-the-wild human videos from BiliBili for qualitative testing.
For each sample, we generate pseudo-material maps using the
pipeline described in Sec. IV-B during training. The resulting
maps provide material priors for reference observations and
target supervision during training.
B. Implementation Details
Training is conducted on 8 NVIDIA H200 GPUs and
requires approximately 7 days. Both the source images and
supervision targets are processed at a resolution of 512×512.
We use the Adam optimizer with β1 = 0.9, β2 = 0.999, zero
weight decay, and a maximum gradient norm of 1.0. Module-
specific learning rates are set to 1 × 10−4 for the material
MLP and heads, the material-encoder, and the pose-dependent
geometry decoder; 2 × 10−4 for the LoRA parameters and
Material Transformer; and 4×10−4 for the remaining trainable
modules. The Geometry Transformer is adapted using rank-32
LoRA [15] with an alpha of 64 and a dropout rate of 0.1.
C. Evaluation Metrics
Quantitative material evaluation is conducted on held-out
Human4DiT-3D and RANA dataset using their reference
albedo and normal maps. We test albedo on Peak Signal-
to-Noise Ratio (PSNR), Structural Similarity (SSIM) [63],
and Learned Perceptual Image Patch Similarity LPIPS [59].
For normals, we test the Mean Angular Error (MAE). We
additionally evaluate relit RGB images using PSNR, SSIM,
LPIPS, and Face Consistency (FC) [11], where the environ-
ment map is held fixed across tests of each sample. All image-
space metrics are computed within the valid foreground region.

D. Comparison with Baseline Methods
1) Baselines Selection: As there is, to the best of our
knowledge, no existing open-source method supports feed-
forward reconstruction of an animatable and relightable avatar
from a single image in a zero-shot manner, we further con-
struct two feed-forward avatar-generation baselines by com-
bining LHM [11] and IDOL [12] with the State of the Art
(SOTA) video relighting model UniRelight [18]. These two-
stage baselines first generate and animate an avatar from the
reference image and then estimate albedo and relighting re-
sults. The same video relighting method and inference settings
are used for both avatar generators so that their differences are
not confounded by different relighting networks.
Following the comparison strategy in PERSONA [48], we
also test two SOTA person-specific relightable avatar recon-
struction methods, IntrinsicAvatar [61] and DNF-Avatar [20]
as reference. A direct comparison between zero-shot methods
and person-specific methods is unfair, as the former take a
single image as input and require no test-time optimization,
while the latter use full subject-specific training sequences and
perform iterative optimization for each identity. We retain this
distinction rather than treating the person-specific methods
as baselines operating on a single image. On the one hand,
this vanilla setting allows the person-specific methods to fully
exploit their capabilities. On the other hand, it enables us
to assess whether our zero-shot model can rapidly achieve
performance comparable to that of methods requiring several
hours of per-subject training. We both test the materials
reconstruction and relighting results for these two methods.
2) Quantitative Comparison: Tables II and III report the
material and relighting comparison results, respectively be-
tween ZARA and baseline methods and person-specific refer-
ences. All experiments are conducted on a single H200 GPU.
Runtime includes material-prior estimation, input encoding,
avatar reconstruction, and rendering/relighting for zero-shot
methods. And for person-specific methods, it denotes per-
subject optimization time. Compared with the zero-shot base-
lines, ZARA achieves better performance across all reported
metrics while also providing higher inference efficiency. Com-
pared with person-specific reconstruction methods, ZARA
obtains comparable material and relighting results on RANA
dataset and even surpasses them in PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS
on Human4DiT-3D dataset. Notably, ZARA reaches this level
of performance within only tens of seconds per sample,
whereas person-specific methods require several hours of
iterative optimization for each subject.

comparisons between ZARA and the baseline methods and
person-specific references in terms of material estimation
and relighting appearance. As the two zero-shot baselines
follow two-stage pipelines and the ability of jointly estimating
albedo and relighting results by UniRelight [18], we therefore
show their intermediate reconstructions, albedo results and
final relighting results under the specified environment maps.
Although both baselines produce plausible reconstructions and
albedo results, their relighting outputs exhibit noticeable facial
identity drift, overexposure, and view-inconsistent rendering.
This behavior arises because the reconstructed 3D avatars

E. Ablation Studies
1) Gaussian-Aligned Material Feature Fusion: We evaluate
the influence of Gaussian-Aligned Material Feature Fusion
in the following, which is shown in Fig. 5. Although the
global material tokens from the material encoder provide the
Material Transformer with informative material cues, these
tokens alone tend to oversmooth regions with variations in
color and geometry. Gaussian-Aligned Material Feature Fusion
instead equips each Gaussian query with material features
from its projected location and local neighborhood, thereby
preserving garment textures and fold details from the reference
image. The quantitative results in Table IV further confirm
that the Gaussian-Aligned Material Feature Fusion mechanism
improves the quality of both material estimation and relighting.
2) MatFace Regularization: To analyze MatFace Regular-
ization, we replace it with the pseudo-label albedo supervision
at same frame used for non-face regions, including L1, MSG,
and LPIPS losses, while keeping the remaining components
and training settings unchanged. As shown in Fig. 6, removing
MatFace Regularization produces blurred and distorted facial
albedo, including degraded facial structures, implausible in-
trinsic colors, and visible identity drift, while these errors also
propagate to the relighting results. In contrast, the full model
preserves facial structure, intrinsic color, and subject identity
more faithfully in the predicted albedo and relighting. The
localized comparisons further reinforce the above conclusion.
These effects are consistent across the samples from different
datasets. Table IV shows that, removing MatFace Regulariza-
tion also degrades FC on the synthetic benchmark. Without
MatFace Regularization, unreliable facial supervision from
pseudo-label adversely affects the learned facial representation
and consequently reduces identity consistency.

fectiveness of Explicit Material Conditioning. For this module-
level ablation, we remove all conditioning pathways that
input material cues into the network and investigate whether
satisfactory results can still be achieved using only a single
RGB image as input while retaining material supervision
during training. As shown in Fig 7, the ablated model without
Explicit Material Conditioning reconstructs overly smooth

VI. DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSION
A. Limitations
Although ZARA enables remarkable reconstruction of ani-
matable and relightable avatars from a single image, it still has
some limitations. First, as shown in Fig. 9, the reconstruction
quality depends on the quality of the pseudo material labels.
Errors in the estimated albedo, roughness, or normals can
propagate to the predicted Gaussian attributes and become
more apparent under novel illumination. Improving robustness
to imperfect material inputs is therefore an important direction
for future work. Second, our current PBR formulation does not
model subsurface scattering, which is important for the soft
and translucent appearance of human skin. It is interesting to
incorporate an efficient skin-specific scattering model could
further improve the realism of faces and other exposed skin
regions.

B. Conclusion
In this paper, we introduce ZARA, a zero-shot feed-forward
framework for reconstructing animatable and relightable 3D
Gaussian avatars from a single image. By coupling a pretrained
Geometry Transformer with a Material Transformer, ZARA
predicts explicit per-Gaussian intrinsic attributes for pose-
driven animation and deferred PBR. During training, we adopt
pseudo-material labels as conditioning inputs and supervision
targets. Gaussian-Aligned materials feature fusion preserves
local material details, while MatFace Regularization improves
facial albedo fidelity under pseudo-label material supervision.
Extensive experiments demonstrate that ZARA outperforms
zero-shot baselines and achieves results comparable to person-
specific methods, while achieving reconstruction time in tens
of seconds. The explicit material representation further sup-
ports direct editing of avatar appearance.
