Abstract
Extrapolation beyond LiDAR coverage is an important yet
under-evaluated challenge of depth completion.
In real
camera–LiDAR systems, partial sensor overlap, occlusions,
and limited field coverage often leave large image regions
weakly observed or completely unobserved by LiDAR. Thus,
we investigate depth completion in this beyond-coverage
regime using only sparse supervision from raw single-sweep
LiDAR, without dense depth labels, LiDAR accumulation,
or pseudo-LiDAR generation. Our approach leverages a
frozen monocular depth foundation model, lightweight per-
frame alignment to sparse LiDAR geometry, and a refine-
ment module that fuses the aligned prior with sparse Li-
DAR anchors to predict dense metric depth. To regular-
ize learning under raw sparse supervision, we introduce
a high-frequency normal-consistency loss defined as the
image-space gradient of the per-pixel normal cosine dis-
crepancy. This term penalizes spatial variation in scalar
angular error; it does not directly match changes in neigh-
boring normal vectors.
We further introduce an evalua-
tion protocol that explicitly separates LiDAR-supported and
beyond-coverage regions, revealing extrapolative behavior
that conventional depth-completion metrics can obscure.
Our method achieves strong depth accuracy beyond LiDAR
coverage while remaining competitive in LiDAR-covered re-
gions, providing a practical way to deploy monocular foun-
dation priors in multi-sensor systems.
1. Introduction
Depth completion estimates dense, metrically consistent
depth from an RGB image and sparse range measurements.
It is a core component of 3D perception in autonomous
driving and robotics, where reconstruction, obstacle avoid-
arXiv:2611.08782v1  [cs.CV]  5 Nov 2026


ance, and planning depend on reliable geometry [12, 17].
However, in real automotive setups, LiDAR is both sparse
and imperfect: single-sweep measurements are affected by
scanline sparsity, multipath returns, calibration errors, and
adverse weather, and large parts of the image may receive
few or no returns at all [6, 9]. This makes extrapolation be-
yond LiDAR coverage, using only sparse and noisy super-
vision, an important but under-emphasized aspect of depth
completion.
Standard benchmarks are dominated by interpolation
and refinement in LiDAR-supported regions, often using
semi-dense targets obtained through multi-frame accumu-
lation and stereo post-processing [5, 7, 26, 32]. In practice,
camera-LiDAR systems frequently exhibit partial overlap,
occlusions, and limited field coverage, leaving large image
regions weakly observed or completely unobserved by Li-
DAR. Moreover, dense and clean ground-truth depth is of-
ten infeasible to obtain in real deployments, especially in
sensor setups where stereo cameras cannot be mounted. A
practical system must therefore do more than denoise and
interpolate sparse depth: it must infer coherent geometry
where no direct LiDAR support is available without relying
on dense depth ground truth.
Monocular depth foundation models provide a natural
prior for this setting, as they produce dense, highly gen-
eralizable depth predictions from a single image. [2, 19, 28,
29, 33], but their outputs are not directly usable as metric
depth because they can suffer from scale, shift, and pro-
jection ambiguities [28, 33]. The key question is therefore
how to combine such priors with sparse LiDAR in a way
that preserves their extrapolative strength while recovering
metric consistency from raw sensor measurements.
In this work, we study exactly this problem in the sparse-
only regime. Given a single RGB image and a raw LiDAR
scan, we predict dense metric depth without dense depth la-
bels, LiDAR accumulation, or pseudo-LiDAR generation.
Our formulation combines a frozen monocular depth foun-
dation model that provides a dense prior, a lightweight per-
frame alignment of that prior to sparse LiDAR geometry,
and a refinement module that fuses the aligned prior with
sparse LiDAR anchors. The overall design is modular, so
individual components can be replaced without changing
the training recipe.
Learning in this regime is challenging because super-
vision is restricted to raw single-sweep LiDAR. Such
supervision can contain localized spurious returns, and
naive sparse-only training may propagate these artifacts
into dense predictions.
To address this, we combine
robust sparse anchoring and surface-normal consistency
with a high-frequency normal-consistency loss that sup-
presses localized geometric corruption while preserving
low-frequency corrections to the dense prior.
Finally, we argue that standard depth-completion met-
rics are insufficient on their own, because metrics dom-
inated by LiDAR-covered regions can obscure both be-
havior in LiDAR-unobserved regions and sensitivity to
sparse measurement artifacts. We therefore introduce an
extrapolation-focused evaluation protocol that explicitly
separates LiDAR-supported and beyond-coverage regions,
allowing us to distinguish interpolation of sparse anchors
from extrapolation of coherent geometry. Hence, we sum-
marize our contributions as follows:
• Sparse-only fusion of aligned monocular priors with
raw LiDAR. We propose a simple fusion formulation
that aligns a frozen monocular depth foundation prior to
sparse LiDAR geometry and refines it with a lightweight
completion module to predict dense metric depth.
• High-frequency regularization and beyond-coverage
evaluation.
We introduce a high-frequency normal-
consistency loss that suppresses localized artifacts un-
der raw sparse supervision, and an evaluation protocol
that separates LiDAR-supported and beyond-coverage re-
gions to distinguish interpolation from extrapolation.
• Sparse-only depth completion beyond LiDAR cover-
age. We study depth completion in a practical setting
where supervision is restricted to raw single-sweep Li-
DAR, and we highlight extrapolation beyond LiDAR cov-
erage as a central but under-evaluated aspect of the task.
2. Related Work
Depth completion has advanced through stronger comple-
tion architectures, richer supervision, and, more recently,
monocular depth foundation models. However, most prior
work is evaluated with metrics dominated by LiDAR-
supported regions, where the task reduces to local interpo-
lation. This leads to a metric saturation that obscures the
model’s ability to reason about global geometry in the ex-
trapolative regime, where no direct LiDAR support exists.
Image-guided depth completion.
Depth completion es-
timates dense metric depth from sparse range measure-
ments, typically guided by an aligned RGB image. Early
approaches relied on hand-crafted interpolation, sparsity-
invariant convolutions, and edge-aware regularization to
propagate sparse depth while respecting image structure [1,
15].
Modern methods instead learn this propagation di-
rectly, including guided and content-adaptive CNNs [10,
24], propagation-based methods such as CSPN [4], NL-
SPN [18], and DySPN [14], and architectures with ex-
plicit confidence handling or pre-propagation of sparse in-
puts [10, 25]. Transformer-based and hybrid models such as
GuideFormer [22] and CompletionFormer [36] further im-
prove image-guided completion by combining long-range
context with local refinement. Geometry-aware approaches
additionally incorporate depth–normal constraints to better


preserve local 3D structure [21, 31]. Our work is comple-
mentary to this line: rather than proposing a new comple-
tion architecture, we study how a completion backbone can
be driven by a frozen monocular prior and raw sparse Li-
DAR under sparse-only supervision.
Supervision regimes for depth completion.
Many depth
completion methods are trained with densified supervision.
On outdoor benchmarks such as KITTI [6, 26], commonly
used reference depth is obtained by accumulating multi-
ple LiDAR sweeps and filtering them with stereo post-
processing cues [26]. As a result, high-performing meth-
ods are often trained on semi-dense labels rather than raw
single-sweep LiDAR [14, 18, 36]. Alternative strategies re-
duce dependence on dense labels through photometric con-
sistency or multi-view constraints [8, 16, 30]. In contrast,
we focus on a sparse-only setting aligned with deployment:
training uses the same single-frame RGB image and raw
sparse LiDAR measurements available at inference time,
without densified labels or multi-frame supervision.
Monocular depth priors and foundation models.
Re-
cent monocular depth models provide increasingly strong
dense geometric priors from a single image, including
DepthAnything [33, 34], UniDepth [19, 20], Metric3D [11,
35], and MoGe [28, 29]. A natural next step is to trans-
fer such priors to depth completion, as demonstrated by
DMD3C [13], which distills a monocular foundation model
into a completion network via a two-stage pipeline: pseudo-
LiDAR generation for large-scale pre-training followed by
fine-tuning on real sparse LiDAR with a scale- and shift-
invariant objective.
Recent work also explores guided
diffusion for zero-shot monocular depth completion and
foundation-model-guided sparse-depth propagation for out-
of-distribution completion [3, 27]. Our approach targets a
different operating point: rather than distilling or propagat-
ing the prior through a separate foundation-model pipeline,
we align the frozen prior directly to sparse LiDAR geometry
and fuse it with a completion backbone trained using sparse-
only supervision from raw single-sweep measurements.
Extrapolation beyond LiDAR coverage.
Most prior
work evaluates depth completion with metrics dominated
by LiDAR-supported regions [13, 14, 25], where the task
is primarily interpolation, denoising, and local refinement.
Yet real camera-LiDAR systems often exhibit partial over-
lap, occlusions, and persistent coverage gaps, making ex-
trapolation beyond LiDAR coverage a practically important
regime. Although methods based on strong monocular pri-
ors can exhibit extrapolative behavior qualitatively, this ca-
pability is rarely isolated or measured explicitly. Our work
addresses this gap by studying sparse-only fusion with raw
LiDAR and monocular foundation priors and by introduc-
ing an evaluation protocol that separates LiDAR-supported
and beyond-coverage regions.
3. Method
We propose a sparse-only fusion framework for depth com-
pletion beyond LiDAR coverage. The method combines
a frozen monocular depth prior, a lightweight per-frame
alignment to raw sparse LiDAR, and a fusion module that
refines the aligned prior into dense metric depth. This de-
composition separates global metric calibration from lo-
cal geometric refinement: the monocular prior provides
dense scene structure, alignment resolves global scale and
shift ambiguity, and the fusion module incorporates sparse
LiDAR measurements where available. To ensure robust
training under raw sparse supervision, we apply geomet-
ric regularization via a high-frequency normal-consistency
loss that suppresses localized artifacts from spurious Li-
DAR measurements while preserving large-scale structure.

3.1.2. Depth Fusion from Aligned Prior and Sparse Li-
DAR
Given the aligned dense prior Dd and the sparse raw LiDAR
depth map Ds, we predict dense metric depth Df with a
lightweight refinement network that fuses both signals. In
our implementation, we use Dynamic Spatial Propagation
Network (DySPN) [14] as the fusion backbone. DySPN
iteratively refines an initial depth estimate using learned,
iteration-dependent neighbor affinities, and serves here as
a representative propagation-based completion module. We
initialize DySPN with the aligned prior Dd and use Ds as
sparse metric anchors.

3.2. Training Objective
The fusion module is trained using only the sparse and po-
tentially noisy LiDAR depth Ds together with the aligned
dense prior Dd. The objective combines a sparse anchor
term for metric consistency at valid LiDAR returns with ge-
ometric regularization that transfers surface structure from
the prior. Two components are standard: a robust sparse
anchor loss and a normal-consistency regularizer. The ad-
ditional high-frequency term applies total variation to the
per-pixel cosine discrepancy.

4. Experiments
We evaluate the proposed framework in the intended sparse-
only, single-frame supervision setting, with particular em-
phasis on extrapolation beyond LiDAR coverage. Our ex-
periments are centered on KITTI, where we report both con-
ventional depth-completion metrics and results under a con-
trolled beyond-coverage protocol. We further use NYUv2
as a clean sparse-only testbed and nuScenes for qualitative
robustness analysis in challenging outdoor scenes.
4.1. Experimental Protocol and Implementation
Details
Unless noted otherwise, training uses only raw sparse depth
as supervision, dense or semi-dense depth is used only for
validation and evaluation.
On KITTI, this means train-
ing from raw single-sweep velodyne measurements even
though standard evaluation is performed against the semi-
dense benchmark depth.
4.1.1. Datasets and evaluation regimes.
We use three datasets with complementary roles. KITTI is
the main benchmark and supports both conventional depth-
completion evaluation and our controlled beyond-coverage
protocol. NYUv2 provides dense and comparatively clean
indoor depth, which allows controlled sparse-only experi-
ments without the additional complications of outdoor Li-
DAR noise and semi-dense benchmark targets. nuScenes is
used for qualitative robustness analysis in challenging out-
door scenes with adverse conditions and noisy automotive
LiDAR.
4.1.2. KITTI beyond-coverage protocol.
For the extrapolation protocol, RGB and depth frames are
resized to 336 × 1120. During training, raw LiDAR depth
is restricted to a horizontal band in the third quarter of the
image and is used both as network input and sparse super-
vision. Evaluation is then performed on the complemen-
tary image region, which contains no direct LiDAR support.
This protocol isolates beyond-coverage prediction from in-
terpolation between nearby sparse measurements.
4.1.3. Baselines.
We compare against a prior-only baseline obtained by
aligning the frozen monocular depth prior without fu-
sion, OMNI-DC [37] as a foundation depth completion
model, and against trained completion baselines including
DySPN [14] and DMD3C [13]. For DMD3C [13], we re-
port both the released pretrained model and a variant re-
trained under our sparse-only protocol.
4.1.4. Implementation details.
We implement the model in PyTorch. The fusion backbone
is trained from scratch, while the monocular foundation

4.2. Extrapolation Beyond LiDAR Coverage on
KITTI
KITTI [26] is our main benchmark for evaluating extrapo-
lation beyond LiDAR coverage. Under the controlled pro-

held from the evaluation region: only a horizontal band of
raw LiDAR depth is retained during training, while perfor-
mance is measured on the complementary region (Fig. 3).
The task therefore requires prediction in regions with lit-

tween nearby sparse observations.

The prior-only baseline isolates the extent to which beyond-
coverage performance is derived from the aligned monocu-
lar prior alone. DMD3C [13] provides a strong learned ref-
erence point as it also distills knowledge from a monocu-
lar foundation prior, enabling a direct comparison between
prior fusion and distillation-based transfer under the same
evaluation protocol.

4.3. Sparse-Only Depth Completion on KITTI
We also evaluate the proposed framework on the standard
KITTI depth completion benchmark, where the reported
metrics are dominated by LiDAR-supported regions.
In
this regime, our method remains competitive under sparse-
only supervision (Table 1). While it does not outperform
the released pretrained DMD3C checkpoint on all metrics,
it improves over the sparse-only retrained DMD3C model
and produces accurate, geometrically coherent reconstruc-
tions without dense supervision, LiDAR accumulation, or
dataset-specific densification.
This comparison complements the beyond-coverage re-
sults with the conventional in-coverage setting. Together,
the two evaluations show that the proposed formulation
remains effective in standard LiDAR-covered completion,
while its main advantage emerges in the beyond-coverage
regime. Qualitatively, our model is also less sensitive to
localized sparse artifacts in LiDAR-covered regions, con-
sistent with the role of the robust sparse anchor and high-
frequency normal-consistency losses (Fig. 5).

4.5.3. Robustness to Sparse LiDAR Outliers
We additionally verify the effect of RSA under syn-
thetic sparse outlier corruption, a critical requirement for
production-ready raw LiDAR fusion.
Because standard
global metrics often average out severe localized artifacts,
we demonstrate in the appendix that RSA effectively iso-
lates and reduces the propagation of corrupted sparse mea-
surements into the reconstructed geometry, yielding struc-
turally robust depth maps where baselines fail.
5. Conclusion
We studied depth completion beyond LiDAR coverage in
the sparse-only supervision regime, where dense metric
depth must be inferred from a single RGB image and raw
single-sweep LiDAR without dense labels, LiDAR accumu-
lation, or pseudo-LiDAR generation. We showed that a sim-
ple fusion formulation based on a frozen monocular depth
prior, lightweight per-frame alignment, and sparse LiDAR
refinement performs strongly in this setting.
A central challenge in this regime is robustness to lo-
calized artifacts induced by raw sparse measurements. We
introduce a high-frequency normal-consistency loss that pe-
nalizes spatial variation in scalar angular discrepancy; its ef-
fect on localized artifacts is demonstrated qualitatively, but
is not established by the reported global metrics.
Beyond the method itself, we argued that conventional
depth-completion evaluation under-emphasizes extrapola-
tion and does not fully reflect robustness to sparse measure-
ment artifacts. We therefore introduced a controlled proto-
col that explicitly separates LiDAR-supported and beyond-
coverage regions. Under this protocol, the proposed frame-
work achieves strong extrapolation performance while re-
maining competitive on standard KITTI depth completion.
Overall, our results suggest that sparse-only fusion with
frozen monocular priors is a practical and effective oper-


ating point for real camera–LiDAR systems. We hope that
the proposed formulation and evaluation protocol help make
extrapolation beyond coverage and sparse-only supervision
more explicit targets in future depth-completion research.

B. Robustness to Sparse LiDAR Outliers
To further isolate the role of the robust sparse anchor (RSA)
loss, we inject synthetic large-depth outliers into the sparse
input depth within a selected rectangular image region.
Concretely, we sample pixel locations inside the box and
overwrite small local patches with far-range depth values,
producing compact clusters of spurious far-range returns
similar to multipath or reflection artifacts. This creates a
controlled stress test for robustness to sparse outliers.
Figure 7 shows that a model trained without RSA prop-
agates these corrupted sparse measurements into the sur-
rounding reconstruction, whereas the full model rejects
them and preserves the local surface geometry. In the exam-
ple shown here, the reconstruction remains visually clean
even after injecting N = 200 outlier points, with identical
RMSE (0.94 m). While this synthetic perturbation does not
replace a dedicated real-world outlier benchmark, it sup-
ports the intended role of RSA as a simple robust estimator
under raw sparse LiDAR supervision.
