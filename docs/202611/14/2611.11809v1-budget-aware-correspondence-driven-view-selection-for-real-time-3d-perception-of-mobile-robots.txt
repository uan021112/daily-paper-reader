Budget-aware Correspondence-driven View Selection for
Real-time 3D Perception of Mobile Robots
Vaishnav Ramesh1, Jacob Carter2, Shivali J. Patel1, Sanjeev J. Koppal2, and Md Jahidul Islam1
1RoboPI Laboratory, ECE Department, University of Florida
2FOCUS Laboratory, ECE Department, University of Florida
Abstract
3D visual perception and scene reconstruction are critical for autonomous robots and intelligent
transportation, yet real-world deployments often generate long, redundant image sequences that hinder
downstream processing. This report investigates BCVS (budget-aware correspondence-driven view
sampling), a pose-free view selection algorithm that offers 22+ FPS on a CPU, enabling online filtering
for efficient 3D perception by mobile robots. Unlike existing approaches that rely on full structure-
from-motion (SfM) pipelines or camera pose estimation, BCVS employs a purely correspondence-based
scoring strategy that prioritizes view novelty while maintaining sufficient overlap for geometric stability.
Given a user-specified budget, BCVS iteratively selects the most distinct frames and suppresses
near-duplicates, producing compact subsets that preserve connectivity and minimize reconstruction
loss. Extensive experiments on benchmark show that BCVS outperforms CPU-friendly baselines at
tight budgets and remains competitive as budgets scale. Field trials with large aerial and marine
robotics data further validate its practicality and scalability: BCVS reduces redundancy up to 16%
while sustaining robust SfM registration and high-quality 3D reconstructions.
Keywords: 3D reconstruction; view selection; image correspondence; real-time vision.

1 Introduction
Fast and accurate 3D reconstruction is critical for
mobile robots operating in ground, aerial, and ma-
rine domains, supporting tasks such as dense map-
ping, long-horizon navigation, mission planning, and
downstream scene understanding, including infras-
tructure inspection, asset monitoring, and structural
assessment [3, 4].
Contemporary visual pipelines
range from geometric approaches such as Structure
from Motion (SfM) and SLAM [5] to learned neural
scene representations [6,7], which transform onboard
multi-view imagery into metrically consistent 3D
structure and radiance fields, offering rich geomet-
ric and appearance cues for perception systems. In
practice, however, mobile robots often depend on
SLAM for real-time autonomy [8] while relegating
high-fidelity reconstruction to post-processing due
to constraints in power, memory, and latency.
Real-world deployments often generate long, re-
dundant mission data that substantially increases
the computational cost of image matching, bundle
adjustment (BA), and downstream training. In SfM
pipelines, computation scales with the number of
input views: retrieval methods are used to prune the
quadratic complexity of pairwise matching, while BA
repeatedly solves large-scale optimization problems.
As a result, the sheer data volume becomes a primary
bottleneck [9,10]. Similarly, neural 3D reconstruc-
tion methods scale with the number of images and
sampled rays, such that retaining fewer but more in-
formative views can both accelerate training and im-
prove numerical conditioning [6,7]. These challenges
motivate budget-aware view selection strategies that
compress missions by preserving only distinctive yet
geometrically informative frames.
Existing approaches to view selection generally
fall into two categories: next-best-view (NBV) plan-
ning and offline subset selection.
NBV planners
actively guide data acquisition by selecting the next
camera pose according to predicted information gain
or uncertainty [2,11,12]. While these methods can
achieve high-fidelity reconstructions, they require
expensive model-in-the-loop updates at each step,
which limits scalability. In contrast, offline subset-
selection methods only operate post-mission, i.e.,
after full data collection. Redundancy-aware strate-
gies [13] leverage images with pre-estimated camera
poses, whereas coverage-based approaches, such as
NeRF Director [2], rely on coarse SfM outputs to en-
force spatially uniform sampling. Although effective
at reducing redundancy, these offline methods re-
main dependent on SfM-derived geometry or camera
pose information, constraining their applicability in
settings where such priors are unavailable or com-
putationally infeasible.

(BCVS) – a fast, camera pose–free algorithm that
selects views by regularizing novelty (information
variance) in views from keypoint correspondences
alone. Using only images as input, BCVS uses a
lightweight feature matching step to estimate inter-
frame motion and select diverse viewpoints, without
relying on external poses or point clouds. Given a
computational budget as prior, the view candidates
are favored when they add new visual information
while retaining enough overlap to remain connected.
Operating in a short temporal neighborhood, BCVS
incrementally prefers informative change, suppresses
redundancy, and then lightly adjusts the set to meet
a target budget (in maximum number of views).
This budget-aware process produces a compact,
viewpoint-diverse subset that can be utilized for
fast 3D reconstruction with minimal loss.
Notably, our focus is on real-time view selection for
3D robot perception with onboard CPU processing
constraints. A fast view selection operation is cru-
cial to keep pace with the camera stream, while the
bulk of the processing power is typically dedicated to
perception and control systems [14]. However, most
existing approaches are mainly offline, requiring a
trained model, large embedding encoders, and/or
a full SfM pipeline with accurate camera poses –
which incur latency and memory overhead [15,16].
To close this gap, we adopt a pose-free and CPU-
only design that relies on lightweight keypoint
correspondences to admit distinct views while pre-
serving overlap for stable reconstruction. In prac-
tice, our system processes frames at 22.3 FPS on
CPU, enabling online filtering close to the camera
rate (30 FPS). A fast budget-adjustment pass then
consolidates the stream into a compact subset for
downstream 3D reconstruction. This hybrid work-
flow delivers the benefits of an efficient, on-board
selection without requiring deep visual inference or

2 Background & Related Work
Modern reconstruction pipelines encompass a range
of methods, including geometric, hybrid, and neural
approaches. SfM and SLAM pipelines recover cam-
era trajectories and sparse 3D structure from multi-
view imagery [10, 17–19]. These systems combine
robust feature matching, incremental mapping, and
bundle adjustment for metrically consistent models
suitable in downstream planning and localization.
Neural scene representations [6] learn a continuous
radiance field that renders high-fidelity novel views,
trading heavy training for photorealism and smooth-
ness. In particular, 3D Gaussian Splatting [7] offers
a fast, explicit alternative: it optimizes a set of
anisotropic Gaussians with per-primitive appear-
ance, enabling real-time rendering while preserving
rich geometry/appearance cues.
Moreover, active NBV (next-best-view) planners
choose the next camera view by maximizing ex-
pected information gain or uncertainty reduction
under a continually updated scene model. ActiveN-
eRF [12] estimates per-ray uncertainty to greedily
reduce it. For instance, FisherRF [20] models the
Fisher information of NeRF parameters and selects
viewpoints by maximizing the expected information
gain, whereas BavsNeRF methods [21] select batches
of complementary views from a partially trained
field. On the other hand, IOVS4NeRF models [22]
target large-scale settings with uncertainty-guided
incremental selection.

Despite considerable progress in recent years, sev-
eral view-selection methods still rely on SfM outputs
or pre-computed camera poses, which are not always
available in real-time robotics applications. Besides,
NBV approaches require a model-in-the-loop or high-
dimensional image embeddings. We address these
limitations with a purely feature-matching-based
method that eliminates the need for camera poses,
enabling real-time use by mobile robots.
3 Methodology
3.1 Overall Computational Pipeline
We introduce BCVS, a fast view-selection algorithm
that produces a compact, viewpoint-diverse subset
S from an image stream {It}t=1..N under a target
budget K for downstream 3D reconstruction. We
construct the subset S in a single pass: for each
incoming frame Ij, we compare it to the last com-
mitted view and to what is already represented in
S, to compute a score Tij that rewards informative
viewpoint change while penalizing near-duplicates,
visually static, or redundant views. Using a short
temporal window G, we retain a candidate with the
highest score and commit it when the window closes;
S thus grows by repeatedly adding the next most
informative, least redundant frame. Fig. 2 depicts
the selection pipeline in detail.
After one pass of processing, we further adjust the
view selection if the budget is under-utilized or over-
shot. If |S| < K, we densify by inserting frames into
the largest temporal gaps in S to improve coverage
and connectivity between neighbors. On the other
hand, if |S| > K, we prune by removing frames with
the smallest marginal contribution to viewpoint di-
versity relative to their neighbors. The resulting
subset S (|S| ≈K) preserves geometric diversity
and connectivity, enabling scene reconstruction with
minimal degradation compared to using all frames.

3.2 Feature Matching and Scoring
Unlike prior methods [2, 13] that rely on camera
poses, we estimate viewpoint redundancy and diver-
sity using only images via feature matching. For
each pair (i, j) in S, we detect ORB [24] keypoints
and binary descriptors; ORB offers repeatability un-
der moderate viewpoint/illumination change and
enables fast matching with a small memory foot-
print. We form putative correspondences using a
BF–Hamming matcher and retain good matches via
Lowe’s ratio test [25]. Let Mij be the resulting set
of good correspondences between Ii and Ij, and let
Ki, Kj be their detected keypoint sets. We define
the match reliability ratio as:
mrij = |Mij| : min(|Ki|, |Kj|).
(1)
This pose-free correspondence layer supplies reliable

view sampling (BCVS) pipeline is summarized in

fixed temporal window of size G to maintain tempo-

with the first frame; then, for each window of candi-

based on Eq. 5. The frame with the highest score is
added to S as the next view, the window advances
from this new anchor, and the process iterates. Us-

content, and steadily accumulates diverse viewpoints
until the entire stream is processed.

4 Benchmark Evaluation
4.1 Experimental Setup
We assess the effectiveness of the proposed BCVS al-
gorithm by training a 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)
model [7] on subsets of training views and measur-
ing reconstruction quality on a held-out test set.
To isolate the effect of view selection, all methods
use the same 3DGS pipeline and identical hyperpa-
rameters. We sweep budgets of 10-90% with 10%
intervals of the full data and report the qualitative
and quantitative results.
Dataset and Metrics.
We use the MipNeRF-
360 [1] benchmark dataset, which provides forward-
facing and 360◦trajectories with high-resolution im-
ages. According to standard practices, for each scene
we construct a test set by assigning every 8th frame;
all remaining frames form the training set. We train
3DGS for 30k iterations per scene/budget using the
default hyperparameters. Reconstruction quality is
reported on the test split using PSNR and SSIM, av-
eraged over test views and then across scenes. To en-
sure fairness, all runs share identical pre-processing,
training resolution, and random seeds; only the
training-view indices differ. In our evaluation, we
use 7 scenes: {bonsai, garden, kitchen, room,
stump, bicycle, counter}; the per-scene image
counts vary from 125 to 311 frames.
Performance Baselines. We evaluate three base-
lines under the same protocol and budget K. The
Random Sampler picks K training views randomly
without replacement; we report mean and standard
deviations over three seeds. The Uniform Sampler

5.1 Field Trials Data: Aerial and Marine Robots
We further evaluate BCVS on two long, noisy, real-
world mission data collected by 6-DOF robots to test
scalability and practical efficiency: (i) A drone orbit
of the Statue of Liberty with 6, 845 frames; and (ii)
An autonomous underwater vehicles (AUV) survey
of a coral reef at the Atlantic, with 3, 752 frames.

images due to the slow motion and near-stationary
viewpoints. To investigate the quality and efficiency
trade-off, we run multiple budgets per mission, re-

of the AUV frames, respectively. For these long
streams of redundant data, we use a larger window
gap (G = 20), so that BCVS searches for the next
distinct view over a wider temporal neighborhood,

5.2 Discussion: Strengths and Weaknesses
BCVS is a pose-free and CPU-only algorithm, ideal
for resource-constrained single-board devices. By
regularizing novelty with overlap, it preferentially
keeps parallax-rich, well-connected views and sup-
presses near-duplicates; as observed in our field data,
the resulting subsets register robustly in COLMAP
(most selected frames are incorporated), whereas
naive FPS-based uniform sampling yields large base-
line jumps or runs of similar frames that fail to
register. However, BCVS is sensitive to the balance
between connectivity and parallax: over-weighting
connectivity risks keeping rotation-only redundancy,
while prioritizing parallax can fragment overlap.
Moreover, the feature matching quality can degrade
in low-texture, dynamic, or heavily blurred scenes,
which in turn weakens the selection signals.

6 Conclusion
This report investigated a budget-aware view selec-
tion algorithm for efficient 3D reconstruction. We in-
troduced BCVS, a real-time, pose-free view-selection
pipeline that leverages keypoint correspondences
to promote informative views and suppress redun-
dancy. BCVS yields compact subsets that preserve
geometric support with minimal reconstruction loss,
running at over 22 FPS on CPU without pose es-
timation or deep encoders. Experiments on bench-
mark datasets as well as real-world aerial and marine
robotics missions show that BCVS selects as little as
4–16% of frames while sustaining clean registration
and high-quality reconstructions. It outperforms
CPU-friendly baselines at tight budgets by signif-
icant margins and remains competitive at higher
budgets.
Field experimental analyses further re-
veal that excessive near-duplicate views can degrade
reconstructions, while budget-aware selection sta-
bilizes geometry. These results demonstrate that
simple correspondence cues are sufficient to achieve
scalable, efficient, and accurate view selection – ideal
for use by mobile robots.
