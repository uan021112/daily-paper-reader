Abstract
Online 3D street-scene asset construction turns multi-view
video into a continually updated scene of independently ed-
itable objects. The central challenge is to accumulate par-
tial, intermittent observations under motion and occlusion
without fragmenting identities or mixing evidence across
objects. To address this challenge, we propose Video2Asset,
an online framework for constructing persistent 3D assets
from multi-view video.
Using occupancy as an interme-
diate representation, we construct identity-aware observa-
tions with Historical Carrier Guidance for instance decod-
ing and an identity encoder fusing instance-aligned 3D fea-
tures with multi-view appearance. Accumulated-Evidence
Association compares current observations with identity,
geometric, and appearance evidence accumulated within
asset records to determine identity inheritance and update
world-coordinate states. We instantiate meshes from an as-

1. Introduction
Editable 3D street scenes allow individual objects to be
moved, replaced, or removed within a shared environment.
Constructing such scenes online from multi-view video re-
quires capturing object-level geometry and semantics, then
accumulating observations into assets with persistent identi-
ties and evolving world-coordinate states. The central chal-
lenge is to accumulate partial, intermittent observations un-
der motion and occlusion without fragmenting one object
across assets or mixing evidence from distinct objects.
Neural radiance fields (NeRFs) and 3D Gaussian Splat-

ting (3DGS) reconstruct street scenes by fitting multi-view
images, with object-level formulations supporting indepen-
dent manipulation of scene elements [5, 24, 33].
Un-
der sparse view coverage, however, fitting image colors
alone can reproduce observed appearance without ade-
quately constraining the underlying 3D structure [23, 43].
Occupancy representations trained with explicit 3D geo-
metric and semantic supervision encode occupied regions
and scene categories [32, 36], providing a structured spatial
basis for street-scene construction.
Temporal occupancy models propagate scene evidence
across frames [35, 44], while 4D panoptic occupancy track-
ing couples geometry and semantics with cross-frame in-
stance identities [4, 20, 21]. These capabilities provide the
temporal perception and association basis for accumulating
observations into persistent assets. However, under occlu-
sion and instance competition, query-based identity prop-
agation suffers from identity fragmentation and erroneous
instance merging, disrupting the object histories required
for consistent asset construction. Turning these predictions
into editable assets further requires consolidating observa-
tions into persistent object records with evolving world-
coordinate states.
To address these challenges, we propose Video2Asset,
an online framework that uses occupancy as an intermedi-
ate representation to construct persistent 3D street-scene as-
sets from multi-view video (Figure 1). First, we construct
identity-aware observations that capture object geometry
and semantics while providing identity evidence for cross-
frame association. Historical Carrier Guidance (HCG) uses
motion-aligned historical instance regions to guide current
feature aggregation, providing object-specific spatial sup-
port when observations are incomplete. An identity encoder
fuses instance-aligned 3D features with multi-view appear-
ance to provide identity evidence for asset association.
Second,
Accumulated-Evidence Association (AEA)
links observations to persistent assets using evidence ac-
cumulated within their records. It constructs observation–
asset relation features by comparing the observation with
each candidate’s identity, geometric, and appearance evi-
dence. Pairwise scores rank the candidates, while a sep-
arate inheritance predictor reads the full candidate relation
set to determine whether to inherit an existing identity. One-
to-one assignment extends matched asset histories and up-
dates world-coordinate states, while unassigned observa-
tions initialize new records. We instantiate meshes from
an asset library using these states and combine them with
an occupancy-derived static background to support object
deletion, trajectory editing, and mesh replacement.
Our contributions are summarized as follows:
• We propose Video2Asset, an online framework that turns
multi-view video into persistent 3D street-scene assets
with world-coordinate states and independently editable
library-mesh instances.
• We develop identity-aware observation construction that
combines HCG-guided instance decoding with an identity
encoder fusing instance-aligned 3D features and multi-
view appearance.
• We introduce AEA, which compares current observations
with identity, geometric, and appearance evidence accu-
mulated within asset records for candidate ranking and
identity-inheritance decisions.
2. Related Work
Appearance-based scene construction.
Radiance-field
and Gaussian methods reconstruct static or dynamic scenes
by fitting multi-view images [8, 9, 13, 22, 37–40]. Object-
level formulations organize these representations for edit-
ing and simulation [5, 24, 33, 42]. Under sparse views,
fitting observed colors alone can overfit appearance while
leaving geometry underconstrained [23, 43], motivating ge-
ometric guidance [10, 41]. We use occupancy as an in-
termediate representation, grounding identity-aware obser-
vations in geometric and semantic supervision to capture
scene structure for asset construction.
Geometry-based scene construction. Point-cloud and
surface reconstruction recover scene geometry through
multi-view depth estimation or implicit surface model-
ing [17, 29, 34]. Semantic scene completion extends this
spatial modeling to jointly infer occupancy and categories
in visible and occluded regions [3, 16]. For surrounding
scenes, occupancy prediction encodes geometric and se-
mantic structure with voxel grids, tri-perspective features,
or semantic Gaussians [11, 12, 32, 36]. Panoptic formu-
lations further distinguish individual instances within oc-
cupied regions [18, 30]. We extend this spatial modeling
across time, preserving object identity continuity and con-
solidating observations into persistent assets with evolving
world-coordinate states.
Temporal scene and asset modeling. Temporal occu-
pancy models integrate cross-frame evidence through prop-
agated scene representations or spatiotemporal memory [15,
35, 44]. TrackOcc, LaGS, and SGE make identities ex-
plicit through propagated instance queries [4, 20, 21]. Un-
der occlusion and instance competition, identity fragmenta-
tion and erroneous instance merging disrupt the object his-
tories needed for asset construction. History-based tracking
accumulates object evidence across observations [2, 7, 14],
while object-level mapping maintains evolving states in
world coordinates [26].
We selectively use accumulated
identity, geometric, and appearance evidence to associate
current observations with persistent assets.

3. Method
As illustrated in Figure 2(a), Video2Asset constructs per-
sistent, editable 3D street scenes online from multi-view
video. We first construct identity-aware observations us-
ing occupancy as an intermediate representation: Histor-
ical Carrier Guidance guides current feature aggregation
with aligned previous instance regions, and an identity
encoder summarizes instance-aligned evidence for cross-
frame comparison (Section 3.2).
Accumulated-Evidence
Association then relates current observations to accumu-
lated asset evidence to determine identity inheritance and
update persistent records (Section 3.3). The associated ob-
servations update world-coordinate asset states, from which
object meshes are instantiated and composed with the static
background.

terms supported by the available positive and negative sam-

Association learning.
Association training follows

continuing with model-controlled rollouts. During rollout,
predicted associations update the histories used by subse-
quent decisions, with state retained across training chunks.
Ground truth supplies supervision targets; observation fea-
tures and geometry remain predicted.

4. Experiments
4.1. Configurations
Datasets.
We evaluate Video2Asset on three multi-
view driving datasets: CarlaOcc, nuScenes, and Waymo.
CarlaOcc [6] comprises 104 synthetic sequences, each
with 1,000 frames captured by six surround-view cam-
eras at 20 Hz, and provides panoptic occupancy labels.
nuScenes [1] comprises 1,000 real-world scenes, each
lasting 20 seconds, with six surround-view cameras and
keyframe annotations at 2 Hz.
Waymo [27] comprises
1,150 real-world scenes, each lasting 20 seconds, with five
cameras operating at 10 Hz.
Occupancy annotations for
nuScenes and Waymo are provided by Occ3D [28].
Implementation.
Experiments are conducted on
NVIDIA A800 GPUs. We follow the original training pro-
cedures of TrackOcc, LaGS, and SGE. The image back-
bones are ResNet-50 for TrackOcc and VoVNetV2-99 for
LaGS and SGE. After occupancy and HCG training, we
freeze perception to train the identity encoder, then freeze
observation construction to train AEA. Inference proceeds
online, retaining observation histories and world-coordinate

Metrics. We evaluate our method along three dimen-
sions: observation quality, temporal consistency, and asset
quality. Observation quality assesses per-frame geometry,
semantics, and instance grouping using mean Intersection-
over-Union (mIoU), binary occupancy Intersection-over-
Union (IoU), single-frame Segmentation and Tracking
Quality (STQ1), and single-frame Association Quality
(AQ1) [20]. Temporal consistency assesses tracking qual-
ity and identity continuity using Segmentation and Track-
ing Quality (STQ) and Association Quality (AQ) [4, 31],
Higher Order Tracking Accuracy (HOTA) [19], and Identi-
fication F1 Score (IDF1) [25]. Asset quality uses framewise
3D oriented bounding-box Average Precision (AP) against
annotated object boxes within the evaluation region, re-
ported at IoU thresholds of 0.25 (AP25) and 0.50 (AP50).
Asset Fragmentation (AF) measures the mean number of
additional persistent asset records per GT object matched at
least once within a continuous evaluation segment.

terval (0–40%) longer than 2 s between high-visibility ob-
servations (80–100%), we compute AF over their complete
scene trajectories. Table 2 shows that Video2Asset reduces
AF from 2.48 to 1.90 on TrackOcc, and by 0.18 and 0.09
on LaGS and SGE, respectively. These results show that
Video2Asset better preserves asset identities on this chal-
lenging subset.

5. Discussion
Limitations.
Although Video2Asset supports persistent,
editable street-scene construction, two limitations remain.
First, class-selected library meshes do not fully capture
instance-specific shape details.
Second, assets support
object-level poses and trajectories, with articulated motion
limited to predefined animations.
Conclusion.
We presented Video2Asset, an online
framework for constructing persistent, editable 3D street-
scene assets from multi-view video. Using occupancy as
an intermediate representation, HCG guides instance de-
coding and an identity encoder fuses 3D features with ap-
pearance to construct identity-aware observations.
AEA
compares observations with evidence accumulated within
asset records to determine identity inheritance and up-
date world-coordinate states for mesh instantiation and
editing.
Multi-dataset experiments show improved ob-
servation quality, temporal consistency, and asset qual-
