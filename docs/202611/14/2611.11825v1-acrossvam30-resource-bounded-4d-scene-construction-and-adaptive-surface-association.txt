ABSTRACT
Combining video generation, dynamic reconstruction and monocular SLAM re-
quires consistent coordinates, explicit handling of missing outputs and predictable
memory use. We present ACROSSVAM3.0, an offline system that converts text,
images or monocular RGB video into a versioned point representation. Frozen
components run sequentially on one GPU; map corrections update cameras, points
and trajectories together. The coordination layer adds no neural parameters. Three
fresh input-mode runs completed with a maximum sampled task-process-tree GPU
footprint of 17.14 GB. In a frozen 16-scene video suite, every scene yielded a
4D reconstruction and 15 yielded a non-empty SLAM map. Strict cross-window
matching reduced local surface error but lost long-range track coverage on TAP-
Vid-DAVIS. We therefore developed multi-hypothesis adaptive surface chaining
(MHAC), which retains several candidate matches and an explicit missing state.
After selection on disjoint development data, MHAC improved Average Jaccard
over a position-only baseline by 0.1394 on a frozen ten-video holdout (95% boot-
strap interval [0.1074, 0.1689]). Across 265 RoboTAP videos, the mean paired
gain was 0.0610 ([0.0510, 0.0715]), with 215 wins, one tie and 49 losses. These
results support resource-bounded scene construction and an external tracking gain
under the tested protocol. They do not establish metric scale, semantic identity or
high-fidelity novel-view rendering.

INTRODUCTION
Video generators, monocular reconstruction models, and learned SLAM systems now provide strong
but different views of a dynamic scene. Large video diffusion transformers synthesize plausible
motion from text or an image (WanTeam et al., 2025); feed-forward reconstructors infer time-
varying geometry and trajectories from monocular RGB (Luo et al., 2026); and learned dense SLAM
estimates camera motion, maps, loop closures, and recovery state (Murai et al., 2025). Combining
these capabilities could provide a common route from an instruction, a reference image, or an
observed video to a dynamic three-dimensional scene. In practice, the combination is fragile. Each
component uses its own coordinate frame, scale, preprocessing, confidence convention, and memory
profile, while generated frames must not be confused with physical observations.
The difficulty lies in coordinating outputs whose assumptions differ. A local reconstruction may
have an arbitrary scale; a SLAM update may change the reference frame; a rejected match may leave
a trajectory without support. Generated images also require different provenance from recorded
observations. We therefore ask whether a common scene representation can preserve these distinctions,
propagate coordinate changes consistently and remain within a single-GPU budget.
We study this problem under a strict operational contract: one process-intensive component at a time,
one 40-GB-class GPU per task, RGB-only inference, short offline scenes, and relative rather than
metric scale. Figure 1 summarizes the resulting system. Text and image inputs are first converted to
RGB video; observed monocular videos bypass generation and additionally enter RGB-only SLAM.
A windowed 4D reconstructor produces dense local point trajectories. Robust Sim(3) alignment
maps accepted windows into a versioned scene frame, while rejected windows remain explicitly local.

Motion evidence, equal-budget trajectory retention, and cross-window surface association operate on
the exported representation instead of modifying pretrained networks.
Cross-window association exposes a second question: should temporal consistency be enforced by
increasingly strict matches? Our predeclared TAP-Vid-DAVIS study gives a negative answer for the
initial method. Aggregating geometry and colour over three overlap frames did not improve Average
Jaccard (AJ) over a simpler one-frame rule and reduced the number of valid chains. This failure
motivates MHAC (multi-hypothesis adaptive surface chaining), which preserves a bounded candidate
lattice and trades geometric precision against coverage before query-specific decoding. We select one
configuration on disjoint development data, pass a frozen ten-video holdout gate, and evaluate it once
on all 265 RoboTAP videos under a hash-bound protocol.
The system provides:
1. an executable three-input system that preserves provenance while producing explicit time-
varying point geometry, camera trajectories, and RGB-only SLAM artifacts under a mea-
sured single-GPU contract;
2. a versioned coordination layer in which local 4D arrays remain immutable and map correc-
tions atomically update cameras, points, trajectories, and coordinate metadata;
3. an evidence-based study of motion ranking, support-aware multi-frame surface correspon-
dence, and fixed-budget trajectory retention on frozen real-video scenes; and
4. a multi-hypothesis association procedure motivated by a failed strict-chain experiment and
evaluated on a disjoint holdout and 265 external videos without post-test reselection.

RELATED WORK
Video generation and 4D reconstruction.
Diffusion and flow-based video models have scaled
generation through latent representations and transformer backbones (Ho et al., 2022; Rombach
et al., 2022; Peebles & Xie, 2023; Lipman et al., 2023; WanTeam et al., 2025). Dynamic scene
reconstruction instead seeks geometry and motion that remain coherent across time. Neural fields and
Gaussian representations support dynamic novel-view synthesis (Wu et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2024;
Lin et al., 2024), while MoSca and Shape of Motion use motion scaffolds or low-dimensional motion
bases to consolidate monocular cues (Lei et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2025). 4RC provides feed-forward
dense 4D reconstruction and conditional temporal querying (Luo et al., 2026). ACROSSVAM3.0
uses these advances as frozen components; its contribution lies in resource-bounded coordination,
failure-preserving evaluation, and surface association rather than a new generative or reconstruction
backbone.
Learned geometry and SLAM in dynamic scenes.
Classical and learned SLAM systems estimate
camera trajectories and persistent maps, with DROID-SLAM demonstrating recurrent dense bundle
adjustment and ORB-SLAM3 providing a mature feature-based baseline (Teed & Deng, 2021;
Campos et al., 2021). DUSt3R and MASt3R recast matching and reconstruction in learned pointmap
space (Wang et al., 2024; Leroy et al., 2024); MASt3R-SLAM builds tracking, local fusion, graph
construction, loop closure, and global optimization on these priors (Murai et al., 2025). Dynamic-
scene systems remove uncertain regions or jointly represent static and moving content (Zheng et al.,
2025; Fan et al., 2026). We retain the full SLAM state machine for observed RGB but keep dynamic
reconstruction outputs separate from map constraints. This avoids treating a generated or moving
surface as evidence for the physical camera trajectory.
Long-range point and surface association.
Tracking-any-point benchmarks measure both lo-
calization and occlusion across long videos (Doersch et al., 2022). TAPIR, PIPs, CoTracker, and
BootsTAP improve temporal refinement, joint tracking, or training data (Doersch et al., 2023; Harley
et al., 2022; Karaev et al., 2024; Doersch et al., 2024); RoboTAP demonstrates the relevance of
persistent point tracks to robot imitation (Vecerik et al., 2024). Our cross-window problem differs
from training a 2D tracker: each node is a dense 3D trajectory inside one reconstruction window, and
edges must reconcile separate local coordinate systems. We use TAP-Vid metrics to quantify the
resulting projected chains while keeping the graph query-independent until decoding.
Resource-bounded composition.
Model compression and efficient adaptation reduce training or
deployment cost, but peak memory in a composed system is also controlled by scheduling, activation
lifetime, preprocessing, and persistent state. ACROSSVAM3.0 treats the resource envelope as an
experimental variable. It records task-process-tree memory separately from unrelated device use,
launches large components sequentially, validates every output before adoption, and reports the exact
resolution, sequence length, and sampling interval to which each memory statement applies.

The operational contract requires a single physical GPU per task and a sampled task-process-tree
peak below 40,000,000,000 bytes. Large neural components run in separate subprocesses and never
remain resident concurrently. This contract is a measured property of a fixed profile, not a proof for

3.2
HETEROGENEOUS INPUT ROUTING
For text and image modes, a frozen 5B Wan2.2 TI2V model produces 17 RGB frames at 832 × 480
using 12 sampling steps (WanTeam et al., 2025). The text encoder executes on CPU and model
weights are offloaded between stages. Image mode records the reference image hash and uses it only
as the generator condition. Video mode decodes at most 60 frames and records source timestamps
when available. Every frame manifest declares whether pixels are observed or generated. Only
observed video enters SLAM.
The pipeline writes a durable stage record before starting each subprocess. A stage is accepted only
when it exits successfully, resource logs are present, array shapes and finite values pass validation,
and output hashes can be reloaded. Existing output directories are never overwritten. This design
keeps implementation failures, preflight rejections, and completed artifacts distinguishable.

3.4
SLAM ANCHORING AND ATOMIC MAP UPDATES
Observed RGB runs a pinned MASt3R-SLAM instance before 4D reconstruction (Murai et al.,
2025). The SLAM branch exports timestamped camera poses, keyframes, pointmaps, graph edges,
loop/relocalization events, and restartable backend state. Retrieval proposals are not reported as loop
closures unless the backend accepts their constraints.
Each 4D window is aligned either to accepted SLAM keyframe geometry or to an already anchored
overlapping window. Candidate static correspondences are split deterministically: 80% fit a robust
Sim(3), and 20% measure held-out reprojection. Alignment is rejected if geometry is degenerate,
consensus falls below 35%, spatial coverage falls below 25%, fewer than 20 held-out pairs have
positive depth, or held-out median reprojection exceeds 3 pixels. Rejection preserves the window in a
named local frame rather than inserting an identity transform and pretending global alignment.
When the SLAM graph changes, common keyframes induce a new window transform A′
k. The update
is committed only if every affected window passes centre-residual and rotation checks. Cameras,
points, trajectories, and metadata then advance from vk to vk +1 together. The source arrays and their
hashes remain unchanged, so every global state can be audited against the same local reconstruction.

EXPERIMENTAL DESIGN
4.1
FROZEN COMPONENTS AND EXECUTION PROFILE
The main neural components contain 13,594,090,959 instantiated parameters: 4,999,787,712 in the
video diffusion transformer, 5,680,910,336 in the text encoder, 704,688,668 in the VAE, 1,520,065,387
in 4RC, and 688,638,856 in MASt3R. The coordination layer adds no neural parameters and performs
no end-to-end training. All weights, source revisions, and file hashes are pinned. The low-memory
profile uses 17 generated frames or at most 60 observed frames, 16-frame reconstruction windows,
four-frame overlap, and point-splat visualization.
GPU use is sampled every 50 ms. We report both the maximum memory attributable to the task
process tree and total device use, and we flag any foreign compute process. This interval can miss
shorter transients; all resource statements therefore retain the sampling qualifier. Runs launch only
when the allowed device is otherwise idle.
4.2

The frozen real-video suite contains 16 short scenes drawn from TUM RGB-D, Bonn dynamic
RGB-D, and DyCheck (Sturm et al., 2012; Gao et al., 2022). Ground-truth depth, pose, or motion
references are isolated from inference and used only by the evaluator. Each scene contributes one
independent unit; frames, pixels, tracks, and overlap folds are nested observations.
Long-range projected tracking uses TAP-Vid metrics at 256 × 256 (Doersch et al., 2022). P4 used
all 30 TAP-Vid-DAVIS videos once under a protocol frozen before payload access. P5 uses the 50
RGB-Stacking videos in a fixed 30/10/10 calibration, selection-validation, and development-holdout

4.3
METRICS AND STATISTICAL UNITS
SLAM is summarized by absolute trajectory error (ATE), one-step translation RPE, and one-step
rotation RPE when timestamp association meets the frozen threshold. Motion ranking uses ROC
AUC and balanced accuracy against a geometric depth-change reference, not semantic labels. Surface
association reports held-out reprojection error on correspondences excluded from fitting. Budget
sampling reports event recall and spatial coverage at a common K = 8,192.
Point tracking reports AJ, average position accuracy across 1, 2, 4, 8, and 16 pixels, and occlusion
accuracy. Macro averages weight videos equally. Confidence intervals use 10,000 scene- or video-
level percentile bootstrap resamples. Because the D25 analysis plan was written after its frozen test
outputs were generated, those intervals are descriptive and no p-values are reported. P4 and P5 use
their preregistered comparison directions and seeds.

5.3
FROZEN REAL VIDEOS EXPOSE BOTH CAPABILITY AND FAILURE
All 16 frozen real-video scenes produced structurally valid 4RC outputs (Figure 3). Fifteen produced
non-empty SLAM maps. The remaining scene, dycheck wheel, exited without a usable map and
was retained as a failure. Of 12 scenes preregistered for pose evaluation, 11 met timestamp-association
requirements. Their median ATE RMSE was 0.0170 m with a descriptive scene-bootstrap 95% interval
of [0.0066, 0.0428] m; median translation RPE was 0.0109 m [0.0024, 0.0211], and median mean
rotation RPE was 0.718◦[0.292◦, 1.074◦]. The nominal metric units follow the evaluation references
and do not convert the complete output to a guaranteed metric-scale reconstruction.
The maximum task-process-tree GPU footprint across these 16 reconstructions was 16,265,510,912
bytes. The single SLAM failure and one unscorable pose case prevent a claim of universal robustness.
They also demonstrate why success-rate accounting must remain separate from numeric metrics
computed only on valid trajectories.
5.4
COORDINATION MODULES IMPROVE TWO NARROW DIAGNOSTICS
The equal-rank motion fusion achieved a median ROC AUC of 0.684 [0.596, 0.769] across 12 scenes
and median balanced accuracy of 0.593 [0.559, 0.696] at the frozen 0.65 threshold. One scene had
AUC 0.491, an explicit counterexample. Since the frozen test retained only the fused score, this result
establishes absolute discrimination against the geometric reference but not superiority over either
component score on test data.
Three-frame geometry–colour correspondence reduced scene-level median held-out reprojection error
by 0.272 pixels [0.174, 0.381], or 27.1% [19.9%, 30.4%], relative to the one-frame rule. Fifteen of
16 scenes improved. This endpoint is restricted to jointly visible surfaces in adjacent overlapping
windows; it does not test object identity across long occlusions.
At the same K = 8,192, motion-diversity sampling increased event recall by a median 0.0993
[0.0967, 0.1035] over uniform sampling and improved all 16 scenes. Median spatial-coverage ratio
was 1.215 [1.197, 1.259]. Because this sampler runs after reconstruction, the result supports storage-
efficient trajectory retention but not lower neural-inference memory.

5.5
EXTERNAL TRACKING FALSIFIES THE STRICT-CHAIN HYPOTHESIS
P4 completed all 30 TAP-Vid-DAVIS videos and 1,999 frames without excluding low-performing
cases (Figure 4). In the preregistered first-query mode, the strict three-frame rule obtained AJ
13.21%, compared with 13.26% for the appearance-aware one-frame rule and 14.63% for the position-
only baseline. The paired P4–B1 difference was −0.050 percentage points with a video-bootstrap
95% interval of [−0.391, +0.275] percentage points. The P4–B0 difference was −1.423 points
[−2.280, −0.626].
The query assignment rate was common across variants, but visible-chain coverage fell from 28.53%
for B0 to 22.11% for B1 and 21.47% for P4. This diagnostic is consistent with premature pruning: the
strict rule can improve local overlap precision yet lose complete projected tracks. It does not by itself
prove that coverage is the only causal mechanism. The maximum task-process-tree GPU footprint
was 26,543,652,864 bytes, and two contamination-triggered attempts were preserved separately
before clean reruns.

FROZEN HOLDOUT AND ROBOTAP TESTS VALIDATE ADAPTIVE ASSOCIATION
The P4 result fixed the design objective for P5: retain a small set of plausible surface edges instead
of tightening a single binary rule. Four candidates advanced from the 30-video calibration split and
were evaluated without label access during reconstruction or graph construction on ten selection-
validation videos. All four passed the prespecified coverage, occlusion, and candidate-count guardrails
(Figure 5).
The deterministic rule selected C02. Its first-query AJ was 0.4993, with a paired improvement
over B0 of 0.1219 and a 95% video-bootstrap interval of [0.0856, 0.1701]; all ten videos improved.
Visible-chain coverage was 0.6233, 0.1349 above B0, and occlusion accuracy was 0.7710. C02
and C03 differed by only 0.00035 AJ, so the evidence supports reproducible selection rather than
statistical superiority of C02 over every alternative.
The selection result was not used as confirmatory evidence. On the frozen ten-video development
holdout, C02 improved AJ over B0 by 0.1394 with a video-bootstrap 95% interval of [0.1074, 0.1689]
and improved all ten videos (Figure 6). Macro visible-chain coverage increased by 0.2020. The mean-
effect, win-count, coverage, and resource checks all passed, authorizing the hash-bound external
protocol without candidate reselection.
All 265 RoboTAP videos then completed and remained in the aggregate. Mean first-query AJ
increased from 0.1994 for B0 to 0.2604 for MHAC. The paired improvement was 0.0610 (95%
interval [0.0510, 0.0715]), with 215 wins, one tie, and 49 losses. Macro visible-chain coverage
increased by 0.0685, and the maximum sampled task-process-tree footprint was 16,265,510,912
bytes (15.15 GiB). All four preregistered external checks passed. The 49 losses show material
heterogeneity, so this result supports external robot-video point-tracking generalization against B0
under this protocol rather than universal superiority.

DISCUSSION
The results distinguish local correspondence accuracy from complete-track coverage. Three-frame
matching lowered overlap reprojection error in the frozen scene suite, yet performed worse than
the position-only baseline on TAP-Vid-DAVIS. The endpoints measure different properties: overlap
error conditions on supported matches, whereas AJ also penalizes missing tracks. Retaining several
candidates is useful when a locally plausible match should not determine the entire chain.
The RoboTAP gain is consistent with this interpretation. Visible-chain coverage increased by 6.85
percentage points alongside the AJ improvement. However, MHAC changes candidate retention,
missing-state decisions and occlusion behaviour together, so the experiment does not isolate coverage
as the sole cause. The 49 videos favouring B0 also show that the selected rule is not uniformly better.
A future mechanism study would need fresh evaluation data and separate interventions on these
decisions.
The systems result concerns coordinate consistency and resource use. Immutable local arrays
preserve the reconstruction evidence, while versioned transforms keep map corrections consistent
across cameras and trajectories. Sequential execution permits the frozen components to share one

AcrossVAM3.0
GPU. This arrangement is useful for offline inspection and reuse, but it does not remove uncertainty
inherited from the components.
The evaluated system has several limits. Text and image runs establish execution for 17 frames; the
separate 121-frame action-quality gate failed. Point splats do not establish high-fidelity novel views,
and projected tracking does not establish semantic identity after occlusion. The external comparison
is restricted to B0 on the frozen robot-video protocol, without a comprehensive comparison to
specialized trackers. Resource measurements are sampled every 50 ms and may miss shorter peaks.
The calibrated Gaussian extension provides a separate view-synthesis demonstration from dense
multiview inputs, with unresolved footwear artefacts; it does not close these monocular evidence
gaps.
7
CONCLUSION
ACROSSVAM3.0 produces versioned dynamic point scenes from three input routes and adds RGB-
only SLAM for observed video. The tested short-scene profile ran within the measured single-GPU
budget. The association experiments show why local geometric precision alone is insufficient: strict
matching lost long-range coverage, while a bounded candidate lattice improved mean AJ over B0 on
the frozen RoboTAP evaluation. The retained failures define the present scope of the result and the
cases that require further study.

SCENE4D FIELD SEMANTICS
Each local submap stores timestamps, frame identifiers, Twc, intrinsics, image size, dense points,
point confidence, RGB, dense trajectories, trajectory identifiers, colour, trajectory confidence, and
ternary visibility. Confidence values are raw model scores and are not interpreted as calibrated
probabilities. Visibility denotes projection/depth support, not ground-truth occlusion. A static-
probability value of −1 denotes unknown. These conventions are validated on load and preserved in
the scene manifest.

FAILURE AND MISSINGNESS POLICY
A task can end as completed, failed, blocked by a frozen prerequisite, or contaminated by an unrelated
GPU process. Failures remain in task counts. A contaminated attempt may be rerun only under a
rule frozen before its outcome is known, and both attempts remain recorded. Numeric summaries do
not impute missing trajectories or empty maps. Predeclared denominator units are scenes or videos,
never frames or pixels.

CONFIRMATORY EVALUATION AUDIT
The final P5 result was admitted only after the frozen completion audit verified the following
conditions:
1. the ten-video holdout passed its four-part gate before external payload access;
2. the final protocol bound the selected method, holdout report, input inventory, analysis seed,
and hashes;
3. all 265 admitted RoboTAP units completed and remained in the aggregate;
4. inference and labels were isolated, task processes used one GPU each, and the sampled peak

proxy state, resource logs, and per-video report hashes; it does not convert the external result into
evidence for claims outside the specified protocol.
