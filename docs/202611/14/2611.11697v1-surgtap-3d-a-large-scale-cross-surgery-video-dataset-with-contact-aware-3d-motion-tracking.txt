Abstract—Understanding 3D scene dynamics in minimally
invasive surgery (MIS) is a fundamental step toward enabling
physically intelligent and autonomous surgical systems. However,
existing public datasets are largely confined to ex-vivo or sim-
ulated environments, offering limited scalability and diversity
in real-world clinical scenarios. Moreover, current annotations
typically focus on either tissue or instruments alone, overlooking
the critical contact dynamics that govern tool–tissue interac-
tions. This gap limits the downstream applications such as
augmented reality guidance and autonomous surgery driven by
vision-language-action (VLA) models or world models, which
demand large-scale, high-quality 3D supervision. In this work,
we construct SurgTAP-3D, a large-scale in-vivo pseudo-3D sur-
gical motion tracking dataset and benchmark, comprising 520K
frames, 4,549 action clips, and 1.4M 2D/3D point trajectories

augmented reality–assisted interventions [10]. These tech-
nologies enhance situational awareness, decision support, and
execution accuracy during complex procedures. Recent ad-
vances in data-driven and multimodal learning have further
accelerated progress toward surgical autonomy [11], includ-
ing language-conditioned imitation learning transformers [12],
vision–language–action (VLA) models for end-to-end sutur-
ing [13], and world models for visuomotor policy learning

based surgical automation to improve execution precision,
reduce surgeon workload, and enhance procedural safety.
Despite these advances, substantial challenges still impede
the realization of fully autonomous, long-horizon surgical
tasks. A primary limitation is the scarcity of large-scale,
high-fidelity endoscopic datasets with synchronized visual
observations and robotic kinematics, which constrains the
scalability and generalization of policy learning frameworks.
Existing surgical datasets with synchronized video and robotic
kinematics remain limited in scale and task diversity [4, 5, 13].
For instance, SRT-H [12] proposes a hierarchical framework
for dexterous and long-horizon surgical automation but re-
lies on trajectories collected from 31 porcine gallbladders,
restricting task diversity and limiting cross-procedure gen-
eralization. Similarly, SutureBot [13] provides 1,890 dVRK
demonstrations for long-horizon suturing; however, these data
are collected in tissue phantoms, leading to a substantial sim-
to-real domain gap. In contrast, recent advances in VLA-based
models [16]–[18] and world models [19]–[21] typically rely
on datasets comprising thousands to millions of temporally
extended interaction sequences in diverse tasks, embodiments,
and environments to achieve robust generalization. Existing
surgical demonstration datasets remain limited in scale and
diversity, restricting the generalization of high-capacity mul-
timodal policies. Therefore, advancing surgical automation
requires the collection of large-scale, high-quality real-world
demonstrations that provide temporally synchronized action
kinematics and endoscopic image streams to support robust
and generalizable policy learning.
Despite the availability of diverse public datasets in surgical
vision research, existing datasets still face several key limita-
tions that hinder the development of scalable and generalizable
models for 3D motion tracking in real-world applications, as
reported in Tab. I. Firstly, most datasets [4]–[6] are collected
in ex-vivo or simulated environments rather than in-vivo surgi-
cal scenes, where visual appearance, illumination, and tissue
dynamics differ substantially from real-world conditions. Sec-
ondly, the scalability and diversity of existing datasets remain
limited, as many focus on a single surgical procedure or a con-
strained acquisition setup (e.g., exclusively manual or robotic).
Such restrictions substantially limit cross-procedure variabil-
ity and hinder generalization across heterogeneous clinical
environments and operational conditions. Finally, annotated
targets are typically restricted to either tissue or instruments,
resulting in limited representational flexibility and a lack of
contact-aware annotations that capture tool–tissue interaction
regions. We define “contact-aware” annotation as explicitly
marking visible tool–tissue interaction regions and using them
to initialize trajectories around surgical interaction areas, with-
out assuming force sensing or contact-physics modeling. This
impedes the development of contact-aware perception and
physically grounded motion understanding, both of which
are essential for autonomous surgical manipulation. These
limitations highlight the urgent need for large-scale, cross-
surgery, and contact-aware 3D datasets to enable robust
perception, reasoning, and planning for autonomous surgical
systems. Motivated by this, we further ask: given the massive
amount of surgical video data available, can we recover high-
quality 3D action trajectories from videos?
Addressing this problem involves two primary challenges.
First, in the absence of priors such as kinematics or cam-
era calibration, recovering accurate camera parameters, scene
geometry, and instrument motion from monocular surgical
videos is inherently difficult [22]. Second, contact-aware la-
beling is labor-intensive, often requiring substantial manual
effort [1, 3] or specialized physical markers, such as tattoo
needles used in STIR [2]. To address these challenges, we draw
inspiration from recent advances in large-scale foundation
models for 3D reconstruction and motion tracking, which


3
have demonstrated the ability to learn accurate geometry and
motion representations in a feed-forward manner. Traditional
multi-stage pipelines, such as structure-from-motion (SfM),
often suffer from drift accumulation and inconsistent scale. In
contrast, modern data-driven reconstruction frameworks [23]–
[26] can directly predict camera poses and dense point
clouds, achieving scale-consistent depth estimation even in
challenging endoscopic environments [25]. Similarly, recent
3D motion tracking methods [27, 28] and depth foundation
models [29, 30] have shown promise in lifting 2D point tracks
to 3D using learned depth priors, paving the way for scalable
3D motion understanding in surgical settings.
Motivated by these insights, we introduce the first unified
framework for surgical 3D point tracking, designed to con-
struct large-scale, cross-surgery 3D datasets that facilitate the
development of spatially aware autonomous surgical systems.
Our goal is to recover high-fidelity 4D trajectories and 3D
geometry from highly dynamic, uncalibrated monocular surgi-
cal videos, capturing complex tool-tissue contact in real-world
clinical settings. There are several core designs in our method.
Firstly, we exploit priors from feed-forward foundation models
in reconstruction, point tracking, and segmentation, to form
a robust and scalable 4D tracking pipeline. Secondly, to
mitigate noises and drifting issues from the priors, we employ
a dynamic bundle adjustment that explicitly leverages the
correspondence field to stabilize and correct long-term drift
in camera intrinsics, extrinsics, and dense depth predictions.
Finally, a global 4D optimization is introduced to jointly refine
geometry and point trajectories by enforcing constraints of
instrument rigidity, articulated flow consistency, and temporal
smoothness. These allow SurgTAP-3D to produce spatially
coherent, temporally smooth, and physically plausible 3D tra-
jectories of both surgical instruments and deformable tissues,
outperforming existing 2D or 3D trackers in surgical settings.
Building upon this framework, we propose SurgTAP-3D,
for Surgical Tracking Any Point in 3D, a large-scale, contact-
aware 3D motion tracking dataset that provides high-quality
and diverse annotations from 4549 sequences, 520K frames,
with 1.4M 3D trajectories, including comprehensive anno-
tations for each clip (i.e., intrinsics, extrinsics, depths, 2D
and 3D pixel-level trajectories, instrument segmentations). Our
dataset aims to support surgical 3D motion analysis and down-
stream tasks by providing pseudo-annotated 4D trajectories
and scale-consistent scene geometry from uncalibrated surgical
videos. Specifically, we curate large-scale sequences from
a broad spectrum of procedures, including cholecystectomy,
prostatectomy, gastric bypass, hysterectomy, proctocolectomy,
etc. Moreover, our datasets are organized into 5 pivotal ac-
tions: dissection, tissue retraction, needle grasping, ves-
sel clipping, and coagulation, capturing the most complex
tool-tissue interactions. Unlike prior benchmarks restricted to
camera-centric coordinates, SurgTAP-3D provides trajectories
in both camera and global coordinates. The global coordi-
nates are obtained by transforming camera-frame 3D points
using estimated camera poses, disentangling camera ego-
motion from tissue and instrument motion. Under monocular
reconstruction, these coordinates remain pseudo-scale unless
additional metric calibration is available, supporting 3D mo-
tion analysis for surgical perception and manipulation-oriented
studies. We further build comprehensive benchmarks to assess
the current state-of-the-art methods for 3D surgical point
tracking during tool-tissue interaction, offering a step toward
scalable 3D learning in real-world surgery. Downstream tasks
like instrument displacement estimation, deformable surface
reconstruction are also conducted and validated to show the
applicability.
Our core contributions are summarized as:
• We build SurgTAP-3D, the first large-scale in-vivo 3D
motion tracking dataset in general action classes, achiev-
ing contact-aware annotation from public datasets, en-
compassing 520K frames with 4549 action clips and
1.4M 2D/3D point trajectories in diverse surgeries.
• We present the first unified framework enabling 3D
contact-aware motion tracking to recover high-fidelity 4D
trajectories and 3D geometry from dynamic, uncalibrated
monocular surgical videos.
• We build a comprehensive benchmark to assess the
current state-of-the-art methods for 2D and 3D point
tracking, forming the first 3D tracking benchmark for
both instrument and tissue tracking.
• Based on our proposed pipeline and dataset, we con-
duct real-world applications, including instrument dis-
placement estimation, deformable surface reconstruction,
demonstrating the applicability to surgical 3D motion
analysis and the potential for future robotic applications.
II. RELATED WORK
A. Surgical Datasets with Motion Annotations
Surgical datasets play a pivotal role in understanding
and modeling scene dynamics for computer-assisted and au-
tonomous surgery. However, unlike the diverse and large-scale
datasets for motion tracking in general computer vision [31]–
[33], only a few surgical datasets provide motion-related anno-
tations such as instrument pose estimation or soft tissue track-
ing. This lack of large-scale, geometry-aware data significantly
limits the development of robust 3D perception, reasoning, and
policy learning models for surgical environments. Regarding
the tool motion tracking, SurgPose [5] provides instance-
aware semantic keypoints and skeletal annotations for surgical
tool pose estimation and tracking. CRCD [4] offers an ex-
vivo dataset collected on pig livers using the dVRK, with an
extended version including keypoint-level annotations of the
instruments. The SurgRIPE Challenge [6] releases a dataset
for 6D pose estimation of dVRK instrument wrists. However,
the data are captured in a controlled laboratory setting rather
than from real surgical procedures. Additionally, Cholec-
Track20 [3], derived from Cholec80 [34] and CholecT50 [35],
focuses on multi-tool 2D tracking through bounding-box an-
notations but lacks fine-grained point trajectories and depth-
aware 3D tracking. StereoMIS [7] provides stereo endoscopic
sequences with manually annotated landmarks for evaluating
2D tracking in real in-vivo surgical scenes, but its scale is lim-
ited and it does not provide 3D tracking annotations. SurgT [1]
is the first standardized benchmark for soft tissue tracking
from Hamlyn [36], SCARED [37], and Kidney boundary [38]


4
datasets. STIR [2] is the first point tracking challenge for soft
tissue, consisting of stereo video clips from dVRK system
and tattoo labels as groundtruth. Overall, as shown in Tab. I,
current datasets remain limited to ex vivo or simulated settings,
lack scale and diversity, and often miss dense 3D motion or
contact-level annotations. To bridge this gap, we propose a
large-scale, contact-aware 3D motion tracking dataset derived
from real surgical videos, offering high-quality annotations
for both instrument and tissue motion, advancing scalable and
data-driven surgical autonomy.
B. Tracking Any 2D and 3D Point
Recent advances in video understanding have revolutionized
Tracking Any Point (TAP) methods, enabling robust 2D and
3D trajectory estimation for arbitrary pixels across frames. In
the 2D domain, TAPIR [39] introduced per-frame initialization
with temporal refinement via transformers, achieving state-of-
the-art occlusion handling and long-range tracking on bench-
marks like TAP-Vid [33]. CoTracker [40] and its enhanced
successor CoTracker3 [41] further improved dense point cor-
respondence by jointly tracking points across sequences using
correlation-based attention, reducing tracking errors by up to
40% on real-world videos with minimal supervision. Extend-
ing to 3D, methods like TAP-Vid-3D [31] provide bench-
marks for monocular 3D point tracking, while DELTA [42]
enables efficient dense 3D tracking for entire videos through
long-range motion estimation. TAPNext [43] formulates point
tracking as next-token prediction using a recurrent architecture
for online tracking, boosting accuracy on dynamic scenes.
For persistent geometry, TAPIP3D [44] represents videos as
spatio-temporal 3D feature clouds, supporting long-term track-
ing in RGB-D inputs. Most recently, SpatialTrackerV2 [28]
jointly estimates scene geometry, camera motion, and 3D
point trajectories from monocular videos, incorporating bundle
adjustment to refine poses. Despite advances in general and
surgical tracking [7, 45, 46], specular reflections, tissue defor-
mation, occlusions, and articulated instrument motion remain
challenging. They often overlook domain-specific priors, such
as contact dynamics or semantic segmentation, leading to drift
in low-texture regions. To address this, we adopt TAPNext
to first obtain the 2D tracking trajectory, and further conduct
global 4D optimization to enforce constraints of instrument
rigidity, flow consistency, and temporal smoothness, tailoring
contact-aware 3D motion tracking in endoscopy.
C. Dynamic Surgical 3D Reconstruction
Dynamic 3D reconstruction aims to recover the geome-
try and poses from dynamic videos, which is an ill-posed
and under-constrained problem. Previous deformable methods
have been dominated by Shape-from-Template (SfT) [47] and
Non-Rigid Structure-from-Motion (NRSfM) [48]. To over-
come the static camera limitation, DefSLAM [49] and SD-
DefSLAM [50] are proposed as monocular SLAM frameworks
to combine the NRSfM and SfM. However, these methods
generally rely on isometric assumptions, which restrict their
utility in medical applications. To address this issue, NR-
SLAM [51] builds a non-rigid monocular SLAM system
based on the dynamic representation composed of a dynamic
deformation graph and a visco-elastic deformation model.
However, traditional methods still fall short of reconstructing
highly dynamic surgical scenes with dense point clouds, due
to their inherent static assumptions and sparse reconstruction.
To address this, recent novel view synthesis techniques have
paved the way for dynamic surgical reconstruction [52]–[58].
EndoNeRF [52] first explores neural rendering for deformable
tissue reconstruction from stereo endoscopic inputs and de-
vises a mask-guided ray-casting strategy to address the tool
occlusions. Following this, many NeRF-based approaches [53,
59]–[61] are presented to further enhance the performance in
rendering, reconstruction quality, and efficiency.
Deform3DGS [54] introduces 3DGS with a flexible defor-
mation modeling scheme to learn tissue dynamics for each
Gaussian. SurgicalGaussian [55] models the spatial-temporal
features by a forward deformation MLP and a regularization
for consistent movement. Endosparse [56] aims to improve the
geometric and appearance quality with sparse observations as
input. However, these methods are either restricted to requiring
the preprocessed priors from COLMAP [62] or requiring per-
scene offline optimization, which is time-consuming for real-
time applications.
D. Feed-forward 3D Reconstruction
Recent 3D reconstruction networks also pave the way
for joint estimation of geometry in an end-to-end frame-
work [23]–[26, 63]–[65]. This avoids the accumulated errors
across decomposed multiple tasks in traditional reconstruction
pipelines, such as feature extraction [66, 67], feature match-
ing [68, 69], and SfM [62, 70]. To address these limitations,
DUSt3R [23] proposes a unified framework that reformulates
stereo reconstruction as a pointmap regression task, eliminat-
ing the reliance on explicit camera calibration. MASt3R [63]
further enhances the matching accuracy by introducing a dense
feature prediction head trained with an additional matching
supervision loss. Feed-forward reconstruction methods are
prone to long-term drift, which can accumulate over time
and degrade geometric consistency and tracking accuracy. To
mitigate this issue, we leverage the priors provided by the
feed-forward network as initialization, while introducing a
dynamic bundle adjustment module to correct drift and enforce
temporal stability. Building upon this, we formulate a global
4D optimization that jointly refines scene geometry and point
trajectories, ensuring multi-view consistency and temporally
smooth 3D motion predictions across the entire sequence.

A. Dataset Source
1) In-vivo Diverse Surgery Types: Existing public datasets
provide useful but limited coverage for in-vivo 3D surgical
tracking. CRCD [4] and SurgPose [5] are mainly collected
from ex-vivo pseudo-surgery scenarios with relatively con-
trolled dVRK setups, while CholecTrack20 [3] contains real
cholecystectomy videos but focuses on a single procedure and
provides no 3D geometric annotations. To construct SurgTAP-
3D, as shown in Tab. II, we therefore curate in-vivo surgical
videos from multiple procedures, including prostatectomy,
hysterectomy, cholecystectomy, gastric bypass, rectal resec-
tion, etc. For each selected clip, we further generate synchro-
nized annotations including camera intrinsics, camera poses,
depth maps, instrument masks, and dense 2D/3D trajectories
for both tissue and instruments.
2) Manual and Robotic Surgeries: The dynamics and oper-
ational paradigms of robotic and manual surgeries differ sub-
stantially. Robotic surgeries [6] often exhibit smooth, stable,
and kinematically constrained tool motions with a fixed cam-
era view, while manual procedures feature freehand endoscope
movement and more dynamic tool trajectories, introducing
frequent camera motion, rapid zoom changes, and viewpoint
shifts. These differences are crucial for downstream policy
learning, scene reconstruction, and motion prediction tasks.
Unlike prior datasets that isolate robotic or manual modali-
ties, SurgTAP-3D explicitly distinguishes and includes both,
enabling cross-domain learning and generalization analysis.
Specifically, we categorize collected videos according to their
surgical modality to facilitate modality-specific training and
benchmark tasks.
3) Unified Action Categories: Previous datasets [3] are
often restricted to a single type of surgery, resulting in incon-
sistent or incompatible action labels across various datasets.
To build a consistent taxonomy, we manually reviewed the
action definitions and visual patterns in widely used surgical
datasets, and defined five action categories that are common
in practice and visually distinguishable: dissection, tissue
retraction, needle grasping, vessel clipping, and coagu-

B. Dataset Preprocessing
First, we extract frames from the videos at their original
frame rates. Based on the unified action categories, we select
sequences that contain visually clear and well-defined actions.
Since our motion tracking begins from prompts given in the
first frame of each clip, we ensure that this initial frame
clearly exposes the active instrument under the camera. To
guarantee the overall quality and reliability of the dataset,
we apply a multi-stage filtering process prior to annotation.
We use a luminance-based filtering method to detect and
remove frames with excessive brightness variation or strong
overexposure. This step removes only frames or short seg-
ments that are unreliable for pseudo-annotation, rather than
normal illumination variations commonly observed in surgical
videos. Next, we perform motion blur detection using gradient-
based sharpness metrics, such as the Laplacian variance, to
identify and exclude frames where tool or camera motion
causes significant blurring. After these filtering steps, the
retained frames form temporally consistent sequences with
stable lighting, clear visual features, and reliable image quality,
providing a robust foundation for 3D reconstruction, trajectory
extraction, and action annotation. Finally, after filtering out
noisy frames, we retain clips ranging from 5–30 seconds in
duration, corresponding to 20–300 frames.
C. Contact-aware Data Annotation Pipeline
Given a sequence of T images as input, our goal is to enable
3D contact-aware motion estimation for the video, generating
labels including pointclouds, camera poses, intrinsics, 2D/3D
tracking trajectories for the contact region. While our pipeline
is generalizable to various scenes with arbitrary point prompts,
we focus on the contact region, which is the local area where
the active instrument (the main tool currently performing an
action) directly interacts with surrounding tissues. Optionally,
our pipeline supports known priors (depths, intrinsics, and
poses) as input, together with images, making it more flexible
and applicable for different dataset types.

D. Data Analysis
1) Statistics Overview: The SurgTAP-3D dataset contains
4,549 sequences and 519,642 frames, with each clip rang-
ing from 5-30 seconds in duration, corresponding to 20-300
frames. As shown in Fig. 3, the dataset covers a wide variety
of surgical scenarios. The top row of charts illustrates the
distribution of action types, procedure types, surgical settings,
and the instruments used, highlighting the diversity and bal-
ance of the collected data. The second row provides further
detail on the length of sequences across different actions and
datasets. Additionally, the contact region analysis shows how
much of the image is occupied by the main surgical activity,
highlighting that the dataset focuses on the areas where tools
and tissues interact most. This diversity and scale ensure that
the dataset is a robust resource for developing 3D tracking
methods that work across many different types of surgery.

IV. EXPERIMENTS
To comprehensively evaluate the proposed pipeline, we es-
tablish a benchmark that systematically compares our method
with state-of-the-art approaches for both 2D and 3D point
tracking on public datasets covering soft tissue and instrument
keypoint tracking. In addition, we assess the dynamic recon-
struction capability by benchmarking camera pose estimation
and video depth estimation against existing methods.
A. 2D and 3D Point Tracking
1) Baselines: To evaluate the tracking performance on
surgery datasets, we build a large benchmark to include
the current state-of-the-art methods for 2D and 3D tracking.
We classify the current methods into three types, type I
refers to the 2D tracking methods taking RGB as input
(BootsTAPIR [86], CoTracker [40], AllTracker [87], TAP-
Next [43], LiteTracker [46]), type II refers to the meth-
ods taking RGBD data as input for tracking under camera
coordinate, using the depths from monocular depth model
(DELTA [42], DELTAv2 [88], SpatialTracker [27]) or depths
from stereo depth model (Online3D [7]); type III includes
the methods taking RGBD and camera poses as input to
achieve 3D tracking in global coordinate (TAPIP3D [44],
SpatialTrackerv2 [28]).

2D and 3D tracking. Following previous work [1, 2], the 3D

uation instead of perfect physical ground truth, we assume

sufficient for 3D tracking validation. Therefore, the limitation

2) Benchmark Datasets: We adopt three public datasets to

preprocessing to rectify the stereo images and use the stereo

paper, using the full sequence without truncation.

outperforms the three representative categories of existing
approaches. As illustrated in Fig. 7, we visualize representative
2D point tracking results, where blue points denote the ground-

trajectories. Point tracking in surgical scenes is particularly
challenging due to intrinsic characteristics of the environment,
including rapid instrument motion, homogeneous surface tex-

Under these conditions, tracking methods such as Lite-

rely on reconstruction-based priors, such as SpatialTrackerV2,

object boundaries, where fast motion and motion blur lead
to premature track termination (rows 1 and 2 in Fig. 7). As

much worse on SurgPose [5], indicating that reconstructing
and tracking fast-moving instruments remains difficult for 4D
Gaussian Splatting-based methods. In contrast, our method
achieves robust and accurate tracking across all challenging
scenarios, maintaining tight alignment with ground truth with-
out trajectory loss or noticeable drift.

D. Ablation Study
1) Ablation Study on Components of the Annotation
Pipeline: We conduct the ablation to analyze the contribution
of each component in the pipeline. As shown in Tab. VII,
Endo3R initialization provides a strong geometric prior and
outperforms the recent general depth models (see Tab. VI).

local geometric accuracy. Dynamic BA further enhances both
depth and pose estimation by suppressing unreliable dynamic
regions, leading to more stable joint optimization of scene
geometry and camera motion. Finally, global 4D refinement
achieves the best overall performance by enforcing long-range
spatiotemporal consistency between trajectories and geometry.
2) Ablation Study on Losses in Global 4D Refinement: We
conduct the ablation to analyze the contribution of each loss
term in the global 4D refinement. As shown in Tab. VIII, the
full objective achieves the best overall performance on both
2D and 3D tracking metrics. Removing Lreg causes a large
degradation, showing the importance of preventing the opti-
mized trajectories from drifting away from image evidence.

worsens depth metrics, showing that flow consistency helps
maintain cross-frame correspondence and geometry consis-
tency. The temporal smoothness loss Ldyn provides smaller
but consistent gains, mainly stabilizing trajectory optimization.
The surface constraint Lsurf and articulated ARAP regular-
ization Larap further improve 3D tracking accuracy, validating
the benefit of constraining instrument trajectories with surface
and local-rigidity priors. Overall, these results show that the
full combination of losses provides the most stable objective
for pseudo-3D trajectory refinement.

motion analysis. By leveraging pixel-wise 3D tracking across

facilitates the recovery of tool translation and movement tra-

VI. DISCUSSION
SurgTAP-3D addresses the lack of large-scale, geometri-
cally consistent 3D surgical data for learning-based surgical
perception. It converts uncalibrated monocular surgical videos
into pseudo-annotated 4D trajectories without requiring dedi-
cated robotic hardware or calibrated capture setups.
Our pipeline combines feed-forward reconstruction, depth,
tracking, and segmentation priors with geometric refinement.
Foundation priors provide scalability, while dynamic bundle
adjustment and global 4D optimization improve camera/depth
consistency, instrument rigidity, articulated motion coherence,
and temporal smoothness. The resulting trajectories are spa-
tially consistent in both camera and global coordinate systems,
supporting relative 3D motion analysis. SurgTAP-3D expands
existing surgical tracking benchmarks and addresses the need
for large-scale, cross-surgery, and contact-aware 3D datasets.
It covers in-vivo procedures across multiple surgery types and
action categories, and provides contact-aware dense 2D/3D
trajectories for both tissue and instruments.
Despite these strengths, several limitations remain. Since
the annotations are generated from monocular videos, the
recovered geometry is sequence-consistent but pseudo-scale,
which is suitable for relative 3D motion analysis but requires
additional calibration for metric robot-workspace reasoning.
In addition, the quality may decrease under particularly chal-
lenging visual conditions, such as long-term instrument dis-
appearance, severe occlusion, fast motion. Future work can
further improve metric accuracy and annotation reliability
by incorporating robot kinematics, stereo calibration, tool
geometry, anatomical priors, and uncertainty-aware filtering.
Overall, SurgTAP-3D demonstrates that large-scale contact-
aware 3D motion annotation from in-vivo surgical videos is
practical and scalable, providing a useful data resource and
construction pipeline for spatially aware surgical perception.
VII. CONCLUSION
In this work, we present SurgTAP-3D, the first unified
framework and large-scale in-vivo dataset for contact-aware
3D surgical point tracking from uncalibrated monocular
videos. By integrating 3D foundation priors with dynamic
bundle adjustment and global 4D optimization, our pipeline
recovers spatially coherent, temporally smooth, and physically
plausible 3D trajectories in both camera and global coordi-
nates. Built upon 4,549 action clips and over a million 2D/3D
trajectories across diverse procedures, SurgTAP-3D substan-
tially advances the scale, quality, and realism of publicly
available surgical motion data. Comprehensive benchmarks
and downstream applications further demonstrate its effective-
ness in improving 3D tracking, reconstruction, and instrument
