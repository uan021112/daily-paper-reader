## Abstract 

Domain adaptive point cloud completion (DA PCC) aims to bridge the geometric discrepancy between labeled source domains and unlabeled target domains for accurate 3D shape completion. Ex-isting methods based on CNNs, Transformers, and Mamba-like sequence models still face challenges from limited receptive fields, high computational complexity, insufficient geometric bias, and serialization sensitivity. Recently, Receptance Weighted Key Value (RWKV) provides a linear-complexity alternative for long-range sequence modeling. However, adapting RWKV to DA PCC remains challenging due to the lack of explicit geometric priors, the mis-match between recurrent modeling and coarse-to-fine generation, and unstable target-domain adaptation. To address these challenges, we propose DAPointRWKV, a novel RWKV-based framework for DA PCC. DAPointRWKV consists of three components: Geometry-Aware RWKV (GA-RWKV) Backbone, RWKV-Oriented Progressive Decoder (RWKV-PD), and Dual Structural Regularization (DSR).

## 1 Introduction 

Point cloud completion (PCC) aims to predict complete 3D shapes from partial observations and plays a fundamental role in multi-media applications involving 3D content understanding [4, 47], immersive interaction [1, 48], autonomous driving [70, 75, 76], and scene understanding [8, 20, 28, 82]. Recent supervised meth-ods [6, 11, 32, 35, 52, 56, 66, 73, 74, 80] have achieved substantial progress in recovering complete shapes from partial point clouds. However, these methods rely heavily on paired partial-complete training data, which are difficult to obtain in real-world scenarios. In practice, clean and complete point clouds are inherently difficult to acquire due to scanner limitations and occlusions [14, 51, 74]. Moreover, their performance often drops significantly when di-rectly transferred from synthetic to real-world scans due to the substantial domain gaps (as shown in Figure 1(a)) between the source and target domains. To address this issue, unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA) techniques [13, 17, 22, 29, 30, 37, 44, 57, 83–86] have been introduced into point cloud completion to narrow the domain shifts between the labeled source domain and the unlabeled target domain. Exist-ing UDA PCC methods can be grouped into several categories, in-cluding adversarial learning [5, 58, 61, 78], data reconstruction [55], disentangled representation learning [19], and self-supervised learn-ing [5, 25, 36, 38, 69]. More recently, DAPoinTr [33] introduces the Transformer-based architecture into UDA PCC to align feature dis-tributions at both local and global levels. However, as the number of point tokens increases, the quadratic complexity of attention yields prohibitive computational and memory costs, as shown in Figure 1(c). To improve the efficiency, DAPointMamba [34] further demonstrates that linear-complexity sequence modeling is a promis-ing direction for UDA PCC. Despite this progress, Mamba-based models still remain limited by weak explicit geometric bias and sensitivity to point serialization. In particular, when sparse and un-ordered point clouds are serialized into 1D sequences, the original spatial topology is disrupted, and fine-grained local geometric cues are lost, thereby hindering effective spatial representation learning. Meanwhile, Receptance Weighted Key Value (RWKV) [41], an-other efficient recurrent architecture, models long-range dependen-cies with linear computational complexity through weighted recur-rent key-value aggregation. Recent studies [9, 10, 15, 23, 45, 46, 53] have demonstrated the effectiveness of RWKV-based methods for both vision and point cloud analysis tasks. Vision-RWKV [10] vali-dates RWKV as an efficient visual backbone with favorable scala-bility and long-range modeling capability, while PointRWKV [23], RWKV3D [46], and Serialized PointRWKV [45] further verify its strong performance and efficiency in 3D point cloud analysis. De-spite its gratifying progress, directly applying existing RWKV-based approaches to UDA PCC remains non-trivial due to the lack of consideration of domain shifts and tailored designs. Therefore, im-proving the transferability of RWKV-based models for UDA PCC remains an underexplored problem. In our work, we aim to enhance the transferability of RWKV-like models in UDA PCC. Our motivations mainly lie in three aspects. 

Firstly , although RWKV offers efficient global context aggregation, it lacks explicit local geometric inductive bias, making it difficult to capture fine-grained structural priors in irregular point clouds. This issue is particularly critical for UDA PCC, where reliable recov-ery of missing regions requires strong local geometric awareness and structural continuity under domain shift. Secondly , PCC is in-herently a coarse-to-fine geometric generation problem, whereas RWKV is originally more suitable for sequential representation learning. As a result, directly replacing an existing encoder with RWKV cannot naturally establish the connection between recurrent token representations and progressive point-wise reconstruction. 

Finally , RWKV’s recurrent weighted aggregation can become un-stable under unlabeled target-domain adaptation. Real-world scans often contain noise, irregular missing patterns, and domain-specific clutter, which may bias recurrent feature propagation and lead to unreliable completion hypotheses. In this paper, we propose DAPointRWKV, the first RWKV-based UDA framework explicitly tailored for domain adaptive point cloud completion. As presented in Figure 1(b), our method offers strong transferability to the target domain, linear computational complex-ity, and effective modeling of long-range dependencies. DAPointR-WKV comprises three key components. Firstly , the Geometry-Aware RWKV (GA-RWKV) Backbone is developed to enhance RWKV with explicit local geometric priors, enabling geometry-aware recurrent token modeling for irregular point clouds. Secondly , we design an RWKV-Oriented Progressive Decoder (RWKV-PD) to bridge RWKV token representations and coarse-to-fine geometric recon-struction through global shape semantics and local token-guided refinement. Finally , we introduce a Dual Structural Regulariza-tion (DSR) module to stabilize target-domain adaptation, where Observation-Anchor Constraint (OAC) enforces visible-structure anchoring, and Hypothesis-Stabilizing Constraint (HSC) regularizes the stability of complete-shape hypotheses under re-observation. Extensive experiments on both synthetic and real-world bench-marks demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed framework. Our DAPointRWKV consistently outperforms existing state-of-the-art UDA PCC methods, verifying the advantage of geometry-aware recurrent modeling for cross-domain point cloud completion. Fur-thermore, these results highlight the potential of recurrent sequence modeling as an efficient alternative for addressing domain shifts in irregular 3D data, providing a new perspective for future cross-domain point cloud understanding. In summary, our contributions are threefold: 

• We propose DAPointRWKV, a novel RWKV-based frame-work for point cloud completion. To the best of our knowl-edge, this is the first work that explores the transferability of RWKV-based models in DA PCC. 

• We introduce three tailored designs for RWKV-based DA PCC, including Geometry-Aware RWKV Backbone to en-hance recurrent token mixing with explicit local geomet-ric priors, RWKV-Oriented Progressive Decoder to bridge RWKV representations and coarse-to-fine point generation, and Dual Structural Regularization to stabilize unlabeled target-domain adaptation. 

• Extensive experiments on both synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that our DAPointRWKV consistently outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods for domain adaptive point cloud completion. ## 2 Related Work 2.1 Domain Adaptive Point Cloud Completion 

Point cloud completion (PCC) aims to recover complete shapes from partial inputs. Existing supervised PCC methods [3, 12, 21, 27, 31, 39, 49, 50, 52, 60, 62, 64, 65, 73, 74] have achieved remark-able progress by learning from paired partial-complete point cloud data. However, their reliance on paired annotations limits their applicability in real-world scenarios, where complete ground truth is difficult to obtain, and substantial domain gaps often exist be-tween synthetic and real scans. To alleviate this issue, unsuper-vised domain adaptation (UDA) techniques have been introduced to PCC. Pcl2pcl [5] initially proposes to align partial and complete latent spaces through dual auto-encoders and adversarial mapping, while ShapeInversion [78] leverages a pretrained GAN prior and performs completion through latent inversion. Based on Pcl2pcl, Cycle4Completion [55] introduces bidirectional cycle transforma-tions between partial and completion latent spaces to improve geometric correspondence. Furthermore, OptDE [19] explicitly dis-entangles occlusion, domain, and shape factors to address both input-domain and output-domain gaps. ACL-SPC [25] further im-proves target-domain generalization through adaptive closed-loop completion. More recently, DAPoinTr [33] introduces Transformer-based sequence-wise features alignment at both the global and local levels into UDA PCC, while DAPointMamba [34] shows that linear-complexity sequence modeling [67, 79] is also applicable to this task. However, existing UDA PCC methods still either suffer from limited receptive fields or high computational cost, motivating the exploration of more efficient transferable architectures. 

## 2.2 Receptance Weighted Key Value (RWKV) 

RWKV [40–42, 63, 71, 72, 77] has recently emerged as an efficient sequence modeling architecture, offering global receptive fields and strong long-range dependency modeling with linear computa-tional complexity. Its effectiveness primarily stems from the linear WKV attention mechanism and the spatial/channel mixing design, which balance local feature interactions and global context propaga-tion through gated recurrent computation. Such a design provides efficient parallel training and low-cost inference, making it particu-larly appealing for large-scale point cloud data. Vision-RWKV [10] demonstrates its effectiveness as a scalable visual backbone with strong scalability and favorable efficiency-performance trade-offs. Building on this, PointRWKV [23] first introduces this architecture to point cloud analysis and combines global recurrent modeling with local feature aggregation. Subsequent works further adapt MM ’26, November 10–14, 2026, Rio de Janeiro, Brazil Yinghui Li et al. 

it to point clouds from different perspectives. RWKV3D [46] im-proves local geometric modeling through redesigned feature mixing and multi-head shift strategies, while Serialized PointRWKV [45] highlights the importance of point serialization and reordering for sequential point modeling. More recently, PointDGRWKV [68] ex-tends it to domain generalization for point cloud classification by improving geometry perception and distribution robustness, while RWKV-PCSSC [24] explores RWKV for point cloud semantic scene completion by redesigning the seed generator and multiple decon-volution. These studies consistently demonstrate the potential of RWKV for efficient point cloud representation learning and show that RWKV-style recurrent modeling can be effectively adapted to diverse 3D tasks. Nevertheless, existing RWKV-based point meth-ods mainly focus on analysis tasks or semantic scene completion, rather than UDA PCC. More importantly, they do not explicitly consider domain shifts and task-specific designs for completion. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work that investigates the transferability of the RWKV-based model for UDA PCC. 

## 3 Methodology 

In this section, we introduce DAPointRWKV, a novel RWKV-based framework for domain adaptive point cloud completion (DA PCC). The proposed framework is motivated by three key challenges in extending RWKV to DA PCC. Firstly, the recurrent token mixing mechanism in vanilla RWKV lacks explicit geometric awareness, limiting its ability to capture local structural priors in irregular point clouds. Secondly, RWKV-like sequential representation learning is not naturally aligned with the coarse-to-fine geometric generation process required for PCC. Finally, the absence of target-domain annotations makes cross-domain adaptation inherently unstable, often leading to unreliable optimization and degraded performance. To address these challenges, DAPointRWKV, as illustrated in Fig-ure 2, consists of three components: Geometry-Aware RWKV (GA-RWKV) Backbone, RWKV-Oriented Progressive Decoder (RWKV-PD), and Dual Structural Regularization (DSR). Specifically, GA-RWKV enhances RWKV with geometry-aware recurrent represen-tation learning to better capture local structural information in ir-regular point clouds; RWKV-PD bridges token representations with progressive geometric reconstruction by combining global shape semantics and local token-guided refinement; and DSR serves as the adaptation module to stabilize unlabeled target-domain learning through observation anchoring and hypothesis stabilization. By integrating these components into a unified framework, DAPointR-WKV achieves robust cross-domain feature learning and accurate point cloud completion. 

## 3.1 Geometry-Aware RWKV Backbone 

RWKV [15, 26, 41, 43, 53] provides an attractive alternative to Transformer-based architectures by enabling efficient long-range dependency modeling with linear-complexity recurrent computa-tion. This property is particularly desirable for PCC, where under-standing missing parts requires both global context aggregation and effective feature interaction across spatial regions. However, di-rectly applying RWKV to serialized point tokens leads to weak local geometric awareness, since spatially neighboring points may be far apart in sequence space. Therefore, explicitly injecting local-level geometric priors into recurrent token mixing is necessary to pre-serve local structural consistency. To address this issue, we propose a Geometry-Aware RWKV (GA-RWKV) Backbone that introduces geometric awareness into RWKV and improves its ability to model local structural details in irregular point clouds. ## 3.3 Dual Structural Regularization 

Although the geometry-aware backbone and RWKV-Oriented Pro-gressive Decoder improve structural modeling and progressive reconstruction, they do not explicitly address the domain shifts between the labeled source domain and the unlabeled target do-main. To enable effective cross-domain adaptation, we further introduce the Dual Structural Regularization (DSR) module, which consists of Observation-Anchor Constraint (OAC) and 

Hypothesis-Stabilizing Constraint (HSC) . These two constraints regularize target-domain completion from two complementary per-spectives, namely visible-structure anchoring and complete-shape hypothesis stabilization. 

Observation-Anchor Constraint. Given an unlabeled target-domain partial point cloud 𝑃 𝑡 𝑝 and its predicted completion ˆ 𝑃 𝑡 , OAC constrains the prediction to remain compatible with the reliable ob-served geometry. Specifically, we impose a one-way point-to-shape constraint from the target partial input to the predicted complete shape:

## 3.4 Training and Inference 

During the training, the source-domain partial point cloud 𝑃 𝑠 𝑝 is supervised by its complete ground truth 𝑃 𝑠 𝑐 , while the unlabeled target-domain samples are regularized by the proposed Dual Struc-tural Regularization (DSR). Accordingly, the total loss function can be formulated as: 

Ltotal = Lsource + 𝜆 OAC LOAC + 𝜆 HSC LHSC (11) where Lsource denotes the source-domain completion loss, and 𝜆 OAC 

and 𝜆 HSC are the balancing coefficients for Observation-Anchor Constraint and Hypothesis-Stabilizing Constraint, respectively. During the inference, only the trained GA-RWKV backbone and RWKV-PD are used to complete target-domain partial point clouds. No source-domain data or additional adaptation process is required. Given a target-domain partial input, the model directly predicts the corresponding complete point cloud, enabling efficient deployment in unseen target domains.

## 4.1 Experiment Settings 

Datasets. Following the standard evaluation protocol widely adopted in UDA PCC [19, 33, 55], we use CRN [54] as the labeled source dataset and evaluate on synthetic and real-world target datasets. Specifically, we use 26,863 samples from categories shared by CRN and target datasets. Synthetic targets include 3D-FUTURE [16] and ModelNet [59]. For real-world scans, we evaluate on ScanNet [7], MatterPort3D [2], and KITTI [18], covering indoor chair/table and outdoor car completion scenarios. Following prior practice, only shared categories between source and target domains are used for training and evaluation. All partial inputs are uniformly down-sampled to 2048 points for unpaired training and inference. 

Implementation Details. All experiments are conducted on an RTX 4090 with 64GB RAM. For the training, we employ an ini-tial learning rate of 1 × 10 −3 and a weight decay of 5 × 10 −2. The batch size is set to 8. To balance losses, weights of L𝑂𝐴𝐶 and L𝐻𝑆𝐶 

are set to 0.2 and 0.2, respectively. We build the basic network by adapting the core Vision-RWKV [10] blocks for point cloud com-pletion. Following previous works [19, 25, 33, 55, 78], we adopt the mean Chamfer Distance (CD) as our metric to evaluate the performance on the synthetic 3D-FUTURE and ModelNet datasets in which complete shapes are available for testing. For real-world scans without ground truth, we evaluate the completion quality using Unidirectional Chamfer Distance (UCD) and Unidirectional Hausdorff Distance (UHD) from partial input to the predicted com-pletion.

## 4.3 Ablation Studies 

In this section, we conduct ablation studies on the 3D-FUTURE benchmark to evaluate the contribution of each proposed compo-nent, including the Geometry-Aware RWKV (GA-RWKV) Back-bone, RWKV-Oriented Progressive Decoder (RWKV-PD), and Dual Structural Regularization (DSR). Following the standard UDA PCC setting, we use the CRN dataset as the source domain and 3D-FUTURE as the target domain. 

Effectiveness of each component. We first constructed a basic point cloud completion network by adapting vision-RWKV [10] blocks (denoted as Baseline ) to train on the source domain and directly tested it on the target domain. As shown in Table 4, the basic PCC model achieves an average CD of 20.66, serving as a lower-bound reference for RWKV-based cross-domain completion. After incorporating GA-RWKV, the performance is consistently im-proved across all categories, reducing the average CD to 19.76. The improvement can be attributed to the geometry-aware recurrent modeling strategy, which strengthens RWKV token interactions with local structural cues, thereby enhancing geometric perception in irregular point clouds. The gains are more pronounced on Lamp, Sofa, and Table, indicating that local geometry-aware recurrent modeling is especially helpful for categories with more complex structures and missing patterns. Building upon GA-RWKV, we fur-ther introduce RWKV-PD, which explicitly couples RWKV token representations with progressive point generation through local token-guided refinement. This further reduces the average CD from 19.76 to 19.10. The improvement is particularly evident in categories with more intricate local structures, such as Lamp (from 22.45 to 21.31), demonstrating that RWKV-PD is effective in translating recurrent token features into finer point-wise geometric recovery. Finally, we investigate the contributions of the two components of DSR (OAC and HSC). Directly applying OAC alone leads to sig-nificant degradation, increasing the average CD from 19.10 to 20.95, which is even worse than the baseline. This result indicates that visible-structure anchoring alone cannot provide sufficiently reli-able target-domain regularization, because constraining predictions to the observed partial input tends to bias the model toward conser-vative completion and suppress recovery of missing structures. By contrast, using HSC alone reduces the average CD to 16.74, with especially large gains on Lamp (from 21.31 to 14.99) and Table (from 18.43 to 14.14), demonstrating the effectiveness of complete-shape hypothesis stabilization across varying partial views. When OAC and HSC are jointly integrated as DSR, the average CD is further reduced to 16.02, yielding the best overall performance. These results show that stable cross-domain point cloud com-pletion requires both visible-structure anchoring and complete-shape hypothesis stabilization. Specifically, OAC regularizes the prediction with reliable observed geometry, while HSC stabilizes the complete-shape hypothesis across varying partial views. Their joint use therefore enables DSR to provide more effective structural regularization for target-domain completion than either constraint alone. Overall, these results demonstrate that all proposed mod-ules are complementary, and their integration leads to substantial improvements in UDA point cloud completion.

## 5 Conclusion 

In this paper, we present Domain Adaptive RWKV (DAPointRWKV), a novel RWKV-based framework aimed at improving the trans-ferability of RWKV for domain adaptive point cloud completion across diverse domains. Through the introduction of three tailored components, including the Geometry-Aware RWKV (GA-RWKV) Backbone, the RWKV-Oriented Progressive Decoder (RWKV-PD), and Dual Structural Regularization (DSR), DAPointRWKV effec-tively enhances local geometric modeling, bridges recurrent token representations with coarse-to-fine geometric reconstruction, and stabilizes unlabeled target-domain adaptation. Extensive experi-ments on both synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that the proposed DAPointRWKV consistently outperforms state-of-the-art methods, particularly in challenging categories.
