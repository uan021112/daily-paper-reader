I. I NTRODUCTION 

Robotic manipulation and interactive simulation require reconstructing an articulated object in 3D, including its geom-etry, movable parts, joint types, axes, pivots and joint states. Although feed-forward 3D foundation models [1–3] recover dense point maps and cameras from uncalibrated images in one pass and token query heads segment static spatial relations [4], arXiv:2611.22244v1 [cs.CV] 17 Nov 2026 2

neither models how object parts move. Conversely, per-scene optimisation methods [5–8] fit explicit motion models to multi-state scans or videos, yet require minutes to hours per object alongside sensor depth, camera poses or known kinematic trees. While recent feed-forward articulation predictors [9–11] avoid per-scene fitting, they regress unconstrained joint vectors or fit continuous displacements directly in 3D, where monocular depth errors corrupt the recovered axis and pivot. Attaching a prediction head or motion fitter directly to a frozen 3D foundation model fails on casual videos because alternating intra- and cross-frame attention [2] treats the scene as static. In deep global-attention layers, moving part edges blur into the stationary body and camera drift mixes with part motion, while cross-frame context biases moving-part depth towards other frames and underestimates displacement along the viewing ray. When the canonical rest pose is also unknown, Fisher information for continuous axis and pivot estimation vanishes quadratically under small opening angles [12], leaving unconstrained regression and motion fitting ill-conditioned. Unlike moving parts, the stationary base is unaffected by cross-frame depth bias and remains geometrically accurate across frames. In manufactured cabinets, appliances and furniture, flat faces meet at right angles, so revolute and prismatic axes align with the three orthogonal directions of the static base’s Manhattan frame [13] while hinge pivots lie along the boundary between the part and the base. Benefiting from this undistorted anchor, we redesign both feature decoding and joint inference around the stationary base. Referencing part queries to the static base cancels camera drift during dense matching, and restricting candidate axes to the static Manhattan frame turns ill-conditioned continuous regression into closed-form three-way selection that tolerates up to 45 ◦ of tilt towards the viewing ray. Based on this design, we propose PhyVGGT, a single-pass framework on a frozen visual geometry model [2] that reconstructs 3D movable parts, joint parameters and unseen articulation states from an uncalibrated monocular video, as shown in Figure 1. First, our Part and Motion Decoder introduces multi-layer per-frame and global token fusion together with an explicit static-base query, recovering sharp 3D part masks, correspondences aligned to the static base and learned Bingham priors. Second, our Kinematic Inference Layer fits an orthogonal Manhattan frame to static surface normals via fourth-order alignment, selects the joint axis in closed form under a Bingham posterior [14] combining pairwise motion scatter with the learned priors, and solves a positive-definite linear system for the hinge pivot to drive unseen states. Our contributions are as follows:  

> •

We introduce PhyVGGT, a single-pass framework on a frozen 3D model that anchors part matching and closed-form joint estimation to the stationary base to reconstruct 3D parts, joints and unseen states from a monocular video.  

> •

We propose a Part and Motion Decoder that fuses multi-layer per-frame and global tokens and references part queries to a static-base query to recover sharp 3D part masks and correspondences aligned on the static base.  

> •

We design a Kinematic Inference Layer that restricts joint estimation to the Manhattan frame of the static base, remov-ing depth bias along the viewing ray through closed-form Bingham axis selection and linear hinge pivot estimation. II. R ELATED WORK 

A. Articulated Object Reconstruction 

Early learning methods predict part coordinates and joint parameters from depth scans [15, 16], whereas per-scene optimisation fits neural radiance fields or 3D Gaussians to discrete articulation states [5, 6] or continuous videos [7, 8]. Articulation in Prime [17] shortens per-object fitting to roughly ten minutes, yet still requires external depth maps, camera poses and part masks. Casual and egocentric pipelines likewise depend on RGB-D input [18]: ArtiPoint [19] fits joints to tracked 3D interaction points under known odometry, MoMa-SG [20] constructs articulated scene graphs from RGB-D sequences, and PAWS [21] lifts 2D hand trajectories with sensor depth and poses before querying a vision-language model over surface candidates. Avoiding per-scene optimisation and depth sensors, single-image predictors infer openable parts from appearance priors [22–25], ArtSplat [9] and FAMOS [10] predict joints from multi-state point clouds, and sim2art [11] estimates part masks and continuous joint vectors from monocu-lar videos. Yet predicting unconstrained vectors in R3 or fitting continuous axes directly to monocular point maps propagates depth errors along the viewing ray into the joint estimate, whereas PhyVGGT anchors part matching and closed-form Bingham inference to the Manhattan frame of the static base in one RGB pass. 

B. Feed-Forward 3D Foundation Models 

DUSt3R [1] and MASt3R [26] predict dense 3D point maps from uncalibrated image pairs in a shared coordinate frame, while VGGT [2] and π3 [3] recover point maps, camera poses and point tracks across an entire video in one forward pass. Recent extensions adapt visual geometry transformers to causal streaming and dynamic 4D scenes [27–30], and such reconstruction priors also support monocular dense mapping across multiple agents [31, 32]. RelationVGGT [4] attaches a lightweight query decoder to a frozen VGGT backbone for static 3D spatial relation segmentation. Although these foundation models reconstruct accurate stationary 3D surfaces in a single pass, their alternating intra- and cross-frame attention blurs moving part boundaries in deep layers and biases moving-part depth towards context frames, underestimating motion along the viewing ray. PhyVGGT overcomes both issues by fusing intermediate per-frame and global tokens relative to a static-base query and anchoring joint inference to undistorted static surface normals. III. M ETHOD

Keeping the VGGT backbone frozen, we train PhyVGGT in two stages on synthetic clips, first optimising the segmentation and correspondence trunk and then training the joint priors and calibration parameters of the Kinematic Inference Layer. IV. E XPERIMENTS 

A. Experimental Setup 

Datasets. We evaluate zero-shot synthetic-to-real transfer across three real-world video benchmarks alongside in-domain and cross-category generalisation on two synthetic video benchmarks. On real-world interactions, Arti4D [19] captures egocentric handheld RGB-D videos of drawers, doors and appliances across 329 held-out test clips in three unseen scenes, D3D-HOI [37] provides 170 third-person static-camera test clips annotated with 3D joint axes, and RBO [38] contributes 357 third-person RGB-D videos across 14 objects with motion-capture ground truth. On synthetic PartNet-Mobility [36] videos, we train on 6,264 clips from ten categories and evaluate on held-out test splits of 290 joints from the ten trained categories (Syn-10) and 510 joints across 38 categories including 28 unseen categories (Syn-38), as well as 48-frame reconstruction across all 20 scenes and 48 joints of Articulat3D-Sim [8]. Component ablations and backbone transfer are evaluated on four disjoint development splits: Arti4D-dev on scene din080 

with 85 clips, D3D-HOI-dev with 86 clips, Syn-10-val with 313 joints and Syn-38-val with 534 joints. 

Metrics. Following [7, 19], we measure axis error as the acute angle on RP 2 and revolute pivot error as 3D line-to-line distance, reporting median errors and joint accuracy within 5◦

and 10 ◦ requiring both correct joint type and axis. 

Baselines. Among uncalibrated monocular RGB methods, we evaluate three complementary baseline groups: same-backbone ablations on frozen VGGT via Direct regression with a continuous MLP joint head trained on the same features, masks and synthetic clips as PhyVGGT and Motion estimate taking the leading eigenvector of Sk without Bingham selection on the Manhattan frame, appearance and point-cloud regressors via single-image OPDMulti [22] testing appearance priors without multi-frame 3D geometry and feed-forward FAMOS [10] testing multi-state continuous point-cloud regression, and continuous trajectory fitting via monocular ArtiPoint [19] on 

π3 [3] and VGGT [2]. Privileged baselines marked with ∗

include posed RGB-D ArtiPoint [19] with sensor depth and ground-truth poses, and per-scene 3D Gaussian optimisation Articulat3D [8] with ground-truth depth, poses, masks and kinematic trees.

V. C ONCLUSION 

We proposed PhyVGGT, a single-pass framework on a frozen 3D model that reconstructs 3D movable parts, joints and unseen states from an uncalibrated video by anchoring part matching and closed-form joint estimation to the stationary base. Our Part and Motion Decoder fuses multi-layer tokens relative to a static-base query for accurate 3D correspondences, while our Kinematic Inference Layer combines Bingham axis selection on the Manhattan frame with linear pivot estimation to remove depth bias along the viewing ray. Trained solely on synthetic data, PhyVGGT achieves state-of-the-art monocular performance across five benchmarks, surpasses posed RGB-D ArtiPoint in median error, runs in 6.8 s per 48-frame video against hours of per-scene optimisation in Articulat3D, and transfers zero-shot across four 3D backbones. Although PhyVGGT already recovers articulated geometry and motion from a casual video, passive RGB frames do not reveal stationary or occluded parts or physical parameters such as mass, friction, springs and latches, which we will recover through interactive manipulation and force sensing in future work.
