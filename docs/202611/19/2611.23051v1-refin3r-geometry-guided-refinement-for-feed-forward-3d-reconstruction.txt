Abstract
Feed-forward 3D reconstruction models predict dense
point maps from multiple images in a single pass. With-
out explicit geometric constraints, however, their predic-
tions are often inconsistent across views and lack fine-
grained precision. Recent methods incorporate geometric
priors such as camera pose or depth, but use them merely
as auxiliary inputs, process each view independently, or
refine the geometry only where the priors are available.
We present Refin3R, which refines feed-forward point maps
with Structure-from-Motion (SfM) points, propagating SfM
geometry over the image grid. In its dual-branch architec-
ture, a 2D branch extends SfM geometry beyond its cov-
erage using geometry-aware backbone features, while a
3D branch reasons over the SfM points of all views, cap-

1. Introduction
Multi-view 3D reconstruction from RGB images is a long-
standing problem in computer vision. Recent feed-forward
methods [27, 50, 52], trained on large-scale datasets, pre-
dict dense point maps for all views in a single forward
pass. Their fast and scalable inference has established this
paradigm as a promising direction, and follow-up works
are rapidly emerging [20, 51, 55]. However, these mod-
arXiv:2611.23051v1  [cs.CV]  17 Nov 2026


els regress point maps purely from learned priors, without
enforcing explicit geometric constraints such as triangula-
tion [16]. As a result, their predictions are often inconsistent
across views and lack fine-grained precision.
Structure-from-Motion (SfM) [1, 32, 40, 58], in contrast,
establishes correspondences across images and triangulates
them under explicit multi-view constraints, yielding consis-
tent and accurate points. Yet it often recovers only sparse
geometry and becomes fragile under wide baselines or low
overlap [35, 41]. Moreover, since it relies solely on cor-
respondences without semantic understanding, mismatches
can introduce outliers [5]. The two paradigms are thus com-
plementary: feed-forward models are dense but imprecise,
whereas SfM is accurate but incomplete.
Recent works incorporate explicit geometric cues, such
as correspondences, camera poses, or depth, into feed-
forward reconstruction.
Optimization-based methods [9,
26] match pixels across views with learned matchers and
jointly refine cameras and dense depth, initialized from
feed-forward predictions. However, they adjust depth only
via a coarse grid of anchor depths or a few per-image
coefficients, limiting fine-grained refinement.
Learning-
based methods instead feed geometric priors into networks:
geometry-conditioned feed-forward models [19, 20] take
cameras or depth as extra inputs, and depth completion
methods [14, 64, 65] densify sparse depth under image
guidance. However, the former use the priors merely as
auxiliary inputs, while the latter process each view inde-
pendently, ignoring the 3D structure shared across views.
Closer to our work, GGPT [6] refines the feed-forward
point maps of all views jointly in 3D space with accurate
points from its SfM pipeline, enforcing multi-view consis-
tency around these points. However, it refines local cubic
patches of points from geometric inputs alone, so its re-
finement remains largely confined to the regions covered
by SfM. Areas without such coverage, typically textureless
surfaces and regions observed by a single view, thus benefit
little from the SfM guidance (Fig. 1).
Motivated by these limitations, we propose Refin3R,
which refines the feed-forward point maps as per-view
depth on the image grid rather than as points in 3D space.
Operating on the image grid allows Refin3R to leverage the
dense image features of the pretrained feed-forward back-
bone [27, 55], which guide the refinement across the whole
image. In addition, Refin3R exploits the SfM points of all
views in 3D, so that each view is refined with the multi-view
structure of the scene rather than only the points it observes.
By combining both, Refin3R recovers accurate point maps
even in regions without SfM coverage (Fig. 1).
We realize this with a dual-branch architecture: a 2D
branch on the image grid and a 3D branch on sparse vox-
els. The 2D branch refines the feed-forward depth of each
view with its own SfM depth, guided by the dense image
features from the feed-forward backbone, whose semantic
and geometric cues let the SfM depth propagate beyond its
coverage. This per-view input, however, holds at most one
depth per pixel, so SfM points occluded in the current view
or triangulated only from other views are left out. The 3D
branch complements it by gathering the SfM points of all
views into sparse voxels within the space seen by each view,
providing a richer 3D structure than the view’s own SfM
depth. As each voxel is indexed by a pixel and a depth bin,
the two branches share the same pixel coordinates and ex-
change information: the 2D branch receives the multi-view
evidence that the image grid alone cannot represent, and the
3D branch receives the image context around each point.
Spatial propagation then fuses their predictions into the re-
fined depth, which is back-projected to obtain the refined
point maps.
Extensive experiments show that Refin3R achieves state-
of-the-art point map accuracy on both standard and out-
of-domain benchmarks, consistently outperforming feed-
forward and geometry-guided methods by a large mar-
gin. Moreover, while sharing its SfM front-end with the
strongest geometry-guided baseline, Refin3R runs substan-
tially faster, making it an efficient refinement module for
feed-forward 3D reconstruction.
2. Related works
2.1. 3D reconstruction
Multi-view 3D reconstruction has long been studied in com-
puter vision, with its foundations in classical multi-view ge-
ometry [2, 13, 15, 32], and has matured into practical SfM
systems [1, 18, 34, 58] such as COLMAP [40]. Learning-
based components have since been integrated through-
out the SfM pipeline, from feature detection and match-
ing [8, 10–12, 24, 30, 39, 43, 63] to track refinement [17,
23, 29].
Beyond individual components, VGGSfM [49]
makes the entire pipeline end-to-end differentiable, while
MP-SfM [35] leverages monocular priors to improve ro-
bustness under low overlap. Recently, feed-forward recon-
struction models infer scene geometry and camera parame-
ters directly from images. Pairwise models [25, 52] regress
point maps from image pairs but require a global align-
ment for more views, whereas subsequent multi-view mod-
els [20, 27, 50, 51, 55] process all views jointly in a single
forward pass. Trained on large-scale 3D data, these mod-
els are fast and robust in sparse-view settings, yet without
explicit geometric constraints their predictions often lack
multi-view consistency and local precision, especially on
out-of-domain scenes.

2.3. Depth completion
Geometry-guided reconstruction is closely related to depth
completion, which densifies sparse depth, typically from
LiDAR [47, 48, 62, 65], under image guidance, with re-
cent methods leveraging monocular depth foundation mod-
els [56, 64]. Following this formulation, Murre [14] per-
forms multi-view reconstruction by guiding a monocular
diffusion model with the SfM depth of each view. However,
these methods process each view independently and only
use the measurements observed in that view. Our image-
domain refinement shares this spirit, but both the image fea-
tures and the SfM evidence in our method are multi-view:
the features come from a multi-view feed-forward model,
and the 3D branch aggregates the SfM points of all views.
3. Method
3.1. Overview
Given N RGB images {Ii}N
i=1, our goal is to reconstruct a
dense point map Pi ∈RH×W ×3 for each view in a shared
coordinate system. As illustrated in Fig. 2, Refin3R first ob-

accurate one from a feed-forward model and a partial but
accurate one from SfM. Representing both as pixel-aligned
depth maps of each view (Sec. 3.2), it then refines the feed-
forward depth on the image grid with per-pixel residuals
guided by the SfM points.
The refinement is performed by a dual-branch network
that takes both reconstructions along with geometry-aware
features from the feed-forward backbone (Sec. 3.3). Its 2D
branch combines the feed-forward and SfM depth of each
view with these features and refines the dense depth on the
image grid. In parallel, its 3D branch refines the depth at
the SfM-observed pixels of the current view, leveraging the
SfM points aggregated from all views in sparse voxels. The
two branches exchange information (Sec. 3.4), and a spa-
tial fusion stage merges their estimates across the image
through iterative spatial propagation (Sec. 3.5). Finally, the
refined depth Dpred

of the SfM cameras to obtain the point map Pi of each view.

4. Experiments
4.1. Implementation details
Inputs and architecture.
For both training and evalu-
ation, we obtain the initial reconstructions from a feed-
forward backbone, either DA3 [27] or Pi3X [55], and the
SfM pipeline of GGPT [6] (Sec. 3.2); we report results for
both versions, each trained separately. The 2D branch fol-
lows the U-Net of PacGDC [47], the DPT head follows the
architecture of the DA3 depth head, and the 3D branch fol-
lows the sparse U-Net of MoGe-3 [22].
Training.
We train on the training splits of Scan-
Net++ [61], TartanAir v2 [53, 63], UnrealStereo4K [44],
and BlendedMVS [60], all of which provide dense ground-
truth depth and camera poses.
Following MapAny-
thing [20], we sample multi-view sets via a covisibility-
constrained random walk, with 4–16 views for Scan-
Net++, 4–8 views for TartanAir v2 and UnrealStereo4K,
and 3–6 for BlendedMVS. We train for 200K iterations on
4 NVIDIA RTX A6000 GPUs using AdamW [31] with a
learning rate of 10−4 and weight decay 0.05. The learn-
ing rate is linearly warmed up over the first 2K iterations
and then cosine-decayed to 10−5. Further architectural and
training details are in the supplementary material.
4.2. Evaluation protocol
Datasets.
Following GGPT [6], we evaluate on Scan-
Net++ [61], ETH3D [42], and Tanks&Temples [21], each
with 4-, 8-, and 16-view splits. Since ScanNet++ is part of
our training data, we refer to it as within-domain, and to
ETH3D and Tanks&Temples as cross-domain. We further
evaluate on two out-of-domain datasets whose appearance
and geometry differ substantially from the training data:
4D-DRESS [54], rendered from high-resolution scans of
clothed humans, and MV-dVRK [4], photorealistic render-
ings of robotic abdominal surgery.
Metrics.
We evaluate point map accuracy with the area
under the accuracy curve (AUC) at several error thresholds.
We align each predicted point map to the metric ground
truth with robust Umeyama alignment [46] implemented in
pycolmap [40], and measure the Euclidean error between
each predicted point and its ground-truth counterpart.
Baselines.
We compare against four groups of methods.
For fairness, all methods that take geometric priors re-
ceive the same SfM output as ours. (1) Feed-forward mod-

Bidirectional interaction.
Adding the 3D branch with
bidirectional interaction (c) brings consistent gains on both
datasets. Since the 3D-branch depth is used only for su-
pervision in this variant, these gains come solely from the
feature exchange, which provides the 2D branch with multi-
view evidence beyond the view’s own SfM depth.
Spatial fusion.
Substituting the 3D-branch depth on SfM-
observed pixels at test time already helps, confirming that
the 3D branch refines these pixels more accurately than the
2D branch. However, hard substitution trusts every triangu-
lated pixel equally and leaves its neighbors untouched. Spa-
tial fusion (d) instead anchors this depth with learned confi-
dence and propagates it through learned affinities, yielding
the best results.
4.5. Efficiency
Tab. 4 reports the inference time and peak GPU mem-
ory on ETH3D. Since all methods in the table receive the
same SfM front-end output, we report only the time of
each method itself; ours and Any2Full process the views in
batches of four. Among them, Refin3R is the fastest at ev-
ery view count, 2.2× faster than Pi3X and 15× faster than
GGPT with 16 views. Our main numbers exclude the DA3
encoder, which already runs to obtain the SfM priors; even
including it, Refin3R remains the fastest.
5. Conclusion
We presented Refin3R, which refines feed-forward 3D re-
constructions on the image grid of each view, guided by the
SfM points of all views aggregated in 3D. Its dual-branch
architecture couples dense image features with multi-view
SfM evidence through bidirectional interaction, and spatial
fusion propagates the refined geometry across the whole im-
age. Refin3R achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on standard
and out-of-domain benchmarks, consistently outperforming
feed-forward and geometry-guided methods while adding
little overhead to the SfM front-end.
