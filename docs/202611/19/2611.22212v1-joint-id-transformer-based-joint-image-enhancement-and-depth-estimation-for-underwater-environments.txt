# Joint-ID: Transformer-based Joint Image Enhancement and Depth Estimation for Underwater Environments 

Geonmo Yang 1, Gilhwan Kang 1, Juhui Lee 1, Younggun Cho 1∗Enhancement Depth estimation Single-task Learning Input Sample 1 Input Sample 2 Multi-task Learning Single-task Learning Multi-task Learning 

Abstract — Underwater imaging is a challenging task due to factors such as scattering, absorption, and tur-bulence, which degrade image quality and limit visibil-ity. In this paper, we propose a novel approach for en-hancing underwater images that leverages the bene-fits of joint learning for simultaneous image enhance-ment and depth estimation. We introduce Joint-ID ,a transformer-based neural network that can obtain high-perceptual image quality and depth information from raw underwater images. Our approach formu-lates a multi-modal objective function that addresses invalid depth, lack of sharpness, and image degrada-tion based on color and local texture. We design an end-to-end training pipeline that enables joint restora-tion and depth estimation in a shared hierarchical feature space. Additionally, we propose a synthetic dataset with various distortions and scene depths for multi-task learning. We evaluate Joint-ID on synthetic and standard datasets, as well as real underwater images with diverse spectra and harsh turbidity, demonstrating its effectiveness for underwater image enhancement and depth estimation. Furthermore, we demonstrate the ability of Joint-ID to perform feature matching and saliency detection for visually guided underwater robots. Our proposed method has the potential to improve the visual perception of underwater environments and benefit applications such as oceanography and underwater robotics. Supplements are available at: https://sites.google.com/view/joint-id/home .

Index Terms — Image Enhancement, Depth Estimation, Underwater, Multi-task learning, Transformer 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Image enhancement is essential for underwater investiga-tions and applications that rely on autonomous robot vision, such as ecological surveys, robot salvage, crack management, and resource exploration. Underwater vision suffers from more complex distortion problems than ground vision, including poor visibility, color distortion, light scattering, absorption, and hazing, which hinder the navigation of autonomous un-derwater robots. To address these challenges, researchers have actively investigated various image enhancement techniques to enable more accurate perception and navigation. Underwater light propagation can be modeled using physical principles, which opens up opportunities for physics-based image enhancement methods [1, 2, 3, 4]. Underwater images often exhibit color distortions such as green or blue biases caused by wavelength-dependent light absorption in water. II. RELATED WORKS 

A. Underwater Image Enhancement 

Several studies have attempted UIE using model, fusion, and learning-based methods that perform robust enhancement through the deep learning. Model-based methods perform color correction using traditional image processing techniques or by estimating depth based on the amount of blurring. A fusion based algorithm is a method of image restoration by using classical image processing techniques such as histogram equalization and laplacian filter simultaneously with a model-based approach. Model- and fusion-based methods have been widely studied for restoring and enhancing underwater scenes. For instance, Galdran et al. [4] improved contrast by recov-ering the color associated with short wavelengths. Berman et al. [1] proposed a haze line-based approach to estimate an ambient color and transmission in a color-biased environment. The retinex theory was also utilized in [3] to improve an image’s contrast or sharpness for underwater dehazing. Ancuti et al. [11] first proposed a fusion-based method that used four weight maps to improve the visibility of far-distance objects deteriorated by medium scattering and absorption. And, Yin et al. [12] restores colorfulness by performing an equalization that is flexible according to the color space, called color-preserving adaptive histogram equalization. Cho et al. [13] proposed a fusion-based method to balance enhancement and detail clearing of images. Deep learning has significantly contributed to the UIE field as well. For example, Peng et al. [5] applied a U-shaped trans-former model to UIE. Other studies have directly generated en-hanced images using generative models [7, 8]. Islam et al. [7] applied a conditional Generative Adversarial Networks (GAN) to real-time robotic vision, adding an objective function that evaluated the perceptual image quality of the learned image for supervision. To correct the color of underwater images in [8], an unsupervised pipeline was furnished with a model that synthesized realistic underwater images by concatenating depth information and non-hazed images. To quantitatively and qualitatively improve performance, Sharma et al. [9] dehazed underwater scenes using an adaptive receptive field in a color channel and applied underwater image semantic segmentation and driver’s 2D pose estimation. The model by Uplavikar et al. [10] learned the content features of the water domain to which an image belongs, while distancing from where it does not belong. Object detection has also been used as an evaluation method. 

B. Depth Estimation 

Due to its importance in robot vision tasks, many stud-ies have investigated depth estimation, utilizing various ap-proaches such as encoder-decoder structures [14, 15, 16, 17, 18]. Yuan et al. [15] used a swin transformer as the encoder and window Fully Connected-CRFs as the decoder, while [16] used a hierarchical transformer that captures the global context as the encoder with the lightweight decoder. Rahman and Fat-tah [17] used two transformers, the dual window self-attention transformer (Dwin-SAT) and the dual window cross attention transformer (Dwin-CAT), which learned pixel-correlation well and performed at the level of SOTA. Cheng et al. [18] predicted depth using a transformer-based encoder-decoder network method through the Multi-Scale Fusion Attention Module. Valdez-Rodr´ ıguez et al. [19] added semantic segmen-tation to provide critical information to the depth estimator. In [20], a classification regression model was formulated in which the regression step directly predicted the continuous depth in pixel units, and the classification step produced probabilistic representations. Li et al. [21] introduced an efficient attention mechanism to model a global context. Local planar guidance was used in [22] to determine whether specific pixels were on the same plane, extending the process to multiple scales to capture both local and global features. Finally, [23] presented a CNN-based architecture that rectifies multiview depth esti-mation, through epipolar and lens distortion corrections. 3

C. Mix Decoder 

The decoder network in the proposed model consists of three components: an initialization block, a mix decoder block, and a dividing block. The initialization block comprises two convolutional blocks and serves as a bridge between the en-coder and decoder. The mix decoder block has two sections, as shown in Fig. 2: a convolutional mix block and a feature mix block. Each layer of the decoder consists of a dividing block and a mixed decoder block comprising Di = [ D1, D 2, D 3, D 4]= [3, 3, 3, 3]. The feature map of each layer in the decoder has a size of Fi ∈ R H    

> 26−i×W
> 26−i×Ci

, where i = 1 , 2, 3, 4. The input to the mix decoder block is simultaneously processed by an MLP and a Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) designed to capture global and local features, respectively. The feature mix block merges the features from the encoder and the convolutional mix blocks to jointly learn both local and global features. Enhancement and depth estimation tasks necessitate a greater emphasis on the extraction of global features as compared to tasks such as object detection or classification. So, it is designed to extract simultaneously local features and these critical global features to optimize performance in enhancement and depth estimation. Finally, the dividing block receives the features from the feature mix block and splits them into two parts: one for the skip connection and the other as input to the next layer. This is for problem of gradient vanishing from the 1x1 Conv in each Mix Decoder Block, i.e., as the Mix Decoder Block layer is stacked, the local features are trained more intensively, and dividing skip connection is used to solve this problem.

V. E XPERIMENTAL RESULTS 

In this section, we first describe training details and ba-sic settings used for the implementation of our proposed 

Joint-ID . We perform qualitative and quantitative image 6

enhancement and depth estimation experiments on several datasets, including synthetic and real underwater scenes, and compare them with other methods, demonstrating the SOTA performance of our model for the two underwater image pro-cessing tasks. And, we then conduct an ablation study, which demonstrates its effectiveness in learning depth information and distortion coefficients. 

A. Experimental Details 

As mentioned in IV, we utilized by paired data ( X, Y ) from IV-F to train the proposed networks for predictive functions 

f1 : X → Y E , Y D , f2 : X → Y E , f3 : X → Y D , where 

f1 is function for multi-task learning, f2 is for UIE, and f3

is for UMDE. In addition, we set the learning rate to 3e−4

to train the f1 network, and to 2e−4 for networks f2, f3.The algorithm by Adamw [34] is used for optimization with a mini-batch size of 4, and the default values of β1, β2 and weight decay are set as 0.9, 0.999 and 1e-8, respectively. We conducted several experiments on synthetic and real-world underwater datasets [7], [5], [35], [8], [36], after re-training the other models on the datasets to verify their imple-mentation in the same environment. All the hyperparameters in the training process were the same as those provided by each model. The training and validation were conducted on Python and Pytorch frameworks using NVIDIA RTX3090ti on Ubuntu 22.04. 

B. Image Enhancement 

We evaluated the proposed joint model (Joint-ID ), including our single model (Single-I ), with model-based method (Berman[1]) and deep learning-based methods (UWCNN[37], UIE-DAL[10], SESR[38], UIECˆ2-Net[39], U-shape[5], DeepWave[9]) for image enhancement task. We conducted the experiments separately in two sections: (A) The datasets with GT (EUVP[7], LUSI[5], UIEB[35]) and (B) The datasets without the GT (MHL[8], SQUID[36]). For fair comparisons, we utilized supervised metrics (Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR), Structural Similarity Index (SSIM), Squared Relative Error (Sq Rel)) and the unsupervised metric (Underwater Color Image Quality Evaluation (UCIQE) [40]). 

Evaluation for datasets with GT. For comparison in supervised metrics, all learning-based methods, excepting (Joint-ID ), were trained on standard datasets (EUVP, LUSI, and UIEB), whereas the joint model training used our synthetic dataset. In comparison to unsupervised metrics, we used the training pipeline presented in each paper for each model. The qualitative comparisons are shown in Table A in Table I; moreover, the quantitative evaluation is summarized in Fig. 7’s first to sixth rows. As Fig. 7 demonstrates, Berman method [1] fall short in hue rectification, while the other models, with the exception of UWCNN and SESR, generally approximate the color of the ground truth well; UWCNN and SESR show good similarity to ground truth on EUVP and LUSI, but not on UIEB. In comparison, Joint-ID , trained in a different way than the others, has a large color intensity difference from the ground truth. However, the qualitative results show the method-based multi-task has better color contrast and colorfulness. For the quantitative comparisons, Table A in I shows that Single-I 

outperforms other models in terms of PSNR, SSIM and achieves often competitive performance in comparison to Sq Rel, whereas in terms of unsupervised metric (UCIQE), it achieves comparable color recovery. This represents the perceptual enhancement performance of the proposed single network design. Fig. 7 and Table I show that Joint-ID is not qualitatively similar to GT and doesn’t score higher on the supervised metrics, respectively. However, we can see that qualitatively GT has a slight color bias and hazing, while Joint-ID -generated images are considerably sharper, achieving compa-rable color recovery and hue rectification. It indicates that our model has more of a qualitative advantage in underwa-ter robot vision where high color contrast is required. This is also demonstrated quantitatively in Table I. The UCIQE comparison in Table I, evaluating image qualities based on colorfulness, sharpness, and contrast, shows that the joint model outperformed the other models. Combining the ex-periments takes conclusions: i) Single-I is more potent in supervised metrics. ii) The multi-task model has more qualitative robustness than the model for only an enhancement task, and the qualitative results show ours has better color contrast and colorfulness. 

Evaluation for datasets without GT. To evaluate MHL [8] and SQUID [36], we prepared two types of trained weights per model, excluding ours, one for the weight given by the model, and the other for the weight retrained using our synthetic dataset. The reason for preparing the retrained weights is to provide a rigorous evaluation. As seen in the seventh to eighth rows in Fig. 7, for the SQUID [36] datasets, all methods except for UIECˆ2 and our joint model, did not effectively remove the blue color bias and hazing from the entire scene. These tendencies are seen similarly in the MHL datasets, which have harsher turbidity. In MHL comparison, UIECˆ2 [39], UIE-DAL [10] and our joint model estimate backscattering coefficient well, while other methods generally suffer from dark scene. Also, the results of UIE-DAL [10] have some artifacts, but our proposed joint model shows no biased color-or edge-preserving ability for objects such as stones. Based on the qualitative results, in I, table B’s first and second row also show UICEˆ2 and our joint model has good results for metric UCIQE. However, the models are strictly not trained using the same dataset (i.e., one is on the UIEB and the other on our dataset), which prevents us from comprehending which model is more robust in the evaluation. The third in Table B shows that our model is best on the metric UCIQE, and it shows that our model has much-improved restoration performance in cases with severe turbidity and some biased water domains.

As shown in Fig. 10(a), the original underwater image exhibits very few ORB features, leading to potential drift errors. VII. C ONCLUSION & F UTURE WORK 

In this study, we presented a new learning-based solution for 3d underwater robot vision that addresses the need for simultaneous enhancement and depth estimation when inter-preting underwater images. Most importantly, by designing joint blocks that share each task features, we demonstrated that a learning approach that targets the objectives of enhancement and depth estimation simultaneously is more powerful and effective than one that targets them separately, and presented a detailed description of the novel network architecture, called Joint-ID , based the approach and end-to-end train-ing pipeline. Furthermore, we created a synthetic dataset of approximately 48000 labeled samples to facilitate training. Finally, we performed extensive qualitative and quantitative evaluations, which demonstrate that the proposed model: i) achieves SOTA performance on UIE and UMDE; ii) exhibits significantly better generalization performance on standard datasets and real underwater images than other existing mod-els; iii) provides high quality results on terrestrial images; and iv) effectively improves underwater feature matching and saliency prediction for underwater 3D robot vision. In the future, the subsequent study will utilize the depth information for 3D robotics tasks (3D reconstruction, 3D object detection, etc.). Also, we aim to incorporate this mechanism into an underwater RGB-D SLAM system with an efficient depth uncertainty module and lightweight network.
