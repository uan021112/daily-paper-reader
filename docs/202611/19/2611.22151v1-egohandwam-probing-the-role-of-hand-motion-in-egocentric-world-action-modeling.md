## 摘要
手部运动能在多大程度上揭示第一人称摄像机接下来会看到什么？EgoHandWAM 根据当前图像和近期手部状态，预测三维腕部与指尖轨迹以及未来视觉特征。我们使用冻结的 DINOv2 编码器，在 751,065 个 EgoDex 时间窗口上进行训练。在留出的开发会话中，与静止预测相比，仅使用动作信息的模型将两秒后的腕部端点误差从 60.26 mm 降至 56.48 mm，但仍会低估较长距离的伸手动作。加入未来特征监督后，轨迹预测精度几乎没有变化。利用记录的手部历史，视觉头相较于直接复制当前特征，将 smooth L1 误差降低了 1.32%。跨会话打乱历史记录后，这一增益消失。在另一项包含四个任务的探测实验中，向观测上下文添加记录的未来位移以及未来跟踪有效性掩码，使视觉特征误差降低了 3.93%。仅使用掩码几乎没有改善。根据相同观测预测得到的轨迹带来的提升也很有限。在两个模拟 RoboTwin 任务中，以对齐的人类示例替换一批辅助机器人示例，在三个训练随机种子下均未能使任务成功率获得一致提升。

## Abstract
How much does hand motion tell us about what a first-person camera will see next? EgoHandWAM forecasts three-dimensional wrist and fingertip trajectories and a future visual feature from the current image and recent hand states. We train on 751,065 EgoDex windows using a frozen DINOv2 encoder. On held-out development sessions, the action-only model reduces two-second wrist endpoint error from 60.26 to 56.48~mm relative to a stationary forecast. It still underestimates long reaches. Adding future-feature supervision gives nearly identical trajectory accuracy. With the recorded hand history, the visual head reduces smooth L1 error by 1.32\% relative to copying the current feature. Shuffling histories across sessions erases this gain. In a separate four-task probe, adding recorded future displacements to observed context and future tracking-validity masks reduces visual-feature error by 3.93\%. Masks alone give little improvement. A trajectory predicted from the same observations adds little. On two simulated RoboTwin tasks, substituting aligned human examples for an auxiliary batch of robot examples gives no consistent increase in task success across three training seeds.
