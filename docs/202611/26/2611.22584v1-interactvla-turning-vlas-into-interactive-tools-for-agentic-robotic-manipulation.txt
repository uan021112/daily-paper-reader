Abstract
Existing agentic manipulation systems treat pretrained
vision-language-action
(VLA)
policies
as
opaque,
language-only primitives:
when a policy call fails un-
der unfamiliar instructions or scene perturbations, the
agent cannot steer its behavior and is forced into futile
retries or costly manual takeover. We present InteractVLA,
a framework that turns pretrained flow-based VLAs into
geometrically steerable and inspectable tools through a
bidirectional geometric interface with vision-language
agents.
In the forward direction, test-time geometric
guidance translates an agent-specified end-effector target
into input gradients through the action expert via a differ-
entiable command-to-pose mapping, while soft-time target
alignment treats arrival time as a latent execution index
to match the target pose without distorting the policy’s
approach motion. In the reverse direction, action-informed
agentic refinement exposes generated action chunks and
calibrated camera reprojections back to the agent, allowing
it to diagnose spatial discrepancies, revise targets for
subsequent VLA calls, or locally adjust motion segments on
top of the guided trajectory. Making the VLA controllable
not only boosts online agentic tool use, but also unlocks
effective
recursive
self-improvement
(RSI),
achieving
state-of-the-art performance of 99.4% on LIBERO-Plus,
48.62% on RoboDojo, and 89.4% on RoboCasa365.

This limitation stems from an inadequate interface be-
tween high-level reasoning and low-level control.
Even
when unfamiliar instructions or scene layouts prevent a
VLA from grounding the right target, its pretrained spatial
perception and motor priors, such as smooth approach tra-
jectories, coordinated rotations, and grasp timing, remain
highly capable [1, 16]. As shown in Figure 1 (right), explic-
itly steering the end effector toward the intended interaction
target allows the exact same policy to redirect its manipu-
lation behavior and complete the task while preserving ex-
ecution precision. Turning a VLA into a genuinely usable
tool therefore requires moving beyond language-only invo-
cation to a bidirectional geometric interface: the agent must
be able to specify explicit spatial targets to guide the VLA’s
action generation, and conversely inspect the resulting mo-
tion proposals to refine subsequent execution [4, 25, 33].
Building such a bidirectional interface at test time poses
two coupled challenges. In the forward direction (Agent →
VLA), the agent supplies a sparse target configuration to ap-
proach rather than a dense trajectory or exact arrival time.
While the VLA should synthesize the intervening motion,
naively applying a fixed-time penalty pulls the wrong exe-
cution step toward the target, whereas penalizing every step
uniformly collapses the approach trajectory altogether. In
the reverse direction (VLA →Agent), a binary success or
failure signal is insufficient for recovery. Once guided by
an agent-specified target, the VLA’s generated action chunk
already reflects the agent’s spatial intent. By comparing
the executed prefix and unexecuted continuation against vi-
sual observations, the agent can determine whether a fail-
ure requires calling the VLA again with a revised target or
merely applying a lightweight local adjustment to the pol-
icy’s well-structured trajectory, making recovery far more
tractable than full manual takeover.
To address these challenges, we introduce InteractVLA,
a framework that turns pretrained flow-based VLAs into
geometrically steerable and inspectable tools for agentic
manipulation (Figure 2).
Drawing on VLM-based pol-
icy steering [20] and visual geometric constraints [12, 13],
InteractVLA bridges agent reasoning and action genera-
tion through three components. First, test-time geometric
guidance uses a differentiable command-to-pose mapping
to translate an agent-specified end-effector target (position,
orientation, and gripper state) into input gradients through
the action expert within a single denoising trajectory, re-
shaping motion toward the agent’s intent while returning
control early if low confidence indicates an incompatible
target. Second, soft-time target alignment formulates arrival
time as a latent execution index, jointly matching complete
target poses at a shared arrival step that emerges naturally
as the trajectory takes shape. Third, action-informed agen-
tic refinement exposes generated action chunks alongside
calibrated camera reprojections and execution history to the
agent, enabling it to diagnose spatial discrepancies, revise
subsequent targets, or locally edit motion segments.
Because InteractVLA makes the VLA steerable rather
than rigidly repeating a single action mode, it also pro-
vides a strong foundation for recursive self-improvement
(RSI): across attempts, the agent iteratively sharpens how
it specifies geometric targets and refines policy-generated

2. Method
InteractVLA connects a vision-language agent and a pre-
trained flow-based VLA at test time through explicit geo-
metric targets and motion feedback (Figure 3). Given ob-
servation ok and instruction ℓ, the policy generates an ac-
tion chunk Ak ∈RH×D guided toward an agent-specified
target Gk = (p∗, R∗, w∗) comprising end-effector position,
orientation, and gripper state. The agent then inspects the
generated motion proposal to refine subsequent decisions.

While the agent specifies a desired interaction pose, its
appropriate arrival step within the horizon H depends on
task progress and the policy’s speed. Imposing a fixed-step
penalty forces an arbitrary timing assumption, whereas pe-
nalizing all steps uniformly collapses the approach motion
toward the target. We instead treat arrival time as a latent
execution index and softly align the target to the trajectory
(Figure 4).

2.3. Action-Informed Agentic Refinement
In the reverse direction, the guided action chunk serves as
both an executable motion and an interpretable proposal
whose approach vector, orientation profile, and grasp tim-
ing help diagnose spatial errors. Rather than returning a bi-
nary signal [7, 15], InteractVLA exposes the full chunk, its
executed prefix, and the remaining actions with observation
history.
To ground this motion proposal in visual evidence,
we map predicted command poses into the camera frame
via calibrated reprojection, comparing them against ob-
served object locations, historical pixel motion, and RGB-D
back-projections. Combined with proprioceptive state, this
visual-geometric correspondence allows the agent to distin-
guish a displaced approach target from a low-level execu-
tion slip or premature grasp.
Based on this diagnosis, the agent selects between two
lightweight recovery routes. When the interaction target
itself is inaccurate, the agent revises Gk and invokes the
guided VLA again to synthesize a new globally coherent
chunk. When the generated trajectory already captures the
right maneuver structure, the agent reuses the proposal and
applies local adjustments via robot-control tools, such as
shifting an approach offset, lift height, or gripper release
timing. Together with early rejection of low-confidence tar-
gets (Ct < e−1), this closes the bidirectional loop: agent-
specified geometry steers VLA generation, and the VLA’s
motion proposal guides the agent’s next correction.
3. Experiments
We evaluate InteractVLA across four parts: (1) how our
bidirectional geometric interface transforms online VLA
tool use by agents, (2) how controllable VLA execution un-
locks recursive self-improvement (RSI), (3) real-world bi-
manual manipulation, and (4) ablations on inference effi-
ciency, temporal alignment, and feedback design.

as Sol), we use Astra for RSI on RoboDojo and Robo-

online tool-use and LIBERO-Plus evaluations to balance
rollout budgets [17, 27]; substituting Astra in those Sol-
based settings would theoretically yield even stronger per-
formance. We compare three guidance modes: GT guid-
ance using training-data reference poses, offline guidance

3.1. VLA Tool Use by Agents
We first examine whether upgrading a VLA from a
language-only black box to a geometrically steerable tool
improves online agentic manipulation across perturbations.
LIBERO-Plus.
As shown in Table 1, steering standalone
VLA with explicit pose targets immediately unlocks latent
execution capability even without online replanning, rais-
ing overall success from 88.5% to 91.3% with GT targets
and 94.5% with offline Sol targets. In the online agent-
VLA loop, replacing language-only invocation (Sol + VLA,
93.1%) with InteractVLA boosts overall success to 98.3%.
The gains are most pronounced under spatial shifts where
language alone cannot ground the shifted geometry, jump-
ing from 89.5% to 98.4% under layout perturbations and
from 87.6% to 98.4% under camera viewpoint shifts. Ex-
plicit pose targets let the agent anchor the target location in
the current scene while relying on the VLA to synthesize
the intervening motion.
RoboDojo.
Table 2 extends this analysis to challenging
bimanual manipulation. While static GT pose targets lift
standalone VLA from 9.2% to 12.17% overall through
gains in generalization (28.0%) and memory (24.0%), they
slightly degrade precision and long-horizon tasks, indicat-
ing that a static spatial constraint alone cannot resolve com-
plex multi-step sequencing or contact dynamics.
Clos-
ing the online loop with InteractVLA overcomes this bot-
tleneck, lifting Sol + VLA from 18.0% to 26.0% over-
all and yielding consistent improvements across general-
ization (26.0%), long-horizon (12.5%), memory (22.2%),
and open-ended manipulation (39.6%). By inspecting mo-
tion proposals and updating targets as execution unfolds, the
agent dynamically adapts its geometric intent rather than
committing to a fixed target stream. Geometric guidance
and online refinement make pretrained VLAs more effec-
tive tools for agentic manipulation, which is also useful for
Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI).
3.2. Performance on Recursive Self-Improvement
During RSI, the agent explores in practice environments
sharing the target task semantics but initialized with distinct
random seeds and scene layouts, while the actual evaluation
scenes remain strictly unexposed.
Because InteractVLA
provides explicit target inputs and interpretable trajectory
outputs, the agent can diagnose failures across practice roll-
outs, consolidate reusable strategies (Figure 5), and transfer
them to held-out evaluation instances.
LIBERO-Plus.
In Table 3, InteractVLA + RSI achieves a
state-of-the-art 99.4% overall success rate, reaching 100%
on background and lighting perturbations and at least 98.4%
across all seven categories.
It substantially outperforms
standalone finetuned VLAs (OpenVLA-OFT at 69.6%,
RIPT-VLA at 68.4%) and prior agentic VLA systems in-
cluding HarnessVLA (86.7%) and RV-ICL (95.8%) [2].

Inference efficiency.
Figure 7 compares Agent + VLA,
which invokes the policy through language instructions;
Agent + InteractVLA, which adds geometric guidance and

generates low-level commands. Costs accumulate all RSI
attempts, including failures and retries, for each case and

agent API latency, VLA inference and guidance computa-
tion, and environment execution. Across all three bench-
marks, Agent + InteractVLA achieves higher success with
lower token consumption and total execution time than both
alternatives.
Target-time alignment.
Table 7 isolates the impact of
our temporal alignment objective under an identical VLA
backbone, pose target, and RSI budget.
Averaging pose
error uniformly across all steps performs worst (34.7% on
RoboDojo) because it pulls the entire approach trajectory
toward the endpoint, while constraining only the fixed fi-
nal step (39.1%) imposes rigid timing. Selecting the hard
minimum-error step improves flexibility (44.2%), and our
soft-time alignment achieves the best performance across
all benchmarks (99.4%, 48.6%, and 89.4%), confirming the
benefit of smoothly sharpening temporal assignment during
denoising at test time.
Action-informed feedback.
Table 8 evaluates the reverse
interface by progressively enriching the feedback returned
to the agent. Starting from visual execution observations
alone (40.3% on RoboDojo, 84.1% on RoboCasa365), ex-
posing the VLA’s generated action chunks raises success to
44.8% and 87.0% by revealing the policy’s intended con-
tinuation.
Adding calibrated camera reprojection further
boosts performance to 48.6% and 89.4%, proving that align-
ing 3D action proposals with 2D visual evidence is crucial
for accurate spatial diagnosis and local motion refinement.

4. Related Work
Classifier-free and geometric guidance.
Classifier-free
guidance (CFG) steers generative sampling by contrasting
conditional and unconditional score estimates [3, 11, 36],
as adapted to goal-conditioned robot policies in BESO [28].
Manipulation objectives can also be specified via 3D value
maps [12], relational keypoints [13], 3D trajectories [14,
18], or VLM-written reward functions in VLS [20]. How-
ever, VLS relies on heuristic VLM-synthesized trajectory
rewards with costly multi-particle resampling and does not
resolve latent arrival timing for complete 6-DoF poses. In
contrast, InteractVLA differentiates sparse agent-specified
poses through the action expert within a single flow trajec-
tory via soft-time target alignment, jointly resolving the ap-
proach motion and arrival step.
Agentic manipulation and runtime correction.
Recent
frameworks pair high-level agents with pretrained VLAs
via retry mechanisms [40], recursive video in-context learn-
ing [2], spatial affordance intervention [35], or phase-
specific runtime action offsets [5]. Meanwhile, recent test-
time flow steering methods adapt frozen policies using hu-
man interventions [31] or execution traces [38], yet lack
a closed-loop interface for autonomous agentic reasoning.
While these works motivate inspecting and correcting pol-
icy behavior rather than treating VLA calls as atomic, they
either rely on external primitives for recovery or apply
black-box residual offsets without steering internal gener-
ation [21, 37]. InteractVLA establishes a closed-loop bidi-
rectional geometric interface: it steers native flow genera-
tion toward explicit 6-DoF targets in the forward pass and
reprojects generated action chunks into the camera frame
in the reverse pass, enabling the agent to choose between
guided regeneration and local trajectory refinement.
5. Conclusion
We presented InteractVLA, a test-time framework that
transforms pretrained flow-based VLAs from opaque
language-only primitives into geometrically steerable and
8


inspectable tools for vision-language agents.
By cou-
pling differentiable test-time geometric guidance and soft-
time target alignment in the forward direction with action-
informed agentic refinement in the reverse direction, In-
teractVLA enables agents to precisely steer policy gener-
ation, diagnose spatial errors via calibrated reprojections,
and iteratively consolidate manipulation strategies through
recursive self-improvement. Extensive benchmark and real-
world evaluations demonstrate that this bidirectional geo-
metric interface unlocks substantial gains in both manipula-
tion success and inference efficiency.
