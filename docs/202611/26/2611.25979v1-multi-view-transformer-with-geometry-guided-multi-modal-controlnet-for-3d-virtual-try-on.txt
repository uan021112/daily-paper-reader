# Multi-View Transformer with Geometry-Guided Multi-Modal ControlNet for 3D Virtual Try-On 

Sarthak Batra, Armin Mustafa, and Adrian Hilton 

University of Surrey, Guildford, UK 

{s.batra,armin.mustafa,a.hilton}@surrey.ac.uk 

Abstract. Editing a 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) capture of a per-son so that they wear a new garment requires every rendered view to agree on the same garment details. Existing 3D virtual try-on (VTON) pipelines, however, edit training views largely independently and fuse them with hand-designed heuristics such as manually chosen reference views and fixed attention-blending weights. The resulting inconsisten-cies are baked into the 3D representation and surface as texture drift, blurred prints, and implausible geometry in novel views. We present 

GS-VTON-MVT , a three-stage framework that combines implicit and 

explicit 3D reasoning for consistent 3DGS try-on. (i) A multi-view in-painting transformer jointly edits a set of training views with full cross-view self-attention, so that cross-view correspondence is learned rather than imposed. (ii) A geometry-guided multi-modal ControlNet conditions the diffusion editor on the garment image together with depth and sur-face normals rendered from the current 3DGS state, supplying explicit structure where visual evidence is weakest. (iii) A pose-adaptive persona-aware attention replaces the fixed blending weight between current-view and reference-view features with weights derived from camera-pose prox-imity. On the 3D-VTONBench benchmark, GS-VTON-MVT improves FID, KID, LPIPS-based cross-view consistency, and user preference over general-purpose 3D editors and prior 3D try-on methods. 

# 1 Introduction 

Virtual try-on (VTON) lets a user see how a garment would look on their own body without a physical fitting. Beyond e-commerce, it is increasingly relevant to AR/VR avatars and telepresence, where a garment must remain plausible as the viewpoint and the body move. The task is difficult because it couples fine-grained appearance transfer (textures, logos, prints) with strict geometric and identity constraints (body shape, pose, self-occlusion, and preservation of the face, hair, and background). Most research has focused on 2D try-on, which has evolved from warping-and-compositing pipelines built on human parsing and dense correspondence [9, 14, 22] to diffusion models [19, 31, 34] that cast try-on as conditional generation or inpainting [10, 24, 44, 45, 50]. These models produce highly realistic single arXiv:2611.25979v1 [cs.CV] 19 Nov 2026 2 S. Batra et al. 

images, but they have no mechanism for keeping a garment coherent across viewpoints, which is exactly what a user expects when turning around to see the side or back. Moving to 3D adds two requirements. Multi-view consistency :garment details such as patterns, seams, and prints must persist across views without flicker or drift. Geometric plausibility : the garment must drape and occlude correctly around the body, including under self-occlusions such as crossed arms or strong torso rotation. The two are tightly coupled: small inconsistencies among edited training images become persistent artifacts in the reconstructed 3D representation, and weak geometric reasoning produces distortions that are most visible in novel views. In parallel, 3D scene editing has advanced on top of differentiable represen-tations such as Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) [28] and 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) [23]. The dominant paradigm renders the current 3D scene, edits the renderings with a 2D diffusion model, and distills the edits back into the scene parameters [8,15,43,53]. Try-on is a demanding instance of this problem: the edit must be local (the garment region only), identity-preserving (face, hair, body, and background stay fixed), and faithful to a garment image rather than to a coarse text prompt, which is why image-prompted 3D editing has become an important direction [52]. Recent 3DGS-based try-on methods [5, 6] adapt this paradigm to garments, but several of their key components are hand-designed rather than learned. (i) Cross-view consistency of the edited training set is encouraged by concatenat-ing attention features from manually selected reference views, rather than by learning correspondences between views. (ii) During 3DGS editing, the diffusion model is conditioned on garment appearance alone and must infer surface ori-entation, depth ordering, and occlusion boundaries implicitly. (iii) Current-view and reference-view attention features are blended with a fixed weight that ig-nores camera pose, so every reference view is trusted equally regardless of how close it is to the current viewpoint [5]. We address these limitations with GS-VTON-MVT , a three-stage frame-work that combines implicit 3D reasoning learned through multi-view attention with explicit geometric guidance through multi-modal conditioning. Our guid-ing principle is that consistency should be learned where supervision is strongest (the observed training views) and enforced where generalization is hardest (novel views). 

(1) Multi-view inpainting transformer. Rather than editing each training view separately and reconciling the results post hoc, we edit several views jointly .All views are tokenized into a single sequence and processed with cross-view self-attention, so that any patch can attend to every patch in every other view. Multi-view alignment thereby becomes a learnable correspondence problem: at-tention can discover which regions depict the same garment surface under differ-ent viewpoints. The resulting edited set is more coherent and provides a cleaner supervision signal for LoRA personalization [20] and 3D optimization. GS-VTON-MVT: Geometry-Guided Multi-View 3D Virtual Try-On 3

(2) Geometry-guided multi-modal ControlNet. Cross-view attention is effective for observed views, but the iterative 3DGS editing stage must also synthesize the garment from viewpoints without direct visual evidence, where the diffusion model has to decide how textures warp and where folds form. We therefore con-dition the editor with a multi-modal ControlNet [46] that receives, in addition to the garment image, depth and surface normals rendered from the current 3DGS state. The garment branch supplies appearance; the normal branch supplies lo-cal surface orientation, which governs shading and fold direction; and the depth branch supplies spatial ordering and occlusion boundaries. 

(3) Pose-adaptive persona-aware attention. Finally, we revisit how reference views are aggregated during 3DGS editing. A fixed blending weight between current-view and reference-view attention implicitly assumes that every refer-ence view is equally informative from every camera pose, which is rarely true. We instead weight reference views by camera proximity (a softmin over pose distances) and predict the overall blending ratio with a lightweight MLP con-ditioned on the current pose. The editor thus relies on reference evidence when the camera is close to a training viewpoint and on its generative prior when the viewpoint is genuinely novel. Together, these components form a hybrid 3D reasoning scheme: cross-view attention provides implicit 3D awareness when editing training views, and geometry-conditioned diffusion provides explicit structure during novel-view editing. On 3D-VTONBench [5], GS-VTON-MVT improves multi-view consistency and ge-ometric fidelity over general-purpose 3D editors and prior 3D try-on methods, with the largest gains under self-occlusion and challenging poses. 

Contributions. In summary: 

– We propose a multi-view inpainting transformer for constructing the edited training set in 3D try-on, replacing heuristic reference-feature con-catenation with cross-view correspondence learned through joint attention. 

– We introduce a geometry-guided multi-modal ControlNet for 3DGS try-on that extends garment-image conditioning with depth and normal maps rendered from the evolving 3D representation, rather than from a fitted body model. 

– We develop a pose-adaptive persona-aware attention that weights refer-ence views by camera proximity and predicts the current/reference blending ratio from the camera pose. 

– We show empirically that combining implicit (attention-based) and explicit (geometry-conditioned) reasoning yields consistent gains in perceptual qual-ity and cross-view consistency for diffusion-based 3D try-on. 

# 2 Related Work 

2.1 Diffusion Models, Personalization, and Controllable Generation 

Denoising diffusion models [19] have become the dominant paradigm for high-fidelity image synthesis and editing; faster samplers [34] made them practical 4 S. Batra et al. 

for interactive editing, and latent diffusion [31] made high-resolution synthesis affordable by denoising in a compact VAE latent space. Vision-language rep-resentations such as CLIP [30] ground generation in text or image conditions. 

Personalization adapts a pretrained model to a specific subject: DreamBooth [33] fine-tunes the full model, while LoRA [20] learns low-rank weight updates at a fraction of the cost. Controllable generation adds structural constraints; Control-Net [46] attaches a trainable copy of the UNet encoder that injects spatial condi-tions such as edges, depth, or pose through zero-initialized convolutions. Multiple geometric conditions have been fused for human generation: Champ [51] condi-tions human animation on depth, normal, and semantic maps rendered from a parametric body model, and multi-view generators such as MV-Adapter [21] condition on rendered position and normal maps. Our multi-modal ControlNet follows this line but derives depth and normals from the evolving 3DGS scene rather than from a template body or a fixed mesh. 

2.2 2D Image-Based Virtual Try-On 

Classical 2D try-on decomposes the task into human parsing, garment warping, and compositing [14]; high-resolution variants reduce misalignment artifacts [9], and parser-free formulations remove the dependence on brittle segmentation [22]. Diffusion-based methods replace explicit warping with conditional generation, producing realistic wrinkles, shading, and fine patterns [50]. Subsequent work improves garment correspondence and identity preservation [10,24], controllabil-ity and efficiency [44,45], and architectural simplicity: CatVTON [11] conditions by simply concatenating person and garment latents, and Leffa [49] regularizes attention to behave like a flow field, which sharpens fine garment details. Multi-view try-on has also emerged in 2D: MV-VTON [39] uses front and back garment images to dress a person seen from different viewpoints. These models, however, still generate each image independently and provide no guarantee of cross-view coherence, which becomes the main failure mode when their outputs supervise a 3D representation. 

2.3 Multi-View Consistent 3D Editing 

NeRF [28] and 3DGS [23] have enabled diffusion-driven 3D editing, in which renderings are edited in 2D and the edits are distilled back into the scene, either through iterative dataset updates [12, 15, 37] or score distillation [8, 53]; TIP-Editor [52] additionally accepts an image prompt. Because independently edited views disagree, much recent work enforces consistency during 2D editing itself. GaussCtrl [43] edits all views jointly with a depth-conditioned ControlNet, using depth rendered from the 3DGS, and aligns attention to a few reference views with a fixed blending weight. DGE [7] jointly edits key views with extended self-attention and propagates features to the remaining views along epipolar lines; VcEdit [40] alternates 2D editing with 3DGS-based consistency modules; Edit-Splat [26] fuses information from the nearest source views by depth reprojection; and InterGSEdit [42] blends 2D and 3D-consistent attention with a learned gate. A related line performs multi-view inpainting directly: NeRFiller [41] inpaints views tiled into grids, MVInpainter [4] shares reference key/value features across views, and Instant3dit [2] fine-tunes an inpainting model on multi-view grids. More recently, large multi-image editors such as Tinker [48] edit several views in a single pass of a diffusion transformer. Our design borrows from these ideas but targets the specific constraints of try-on: edits are restricted to the garment re-gion, must reproduce a given garment image, and must keep the person’s identity intact. 

2.4 3D Virtual Try-On 

Early 3D try-on dressed parametric body models using captured, simulated, or learned garments [29]. Recent methods instead edit real multi-view captures by lifting 2D try-on models into 3D. GaussianVTON [6] edits a 3DGS scene in multiple stages with a 2D try-on model as an image prompt. GS-VTON [5], on which we build, personalizes an inpainting diffusion model with LoRA on a reference-driven, jointly edited set of training views, and then edits the 3DGS scene with a persona-aware attention that blends current-view and reference-view features using a fixed weight; it also introduces the 3D-VTONBench bench-mark. VTON 360 [17] treats 3D try-on as an extension of 2D try-on: it jointly inpaints multi-view renderings of a clothed human using multi-view attention modulated by relative camera pose, conditions on SMPL-X normal maps, and reconstructs the result in 3D. GS-VTON-MVT differs from these methods in three respects: (i) correspondence between the edited training views is learned with full token-level cross-view attention rather than through single-reference feature sharing; (ii) geometric guidance comes from depth and normals rendered from the evolving 3DGS scene, which track the subject’s actual silhouette, hair, and loose clothing, rather than from a fitted SMPL-X body; and (iii) the cur-rent/reference blending used during 3DGS editing adapts to the camera pose instead of being fixed.

# 4 Experiments 

4.1 Dataset and Evaluation Protocol 

We evaluate on 3D-VTONBench [5], a multi-view benchmark for 3D try-on containing 60 subjects captured in a variety of poses and garments. In our setup 12 S. Batra et al. 

each subject has 12 –16 views, which we process at 1024 × 1024 resolution. For each subject, we select n=4 training views for Stage 2 multi-view editing and hold out the remaining views for novel-view evaluation. During Stage 3, we optimize the 3DGS representation [23] with cameras sampled along the capture trajectory. 

4.2 Metrics 

We report perceptual, consistency, and geometric metrics. (i) FID [18] and 

KID [3] measure distributional realism of the rendered views. (ii) LPIPS [47] between renderings of random view pairs of the same subject and garment serves as a proxy for multi-view consistency; lower values indicate fewer view-dependent changes. (iii) PSNR is computed on held-out camera views against the origi-nal capture and is restricted to pixels outside the garment mask, because no ground-truth image of the subject wearing the target garment exists; it there-fore measures how well identity, background, and unedited clothing are preserved in novel views. (iv) Normal MSE compares normal maps rendered from the edited scene with normals rendered from the pre-edit 3DGS scene, again out-side the garment mask, and measures geometric drift in regions that should not change. 

4.3 Baselines 

We compare against the 3D try-on method GS-VTON [5] and the general-purpose 3D editors used in its evaluation: Instruct-NeRF2NeRF [15], Instruct-GS2GS [37], GaussianEditor [8], GaussCtrl [43], and ViCA-NeRF [12]. All meth-ods receive the same garment image and camera splits; methods not designed for try-on are restricted to the clothing-region mask and given consistent prompt and conditioning templates. More recent multi-view consistent editors (DGE [7], EditSplat [26]) and the multi-view try-on method VTON 360 [17] are not yet included in this comparison; we discuss them in Sec. 5. Neither GS-VTON nor the general-purpose editors report FID, KID, or LPIPS on 3D-VTONBench. All baseline numbers in Tables 1 and 2 are therefore obtained by running the public implementations ourselves on the same subjects, garments, and camera splits as our method. 

4.4 Implementation Details 

All views are processed at 1024 × 1024 resolution. Stage 2 jointly edits n=4 

training views per subject, with pseudo-targets for training the MVT produced by a 2D diffusion try-on model [10]. The edited views are used to personalize a Stable Diffusion inpainting model [35] with LoRA [20]. In Stage 3, the depth and normal ControlNet branches are pretrained and frozen, the garment branch follows GS-VTON [5], the three branches are combined with the attention fusion, and the blending MLP of Sec. 3.7 is optimized per scene. 4.7 Qualitative Results 

Figure 2 shows try-on results for three in-the-wild subjects outside the bench-mark. For each subject we run the full three-stage pipeline on a multi-view cap-ture, exactly as for the benchmark subjects, and show two of the views together with the corresponding outputs of the baseline. GS-VTON-MVT reproduces the print of the target garment in both views and leaves the background and the re-maining clothing of the subject unchanged. The baseline alters parts of the scene outside the garment (for example, the door behind the second subject changes color) and distorts printed text. More generally, we observe three recurring fail-ure modes of prior methods that GS-VTON-MVT mitigates. (1) Pattern in-tegrity: baselines often smear high-frequency textures such as stripes, logos, and printed text across views, whereas cross-view attention keeps patch-level correspondences stable. (2) Self-occlusion: for crossed arms or hands in pock-ets, depth conditioning preserves the correct front-to-back ordering and reduces hallucinated garment layers. (3) Drape and shading: normal conditioning improves fold orientation and avoids a flat, “stuck-on” appearance, producing wrinkles that stay coherent across nearby viewpoints.

Limitations and future work. Several limitations remain. Geometry of the orig-inal garment. The depth and normal conditions are rendered from the current 3DGS scene, which initially encodes the subject’s original clothing; when the target garment has a different silhouette ( e.g ., long sleeves replacing short ones, or a dress replacing trousers), this guidance can bias the edit toward the old shape, and the normal-preservation term Lgeo can likewise resist legitimate geo-metric change. Restricting Lgeo to regions outside the garment mask, dilating the mask, or blending in body-model geometry are natural remedies. Noisy rendered geometry. Depth and normals rendered from 3DGS are noisy, particularly at thin structures and hair; more geometrically accurate Gaussian representations could make the guidance more reliable. Scalability. Full cross-view attention is quadratic in the number of views, which currently limits Stage 2 to a handful of views; sparse, epipolar, or factorized attention could relax this. Hard cases. 

Long hair crossing the garment and severe self-occlusion still produce occasional artifacts, suggesting the need for better human parsing and stronger occlusion priors. Evaluation. Our comparison does not yet include recent multi-view con-sistent editors [7, 26] or the multi-view try-on method VTON 360 [17], and our consistency metric is LPIPS-based; evaluating with dedicated multi-view consis-tency metrics such as MEt3R [1] and with garment-fidelity measures computed on the garment region is an important next step.
