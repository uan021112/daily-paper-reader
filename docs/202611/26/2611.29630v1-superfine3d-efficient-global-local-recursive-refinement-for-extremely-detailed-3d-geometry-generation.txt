Abstract
High-resolution images contain rich visual details that re-
quire fine geometric structures for faithful 3D reconstruction.
However, generating extremely detailed 3D geometry from
images requires substantial computational resources and
training data, making high-resolution geometry generation
challenging for existing state-of-the-art models. In this paper,
we present SuperFine3D, an efficient framework for gener-
ating extremely detailed 3D geometry through global-local
recursive refinement. SuperFine3D learns a shared refine-
ment operator from paired coarse-to-fine representations
and recursively reuses it to progressively synthesize finer
geometry beyond the training resolutions, without requir-
ing additional paired supervision at target resolutions. To
make recursive refinement computationally efficient, we in-
troduce spatial-cell attention, which restricts high-resolution
interactions to local spatial neighborhoods while maintain-
ing bounded communication across neighboring cells. We
further propose spatially anchored cross-scale guidance,
which leverages globally contextualized coarse geometry

sistency. In addition, geometry-aware training objectives
encourage the refinement process to preserve stable geomet-

Experiments demonstrate that SuperFine3D generates ge-
ometry at a 40963 decoder-grid resolution, corresponding
to a sparse 2563 latent lattice, using a shared refinement
operator trained only on 5123–10243 representations. Com-
pared with a full-attention recursive baseline, SuperFine3D
achieves a 9.4× speedup in end-to-end inference and re-
duces peak GPU memory usage by 23.0%, while improving
cross-scale structural consistency and producing cleaner
surfaces with finer geometric details.
1. Introduction
Generating detailed 3D assets from images is an important
capability for digital content creation, simulation, and in-
teractive applications. Diffusion Transformers provide a
scalable generative architecture [27], while structured la-
tent representations support detailed 3D generation [44, 45].
However, generating extremely detailed geometry at ultra-
high resolutions remains challenging. The increasing num-

ber of active tokens makes high-resolution denoising com-
putationally expensive, while learning to generate finer ge-

target resolutions or separately adapted generators. These
requirements introduce substantial computational and data
demands.
In this paper, we address this problem by formulating
high-resolution 3D generation as recursive geometric refine-
ment. Instead of training a separate generator for each target
resolution, we learn a shared refinement operator from paired
coarse-to-fine representations. The operator learns the ge-
ometric corrections associated with a resolution-doubling
transition and is recursively reused to progressively synthe-
size finer geometry. This enables generation beyond the
training resolutions without requiring additional paired su-
pervision at each target resolution. As illustrated in Fig. 1,
SuperFine3D generates extremely detailed 3D geometry that
captures fine structures and details from high-resolution im-
age inputs.
The key insight of SuperFine3D is to combine compu-
tationally efficient local refinement with globally informed
structural guidance. To achieve this, we introduce spatial-
cell attention, which restricts high-resolution interactions
to tokens within each spatial cell and a bounded neigh-
borhood of adjacent cells. By prioritizing communication
across cell boundaries, it preserves local geometric conti-
nuity while avoiding global all-pairs attention over the en-
tire high-resolution token set. Complementing this local
computation, we introduce spatially anchored cross-scale
guidance, which leverages globally contextualized coarse
geometry to guide local detail synthesis. Spatially indexed
cross-scale attention and continuous interpolation anchors
establish correspondence between irregular sparse supports
at different resolutions, allowing high-resolution tokens to
access both object-level context and spatially relevant geo-
metric information. Furthermore, we introduce geometry-
aware training objectives that distinguish stable geometric
structures from detail-bearing corrections, encouraging the
refinement operator to preserve existing geometry while in-
troducing fine-grained surface details. At inference time,
each generated latent provides both the structural guidance
and sparse support for the next refinement stage, forming a
self-contained recursive generation pipeline.
We instantiate SuperFine3D on the TRELLIS.2 sparse
latent representation and its pretrained geometry decoder.
The shared refinement operator is trained using paired rep-
resentations at 5123 and 10243 geometry resolutions, and
is recursively applied to generate geometry at 20483 and
40963 resolutions. The resulting 40963 decoder-grid geome-
try corresponds to a sparse 2563 latent lattice. Experiments
demonstrate that SuperFine3D achieves a 9.4× speedup in
end-to-end inference and reduces peak GPU memory usage
by 23.0% compared with a full-attention recursive baseline.
It also improves cross-scale structural consistency and pro-
duces cleaner surfaces with finer geometric details.
Our main contributions are summarized as follows:
• We present SuperFine3D, an efficient framework for
ultra-high-resolution 3D geometry generation through
recursive refinement. A shared refinement operator
learned from 5123–10243 representations progressively
synthesizes geometry up to 40963 and beyond, without
requiring additional paired supervision at the target
resolutions.
• We introduce spatial-cell attention with bounded,
boundary-prioritized cross-cell communication to effi-
ciently denoise large high-resolution sparse supports.
• We propose spatially anchored cross-scale guidance
and geometry-aware training objectives to ground lo-
cal detail synthesis in coarse geometric structure, pre-
serving structural consistency while introducing fine-
grained details.
2. Related Work
2.1. Image-to-3D Generation and Representations
Image-to-3D generation has moved from per-instance opti-
mization to feed-forward reconstruction and native 3D gen-
eration. Score distillation lifts 2D diffusion priors to 3D
from text [28, 18] or a single image [21, 29, 37]; multi-
view diffusion improves cross-view consistency [31, 22],
and large reconstruction models predict 3D directly from
images [8, 39]. Representations range from triplanes [8, 42]
and 3D Gaussians [36, 2] to implicit functions [12] and
vector-set latents [50], as used by Hunyuan3D-2.1 [10]; the
attainable geometric detail depends on both the representa-
tion and the generator.
Sparse structured latents concentrate computation on ac-
tive regions [44, 45], and TRELLIS.2 pairs them with native
geometry decoding [45]. Scaling to 40963, however, re-
quires the generator itself to synthesize features over far
larger supports while keeping object structure coherent. Sep-
arately, monocular normal estimators recover sharp surface
detail from a single image [46, 47], and Hi3DGen uses nor-
mals as an intermediate representation for generation [47].
SuperFine3D combines the two: estimated normals constrain
the geometry at every level, and a shared refinement operator
lifts the result across resolutions.
2.2. Coarse-to-Fine and Recursive Generation
Coarse-to-fine generation separates object-level structure
from finer-scale synthesis. Cascaded image diffusion models
use successive super-resolution stages to increase output res-
olution [7, 30]. Low-resolution structural conditioning can
also guide localized high-resolution image synthesis [52].

40963 level would require roughly an order of magnitude

its resolution and its conditioning geometry change across
stages. SuperFine3D learns an explicitly LR-conditioned op-

with local attention that keeps such token counts tractable,
and re-anchors every stage to the input image through ex-
plicit detail injection, without paired supervision at the target
resolutions.
2.3. Efficient Attention in Diffusion Transformers
Global self-attention in Diffusion Transformers [27] scales
quadratically with token count.
FlashAttention [5] and
RingAttention [20] execute dense attention more efficiently
without changing its pairwise structure, while windowed
attention [23], sparse convolutions, and octree attention
[3, 35, 40] exploit spatial locality. Direct3D-S2 uses spatial
sparse attention for scalable 3D generation [43]. On irregular
3D surface supports, fixed windows cover ever smaller parts
of the object as resolution grows; SuperFine3D therefore
pairs local spatial-cell attention with a globally contextual-
ized LR pathway.
3. Method
3.1. Image-Guided Recursive Refinement
SuperFine3D learns a resolution-doubling refinement opera-

same weights at higher resolutions (Fig. 2). We build on the
frozen TRELLIS.2 generator, image encoder, and geometry
autoencoder [45], where a shape at decoder-grid resolution
R3 is a sparse latent z(R) = (S(R), F(R)) on a lattice of
edge length R/16.

HR supports) with conditional flow matching [19], randomly
perturbing the LR latent (local token deletion and feature
noise) so that the operator tolerates generated LR inputs.

latent with the frozen decoder and supervise it geometrically,
separating target-supported detail from regions that should
remain stable (Fig. 3).

4.3. Computational Efficiency and Scalability
We benchmark the same asset on an NVIDIA RTX PRO
6000 for the three methods that reach a 40963-class grid,
each with its own default sampling settings. Each run starts
on an idle GPU and includes image conditioning, coarse
generation, all refinement stages, geometry decoding, and
mesh export; for SuperFine3D this also includes camera
fitting and detail injection. UltraShape extracts its surface
with a memory-bounded, chunked decoder.1 Table 2 reports
latency and peak allocated GPU memory.
At 40963, SuperFine3D is 9.4× faster than TRELLIS.2–
Rec. with 23.0% less peak GPU memory, and about 12.6×
faster than UltraShape, whose extraction fits on the GPU
only by keeping the field in host memory.

4.4. Improving Structure with Multiple Views
SuperFine3D also accepts multiple posed images. We es-
timate cameras and the focal length with MASt3R [16],
average pixel-aligned features across views in the coarse
generator, and refine the result to 40963 with the unchanged
pipeline (detail injection uses the first view). This targets
errors that refinement cannot fix: a wrong coarse layout
propagates through every later stage, whereas a second view
constrains depth and occluded parts from the start.
In Fig. 6, the inputs are 50◦apart and a held-out photo
is the reference. With one view, the knight stands upright
and the dragon is stretched sideways; with two, both match
the held-out photo, raising silhouette IoU from 0.22 to 0.64
(0.48 to 0.81 on a second held-out photo).

5. Conclusion
We presented SuperFine3D, an efficient framework for gener-
ating extremely detailed 3D geometry from images through
global-local recursive refinement. To enable efficient high-
resolution refinement, we introduced spatial-cell attention for
local computation and spatially anchored cross-scale guid-
ance to preserve global structure while synthesizing local
details. Geometry-aware training objectives further encour-
age structural preservation and fine-grained detail synthesis.

Experiments demonstrate that SuperFine3D generates geom-

compared with a full-attention recursive baseline. Future
work will explore scaling SuperFine3D to larger and more
complex 3D structures.

A. Scope and Resolution Conventions
This supplement details the image-guided recursive infer-
ence procedure and its coarse start, the detail-normal estima-
tor and height-field injection, multi-view input, geometric
supervision and training, baseline configurations, evalua-
tion metrics with additional comparisons, and the efficiency
analysis. All resolutions refer to geometry unless otherwise
stated. Figures and tables numbered without the prefix S are
those of the main paper. For the TRELLIS.2 [45] representa-
tion, a geometry grid of edge length R is represented by a
sparse latent lattice of edge length r = R/16. Consequently,
5123, 10243, 20483, and 40963 geometry correspond to 323,
643, 1283, and 2563 latent lattices. Only active lattice sites
are instantiated, so computation follows the object surface
rather than the full R3 volume.
The refinement operator is trained only on full-object
5123–10243 pairs and is deployed with shared weights at
20483 and 40963. At every level, an explicit, training-free
injection step transfers fine detail from the input image, so
each new resolution is grounded in the image. Each level
synthesizes new latent features on a finer lattice, which adds
geometry that a fixed continuous field sampled more densely
cannot contain.

B.2. Camera estimation
The input camera relative to the generated shape is esti-
mated from the image and the generated 5123 mesh, without
camera supervision. A camera looking at the origin from
direction (yaw, pitch) at the training-render distance, with
free roll, field of view, and a 2D similarity on the condition-
ing crop, is fitted in two steps: (i) a grid search followed by
Powell refinement of the silhouette IoU between the input
alpha mask and the projected mesh, and (ii) an appearance-
aware refinement that minimizes (1 −IoU) + λ (1 −NCC),
where NCC compares Sobel edge magnitudes of the input
crop and of a shaded render of the mesh at 512 px. Step
(ii) also evaluates the mirrored candidates (pitch →−pitch,
yaw →yaw+180◦, and both), which resolves the front/back
ambiguity of silhouettes. The same camera is reused at all
levels. The injected image is the conditioning crop at its
native resolution (at most 4096 px), masked by the dilated
silhouette of the generated mesh.

cell, attention includes every active token. A bounded ad-
ditional set NB of at most B tokens is selected from the
26 neighboring cells, prioritizing tokens near face-adjacent
boundaries and then spatially distributed neighbors. Routing
is computed from coordinates and reused within the stage
because the denoising support remains fixed.

The LR stream is conditioned on the image and globally

modulation is zero-initialized at the output, preserving pre-

and LR attention readout are fused through the two gates
defined in the main paper.
B.5. Caching and memory management
Within a refinement stage, the LR latent and image condition
are fixed. The LR stream uses tL = 0 and is evaluated inde-
pendently of the evolving HR denoising state, so its encoded
context and layerwise LR keys and values are cached for
spatially indexed HR access; the keys and values are kept in
pinned host memory and copied to the GPU one layer ahead
of use on a separate stream. Time-dependent gates and HR
activations are evaluated at every step. Memory-bounded
decoding and rendering retain the complete geometry at the
requested resolution.
B.6. Index-based attention and fused kernels
Index-based attention.
HR tokens are sorted by cell, so
the queries of a cell form one variable-length sequence
whose keys are the cell’s own tokens and its neighbor set NB
(Sec. B.4). We extend the variable-length FlashAttention-
2 [4] kernel to load key and value rows through a per-
sequence array of row indices into the shared K/V tensors.
All cells are processed in one launch, and no per-cell copy
of keys and values is formed. The HR-to-LR read uses the
same kernel with indices into the cached LR keys and values.
The output is bit-identical to gathering the rows and calling
the original kernel.
Fused kernels.
Hand-written Triton [38] kernels fuse (i)
the per-head RMS normalization [49] of queries and keys
with the 3D rotary embedding [34], (ii) LayerNorm [1] with

fp32 statistics and the adaLN [27] scale and shift, (iii) the
gated residual updates, and (iv) the query and key normal-
ization of the image cross-attention; the LoRA [9] updates
are merged into the attention projections. The kernels ac-
cumulate in fp32, round to bf16 where the reference imple-
mentation stores bf16 tensors, and use 64-bit offsets, since
element offsets exceed 231 on the largest supports. The ker-
nels compile once and are cached on disk, so a stage with a
single network evaluation pays no compilation cost.

Resolution gate.
An injection can only transfer detail that
the image resolves at the scale of the current level. Before
each injection, we therefore measure how many input-image
pixels one voxel of the level spans, at the object centre along
the camera’s horizontal axis. Each level halves the voxel and
thus this footprint; for the 4096 px input of Fig. S3 it is 8.4,
4.2, 2.1, and 1.05 pixels at 5123, 10243, 20483, and 40963.
Below 0.5 pixels per voxel, more than two voxels share one
pixel, so the image holds no information at the scale that the
level adds, and fitting the height field would only imprint
pixel-level estimator noise. Such a level is not injected; its
detail comes from refinement alone, while all coarser levels
are injected as usual.
C.4. Input resolution
Injection transfers detail that is present in the input image,
so the finest relief SuperFine3D forms is set by the detail the
image genuinely resolves, not by its pixel count. Figure S3
isolates this with three versions of one generated image, all
refined from the same 5123 start with identical settings: (i)
the native 4096 px image, which stands for a genuinely high-
resolution input such as a high-resolution photograph or a
native 4096 px generation; (ii) the image downsampled to
1024 px, which stands for a genuinely low-resolution input
such as a typical 1024 px web image or generator output;
and (iii) that 1024 px image upsampled back to 4096 px by
Lanczos interpolation, which stands for a low-resolution
image enlarged to a high pixel count: it has the pixel count
of (i) but only the detail of (ii).
With input (i), SuperFine3D forms the openwork eye rim
and the leaf scrolls of the chest plate (A, B). With input (ii),
these structures appear only in coarse form: the image does
not resolve them, and the resolution gate (Sec. C.3) injects at
5123 to 20483 but skips the final 40963 injection, where one
voxel spans only 0.26 pixels. Input (iii) has four times as
many pixels and passes the gate at every level (1.05 pixels per
voxel at 40963), but the gate measures the pixel footprint, not
the detail: its pixels carry no detail beyond that of (ii), every
14


injection transfers the smooth interpolated normals, and the
relief comes out rounded. Genuine resolution thus translates
directly into geometric detail, whereas interpolation adds
pixels but no detail.
Practical note.
High-resolution results therefore require
an input image with genuine high-frequency detail; a high
pixel count alone is not sufficient. Images should be used
at the resolution at which they were captured or generated,
and not upsampled by interpolation beforehand. A simple
spectral check separates the two cases: the share of spectral
power above the Nyquist frequency of a 2048 px image is
3.0% for input (i) and 0.007% for input (iii); all inputs of
Fig. 5 lie at 1.2–3.0%.
C.5. Settings of figures and evaluation
Figures 1, 5, 6, and S3 use t0 = 0.05 at the 40963 stage. In
these figures, textured inputs use g = 1.5 and keep the finest
band (gf = 1.2, κf = 4 px). Before the target is formed,
the estimate is warped onto the rendered mesh normals by
a smooth dense flow (DIS optical flow [14] on low-passed
normal images, Gaussian-smoothed and bounded to 96 px),
so image detail lands on the mesh’s own structures. Figures 1,
5, and S3 also average the estimate over two tile offsets and
a horizontal mirror.
Quantitative evaluation.
Tables 1, S3, and S4 use one
setting for all inputs: t0 = 0.05 at the 40963 stage, unit
gains (g = gf = 1), and the evidence gate with θ = 8◦
(16◦at the final 40963 injection). Each finer level injects
only finer bands, with κ = 64 px at 5123 and 10243, 32 px at
20483, and 16 px at the final 40963 injection. The estimate
is computed once at the first level and reused at all levels,
without flow warp or test-time averaging.

J. Efficiency Analysis
SuperFine3D reaches a 40963 decoder grid in 176.2 s from
a single image (Table 2). Three properties of the design pro-
duce this speed: each refinement stage needs one evaluation
of the refinement network on a sparse support; spatial-cell at-
tention runs as index-based kernels that never copy neighbor
keys and values; and fused kernels merge the memory-bound
operators of each transformer block. This section quantifies
each of them.
J.1. Measurement protocol
All measurements use one NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Black-
well (Max-Q) GPU. A measurement starts only on an idle
GPU: no other compute process, utilization at most 2% and
less than 1.5 GiB in use over three checks 30 s apart, and host
load below half of the CPU cores. During the measurement
the GPU is polled every second, and a run during which
any other process appears is discarded and repeated. Stage
boundaries are CUDA-synchronized. End-to-end runs start
from the input image and end with the exported mesh, in-
cluding model loads, host–device transfers, and file I/O, and
excluding interpreter start-up and imports. Helper processes
(the coarse-shape generator and the voxelization workers)
are started before the measurement, so the same exclusion
applies to them; all of their work, including model loads, is
measured. Kernel and block measurements report the median
of 15 and 9 synchronized repetitions after warm-up; block
measurements, including those in Fig. 1, replay transformer
block 0 (conditional branch, batch size one) on matched
supports. Memory is the peak allocated GPU memory.
