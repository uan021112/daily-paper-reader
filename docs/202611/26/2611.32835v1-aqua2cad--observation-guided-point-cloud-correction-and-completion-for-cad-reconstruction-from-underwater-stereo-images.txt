Abstract—Recovering editable computer-aided design (CAD)
models from underwater stereo images requires accurate and
complete geometry, yet stereo reconstruction produces displaced
points and missing regions. The original views retain cross-view
disagreement and ray evidence lost during point-cloud fusion.
Aqua2CAD exploits this evidence in two stages: view-conditioned
correction and quality filtering establish reliable geometric sup-
port, while multi-view observations condition a learned shape
prior to infer missing surfaces in the original metric frame.
The recovered points support surface fitting and solid assembly
with manually revised face groups. On an OceanSim-based
synthetic benchmark, Aqua2CAD reduces point-cloud Chamfer
distance by 60% relative to the stereo reconstruction. Compared
with a matched point-only completion backbone, it improves
missing-region recovery at 5 mm from 16.27% to 24.16%. With
a shared CAD backend, exterior acceptance increases from 6/19
to 14/19. Qualitative results on selected real underwater captures
demonstrate recovery of the main structures of six 3D-printed
objects.

I. INTRODUCTION
Underwater inspection and maintenance frequently require
accurate measurements of existing components for replace-
ment, adaptation, and fabrication, for example in subsea spool
installation and retrofitting [1], [2]. Such tasks benefit from
computer-aided design (CAD) models with editable surfaces,
explicit boundaries, and connectivity [3]. Calibrated stereo
cameras, which are commonly available on underwater plat-
forms, provide metric geometry under sufficient visibility [4],
[5] and are therefore a natural source for recovering such
models.
Recovering such models underwater, however, is consider-
ably harder than on land. Wavelength-dependent attenuation,
scattering, turbidity, and changing illumination severely de-
grade underwater images [6], [7] and, together with weak
texture, impair stereo matching [5]. Since a practical route
to CAD first reconstructs a point cloud and then fits sur-
faces [3], [8], the resulting errors take two forms: displaced
points bias the fitted surface parameters, whereas sparse or
missing regions leave surface extents and intersections poorly
constrained. Addressing these challenges requires solving
two problems with different operations: correcting displaced
points, which cannot recover an absent surface, and recover-
ing missing regions, whose generated points introduce shape
hypotheses that must be constrained by the available evidence.
Our key observation is that a region missing from the fused
point cloud is not necessarily unobserved. Matching, filtering,
arXiv:2611.32835v1  [cs.CV]  24 Nov 2026


and fusion may discard geometry for which individual views
still contain useful cues, and the original views retain appear-
ance, depth disagreement, and viewing directions that the fused
point set does not fully represent. Point cloud denoising and
completion methods that operate on coordinates alone [9]–[11]
cannot revisit this evidence, whereas image-guided completion
exploits complementary image information [12], [13].
This observation assigns two distinct roles to the original
observations. For existing samples, disagreement across views
exposes biased points, which can be corrected locally and
filtered when they remain unreliable. For missing regions, the
views indicate where a surface is supported and where space
is free, so they can constrain a learned shape prior wherever
they remain informative, while the prior supplies hypotheses
only for the truly unobserved portions. We therefore decouple
the correction of reconstructed samples from missing-shape
inference and provide both stages with the original multi-
view evidence: the first improves the reliability of the observed
support, and the second extends its coverage by observation-
conditioned generation.
We instantiate this design in Aqua2CAD (Fig. 1). An
observation-guided local regressor aggregates view-associated
image and geometric evidence and predicts bounded displace-
ments in a geometric vector basis, and a separate quality
classifier filters the corrected points. A multi-view assisted
completion model combines the shape features of the filtered
points with global image tokens, spatial ray evidence, and
local projected features to generate additional points in the
metric frame of the reconstruction. The resulting geometry
supports analytic surface fitting and finite-solid assembly.
Since the CAD stage partitions points into face groups with
ParSeNet [14] followed by manual revision, we treat CAD
recovery as a human-assisted downstream test of the recovered
geometry and do not claim fully automatic image-to-CAD
reconstruction.
Our contributions are:
• A view-associated correction mechanism that retains per-
view evidence after stereo fusion and uses it for bounded
geometric updates and quality filtering.
• A multi-view assisted completion model that conditions
a learned shape prior on global image context, spatial
ray evidence, and local projected features to extend the
corrected points into missing regions.
• An evaluation on an OceanSim-based synthetic bench-
mark and on real underwater captures, covering geometric
accuracy and coverage, component analyses, and the
downstream CAD models recovered with a shared fitting
backend and human-revised face grouping.
II. RELATED WORK
Aqua2CAD lies at the intersection of three lines of work.
Underwater 3D reconstruction provides metric geometry and
the original observations, point cloud refinement corrects and
completes geometry, and CAD reconstruction turns geometry
into editable models.
a) Underwater and multi-view 3D reconstruction.: Un-
derwater methods improve stereo through synthetic training
and self-supervised adaptation [5] or model the water medium
in neural scene representations [15]–[17]. Multi-view stereo
fuses per-view depth maps after visibility and consistency
checks that keep or discard samples with fixed rules [18]–
[21], and Point-MVSNet [22] refines depth with multi-view
point features. These methods target dense surfaces or novel
views, and the per-view evidence used during fusion is no
longer available once the point cloud is formed. We retain
this evidence after fusion and reuse it to correct points and to
infer missing geometry.
b) Point cloud denoising and completion.: Learned de-
noisers move noisy samples toward the underlying surface
using a learned score [9], iterative filtering [23], straight-
line displacement trajectories [24], structural priors [25], or
deterministic diffusion [26], and Noise2Score3D [27] learns
without clean targets. Completion methods recover missing
structure through geometry-aware transformers [10] or by
jointly predicting completed points and assembly-ready primi-
tives [11]. These methods operate on coordinates alone. Image
guidance has been added to both tasks: joint bilateral upsam-
pling [28] and stereo pipelines [29] use color and confidence
to refine depth or points, P2P-Bridge [30] conditions each
point on RGB and DINOv2 [31] features, and ViPC [12],
GAPM [13], EGIInet [32], and IAET [33] combine a partial
cloud with a single reference image. In these methods, image
features are attached to points or drawn from one view, without
the per-view depth and viewing rays of calibrated multi-view
capture. Our correction keeps the evidence tied to each viewing
ray, and our completion conditions a shape prior on calibrated
multi-view evidence at global, ray, and local scales.
c) CAD reconstruction from point clouds and images.:
Point-based CAD reconstruction predicts B-rep primitives and
their incidence [34], fits analytic and neural surfaces and
recovers edges and topology by intersection [3], or translates
points into executable CAD code [35]. Image-based methods
generate parametric CAD models from images [36], [37], and
BrepGaussian [38] recovers B-reps from multi-view images
through Gaussian splatting and labeled point sampling. In
fitting-based pipelines, sample placement determines the sur-
face parameters, whereas coverage constrains surface extents
and intersections, so their results depend on input quality that
underwater stereo does not guarantee. Rather than proposing
a new CAD backend, we improve the input geometry and
measure its downstream utility with a shared fitting backend.

b) Local refinement and metric output.: Ray evidence
indicates where a surface is supported but does not fix the
exact position of each generated point on it. The decoded
points are therefore projected back into the V views to gather
local image, depth, and ray evidence, and an attention-based
refiner updates their coordinates while down-weighting views
that confidently place a point behind an observed surface.
A final patch-height head displaces generated points along
normals estimated from nearby observed support, falling back
to normals of the generated patch when this support is in-
sufficient. The model is initialized from the UniCo backbone,
trained on the corrected and filtered inputs with the external
point and image encoders frozen, and minimizes the symmetric
squared Chamfer distance between the union of retained and
generated points and samples of the training CAD surfaces
(Appendix A-C). The Ng generated points are mapped back
by N −1(q) = sRTq + c and appended to Pr, preserving the
metric frame without post-hoc alignment. Although the obser-
vations condition shape inference, the generated points remain
hypotheses, particularly in regions without direct support.
c) CAD fitting and finite-solid assembly.: We partition
the recovered point cloud into face-instance groups with
ParSeNet [14] and manually revise the groups so that each
represents a single surface patch. This is the only human-
assisted step of the pipeline (Appendix B-C). A Point2CAD-
derived backend [3] fits each group with the best-scoring ana-
lytic surface among planes, cylinders, cones, spheres, and tori,
and estimates its finite extent from the recovered points and the
multi-view observation support. The resulting solid primitives
are combined by Boolean union and cavity subtraction in
Open CASCADE [44], ranked by point support, and refined
by observation-supported member repair (Appendix D-D). An
accepted output is a closed, positive-volume B-rep solid that
survives STEP and native B-rep round trips and has explicit
surface geometry and face–edge topology that standard CAD
software can edit.
IV. EXPERIMENTS
A. Datasets and Implementation Details
a) Synthetic data.:
Our synthetic dataset, built with
OceanSim [45], contains 185 CAD objects in three groups
of increasing geometric and structural complexity: 75 simple
pipes and joints (Layer1), 75 complex assemblies such as
pipe networks and support frames (Layer2), and 35 industrial
parts from the ABC dataset [46] (Layer3). Each object is
rendered against five largely featureless seabed backgrounds,
such as sand and gravel, along two randomized trajectories
per background, under moderate scattering combined with ran-
domly sampled moderate or severe motion blur. Each sequence
contains 100 calibrated stereo pairs at 960 × 540 resolution,
and the source CAD models provide metric reference surfaces
for supervision and evaluation. We split the objects into 148
training, 18 validation, and 19 test objects with 1,472, 180, and
190 sequences, respectively. The test split follows the group
proportions with 8, 8, and 3 objects from Layer1, Layer2, and
Layer3. All learned components are trained in stages on this
split.
b) Real data.: We fabricated six objects by 3D printing
their design CAD models: a 90° elbow, a hollow cylinder, a
pipe network, a pipeline end termination (PLET), a T-joint,
and a Y-joint. The objects were placed in a water pool and
recorded with the stereo camera of an underwater remotely
operated vehicle (ROV) along several trajectories of 100 stereo
pairs each. The design models serve only as unregistered visual
references, so the evaluation is qualitative.
c) Implementation details.: The completion model uses
V = 8 views and generates Ng = 8,192 points. Appendix A-A
lists the other hyperparameters, their selection, and the training
settings.
B. Evaluation Metrics
a) Point clouds.: We report the bidirectional Chamfer
distance (CD, in mm) between output points and the reference
CAD surface, together with the F-scores F5 and F20 at 5
and 20 mm. For missing-region recovery, reference samples
farther than 20 mm from the completion input form the missing
set, and Rτ is the fraction of this set lying within τ mm of
the output. We report R5 and R20. Exact definitions and the
aggregation over objects are given in Appendix B-A.
b) CAD models.: An output is closed if it is a positive-
volume solid that survives STEP and native B-rep round trips.
A closed output passes exterior acceptance if its mean surface
distance to the reference (CAD CD), its two-sided surface
coverage, its largest dimension error, and its volume error
all fall within fixed tolerances relative to the reference size
(Appendix B-B).
C. Reconstruction Results and Comparisons
On the 190 test sequences of 19 objects, Aqua2CAD
improves both the accuracy and the coverage of the recovered
geometry (Table IV, first and fourth rows). Relative to the
foreground-input reconstruction, it reduces CD from 16.29 to
6.48 mm (about 60%) and raises F5 from 41.75% to 61.40%
and F20 from 82.86% to 94.31%. On the 170 sequences
of 17 objects with nonempty missing regions, it achieves
R5 = 24.16% and R20 = 83.54% (qualitative results and
failure modes in Appendices E and D-E).

Before quality filtering, our local regressor raises F5 from
43.62% to 51.21% relative to PGD, the strongest coordinate-
only baseline on all three metrics (Table I). Quality filtering
further reduces CD from 14.82 to 10.95 mm on the same
subset. Visually assisted P2P-Bridge attains the lowest CD
among the external denoisers, whereas our regressor achieves a
higher F5. With quality filtering, our method obtains the best
CD and F-scores in this comparison. Appendix C-B details
baseline training, checkpoint selection, and test coverage.
b) Comparison with completion methods.: With identical
filtered inputs, our multi-view assisted completion achieves the
highest F5 and R5 among the compared methods (Table II).
All methods are trained on the 1,472 in-domain sequences:
EGIInet [32] and IAET [33] each receive one underwater im-
age, point-only UniCo [11] receives coordinates only, and our
model uses eight views. Point-only UniCo and our model use
the same training data and training budget, so their comparison
reflects the added multi-view conditioning. Relative to point-
only UniCo, our model improves F5 by 1.24 and R5 by 7.89
percentage points, whereas UniCo retains a slightly lower CD
and higher F20 and R20. Our gain therefore concentrates on
recovery at the tighter 5 mm tolerance. Our R5 is higher on
14 of the 17 objects with missing regions, and Fig. 5 shows
an example in which both methods cover a similar extent of
the missing surface at 20 mm, but our points lie much closer
to it (see Fig. 7 for a second example).
Both point-only UniCo and our model outperform the two
single-image methods on all five metrics. Appendix C-C gives
the input and training protocols, and Appendix C-D reports an
adapted SuperPC [47].

E. Ablation Studies and Analysis
a) Component contributions.: Table IV isolates the suc-
cessive contributions of local regression, quality filtering, and
completion on all 190 test sequences. Regression improves
the placement of existing samples and raises F5 from 41.75%
to 52.01%. Quality filtering further reduces CD from 14.98
to 10.53 mm, and completion extends the retained geometry,
raising F5 to 61.40% and F20 to 94.31%. The pipeline
thus improves the reliability of the observed support before
extending its coverage. When foreground-input, corrected, and
filtered points are passed through the same frozen completion
checkpoint, upstream correction and filtering raise the final
F5 from 50.65% to 61.40% (Appendix D-C), confirming that
completion benefits from a more reliable input. Among the
regressor ablations, restricting the regressor to a single valid
view per point causes the largest drop in F5, 8.94 percentage
points (Appendix D-A).
b) Completion components.: To isolate the parts of the
completion model, we remove one component at a time and
fine-tune each variant from the same initialization as the full
model, with identical data, loss, optimizer, and validation-
based selection (Table IV, Appendix D-B). Removing local
refinement causes the largest drop and halves R5 from 24.16%
to 12.35%, so generated points need view-guided adjustment
to lie on the surface. With a single view, R5 falls to 16.71%,
close to the 16.27% of point-only UniCo (Table II), which
attributes the gain in missing-region recovery to multi-view
evidence. Removing ray evidence lowers R5 by 2.5 and F5 by
1.8 percentage points, whereas removing global image context
changes every metric by about one point or less and slightly
raises R20, so it contributes least of the four components.
V. CONCLUSION
We presented Aqua2CAD, which uses multi-view obser-
vations both to correct reconstructed underwater points and
to condition the inference of missing geometry. On synthetic
data, it improves point accuracy and coverage, and with a com-
mon human-assisted CAD backend, exterior acceptance rises
from 6/19 for the foreground-input reconstruction to 14/19.
On pool captures of six 3D-printed objects, the recovered
CAD models reproduce their main structure. Face grouping
remains human-assisted, and surface selection and finite-solid
assembly remain bottlenecks. Automating these steps is a
natural next step toward fully automatic underwater image-
to-CAD reconstruction.

APPENDIX A
IMPLEMENTATION DETAILS
A. Reconstruction and Training
a) Reconstruction.: Of the 185 × 5 × 2 = 1,850 planned
synthetic sequences, six training sequences failed rendering
or acquisition checks and two failed SfM registration. Camera
motion is estimated from all 100 stereo pairs of a sequence
with COLMAP [39]. To reduce computation, Foundation-
Stereo [40] processes 50 uniformly spaced pairs at the original
resolution with 32 iterations.
b) Training.: The local regressor is trained from scratch
with AdamW, a learning rate of 10−3, and a weight decay of
10−4. Each batch contains four sequences with 256 randomly
sampled points per sequence, and object-balanced weighting
compensates for unequal sequence counts. After the regressor
is frozen, the HGB classifier is trained on statistics of the
original observations, with labels defined by the proximity of
the corrected points to the training surfaces, and a retention
threshold of 0.7. The multi-view assisted completion model
is initialized from UniCo trained on the full training split
and then trained on the corrected and filtered point clouds.
The three components are trained in this order, so that each
downstream component learns from the outputs of the trained
upstream stages.
c) Hyperparameters.:
The regressor associates each
point with up to K = 16 views and caps the normalized
per-view displacement at cδ = 4. The displacement radius
ri in Eq. (5) uses λr = 1.4 and [rmin, rmax] = [2, 12] mm,
which gave a higher F5 than 1–6 mm in an earlier design
study (Appendix F-B). The quality classifier labels corrected
points within ϵq = 20 mm of the training surface as reliable,
matching the F20 tolerance, and retains points with τq = 0.7,
selected on validation data. The completion model samples
Nin = 2,048 input points, uses V = 8 views chosen greedily
to cover distinct camera directions, and generates Ng = 8,192
points, 16 around each of its 512 anchors. Ray discrepancies
use σmin = 3 mm and ρmax = 8.

B. CAD Evaluation
Table III varies the input point clouds while keeping the

RL in Sec. C-E.
C. Human-Assisted Face Grouping
Face grouping is the only manual step of the pipeline
(Sec. III-C0c). An annotator revises the groups in a browser
tool that displays the recovered point cloud, colored by its
ParSeNet groups, together with ten key frames of the under-
water video. The tool runs no fitting and exposes no reference
geometry, reference face labels, surface types, or parameters.
The annotator merges groups that belong to the same surface
and splits groups that span several surfaces by selecting points
with polygon or brush tools. Selection can be restricted to the
visible surface, and completed points can be hidden during
selection but are never removed. Surface types and coaxial
relations can optionally be specified. When they are left empty,
the fitter estimates the surface types as in the automatic setting.
Every edit is saved with undo and a revision history, and a
sequence enters reconstruction only after it is marked complete
and submitted as an immutable snapshot that preserves the
coordinates, order, and identities of all points. For the 17
real-world trajectories in Sec. IV-D, the annotator changed
the ParSeNet grouping on 14 and confirmed it unchanged on
three, yielding 109 face groups. No optional surface types or
relations were entered.

A. Comparison Setup
All point-cloud stages are evaluated on the same 190 test
sequences and reference surfaces. For the denoising compar-

retains its unsupervised training objective. We evaluate both
coordinate-only P2P-Bridge and a visually assisted variant that
conditions each point on RGB and DINOv2 [31] features from
eight underwater views. Coordinate-only baselines receive
point coordinates, whereas our regressor additionally uses
the associated multi-view evidence. The comparison therefore
controls the in-domain training data while each method retains
its own inputs and initialization. Coordinate-only P2P-Bridge
requires at least 512 input points and therefore excludes ten se-
quences of one test object, whereas the visual variant supports
all 190 sequences. DPCD has one additional patch-stitching
failure, so the matched denoising comparison uses 179 test
sequences of 18 objects. For SuperPC [47], we reconstruct


the unreleased training components from the paper and the re-
leased code while retaining the released visual architecture and
pretrained initialization. We refer to this version as SuperPC
(adapted) and describe it in Sec. C-D. The same SuperPC
checkpoint, fine-tuned on the 1,472 filtered-input training
sequences and selected on completion validation, is evaluated
in both settings under the protocols of Tables I and II: its native
2,048-point prediction from foreground inputs, and its native
output appended to the full filtered input. Section C-D reports
both results. Sections C-B and C-C report test coverage and
baseline training details.
B. Denoising Baselines
a) Coordinate-only
baselines.:
StraightPCF,
IterativePFN,
and
coordinate-only
P2P-Bridge
are
fine-

the released weights with a fresh optimizer. Target points
are the nearest projections of the training observations onto
their training CAD surfaces. Training patches contain actual
stereo reconstruction errors, and no synthetic Gaussian noise
is added. When an input contains fewer points than the
training patch size, observed indices are repeated together
with their paired targets. Each adaptation retains the complete
released network and its losses: StraightPCF uses two velocity
modules with two internal iterations, IterativePFN retains its
four modules, and P2P-Bridge uses its full coordinate-based
model with five inference steps. Each epoch samples eight
patches per sequence with a batch size of eight. We train
for at most 100 epochs, validate every five epochs, and
stop after ten validation checks without improvement. The
learning rates are 10−5 for StraightPCF and IterativePFN
and 3 × 10−5 for P2P-Bridge. The former two use Adam,
whereas P2P-Bridge uses AdamW with a weight decay of
10−5, gradient clipping at 1, and mixed precision. Batches
with non-finite mixed-precision outputs are replayed in full
precision.
Checkpoint selection maximizes the object-averaged valida-
tion F5 under a fixed reference-sample distance proxy, subject
to input-relative constraints: CD at most 1.02 times, recall at
most 0.01 below, and the 95th-percentile point-to-reference
distance at most 1.05 times the corresponding input values. If
no checkpoint satisfies these constraints, the checkpoint with
the highest validation F5 is selected. This proxy is used only

Sec. B-A. StraightPCF reaches the 100-epoch limit and selects

the fallback rule selects the released weights for both.
Pairing observations with surface targets requires three
method-specific adaptations. For StraightPCF, a zero-residual
loss ratio is replaced by its continuous limit, leaving all
nonzero divisions unchanged. IterativePFN uses n paired
projected targets instead of its original oversampled clean
target set while retaining its internal noisy intermediate tar-
gets, whose noise scale is computed from the observation-
to-projection residual. P2P-Bridge uses mean and maximum-
radius normalization of the observed patch with index-paired
projected targets, and retains its released online-weight infer-

patches of min(1000, n) points without padding, where n is
the number of input points. P2P-Bridge uses min(2048, n)
points and marks inputs with n < 512 as unsupported. The ten
such test sequences belong to one industrial part and remain in
the 190-sequence coverage denominator. The same rule yields
170 supported validation sequences for P2P-Bridge, whereas
the other two methods use all 180. Over all 190 test sequences,
StraightPCF obtains 16.49 mm CD, 44.59% F5, and 82.33%
F20, and IterativePFN obtains 17.48 mm, 42.91%, and 80.92%.

E. Failure Modes
a) Large missing regions.: Completion performs poorly
when most of the surface is absent from its input. We assign
a test sequence to the large-gap group if more than 50% of
its reference surface samples lie farther than 20 mm from its
completion input. This holds for 22 of the 190 sequences:
all 20 sequences of two ROV cages, one Y-joint sequence,
and one industrial part. On these sequences, the final model
recovers only 1.76% of the missing surface within 5 mm and
38.50% within 20 mm, compared with 27.18% and 89.31%
on the remaining sequences, and its CD rises to 32.91 mm
(Table VIII). With so little observed support, the multi-view
evidence constrains only a small part of the shape, and
the generated points cover the missing region coarsely and
incompletely. In the CAD comparison of Table III, both large-
gap sequences, the two ROV cages, fail exterior acceptance for
all three inputs.
b) CAD failures.: Five of the 19 completed inputs still
fail exterior acceptance (Table III and Fig. 10). Figure 9
includes the sequence with the largest gap, a Y-joint with
91.84% of its reference samples farther than 20 mm from
the completion input, and the sequence with the largest CAD
error, an ROV cage. With manually revised face groups, these
failures reflect the recovered geometry, surface selection, and
finite-solid assembly rather than segmentation errors.

C. CAD Backend Diagnostics
A separate 19-object study used fixed earlier point clouds
and automatic ParSeNet [14] groups with a Point2CAD-
derived fitting backend [3] and Open CASCADE [44] as-
sembly. Two variants relabel completion points with uniform
or observation-derived neighborhood weights, retaining the
groups of observed points and requiring strong local consen-
sus. Table XI compares these automatic settings with reference
face groups. This study uses input point clouds and settings
different from those of Table III, but the same closure and
exterior acceptance criteria (Sec. IV-B). Correct grouping
alone does not resolve all fitting and assembly errors. The
study involves no manual editing and precedes the current
point-cloud pipeline.
We also evaluated BrepGaussian [38] on six objects, two
per shape group. Each object uses 50 underwater left images,
stereo-SfM camera poses, 30,000 geometry-and-edge opti-
mization steps, and 15,000 segmentation optimization steps.
Masks receive no manual correction, and the surface fitter
exports solids through Open CASCADE. One of the six ob-
jects yields a valid closed solid, and none passes the complete
acceptance checks of that study, which include observed-input
coverage and volume consistency after STEP round trips.
This automatic six-object study does not provide a matched
comparison with the current human-assisted pipeline.
