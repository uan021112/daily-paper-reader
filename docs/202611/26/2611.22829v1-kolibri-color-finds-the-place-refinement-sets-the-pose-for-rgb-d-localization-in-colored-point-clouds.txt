Abstract 

An agent or robot in a scanned building must locate its camera in rooms that no model saw during training. Point cloud registration methods can align each RGB-D frame with the building’s colored point cloud, accurately but at a high cost. We observe that geometric refinement brings nearby initial poses to the same final pose, so the learned component only needs to find the place. Kolibri does this with a graph network of 55,712 parameters that describes 10 cm cubes of points, called cells, by their color, local shape and neighbors. With the same refinement, it matches GeDi on 96 unseen ScanNet rooms (81.4% against 80.3% within 5 cm and 5°) and on a held-out test of 10 further rooms, in 82 ms per frame on an RTX 2080 Ti. The saved time allows for search without a room label. In one point cloud of 100 rooms, Kolibri loses 1.3 points against knowing the room, while retrieving the room first loses 56.0. In a hospital emergency department scene from a real scan, it localizes 86.5% of the frames rendered from the scan in 384 ms each, where GeDi needs 91 minutes per frame on cubes of the scan for a similar recall. The method needs colors that agree between the frame and the point cloud. On gray frames and across cameras with different color responses, point cloud registration methods keep the lead.

Each view forms a RGB-D frame, with paired color with depth, so point cloud registration methods can align it with the point cloud. The difficulty is cost. On views rendered from the scan with sim-ulated depth noise, GeDi [33] needs 91 minutes per frame to search overlapping cubes of the scan, while Kolibri, the method we present here, reaches a similar recall on the whole scan in 384 ms. The rendered colors agree with the point cloud by construction, and the baseline comparison uses 30 frames, one per walk (Sec. 4.5). The hospital scan motivated the method but remained untouched while we developed it on public benchmarks. A descriptor summarizes the shape or color around a point as a vector, so that the same surface seen twice can be matched. Point cloud registration methods spend time computing these descriptors and searching for consistent matches. Even in a single room, BUFFER-X [39] takes a median 1.4 s per frame and GeDi 24 s on an RTX 2080 Ti when assigned to align overlapping fragments. At building scale, searching separate rooms or cubes repeats that work. Image retrieval can narrow the search, but choosing the wrong room loses the frame before pose estimation begins. Among 100 ScanNet rooms, retrieval ranks the true room first for only 28.6% of frames. Our starting point is that finding the place and refining the pose need different levels of accuracy (Fig. 2). Point-to-plane refinement adjusts a pose by pulling frame points onto nearby surfaces of the point cloud. It is a variant of iterative closest point (ICP) [3, 8]. Nearby initial poses often converge to the same result, within a region we call the refinement basin. On real ScanNet frames, Kolibri and BUFFER-X both start in this region for 62% of frames. Refinement gives the same success or failure outcome on 99% of those frames (Fig. 4a). The learned component therefore needs to find candidate places close enough for refinement. Color helps: adding color to Fast Point Feature Histograms (FPFH) [36], a geometric descriptor, doubles recall at 5 cm with our pose estimator (Tab. 4). Kolibri uses a graph network of 55,712 parameters to describe cells, 10 cm cubes of pooled points, from their color, local shape and neighbors (Fig. 3). The point cloud is described once and reused. Groups of consistent cell matches propose candidate poses, refinement adjusts them, and a geometric fit score selects the result. Color proposes the place and geometry decides, as color aids coarse scene recognition in humans [27]. This division of work needs colors that agree between the frame and point cloud up to a change in brightness and contrast. On gray frames, RGB GeoTransformer, a point cloud registration method with color input, has 43 points higher recall than Kolibri at 5 cm. Point cloud registration methods also achieve higher recall in a laser scan colored by a different camera (Sec. 4.6). Our contributions are: • A division of RGB-D localization into place finding and pose refinement, supported by measurements of when different initial poses give the same refinement outcome. • Kolibri, a small graph network that finds the place from color. With matched refinement, it is competitive with point cloud registration methods on unseen rooms and a held-out test, at a small fraction of their cost. • Search without a room label. Kolibri searches 100 rooms within 1.3 points of its room-given recall, and a hospital emergency department in 384 ms per frame. 

2. Related Work 

Point cloud registration. Point cloud registration methods combine descriptors with an estimator that rejects inconsis-tent matches [14, 46]. Descriptors progressed from hand-crafted local geometry [36] to learned representations and matchers [1, 18, 33, 34, 48, 49], including methods that trans-fer across sensors and scales [39]. Colored ICP [31] and Col-orPCR [25] use color during alignment. These methods start anew for each pair. Our reference point cloud is known in advance, so its descriptors can be reused. The layer exchang-ing features between neighboring cells, following Edge-Conv [45], only needs to find starting poses for refinement. 

Scene-specific relocalization. Scene coordinate regres-sion trains a model for each scene to predict the scene po-sition of each image pixel [4, 6, 40]. It is accurate on 7-Scenes [40] and 12-Scenes [43], where uncertainty in the reference poses limits measurable accuracy [5]. 

Relocalization in unseen scenes. Other methods predict scene coordinates or relative poses without training on the query scene. They use reference images with known poses or spatial coordinates [11, 35, 41, 47]. MeshLoc [30] matches 2against rendered mesh views, while general-purpose models of scene geometry match image pairs [22, 44]. Our depth input instead supports direct matching to an existing point cloud. MASt3R and Reloc3r, which relate the query to reference images, have lower recall than Kolibri when those images are rendered from the point cloud (Sec. 4.2). 

Localization at building scale. A common approach re-trieves likely places with an image descriptor [2, 28], then estimates the pose within them [37]. Place recognition from laser scans retrieves the matching region of a large point cloud [20, 42]. Image-to-point-cloud methods also use retrieval or restrict the search to regions near known poses [21, 23, 32]. Systems that estimate scene geometry and camera poses together [19, 26] solve a different task from localization in a prior scan. Kolibri searches all rooms together, without an earlier room decision that can discard the correct place. 

3. Method 

3.1. Task, settings and metric 

The inputs are an RGB-D frame with known camera intrin-sics and a colored metric point cloud S known in advance. The output is the camera pose T ∈ SE (3) , a rigid rotation and translation that places the frame in S. With the room given , S contains only the frame’s room (Tabs. 2 and 3). With the room unknown , it joins rooms or covers a hospi-tal emergency department (Secs. 4.4 and 4.5). Recall is the share of frames whose position and rotation errors both fall below a threshold pair: 5 cm and 5°, 0.25 m and 2°, or 0.5 m and 5°. A frame without a pose counts as a miss. No evaluation room or scan enters our training. Kolibri first describes cells in the frame and point cloud, then groups consistent cell matches into candidate poses, and finally refines and scores those poses (Fig. 3). Only the cell descriptor is learned. The point cloud descriptors are computed once and reused. 

3.2. Cell descriptors 

Color distinguishes places even at a coarse scale. We back-project the frame’s depth into space and pool its points into cubic cells of side 10 cm. Each cell i stores its points’ mean position pi and color ci. Links to its k = 16 nearest cells form the neighborhood N (i). The cell normal ni points in the direction of least spread among offsets to these neigh-bors, given by the smallest-eigenvalue eigenvector of their covariance. The input feature xi contains eight values: color centered on mid-gray, the mean and variance of log edge lengths, and linearity, planarity and scattering from the same covariance. The edge feature eij contains its log length and the absolute cosines between the offset and each normal, and between the normals. Absolute values remove sensitivity to normal orientation. A message-passing layer [15] lets each cell combine its features with those of its neighbors. It has width 128 and produces the descriptor fi:

3.3. Candidate poses and refinement 

Each frame cell is matched to its 5 most similar point cloud cells. A softmax converts sia over all point cloud cells into match weights. Matches (i, a ) and (j, b ) are consistent if their distances agree: ∥pi − pj ∥ − ∥ qa − qb∥ < ρ , where 

qa and qb are point cloud cell positions and ρ = 25 cm. The 256 matches with the most consistent partners each seed a 

consensus set , an idea from the spatial-consistency estimator SC 2-PCR [9]. This set contains the starting match and its most consistent partners, one in 10 of all matches and at least 8. A rigid fit weighted by the match weights gives a candidate pose. Repeated furniture can produce a large group of consis-tent matches at the wrong place. The estimator therefore keeps several distinct candidates. It retains the 4 best first-round candidates separated by at least 0.5 m or 10°. It re-moves matches explained by these poses, with residuals below 0.5 m, then makes 2 further rounds. Each round adds its best candidate if distinct and supported by at least 50% as many agreeing matches, or inliers, as the best first candidate. There are at most 6 candidates. Point-to-plane ICP refines each candidate using 1,000 frame points sampled at even steps through the frame. 3.4. Operating points 

The pipeline above is our standard setting, Fast . Two op-tional operations trade time for accuracy or tolerance to color changes, without retraining. Precise adds a tighter second refinement to every candidate. It uses point-to-plane ICP with Tukey weights, which suppress large residuals, at scale 5 cm and a radius that shrinks from 10 cm. The color pass 

adds another reading of the frame. A shared affine transform of its color channels matches its luminance mean and spread to those of the point cloud. Both the original and adjusted colors go through the full pipeline. The fit score selects the adjusted reading only if it fits strictly better. Unless stated otherwise, Kolibri uses Fast with one color reading. 

3.5. Training 

Training uses 170 rooms, disjoint from every evaluation set: 165 ScanNet rooms [10] and 5 spaces in a university build-ing. Color and depth views rendered along walks provide the training frames. No real camera frame enters training. Augmentations include depth noise that grows with squared range, changes in scale, brightness, contrast and white bal-ance, and missing sectors. For each frame cell, the positive match is the point cloud cell nearest its true position, if within 0.5 m. The loss is cross-entropy over the softmax matching weights. Training uses AdamW with learning rate 0.001 for 18,600 steps of 8 views, taking at most 53 minutes on an RTX 4090.

Baselines. The point cloud registration methods are BUFFER-X [39] and its released light setting BUFFER-X-Lite, GeDi [33], ColorPCR [25], RoITr [48], PREDA-TOR [18], FPFH with the robust estimator TEASER++ [36, 46], and RGB GeoTransformer. Statistics and timing. A walk is a run of consecutive frames. Recall differences carry 95% intervals from a boot-strap that resamples whole walks, pairs methods on the same frames, and averages over three training seeds. The ± en-tries in Tabs. 2, 3 and 5 give interval half-widths. Methods are equivalent when the 90% interval lies within ±3 recall points. A method leads when the 95% interval excludes zero in its favor. Equivalence and a lead can both hold. A compar-ison meeting neither criterion is inconclusive . Resampling rooms or seeds gives the same verdicts, and the largest stan-dard deviation of Kolibri recall across seeds is 0.6 points. Unless stated otherwise, time and memory are medians over 200 fixed ScanNet-10 frames, processed individually on four CPU threads and an RTX 2080 Ti. Kolibri and GeDi reuse point cloud descriptors. Other baselines run as released. 

4.2. Room given: accuracy and cost 

With the room given and refinement matched, Fast localizes 81.4% of ScanNet-96 frames within 5 cm and 5°, against GeDi’s 80.3% (Tab. 2). The methods are equivalent at this threshold, and Fast leads BUFFER-X. Across both ScanNet-96 sets, all four operating points and all three thresholds, none of the 240 matched-refinement comparisons shows a significant deficit for Kolibri. Development-set compar-isons are also equivalent, in favor of Kolibri, or inconclusive (Tab. 3). Fast takes 82 ms per frame, while BUFFER-X takes 18 times as long. It also uses less GPU memory than BUFFER-X and GeDi. Even after varying their settings, no baseline reaches Fast ’s ScanNet-10 recall in less than 15 times its time (Fig. 1b). The held-out test supports the same result on rooms un-used in any design choice. On its sensor frames, Precise 

reaches 73.1% within 5 cm and 5°, against 72.6% for GeDi with the same refinement stages. They are equivalent, as are 

Fast and GeDi with the shared refinement at this threshold. On rendered views, Precise is equivalent to ColorPCR within 0.5 m and 5° and leads GeDi and BUFFER-X. 4.3. Why a small network is enough 

Refinement reduces the accuracy needed from the learned descriptor. The success rate falls to half at an initial posi-tion error of 7.7 cm (Fig. 4a), which defines the measured basin boundary. Even when Fast ’s best initial candidate lies outside this basin, another candidate rescues 51% of frames, against 21% for BUFFER-X. Increasing capacity has little effect over the tested range. From 4,736 to 915,584 param-eters, recall stays within 1.0 point of our network on the ablation rooms (Fig. 4b). Training on 22 rooms also gives recall equivalent to training on all 170 (Fig. 4c). What matters is the information exchanged between cells (Tab. 4). With Precise on the ablation rooms, removing message passing costs 13.4 points, and removing color costs 26.2. Keeping only color on the same graph loses 3.4 points, so local geometry still matters. GeDi’s descriptor uses 56 times as many parameters and localizes more frames before refinement. That advantage disappears after refinement. 

4.4. Search without a room label 

To separate search scale from changes in scene geometry, we place rooms apart on a grid with pitch 20 m. They share no walls. The ten-room and seven-room point clouds join ScanNet-10 and 7-Scenes respectively. The largest joins all 100 rooms from which ScanNet-96 is drawn, totaling 15.0 million points. Retrieval references use the image descriptor DINOv2 [28] to rank rooms against 64 rendered views per room. Retrieve-3 + BUFFER-X registers in the top three rooms. Retrieve-1 + Fast runs Kolibri in the top room. On the ten-room point cloud, Fast reaches 74.3% within 5 cm in 99 ms per frame (Figs. 1 and 5a). Its recall is equiva-lent to BUFFER-X given the true room. Retrieval includes the true room in its top three for only 80.8% of frames. Retrieve-3 + BUFFER-X therefore trails Fast by 14.0 points and takes 47 times as long. Searching the 100-room point cloud gives 79.9% recall, only 1.3 points below a paired run given the true room. That reference is separate from the room-given run in Tab. 2. Keeping several candidates is important: with one candidate, recall falls to 46.2%. 4.5. A hospital emergency department 

The emergency department returns to our motivating setting: many connected rooms captured on foot with a handheld laser scanner and colored by its camera. The colored point cloud spans 49 m by 42 m in plan and played no part in development or tuning. There is no RGB-D recording. The test therefore contains 440 RGB-D frames rendered from the scan along 30 walks, with simulated depth noise. Their colors agree with the point cloud by construction, which favors color-based methods, and their reference poses are exact. Kolibri searches the whole scan without a room label. Baselines take minutes per frame, so they use 30 frames, one from the start of each walk. 

Fast localizes 86.5% of all rendered frames within 5 cm and 5°, in 384 ms per frame on an RTX 2080 Ti (Fig. 6 and Tab. 6). GeDi on the whole point cloud reaches 26.7% on the baseline subset. Its random sample consensus (RANSAC) estimator fits poses from sampled matches, but ends with fewer than three agreeing matches on most frames.

4.6. Operating points and dependence on color 

The second refinement stage in Precise adds 35 ms per frame (Tab. 5) and gains 4.0 points at 5 cm on ScanNet-96. The 

color pass takes 2.3 times as long as Fast . Across the tested color changes, including brightness shifts and conversion to gray, it gains an average 10.7 points at 0.25 m and 2° on ScanNet-10 and 11.8 on 7-Scenes. These options do not remove the dependence on color. At the same threshold, RGB GeoTransformer leads Kolibri by 9.2 points averaged over the color shifts, and by 47.2 on gray frames. The same limitation appears across cameras. Bonn [29] pairs real ASUS Xtion frames with a terrestrial laser scan colored by the scanner. Fast reaches 26.3% within 0.5 m, or 47.2% with the color pass , against 91.5% for GeDi on cubes. GeDi takes 11 minutes per frame. Changes be-tween recording sessions affect Fast and the six strongest refined baselines alike: they lose 16 to 25 points at 5 cm on ScanNet-96-S2 (Tab. 2). 

5. Conclusion 

Kolibri uses color to find candidate places and geometric refinement to set the pose. With matched refinement, a small network reaches the accuracy of point cloud registration methods in single rooms at a fraction of their cost. The saved time makes it practical to search a point cloud spanning 100 rooms or a hospital emergency department without a room label. Agreement in color remains essential, and point cloud registration methods retain the lead when it breaks. Extending search to whole buildings calls for a hierarchy that reduces work without discarding the correct place before refinement. 8Author Contributions

S1. What color contributes 

Human scene recognition. People recognize scene cat-egories from brief glimpses [16, 38], using coarse visual structure before fine edges [38]. Color helps most at this coarse scale [7, 27], especially when a scene category has characteristic colors [27]. Recognizing a place and orienting within it engage different brain systems [12, 13]. Facing di-rection is represented relative to the local environment [24], and young children reorient using room shape even when a wall has a distinctive color [17]. This distinction has a parallel in Kolibri: color identifies candidate places, and geometry sets the pose. 

Which rooms depend on color? Color diversity differs between rooms. We measure it by the entropy of the point cloud cells’ chromatic coordinates, which describe color in-dependently of lightness. Across 29 rooms, greater diversity accompanies a larger drop in Fast recall when frames be-come gray (Fig. S1a). At 0.25 m and 2°, Spearman’s rank correlation ρ is +0.63, with a room-bootstrap interval of 

[+0 .38 , +0 .79] . The relationship also holds within ScanNet and 7-Scenes, with correlations of +0.66 and +0.75. For the gain from the color pass under color shifts, the corre-sponding correlation is ρ = −0.27 [ −0.66 , +0 .23] , with an interval that includes zero (Fig. S1b). 

Changes to depth and color. Figure S2 gives both panels of the damage tests from Sec. 4.6. Across 12 levels of point loss, depth noise, reduced resolution, range cuts and holes, Fast has no significant deficit against BUFFER-X or GeDi. At 0.25 m and 2°, its largest leads are 8.6 and 34.3 points respectively (Fig. S2a). Color changes expose the dependence on color (Fig. S2b). On gray frames, RGB GeoTransformer leads Kolibri by 47.2 points at the same threshold.

The size of the searched region. Table S5 separates GeDi’s descriptors from the size of its search. The descriptors work in the emergency department, and the whole-scan result is the cost of matching against a large point cloud. The cube search recovers recall by running many small searches and keeping the cube with the most agreeing matches.

S8. Camera and lens properties 

A deployed camera can differ from the development cameras in its lens and its calibration. We change one property of the query camera at a time on the 871 real ScanNet-10 frames and leave the colored point cloud unchanged (Tab. S7). A narrower lens keeps only the points inside a horizontal field of view of 45° or 30°, where the ScanNet depth camera sees 58°. A focal length 5 or 10% too long or too short scales each frame across the optical axis. Uncorrected radial distortion with coefficient k1 moves each point away from the image center for positive k1 (pincushion) or toward it for negative 

k1 (barrel), in proportion to its squared distance from the center. A color offset pairs each depth pixel with the color 5 or 10 pixels to its right. BUFFER-X and GeDi receive the same frames and the shared refinement. Calibration errors distort the frame itself, so they cost every method alike. With a focal length 10% too long, Fast 

localizes 30.3% of the frames within 0.25 m and 2°, GeDi 29.5% and BUFFER-X 30.3%, against 86.9% for Fast on unchanged frames. The place is still found: within 0.5 m and 5°, Fast keeps at least 90.3% at every focal error. The scaled frame cannot fit the point cloud closely, and the second refinement stage of Precise ends below Fast at all 4 focal errors. Distortion costs Fast at most 8.0 points. In 16 of 16 comparisons over the focal and distortion levels, the losses of Fast and of a baseline are equivalent within 3 points (Sec. 4.1). A color offset of up to 10 pixels changes Fast by at most 0.5 points. A narrow lens separates the methods.
