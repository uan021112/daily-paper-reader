## Abstract 

Vision-based 3D occupancy prediction infers voxel-wise ge-ometry and semantics from multi-view images, where 2D-to-3D view transformation is a crucial step. Many exist-ing methods adopt a backward-projection paradigm, pro-jecting uniformly distributed 3D reference points onto im-age planes to aggregate multi-view features. However, their scene-agnostic sampling patterns overlook the non-uniform distribution of scene geometry, allocating many ref-erences to empty space and reducing sampling efficiency under a fixed sampling budget. To address this challenge, we propose SAP , a scene-adaptive projection framework that leverages depth-based geometric priors to guide view transformation. Specifically, we back-project metric depth maps predicted by a frozen depth model into 3D space to obtain geometric priors. To leverage these priors, we de-sign a Scene-Adaptive Projection Encoder comprising two complementary components. First, Prior-Guided Refer-ence Generation constructs a hybrid reference set for fea-ture sampling, allocating references within prior-supported height regions and retaining full-range sampling where valid support is unavailable. Furthermore, to account for potential spatial misalignment caused by depth estima-tion errors, Radial-Structured Feature Aggregation explores multiple candidate locations through learned radial offsets to aggregate potentially displaced feature evidence. Exten-sive experiments on Occ3D demonstrate that SAP achieves state-of-the-art performance and consistently improves re-sults across diverse occupancy frameworks. 

## 1. Introduction 

3D occupancy prediction aims to infer voxel-wise geom-etry and semantics of the surrounding environment from multi-sensor observations, providing a unified scene rep-resentation for holistic perception and motion planning in autonomous driving [8, 24, 31, 50, 56]. Among different sensing modalities, vision-based occupancy prediction has attracted growing attention due to the rich semantic infor-mation and low deployment cost of cameras.

Despite recent advances, effectively transforming 2D image features into 3D space remains a critical challenge. Many existing methods [22, 23, 25, 49] follow a backward-projection paradigm, where predefined 3D reference points are projected onto image planes to aggregate multi-view features. As illustrated in Fig. 1(a), these methods typically adopt scene-agnostic reference patterns, applying the same sampling strategy across BEV locations regardless of the underlying scene geometry. However, real-world scenes ex-hibit highly non-uniform geometric distributions, with rele-vant structures occupying only a fraction of the predefined sampling space. Consequently, many reference points may fall into regions with limited geometric support, reducing sampling efficiency under a fixed reference budget. To address this challenge, we propose SAP, a scene-adaptive projection framework that leverages depth-based geometric priors to guide 2D-to-3D view transformation. Specifically, we employ a frozen depth prediction model to estimate metric depth maps, which are back-projected into 3D space to obtain geometric priors reflecting the spatial distribution of scene structures. These priors provide scene-adaptive geometric guidance for reference sampling. Based on this, we introduce a Scene-Adaptive Projection Encoder comprising two complementary components. We first introduce Prior-Guided Reference Generation (PGRG), which leverages geometric priors to construct a hybrid reference set. Specifically, PGRG identifies sup-1arXiv:2611.28629v1 [cs.CV] 21 Nov 2026 ported height intervals within each BEV pillar, preserv-ing potentially discontinuous and multi-layer vertical struc-tures. For pillars with valid prior support, references are al-located within these intervals, whereas full-range sampling is retained for BEV pillars without valid support. As illus-trated in Fig. 1(b), the resulting hybrid reference set concen-trates references in geometrically supported regions rather than empty space, enabling scene-adaptive sampling. While geometric priors provide valuable guidance, depth estimation errors can displace back-projected points along camera rays, introducing approximately radial spatial mis-alignment in BEV space. To account for this potential mis-alignment, we further introduce Radial-Structured Feature Aggregation (RSFA), which enables each BEV query to ex-plore multiple candidate locations through learned radial offsets and aggregate potentially displaced BEV features. In summary, our main contributions are as follows: • We propose SAP, a scene-adaptive projection framework that leverages depth-based geometric priors to guide 2D-to-3D view transformation. • We design a Scene-Adaptive Projection Encoder: PGRG exploits geometric priors to construct a scene-adaptive hybrid reference set, while RSFA aggregates potentially misaligned features through learned radial offsets. • Extensive experiments demonstrate that SAP achieves state-of-the-art performance on Occ3D and consistently improves results across different occupancy models. 

## 2. Related Work 

2.1. Vision-based 3D Occupancy Prediction 

Vision-based 3D occupancy prediction [40, 41, 44] aims to infer voxel-wise occupancy and semantics from multi-view images. Early methods [2, 14, 23, 46, 55] laid the foun-dation for learning dense 3D scene representations from images. Subsequent studies have focused on several key challenges, including annotation costs, computational ef-ficiency, and temporal modeling. To reduce reliance on dense 3D annotations, RenderOcc [35], SelfOcc [15], Occ-NeRF [54], GaussianOcc [9] and DeGO [10] exploit neu-ral radiance fields or 3D Gaussian representations to en-able self-supervised learning. LOcc [53] generates dense, fine-grained occupancy annotations through semantic tran-sitive labeling, while QueryOcc [26] learns a continuous 3D occupancy field using spatiotemporal queries. For efficient inference, FlashOcc [51] and FastOcc [12] re-place costly 3D processing with a channel-to-height trans-formation, whereas VoxFormer [21], SparseOcc [29] and OPUS [42] adopt sparse query-based representations to re-duce computation. StreamOcc [34], S2GO [36] and View-Former [18] further leverage historical observations through streaming architectures to enhance temporal feature aggre-gation. More recently, OccAny [3] and OccAnyScene [30] have explored generalizable occupancy prediction across diverse datasets. In contrast, our work focuses on injecting depth-based geometric priors into the view transformation. 

2.2. 2D-to-3D View Transformation 

Transforming 2D image features into 3D representations is fundamental to 3D perception tasks [13, 16, 17, 57]. Ex-isting approaches can be broadly categorized into forward and backward projection paradigms. Forward-projection methods [13, 19, 20, 37, 47], exemplified by LSS [37], es-timate per-pixel depth distributions to lift image features along camera rays and aggregate them into BEV represen-tations. In contrast, backward-projection methods [23, 28, 48], such as BEVFormer [23], project predefined 3D ref-erence points onto image planes to aggregate multi-view features. To leverage the complementary strengths of both paradigms, recent studies [22, 25, 58] have explored hy-brid strategies. FB-Occ [22] combines forward and back-ward projection to construct and refine spatial representa-tions, while COTR [33] integrates explicit and implicit view transformations to obtain compact 3D occupancy features. STCOcc [25] further explores occupancy-guided cascading refinement to enhance spatiotemporal 3D features. More recently, Dr.Occ [58] leverages foundation-model depth predictions to improve the geometric alignment of voxel features. Nevertheless, conventional backward-projection methods typically rely on predefined sampling patterns that do not explicitly adapt to the non-uniform distribution of scene geometry. In contrast, our method leverages geomet-ric priors to guide the 2D-to-3D view transformation.

3.2. BEV Encoding 

Given multi-view images, we first extract semantic image features F sem using a 2D encoder [11]. Following the LSS-based paradigm [37], a categorical depth distribution is pre-dicted for each image feature, based on which the features are lifted into 3D frustums and subsequently aggregated onto the BEV plane. This produces an initial BEV repre-sentation F BEV , which serves as the BEV queries for the subsequent Scene-Adaptive Projection Encoder.

3.5. Loss Function 

Following [5, 29], we supervise the query-based 3D decoder using classification, mask, and dice objectives. The total training loss is formulated as: 

L = λcls Lcls + λmask Lmask + λdice Ldice , (15) where Lcls is the cross-entropy loss for query classification, 

Lmask is the cross-entropy loss for occupancy mask predic-tion, and Ldice measures the overlap between predicted and ground-truth masks. We set the corresponding loss weights to λcls = 2 .0, λmask = 5 .0, and λdice = 5 .0.

## 4. Experiments 

4.1. Experimental Setup 

Datasets and Metrics. We evaluate our method on the nuScenes dataset [1], which consists of 700 training scenes and 150 validation scenes annotated at 2 Hz. Based on nuScenes, Occ3D [40] provides voxel-level occupancy an-notations within a spatial range of [−40 , 40] m along the 

X and Y axes and [−1, 5.4] m along the Z axis, with a voxel size of 0.4 m. The benchmark defines 18 seman-tic classes, where occupied voxels are categorized into 17 semantic classes, including 16 common categories and an additional “others” class. Following prior works [5, 25], we report mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) for models trained with the camera visibility mask, and both mIoU and RayIoU for those trained without the mask. 

Implementation Details. We develop two variants, SAP-mini and SAP, both based on the ALOcc [5] series. SAP-mini adopts a reduced channel width for a lightweight con-figuration. UniDepth-V2 [38] is utilized as the frozen depth prediction model for both variants. All models are opti-mized with AdamW [32] using an initial learning rate of 

2 × 10 −4 and a total batch size of 16. Our implementation is built upon MMDetection3D [7], and all experiments are conducted on four NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090 GPUs.

## 5. Generalization 

To evaluate the generalization of SAP, we integrate it into FlashOcc [51], FB-Occ [22], and the ALOcc series [5]. As shown in Fig. 9, SAP consistently improves performance across these frameworks, achieving mIoU gains of 4.59 and 2.13 points on FlashOcc and FB-Occ, respectively. It also improves ALOcc-mini from 41.43 to 43.53 mIoU and ALOcc from 45.45 to 46.59 mIoU. These results demon-strate the generalizability of SAP across different model ar-chitectures and model capacities. 

## 6. Conclusion 

In this paper, we present SAP, a scene-adaptive projection framework for 3D occupancy prediction. Specifically, we back-project depth maps from a frozen depth model into 3D to obtain geometric priors, which are incorporated through a Scene-Adaptive Projection Encoder. Within this encoder, we design Prior-Guided Reference Generation to construct a hybrid reference set that directs more references toward geometrically supported regions rather than empty space. Furthermore, to account for potential misalignment caused by depth estimation errors, we introduce Radial-Structured Feature Aggregation to explore multiple radial candidate locations and aggregate potentially displaced BEV fea-tures.
