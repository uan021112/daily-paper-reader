Abstract
3D scene understanding tasks are often approached by build-
ing explicit 3D scene representations or by training spe-
cialized multi-modal models over point clouds, voxels, or
scene graphs. We take a complementary view: for many
queries, the main bottleneck is not the absence of a stronger
3D representation but the selection of a small subset of multi-
view RGB views that actually makes the query answerable.
We present a training-free, query-conditioned multi-view
pipeline that performs diverse tasks using only 2D images
and a set of tools used purely for inference. Our method first
decomposes each query into a set of concepts that identify
unique attributes that assist with reasoning. This drives a
coarse-to-fine evidence selection process: a coarse retrieval
selects and scores all views, a diversity-aware selector re-
duces redundancy, and a reranking stage helps shortlist
views by target visibility or answerability. The resulting ev-

explicit localization, and a self-verification step helps refine

We further propose a unique judge-based evaluation system,
motivating a judge who takes into account many semanti-
cally correct or partially correct answers, thereby improving
holistic scene understanding.
1. Introduction
3D reconstruction has advanced rapidly in recent years,
driven in part by Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) [21, 32]
and 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) [20, 37, 55], enabling
high-quality 3D asset generation [34, 39, 47] and Simul-
taneous Localization And Mapping (SLAM) from dense
multi-view images [31]. However, in practical cases, recon-
struction alone is insufficient to achieve optimal outcomes.
An agent must not only recover scene geometry but also
reason about objects, attributes, and spatial relationships
to act effectively. This motivates the integration of Multi-
modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) [23, 44, 51] for
3D-aware spatial reasoning. While recent MLLMs have
shown strong outcomes in 2D visual understanding [44, 52],
they still struggle to perform reliable reasoning in 3D en-
vironments [19]. Prior work has primarily addressed this
limitation by incorporating explicit 3D structure into the
reasoning pipeline, including language-aligned 3D represen-
tations [13, 46, 56], direct use of depth cues [16, 53, 57], and
joint representation learning between 3D features and lan-
guage [13, 40, 54]. Although these methods are promising,
they often rely on costly 3D information [48, 57], special-
ized representation supervision [15, 22, 45], or reconstructed
scene assets [47]. As a result, they remain brittle when han-
dling viewpoint-dependent spatial relations, occlusions, or
compositional queries that require integrating multiple as-
pects of evidence.
A complementary direction leverages multi-view in-
puts [43], bird’s-eye-view (BEV) maps [36], and auxiliary
scene priors [36, 48] to provide a broader spatial context.
However, these approaches face persistent trade-offs be-
cause they must preserve global scene understanding while
retaining the fine-grained local evidence needed to accurately
answer a particular question. In contrast to these existing
approaches, we ask an orthogonal question: Can an off-the-
shelf MLLM reason about a 3D scene using only multi-view
2D observations without necessitating 3D reconstruction?
Instead of assuming that reliable 3D reasoning requires an
intermediate 3D representation, we investigate whether 3D
visual reasoning can be recovered from 2D evidence alone.
Our central premise is that spatial queries may be answerable
when the system can retrieve the appropriate viewpoints, iso-
late the relevant visual evidence, and integrate this evidence
through robust multimodal reasoning. As shown in Figure 1,
even a seemingly simple question such as “What color is the
office chair?” requires identifying the relevant entities and
spatial relations, identifying views in which they are jointly
visible, and reasoning over the collected information.
To address these challenges, ViewAgent3D is a
training-free, test-time reasoning pipeline for 3D scene
understanding over multiview image observations of a
scene. Rather than necessitating explicit 3D reconstruc-
tion or point clouds per scene, ViewAgent3D tackles
query-conditioned information selection as the core prob-
lem. Given a natural-language query and a set of multi-view
images, ViewAgent3D first parses it using a Concept De-
composition step to extract relevant semantic components,
including target objects, attributes, and relations, as shown in
Figure 2. ViewAgent3D then performs a View Selection
process to perform coarse retrieval, producing a compact set
of candidate views conditioned on the input. This step is
crucial because, for most questions, the majority of frames
are irrelevant, and many relevant frames may be redundant.
Over this set, to remove redundant views and increase diver-
sity, we mix the retrieved views with diverse ones to form
a well-informed set of views for the query. Next, a VLM
Ranking step is used to perform a visibility-aware ranking
to identify which views actually contain the evidence needed
to answer the question. To aid with this step, a Conditional
Grounding tool is invoked selectively only when explicit
localization is likely to disambiguate the relevant concepts.
This process ends with a self-verification step that generates
the final answer when the curated information is extracted
from the selected views. We further introduce SEU, a holistic
judge with metrics for open-ended cases where a prediction
differs lexically from the ground truth but preserves the
same meaning.
Extensive experiments across 3D VQA, dense caption-
ing, and Embodied QA show that ViewAgent3D achieves
strong performance despite being training-free and RGB-
only. By extracting compact, query-relevant information,
our method substantially outperforms previous video-based
approaches and surpasses several methods that incorpo-
rate explicit 3D information.

In summary, our main contributions are as follows:
• We introduce ViewAgent3D, a training-free, 2D-only
pipeline for 3D scene understanding tasks that reasons
directly over multi-view RGB observations without the
need to construct an explicit 3D scene representation.
• We propose a query-driven reasoning and tool utilization
framework that combines concept extraction, retrieval of
views, and relevance-diversity balanced viewpoint selec-
tion, visibility-aware ranking, selective grounding, and
self-verified response generation.
• We introduce a holistic evaluation setup using a Judge-
based mechanism that improves evaluation pertaining to
open-ended cases by evaluating responses more holisti-
cally rather than deterministic metric evaluation, and im-
proves recovery by around 15%.
• We demonstrate strong performance on diverse tasks, in-
cluding 3D QA benchmarks, dense captioning, and Em-
bodied QA, providing gains by over ∼10% on average, all
while without any task-specific fine-tuning or usage of any
3D information.
2. ViewAgent3D
An illustration of ViewAgent3D is shown in Figure 2. Our
method treats language-driven 3D scene understanding as
a test-time reasoning problem over RGB views: instead of
utilizing an explicit 3D scene representation, it reasons about
which informative subset of observations makes the query
answerable. To this end, Sec. 2.1 abstracts the query into a
compact semantic decomposition that exposes the entities,
relations, and cues the system should reason over; Sec. 2.2
uses this structure to retrieve candidate views, remove redun-
dancy, and allow the VLM to observe and reason over views
that are actually answerable; Sec. 2.3 invokes 2D ground-
ing only when explicit localization is likely to sharpen the
evidence; and Sec. 2.4 produces the final response from the
selected evidence. This enables the VLM to not only answer
but also reason and decide which evidence can be useful
before generating a response.

3.1. ViewAgent3D: 3D Question Answering
We evaluate ViewAgent3D on 3D QA benchmarks

5. Conclusion
In this work, we presented ViewAgent3D, a training-free,
test-time reasoning pipeline for 3D scene understanding over
multi-view RGB images. Instead of constructing an explicit
3D representation, ViewAgent3D treats 3D reasoning as a
problem of selecting and reasoning over the right evidence:
it decomposes the query, retrieves and diversifies candidate
views, conditionally invokes 2D grounding, and answers
from the selected observations. Across 3D QA, dense cap-
tioning, and embodied QA, this simple design remains com-
petitive with more representation-heavy 3D pipelines, while
our analyzes show that the main gains come from selecting
better views rather than simply using more of them. Overall,
our results suggest that 3D scene understanding can be per-
formed from 2D observations alone when frozen models are
given the right information.
