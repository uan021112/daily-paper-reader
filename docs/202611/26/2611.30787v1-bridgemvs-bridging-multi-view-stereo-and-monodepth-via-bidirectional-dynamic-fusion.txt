Abstract
Multi-View Stereo (MVS) and Monocular Depth Estimation (MDE) provide com-
plementary cues for dense 3D reconstruction. While MVS leverages explicit geom-
etry for metric depth, it struggles in challenging regions where feature matching is
unreliable. Conversely, MDE provides robust structural priors but typically yields
scale-ambiguous results. Existing hybrid methods mainly exploit monocular cues
in a unidirectional manner, leaving the mutual enhancement between MDE and
MVS insufficiently explored. To address this limitation, we propose BridgeMVS, a
unified cascaded multi-stage MVS framework that bridges monocular depth fea-
tures and multi-view cost volume representations through Bidirectional Dynamic
Fusion (BDF). At each stage, BDF performs dynamic mono-to-cost and cost-to-
mono updates, where monocular structural features guide cost volume refinement
for more robust MVS depth estimation, while multi-view geometric evidence re-
calibrates monocular representations to improve their metric-scale consistency.
Furthermore, the fused monocular and multi-view representations are progressively
propagated from coarse to fine stages and constrained by stage-wise relative-scale
MDE supervision and metric-scale MVS supervision, enabling cross-branch in-
teractions to benefit subsequent high-resolution depth estimation. In addition,
BDF is designed as a plug-and-play module that can be conveniently integrated
into existing cascaded MVS frameworks. Extensive experiments demonstrate
that BridgeMVS achieves state-of-the-art performance on the DTU, Tanks-and-
Temples, and ETH3D benchmarks, while simultaneously boosting the accuracy of
monocular depth estimation. Notably, BridgeMVS ranks first on both the Tanks-
and-Temples Intermediate and Advanced benchmarks. The source code is available
at https://github.com/JianfeiJ/BridgeMVS.

Introduction
Multi-View Stereo (MVS) is a fundamental task in computer vision that aims to reconstruct dense 3D
geometry from a set of calibrated images, which plays a crucial role in various applications [1, 2, 3, 4].
Traditional MVS methods [5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11] rely on handcrafted photo-consistency metrics,
which limits their robustness in real-world scenarios. Learning-based MVS methods [12, 13] replace
handcrafted matching costs with learned feature representations and differentiable cost volumes
constructed via the plane-sweep algorithm [14], achieving substantial progress.
Despite this progress, existing learning-based MVS methods still struggle in challenging regions,
such as weakly textured areas and reflective surfaces. In these regions, reliable correspondences

improves feature representation by introducing CNN variants [15], Transformers [16, 17, 18], and
Mamba [19, 20] for feature aggregation. Another line focuses on cost volume regularization, evolving
from single-stage 3D CNNs and RNNs [21, 22, 23, 24, 25] to cascade frameworks [26, 27, 28], and

feature matching, making MVS vulnerable when visual correspondences are unreliable.
Recent Monocular Depth Estimation (MDE) models [41, 39] provide promising complementary
cues for MVS by encoding rich structural priors from large-scale training. These priors are naturally
complementary to MVS: MDE offers strong structural awareness but predicts scale-ambiguous
relative depth, while MVS provides explicit multi-view geometric constraints and metric-scale depth.
Recent MonoMVSNet [35] explores this complementarity by introducing monocular priors into the
MVS pipeline. However, it mainly follows a unidirectional paradigm, where MDE only serves as
external guidance for MVS. Such one-way interaction limits the use of monocular priors and prevents
MDE from benefiting from multi-view geometric evidence, making it difficult to jointly improve
metric MVS depth and relative monocular depth, as shown in Fig. 1.
To address these limitations, we propose BridgeMVS, a unified cascaded MVS framework that
bridges monocular depth features and multi-view cost volume representations through Bidirectional
Dynamic Fusion (BDF). Instead of simply injecting monocular cues into the MVS branch, BDF
performs bidirectional feature interaction at each cascade stage. In the mono-to-cost direction,
monocular structural features are adapted to the cost volume space and used to guide cost volume
refinement, helping the MVS branch distinguish ambiguous or redundant matching responses. In the
cost-to-mono direction, geometry-aware information is aggregated from the regularized cost volume
and fed back to the MDE branch, recalibrating intermediate monocular representations with explicit
multi-view evidence. Through this bidirectional update, BridgeMVS jointly improves multi-view
reconstruction and monocular depth estimation, leading to superior benchmark performance and
more accurate depth predictions, as illustrated in Fig. 1.
Furthermore, BridgeMVS exploits the inherent multi-scale representations of the DPT-based MDE
branch and aligns them with the cascaded MVS structure. The fused monocular and multi-view
representations are progressively propagated from coarse to fine stages, allowing early cross-branch
interactions to benefit subsequent high-resolution depth estimation. Meanwhile, stage-wise relative-
scale MDE supervision and metric-scale MVS supervision provide complementary structural and
geometric constraints throughout the cascade. By preserving fused representations across stages,
BridgeMVS enables monocular priors and multi-view geometric evidence to be gradually refined.
Since BDF operates only on intermediate monocular features and cost volume representations, it can
be used as a plug-and-play module for existing cascaded MVS frameworks.

The main contributions of this work are summarized as follows:
• We propose a plug-and-play Bidirectional Dynamic Fusion (BDF) module that performs
mono-to-cost and cost-to-mono updates between monocular depth features and multi-view
cost volumes, and can be conveniently integrated into existing cascaded MVS frameworks.
• We propagate fused monocular and multi-view representations across cascaded stages from
coarse to fine, and introduce stage-wise relative-scale and metric-scale supervision to enable
progressive multi-scale refinement under both structural and geometric constraints.
• Extensive experiments show that BridgeMVS achieves state-of-the-art performance on
multiple MVS benchmarks, while also substantially improving monocular depth estimation.
2
Related Work
2.1
Learning-based MVS
Multi-View Stereo (MVS) reconstructs 3D geometry from calibrated multi-view images. MVS-
Net [12] firstly introduces a plane-sweep cost volume with 3D CNN-based regularization, estab-
lishing the standard learning-based MVS pipeline of feature extraction, cost volume construction,
cost regularization, and depth prediction. Recent methods mainly improve MVS from two aspects.
The first focuses on feature representation. Transformer-based methods [16, 17, 33, 18] enhance
intra-view and inter-view feature aggregation with attention mechanisms, while MVSMamba [20]
introduces state-space modeling for efficient global feature aggregation. The second focuses on
cost volume regularization. WT-MVSNet [30], CostFormer [31], and RRT-MVS [32] adopt window
attention [42] to aggregate matching costs, while MVSFormer++ [18] uses flash attention [43] for
cost regularization. GoMVS [34] further incorporates normal priors to address local geometric
inconsistency. Despite these advances, the cost volume is still fundamentally constrained to be
constructed from multi-view feature matching, making MVS unreliable in weakly textured, reflective,
or repetitive regions.
2.2
MVS with MDE
Monocular Depth Estimation (MDE) [44] predicts depth from a single image and provides rich
contextual and structural priors. Early models such as MiDaS [45] and DPT [46] improve cross-
dataset generalization with robust dense prediction architectures. Recent monocular foundation
models, including Depth Anything [41, 39] and MoGe [40, 47], further demonstrate strong zero-shot
generalization and produce coherent depth structures in challenging regions. Monocular priors have
recently been introduced into stereo matching [48, 49, 50, 51, 52] and MVS to improve robustness
when correspondence estimation is ambiguous. Among stereo matching methods, MonSter [49]
performs bidirectional refinement in the disparity space through scale-shift alignment and recurrent
updates, while BridgeDepth [50] employs cross-attention for single-stage feature alignment after
DPT fusion. For MVS, MVSAnywhere [53] proposes a cost volume patchifier module to fuse cost
volume and monocular encoder feature, and leverages large-scale training for zero-shot MVS depth
estimation. MonoMVSNet [35] introduces monocular features and monocular depth priors through
feature guidance, dynamic depth sampling, and relative-consistency supervision. Concurrent work
SPE-MVS [54] incorporates monocular depth priors through spatial position encoding to enhance
reconstruction performance. These MVS methods demonstrate the effectiveness of monocular priors
but primarily use them to guide MVS in a unidirectional manner. In contrast, our method establishes
multi-stage bidirectional interaction between intermediate DPT features and regularized multi-view
cost volumes, enabling mutual enhancement of both MVS and MDE.

MVS framework. The network consists of a cost volume-based MVS branch, a DPT-based MDE
branch [39], and a Bidirectional Dynamic Fusion (BDF) module (Sec. 3.2) inserted at each stage.
MVS Branch follows the main pipeline of existing cascaded multi-scale MVS methods [26, 55]
except for the inserted BDF module. Specifically, all images are fed into a Feature Pyramid Network
(FPN) [56] to extract multi-scale image features. At stage s, each source-view feature is warped to the
reference view to compute group-wise feature correlations [57, 58] with the reference-view feature
over the predefined depth hypotheses. Pixel-view weights [59] are then used to fuse all pairwise
correlations into a single cost volume. Following common practice, a lightweight 3D U-Net [55] is
applied for cost volume regularization. Instead of directly predicting the metric MVS depth from the
regularized cost volume Rs, BridgeMVS couples the regularized cost volume with the monocular
feature (from the corresponding stage of the MDE branch) through BDF. The BDF-refined cost
volume is converted into a probability volume by applying softmax along the depth dimension,
and the metric MVS depth is obtained using a winner-take-all strategy. More details on cascaded
multi-scale MVS can be found in [26, 55].
MDE Branch follows the pipeline of the monocular depth foundation model (e.g., Depth Anything
V2 [39]) except for the BDF module. It consists of a DINOv2-based encoder [60] and a DPT-based
decoder [46]. Different from the MVS branch, only the reference image is fed into the MDE branch.
The DPT decoder produces monocular features in a multi-stage manner, which naturally corresponds
to the cascade multi-stage in the MVS branch. At stage s, the DPT monocular feature Ms is fused
with the regularized cost volume Rs through BDF. The BDF-refined monocular feature is decoded
into a relative monocular depth map. Meanwhile, the BDF-refined monocular feature is propagated
to the next stage in the DPT decoder, allowing multi-view geometric evidence injected at coarser
stages to guide subsequent high-resolution monocular feature representations.
Through this design, BridgeMVS enables bidirectional interaction between multi-view and monocular
representations at every cascade stage. The MVS branch benefits from monocular structural priors for
robust cost volume refinement, while the MDE branch benefits from multi-view geometric constraints
for improved relative consistency. The fused representations are progressively propagated from coarse
to fine stages, producing both metric MVS depth and relative monocular depth.

Experiments
4.1
Datasets and Benchmarks
DTU [36] is an indoor MVS dataset captured under controlled laboratory conditions. Blended-
MVS [64] is a large-scale synthetic dataset containing both indoor and outdoor scenes. Tanks-and-
Temples (T&T) [37] is a real-world MVS benchmark with complex geometry and diverse acquisition
conditions. It includes 8 scenes in the intermediate set and 6 scenes in the advanced set. ETH3D [38]
benchmark contains high-resolution indoor and outdoor scenes with large viewpoint variations, and
is divided into training and test sets. RobustMVD [65] is a multi-view depth estimation benchmark
comprising five datasets: KITTI [66], ScanNet [67], ETH3D [38], DTU [36], and T&T [37].

Implementation Details
BridgeMVS is implemented in PyTorch [68] and optimized using Adam [69]. Following common
practice, we first train and evaluate BridgeMVS on DTU [36]. For DTU training, we use 5 input
views at a resolution of 512×640 for 15 epochs, with an initial learning rate of 1e-3. To improve
generalization, we further fine-tune the model on a mixture of DTU and BlendedMVS [64], using
11 input views at a resolution of 512×640 for an additional 15 epochs, with an initial learning rate
of 4e-4. In addition, following [55, 33, 18, 20], we conduct high-resolution training on DTU using
5 input views at a resolution of 1024×1280 for 10 epochs, with an initial learning rate of 1e-3 (the
model is denoted as BridgeMVS*). We use the ViT-B version of Depth Anything V2 [39] as the
MDE branch, freezing the encoder and fine-tuning the DPT decoder with a learning rate 0.05× that of
the rest of the network. BridgeMVS uses four coarse-to-fine stages with 32-16-8-4 depth hypotheses
and 4-4-4-4 grouped feature correlations. We using the dynamic fusion strategy [70] to obtain the
final reconstructed point clouds.

Ablation Study
We conduct ablation studies on the DTU [36] evaluation set using both point cloud and depth metrics.
For MVS depth estimation, we report MAE and RMSE. For monocular depth estimation, we report
rel and τ. Unless otherwise specified, all ablation experiments use the model trained on DTU at
low resolution and are evaluated with 5 input views at a resolution of 832 × 1152, with all other
hyperparameters kept consistent.
Structure Analysis of BDF.
Tab. 5 analyzes the structure of the proposed BDF module. Compared
with the baseline (a), the one-way Mono→Cost variants (b) and (c) improve MVS reconstruction,
showing that monocular structural priors benefit cost volume refinement. Adapting the monocular
decoder in (c) further improves the results over the frozen variant in (b). Conversely, Cost→Mono
in (d) substantially improves monocular depth estimation but provides only a small MVS gain,
suggesting that multi-view evidence effectively refines monocular representations. Combining both
directions, the full BDF in (g) improves Overall from 0.276 to 0.268 and monocular rel from 2.15 to
0.92 over the strongest one-way variant (c), yielding the best joint result. Without monocular supervi-
sion (f), MVS remains competitive, but monocular rel deteriorates to 8.09. Removing monocular
pretraining (e) degrades both branches. These results suggest that pretraining supplies the structural
prior, while stage-wise MDE supervision helps maintain informative monocular representations
during bidirectional fusion.

5
Conclusion
In this paper, we propose BridgeMVS, a cascaded multi-stage MVS framework that bidirectionally
fuses monocular depth features and multi-view cost volumes. The proposed BDF module serves as a
plug-and-play component for existing cascaded MVS frameworks, where monocular structural priors
refine MVS cost volumes and multi-view geometric evidence improves monocular representations.
Extensive experiments show that BridgeMVS achieves state-of-the-art performance on multiple MVS
benchmarks and also improves monocular depth estimation.

Static bidirectional fusion.
We construct four static baselines by combining two cost-volume
organizations with concatenation or element-wise addition. In the C × D-folded variants, the cost-
channel and depth-hypothesis dimensions are merged into a single feature dimension. The resulting
15


cost volume and the monocular feature are projected to the same dimension before fusion. In the
per-depth variants, the depth dimension is retained, and the projected monocular feature is fused with
each projected cost hypothesis. Each shared fused representation generates updates for both branches
without input-dependent affinity weights. BDF achieves best performance than all four variants with
comparable memory usage and runtime. The improvements across both branches support the benefit
of selectively incorporating cross-branch evidence through direction-specific adaptive aggregation.
BridgeDepth-style IBA.
We replace BDF with a BridgeDepth-style IBA module using local
bidirectional cross-attention. With similar fusion parameters, BDF improves Overall from 0.276 to
0.268, MVS depth MAE from 7.95 to 6.56, and auxiliary monocular relative error from 1.21 to 0.92.
It also reduces runtime from 0.50 s to 0.34 s and memory usage from 13,968 MB to 3,132 MB. This
comparison concerns the transplanted fusion mechanisms rather than the complete native pipelines:
IBA performs alignment at a single post-DPT stage, whereas its adaptation here operates on full
MVS depth volumes at all four cascade stages, substantially increasing memory requirements. BDF
instead uses cross-branch affinities to guide aggregation of the target branch’s own values, supporting
multi-stage interaction without direct cross-branch value transfer over all depth hypotheses.

C
Limitations
BridgeMVS remains subject to limitations of both monocular prediction and calibrated MVS. First,
because the Cost-to-Mono update relies on the regularized cost volume, the enhanced monocular
output requires multi-view inputs and is not a standalone single-image depth estimator. Second, BDF
relies on complementary MDE and MVS cues for mutual enhancement. When both are unreliable,
as can occur with complex materials or depth boundaries, bidirectional updates may propagate
errors because BDF does not explicitly estimate cue reliability. Third, BridgeMVS assumes known
intrinsics/extrinsics and an initial metric depth range, as required by its calibrated plane-sweep
MVS backbone. Relative monocular depth alone cannot provide metric bounds. Future work will
investigate uncertainty-aware fusion, robustness to perturbations in the required camera parameters,
and using monocular depth priors to anchor the initial MVS depth range.
