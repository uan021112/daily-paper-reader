# Pixel2Perspective: A Benchmark for Multi-Modal and Multi-View Generalization in Vision-Language Models 

Aditya Gandhamal 2⋆, Aniruddh Sikdar 1⋆, Arya Kishor 1, and Suresh Sundaram 1

Abstract. Embodied AI systems, such as drones and autonomous vehi-cles, improve navigation by enhancing perception in environments con-taining unseen objects. While Visual Language Models (VLMs) offer strong image understanding, their real-world application is underexplored. For effective deployment, open-vocabulary semantic segmentation (OVSS) models, leveraging models like CLIP to assign pixel-level labels from text, must be robust to outdoor conditions, adverse weather, and varying view-points. Despite their potential, these models encounter significant chal-lenges in cross-domain generalization, hindering their practical use in embodied AI applications. In this work, we present Pixel2Perspective, a benchmark for multi-angle object localization and open-vocabulary segmentation across both aerial and ground perspectives. This bench-mark supports thorough evaluation across different viewpoints and sen-sor modalities. We assess foundational models like CLIP and advanced OVSS models using a curated dataset that spans varied outdoor condi-tions, sim-to-real scenarios, and multiple sensors, including infrared, to support the development of robust and generalizable perception systems. We also present a novel framework, Multi-Source RGB–IR Uncertainty Quantification (MSRIQ), which jointly models aleatoric and epistemic uncertainties across RGB and infrared imagery. This approach supports robust evaluation of open-vocabulary segmentation models across diverse environmental conditions. Our work sets a robustness-driven benchmark, providing key insights and laying the foundation for future research in open-world embodied AI perception. Embodied AI systems require advanced perception capabilities to operate reliably in adverse weather and low-light conditions [3, 25]. To address this, benchmarks such as the Multi-domain Evaluation of Semantic Segmentation (MESS) [1] have been developed to evaluate model performance across a wider range of complex, domain-specific datasets. However, leveraging foundation mod-els like CLIP [21] for advanced perception presents three key challenges that remain unexplored in previous benchmarks: (1) fine-tuning on limited training dataset, for downstream segmentation tasks while preserving pretrained knowl-edge, (2) handling significant domain shifts arising from out-of-distribution data, and (3) accommodating substantial variations between seen and unseen classes, particularly those arising from differing viewing perspectives. These challenges are key to assessing model robustness across tasks. Reliable systems must work with visible (RGB) and infrared (IR) data, staying resilient to modality shifts and varying conditions like day-night cycles, fog, rain, and glare. Additionally, they must be capable of recognizing objects from multiple viewpoints, each of-fering unique semantic cues, despite often lacking the ability to choose the most informative perspectives [23]. However, current standard benchmarks primarily target in-domain tasks, offering limited insight into how models perform across domains. This limitation restricts our understanding of zero-shot segmentation models’ ability to generalize to unseen domains and handle diverse sensors or vocabularies. To tackle these challenges, we introduce Pixel2Perspective: a benchmark for multi-modal and multi-view generalization in vision-language models. It is an open-vocabulary RGB–Infrared segmentation benchmark designed for multi-angle object localization across drone, satellite, and ground-level perspectives, as shown in Figure 1. We evaluate state-of-the-art models such as CLIP for fine-grained object localization, alongside leading open-vocabulary semantic segmen-tation (OVSS) models across 13 datasets. These datasets are carefully curated to span diverse object classes, sensor modalities, and out-of-distribution domains, with an emphasis on varied viewpoints to comprehensively assess generalization. We also introduce Multi-Source RGB–IR Uncertainty Quantification (MSRIQ), a benchmark framework that jointly models aleatoric and epistemic uncertain-ties across co-registered RGB and IR modalities, enabling robust evaluation of OVSS models in diverse conditions. Our key contributions are as follows: (1) Pixel2Perspective Benchmark: We introduce Pixel2Perspective, a benchmark for evaluating state-of-the-art Vision-Language Models. Leveraging a quantitative taxonomy of diverse segmentation datasets, it rigorously assesses out-of-domain object localization and OVSS per-formance across ground-level, aerial, and bird’s-eye perspectives, under varied weather conditions, and across multiple modalities including RGB and IR. (2) Systematic Evaluation of VLMs: We conduct a comprehensive evaluation of Vision-Language Models on object localization and open-vocabulary semantic segmentation, offering an in-depth analysis across diverse settings. ## 2 Related Works 

Open-vocabulary semantic segmentation Large-scale pre-training has revo-lutionized computer vision by training models on image-text pairs, enabling rep-resentation of visual and textual semantics in a shared space. Open-vocabulary semantic segmentation (OVSS) aims to bridge the gap between image-level and pixel-level understanding by enabling segmentation of unseen classes. OVSS models are generally categorized into two main types: (i) mask-proposal net-works [6, 10, 33–36], and (ii) cost-aggregation networks [4]. Mask-proposal ap-proaches generate class-agnostic region proposals, which are then semantically aligned with CLIP-derived textual embeddings to facilitate open-vocabulary classification at the pixel level. For instance, OpenSeg [10] leverages local im-age regions to form proposals, whereas models like ZegFormer [5] and ZSseg [35] adopt a two-stage architecture to address this task. CAT-Seg [4] enhances robust-ness by using similarity scores to combine CLIP’s image and text embeddings into a cost volume, rather than relying on direct embeddings. OVSS models are commonly evaluated on standard datasets like ADE20K [40] and Pascal VOC [7], which primarily feature everyday, in-domain images. However, such evaluations fail to capture real-world conditions with inherent domain shifts and environ-mental diversity.

The Segmentation in the Wild (SegInW) benchmark [42] includes 25 datasets, however, most of them still feature everyday, in-domain images, with only two notable exceptions. Robust benchmarks are crucial for advancing the vision community, providing standardized ways to evaluate performance across diverse tasks [19]. Inspired by HELM [17] for LLMs, the MESS benchmark [1] was introduced to assess OVSS models across domains such as medicine, engineering, earth observation, biology, and agriculture. However, a key limitation lies in the relatively low number of classes within these datasets, resulting in high random mIoU scores. To the best of our knowledge, open-vocabulary semantic segmen-tation (OVSS) models have not been systematically evaluated on datasets that account for sensor shifts and variations in look-angle, as commonly encountered in drones and autonomous vehicles. This work presents the first comprehensive effort to address these challenges, aiming to provide new insights and advance the robustness of OVSS models in practical settings.

3.1 Dataset Taxonomy 

Robustness challenges in real-world deployment of perception models are fre-quently overlooked in existing research. To tackle this, we introduce Pixel2Perspective-Benchmark, an open-vocabulary RGB–IR benchmark for multi-angle segmenta-tion across drones, satellites, and ground perspectives. The benchmark focuses on two key aspects, where systematic evaluation is essential: (1) assessing multi-modal backbones to measure the fine-grained capabilities of encoders, given their crucial role in retaining essential world knowledge, and (2) evaluating OVSS methods for fine-grained segmentation tasks. As part of creating this benchmark, we reviewed a large collection of open-source datasets and filtered them based on three exclusion criteria: (1) viewing angle, (2) sensor modality, and (3) environmental conditions (day, night, fog, rain). Guided by the proposed taxonomy, we grouped the datasets into three pri-mary viewpoint categories: (1) ground-level, (2) slant-angle, and (3) bird’s-eye view. This categorization facilitates evaluating a model’s ability to segment ob-jects across significantly different viewpoints. We also assess performance using both RGB and IR modalities to ensure robustness under varied environmental Title Suppressed Due to Excessive Length 7

and weather conditions. Datasets with unsuitable resolution or limited to binary labels were excluded from the benchmark. Guided by the developed taxonomy, we carefully curated a representative set of datasets to ensure the benchmark is both informative and reproducible. Through iterative refinement, we identified 13 most relevant datasets for each viewpoint category and assessed model performance across diverse domain and sensor variations. For ground-level perspectives, we selected the MSRS [28], MVSeg [12], SemanticRT [11], MFNet [37], and InfraParis [8] datasets, all of which feature IR modalities. To evaluate performance under adverse weather conditions, we used the ACDC dataset [22]. To represent varying slant-angle perspectives, we incorporated the UAVid [18], IndraEye [25], and CART [15] datasets, with IndraEye and CART also providing IR imagery. For bird’s-eye views, we selected the ISPRS dataset [27], Worldfloods [20], OpenEarthMap [31], and LoveDA [29] datasets. (A detailed table of selected datasets, instance counts, and benchmark comparisons is provided in the supplementary material.) 

3.2 Fine-Grained Assessment Settings 

For object localization, we employ CLIP and other CLIP-based models, tai-lored for fine-grained tasks, in a zero-shot setting. For the OVSS task, we define multiple evaluation settings: (1) training on COCO-Stuff [2], (2) training on the slant-angle IndraEye (RGB) dataset, (3) training on the ground-level MSRS and ACDC datasets, and (4) Training on bird’s-eye view OpenEarthMap, covering both RGB and sim-to-real setups. These settings assess the models’ ability to generalize across different viewpoints by training on a specific angle and vali-dating across all perspectives. Setting (1) represents the standard evaluation for fine-grained OVSS tasks and evaluates the effectiveness of VLMs in leveraging pre-training knowledge across diverse look angles. Other Settings introduce ad-ditional challenges with limited training samples. Datasets were selected based on official test splits, high-quality annotations, and sufficient image resolution, and were used in compliance with their licenses without ethical concerns. This benchmark is well-suited for evaluating model robustness, testing performance across multiple look angles, and addressing challenges such as weather variations, sensor degradation, complex environments, and viewpoint-dependent semantic changes. 

3.3 Multi-Source RGB–IR Uncertainty Quantification 

Uncertainty in perception can be categorized into two types: aleatoric uncer-tainty, which arises from noise inherent in observations such as sensor limita-tions, and epistemic uncertainty, which originates from the model itself [13]. As shown in Fig. 2, aleatoric uncertainty is evident in the RGB image when the sensor struggles to detect a person in low-light conditions, resulting in uncertain OVSS predictions. Although the IR sensor can detect the person through ther-mal radiation, the model’s predictions remain uncertain due to the domain shift 8 A. Gandhamal et al. 

from RGB to IR caused by RGB-based pre-training, highlighting epistemic un-certainty. This emphasizes the need to assess such models on IR data to quantify cross-modality uncertainty. To address uncertainties from both RGB and IR sen-sors, we propose Multi-Source RGB–IR Uncertainty Quantification (MSRIQ), a framework that jointly models aleatoric and epistemic uncertainties across co-registered RGB and infrared modalities to evaluate segmentation performance. As shown in the Fig. 2, the Intersection over Union (IoU) for the RGB image is computed as: 

IoU = |PRGB ∩ GroundT ruth ||PRGB ∪ GroundT ruth | (1) where PRGB and PIR are the OVSS model’s predictions for the RGB and IR images, respectively, compared against the GroundTruth to compute the IoU. We define Multi-Source RGB–IR Uncertainty Quantification (MSRIQ) as: 

IoU M SRIQ = |PRGB ∩ PIR ||PRGB ∪ PIR | (2) This metric quantifies the pixel-level spatial overlap between the RGB and IR predictions. An MSRIQ value close to 0 indicates little or no overlap between the predictions. This approach enables a more robust evaluation of open-vocabulary segmentation models across diverse and challenging environmental conditions. For scenarios where the pixels are not co-registered, we propose using Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) to compare the distributions.

This illustrates a trade-off: while fine-tuning on a single perspective enhances within-domain alignment, it diminishes the model’s ability to generalize to other viewpoints. ## 5 Conclusion 

This paper presents Pixel2Perspective, a benchmark for evaluating OVSS model robustness across multiple views and sensor modalities, a crucial step toward real-world deployment. Through systematic evaluation of state-of-the-art models in object localization and OVSS tasks, we reveal a substantial gap between current model capabilities and real-world demands, highlighting the need to enhance semantic understanding. Our robustness evaluation framework, along with the proposed Multi-Source RGB–IR Uncertainty Quantification (MSRIQ), provides valuable tools for developing more resilient models. By addressing these challenges, the benchmark provides key insights for embodied AI systems aiming to enhance perception and autonomous navigation in ground vehicles and drones. It also lays the groundwork for future research into why generalization across viewpoints remains difficult in practical settings.
