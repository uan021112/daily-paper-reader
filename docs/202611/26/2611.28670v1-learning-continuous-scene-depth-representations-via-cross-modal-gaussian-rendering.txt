# Learning Continuous Scene Depth Representations via Cross-Modal Gaussian Rendering 

## Zhiwei Zhong Lingyu Zhu Baoliang Chen Peilin Chen Shiqi Wang City University of Hong Kong 

## Abstract 

Depth super-resolution with color guidance is a fundamen-tal problem in geometric scene understanding that aims to reconstruct high-resolution depth using structural cues from color images. In practical RGB-D systems, the res-olution gap between depth and color images often varies, requiring flexible upsampling factors. However, many ex-isting methods are designed for predefined integer scales. Although implicit methods support arbitrary-scale recon-struction, they typically rely on computationally demand-ing per-pixel neural decoding and may struggle to recover sharp depth boundaries and fine structures. To this end, we propose a cross-modal Gaussian rendering framework that learns continuous depth representations for arbitrary-scale depth super-resolution. Specifically, we first construct hier-archical Gaussian representations for progressive depth re-construction, with explicit spatial support defined by learn-able Gaussian primitives. Building on this representation, we design a Structure-Aware Gaussian Aggregation mod-ule that combines spatial weights with cross-modal feature affinities to reduce aggregation across depth discontinu-ities. We then develop a Scale-Adaptive Gaussian Render-ing module that adapts Gaussian spatial kernels to the tar-get pixel footprint. To further facilitate cross-modal learn-ing, we propose a training strategy that gradually intro-duces higher-frequency guidance information during train-ing. Extensive experiments on benchmark datasets demon-strate the effectiveness of the proposed method at both in-distribution and out-of-distribution upsampling scales.

Learning-based GDSR methods have improved depth re-construction by modeling cross-modal interactions between RGB images and depth maps [6, 15, 21, 29, 32, 34, 35]. Nevertheless, many existing methods are designed for pre-defined integer upsampling factors and often require sepa-rate models to support different factors. Such scale-specific designs limit flexibility in accommodating varying resolu-tion ratios across RGB-D devices and the output resolutions required by downstream tasks. Implicit neural representa-tions offer a flexible solution by modeling depth as a contin-uous function of spatial coordinates. For example, JIIF for-mulates GDSR as neural implicit interpolation and jointly predicts interpolation weights and values [26]. GeoDSR introduces a scale-modulated distance field into a geomet-ric spatial aggregator to model depth continuously across spatial locations and scales [28]. GIDF further incorpo-rates scale information into feature encoding and decoding through cross-modal fusion and implicit attention [39]. Despite this flexibility, implicit methods still face chal-lenges in computational efficiency and geometric detail re-1arXiv:2611.28670v1 [cs.CV] 21 Nov 2026 covery. First, existing methods rely on per-pixel neural de-coding to reconstruct depth from neighboring features, lead-ing to increasing computational costs as the output reso-lution grows. Second, local feature aggregation may mix information from different surfaces near depth discontinu-ities, making fine geometric details difficult to preserve. These challenges motivate exploring an explicit representa-tion that supports continuous depth reconstruction with con-trollable spatial support and structure-aware aggregation. Recent advances in arbitrary-scale image SR suggest that 2D Gaussian primitives provide a suitable representation for this purpose [1, 11, 24]. These methods represent an image or its features as a collection of learnable Gaussian primi-tives whose spatial support adapts to local image structure. Output pixel values are reconstructed by combining Gaus-sian contributions at the query locations, so the same rep-resentation can be evaluated on different grids to produce images at different resolutions. This explicit formulation provides a promising basis for arbitrary-scale depth recon-struction. However, directly applying Gaussian rendering to GDSR still presents several challenges. First, spatial proximity alone cannot determine whether Gaussian primitives lie on the same depth surface. Aggre-gating nearby primitives across a depth discontinuity can blur object boundaries. Guidance provides useful structural cues, but its appearance variations do not always correspond to depth changes. Reliable aggregation therefore requires jointly considering depth and RGB structure. Second, each output pixel covers a spatial region whose size depends on the requested resolution. Over the same image domain, a coarse-grid pixel covers a larger area than a fine-grid pixel. Evaluating the Gaussian response only at the pixel center ignores this difference. The spatial support used for render-ing should therefore adapt to the target pixel footprint. To address these challenges, we propose a cross-modal Gaussian rendering framework for arbitrary-scale GDSR. The framework predicts hierarchical Gaussian representa-tions from the input and progressively reconstructs depth by combining their rendered maps. To preserve geometric boundaries, we develop Structure-Aware Gaussian Aggre-gation (SAGA), which combines spatial weights with cross-modal feature affinities to reduce the influence of primi-tives across depth discontinuities. To accommodate the spa-tial coverage of output pixels, we introduce Scale-Adaptive Gaussian Rendering (SAGR), which incorporates the target pixel footprint into Gaussian kernels and adjusts their am-plitudes to preserve each kernel’s integral. Finally, to facili-tate cross-modal learning, we introduce a progressive RGB guidance training strategy that gradually incorporates finer RGB details by reducing guidance blur during training. The main contributions are summarized as follows: • We propose a cross-modal Gaussian rendering framework for arbitrary-scale GDSR that progressively reconstructs depth using hierarchical Gaussian representations. • We develop a Structure-Aware Gaussian Aggregation (SAGA) mechanism that combines spatial weights with cross-modal affinities to reduce aggregation across depth discontinuities and preserve geometric boundaries. • We propose a Scale-Adaptive Gaussian Rendering (SAGR) scheme that incorporates the target pixel foot-print into Gaussian spatial kernels, adapting their support to the sampling area of each output pixel. • We introduce a progressive training strategy that facili-tates cross-modal learning by gradually reducing guid-ance blur. Experiments on both datasets demonstrate accurate arbitrary-scale reconstruction and a favorable trade-off between accuracy and model size (Fig. 1). 

## 2. Related Work 

Guided Depth Super-Resolution. Guided depth super-resolution exploits the structural correspondence between RGB images and depth maps to recover spatial details miss-ing from low-resolution depth observations. Existing ap-proaches can be broadly categorized into filtering-based, optimization-based, and learning-based methods [36]. Filtering-based methods use spatial proximity and guid-ance information to determine local filtering weights [9, 18]. These methods are efficient and require no training, but discrepancies between RGB appearance and depth ge-ometry can lead to texture transfer or boundary blurring. Optimization-based methods combine observation fidelity with image-guided geometric regularization [5, 7]. They ex-plicitly enforce data consistency and spatial regularity, but depend on the chosen priors and often require iterative op-timization. Learning-based methods learn cross-modal fu-sion or adaptive filtering from data [4, 14, 17, 23, 30, 34, 35]. Recent approaches further incorporate degradation-aware fusion and foundation-model guidance to better ex-ploit cross-modal information [29, 32]. Despite these ad-vances, most existing methods are designed for predefined upsampling factors, limiting their flexibility when input res-olution ratios or target output sizes vary across RGB-D sys-tems and downstream visual perception tasks. 

Arbitrary-Scale Super-Resolution. Arbitrary-scale SR reconstructs images at different resolutions using a uni-fied model. Meta-SR predicts scale-dependent upsam-pling filters [12], LIIF learns continuous image represen-tations through local implicit functions [3], and LTE incor-porates local frequency information to improve texture re-covery [19]. CLIT combines cross-scale local attention and frequency encoding with a cascaded architecture to exploit multi-scale features for progressive reconstruction [2]. For GDSR, JIIF introduces guided implicit interpola-tion [26], GeoDSR incorporates scale-modulated geomet-ric aggregation [28], and GIDF develops scale-aware cross-modal feature fusion and implicit decoding [39]. Although 2these methods enable continuous depth reconstruction, they may struggle to recover fine geometric details. Moreover, repeated neural decoding at query locations can increase computational costs at high output resolutions. 

Gaussian-Based Image Reconstruction. Anisotropic Gaussian primitives represent 3D scenes and enable real-time novel-view synthesis through differentiable splat-ting [16]. To improve surface reconstruction, Huang 

et al. [13] introduce 2D Gaussian splatting with oriented planar Gaussian disks embedded in 3D space. For im-age restoration, Gaussian primitives are instead defined directly in the 2D image domain to represent images or their features. GaussianSR [11] employs continuous Gaus-sian feature representations for arbitrary-scale SR, while GSASR [1] and Pixel to Gaussian [24] develop efficient Gaussian prediction and rendering methods for continuous image reconstruction. These image-domain methods aggre-gate Gaussian responses on query grids, allowing a single set of predicted primitives to be reused for image recon-struction at different output resolutions during inference.

3.4. Progressive RGB Guidance Training 

Arbitrary-scale super-resolution methods commonly adopt randomly sampled upsampling factors during training. For GDSR, varying these factors changes the amount of struc-tural detail retained in the depth input, while RGB guidance consistently contains rich appearance details. At larger fac-tors, fine RGB structures may have little corresponding ev-idence in the depth input, making it difficult to establish cross-modal correspondences early in training. We there-fore introduce a progressive RGB guidance training strat-egy that first emphasizes coarse structures shared by both modalities and gradually incorporates finer RGB details. Specifically, we retain random-scale sampling and apply Gaussian blur to RGB guidance with a given probability, progressively reducing the blur strength during training. 4.2. Comparison with Existing Methods 

Compared methods. We compare with sixteen learning-based GDSR methods. Among them, GeoDSR [28], JIIF [26], and GIDF [39] employ implicit representations to support arbitrary-scale reconstruction. For a fair com-parison, we retrain all competing methods using their offi-cial implementations under a unified protocol. All meth-ods share the same dataset splits, LR input preparation, and evaluation metrics. During training, additional down-sampling factors are sampled uniformly from [1 , 16] . Each method uses one model per dataset across all test factors. 

Quantitative comparison. Tables 1 and 2 present quanti-tative results on TOFDSR and RGB-D-D, respectively. Our method achieves the best performance across all three met-rics at every evaluated factor on both datasets. This ad-vantage extends to large downsampling factors beyond the training range, where sparse depth observations make re-construction more challenging. These results demonstrate effective reconstruction across varying input resolutions and generalization to unseen downsampling factors. 

Computational efficiency. We further compare reconstruc-tion accuracy and model complexity, as shown in Fig. 1. Our method achieves a favorable trade-off between accu-racy and model size, delivering more accurate depth recon-struction with fewer parameters than JIIF and DuCos. 

Qualitative comparison. Figures 3 and 4 show recon-structed depth maps on RGB-D-D at ×20 and TOFDSR at ×16 , respectively. On RGB-D-D, the enlarged curtain and plant regions illustrate differences in boundary local-ization and the recovery of thin structures. On TOFDSR, the boundaries around the head, toy, and flowers reveal the effects of sparse depth sampling, while the corresponding error maps expose deviations that are less apparent in the depth colors. Our results preserve object contours and re-duce errors in the highlighted boundary regions.

Hierarchical Gaussian representation. We replace the hi-erarchical representation with a single Gaussian level to as-sess the role of hierarchical reconstruction. Figure 5b shows that this replacement increases RMSE at every evaluated factor, with larger increases beyond the training range. This supports combining Gaussian fields across levels for depth reconstruction at varying input resolutions. 

Cross-modal affinities. We evaluate three SAGA variants: removing depth affinity, removing RGB affinity, and using spatial weights alone. Both modalities remain available for Gaussian prediction. As shown in Fig. 5c, removing either affinity increases RMSE, while spatial weighting alone pro-duces the largest increase at every factor. Joint depth and RGB affinities thus improve reconstruction over aggrega-tion based solely on local spatial proximity. 

Pixel-footprint rendering. We evaluate SAGR using point queries without footprint convolution and footprint-aware kernels without amplitude correction. The latter retains covariance broadening but removes the integral-preserving amplitude factor. Both variants increase RMSE across all evaluated factors in Fig. 5d. These results support account-ing for the output pixel’s spatial coverage and preserving the kernel integral, with both components contributing to reconstruction within and beyond the training range. 

Gaussian parameter sensitivity. We assess the role of learned Gaussian geometry by replacing anisotropic ker-nels with isotropic ones, resetting centers to grid locations, and rotating kernel orientations at inference. As shown in Fig. 6a, all tested perturbations increase RMSE on RGB-D-D at ×16 . Isotropic kernels and grid-reset centers raise RMSE from 5.33 to 6.53 and 7.69, respectively, while a 90 ◦

rotation yields the largest reconstruction error of 9.15. Figure 6b shows the corresponding local reconstruc-tions at ×16 . The learned Gaussians follow the depth contour with varying shapes and orientations. Isotropic kernels introduce irregular boundaries and surface ripples, while resetting centers removes fine contour details. Ker-nel rotations produce jagged edges and surface artifacts. The learned geometry preserves a clearer boundary and smoother surface interiors. Together, these observations suggest that adaptive Gaussian support follows local depth structure, promoting aggregation within surfaces while pre-serving depth discontinuities along object boundaries. 

## 5. Conclusion 

In this paper, we propose a cross-modal Gaussian ren-dering framework for arbitrary-scale guided depth super-resolution. The framework learns hierarchical Gaussian representations that support continuous depth reconstruc-tion. Structure-aware aggregation combines depth and RGB feature affinities to preserve depth boundaries, while scale-adaptive rendering accounts for the target pixel footprint. A progressive RGB guidance strategy further improves re-construction by gradually introducing finer RGB details during training. Experiments on two real-world bench-marks demonstrate consistent improvements over compet-ing methods at scales within and beyond the training range, while maintaining a favorable balance between reconstruc-tion accuracy and computational efficiency. 8LLM Usage Statement
