Abstract
Predicting the outcomes of robotic manipulation requires
modeling both visual appearance and evolving 3D geom-
etry. This poses two challenges: video compression can
obscure fine-grained robot motion, while jointly predict-
ing RGB and geometry demands sufficient generative ca-
pacity. We introduce Loop4D, a framework that addresses
these challenges through motion-preserving 4D represen-
tations and recurrent generative refinement. Our 4D VAE
encodes RGB and pointmap sequences into compact spa-
tiotemporal latents while preserving motion details and
geometric structure. Our RGB VAE combines geometry-
guided supervision with predictive reconstruction to reduce
compression-induced motion artifacts. Our geometry VAE
uses lightweight adapters to adapt pretrained RGB rep-
resentations while preserving their priors.
Building on
these representations, our Dual-Stream Looped Diffusion
Transformer maintains separate appearance and geome-
try streams with shared Transformer weights and cross-
modal connections. By reusing Transformer blocks across
loops, we increase effective depth without replicating back-
bone parameters, enabling models with fewer parameters
to improve joint RGB–geometry prediction through addi-
tional computation. Experiments demonstrate high-quality
4D generation and validate Loop4D in simulated robotic
manipulation tasks.

1. Introduction
Latent generative models [2, 46, 50, 71, 73] have made vi-
sual generation more scalable by separating compact rep-
resentation learning from generative prediction. VAEs in
image and video [6, 7, 19, 22, 24, 29, 40, 54, 77, 78] re-
duce the dimensionality of observations, while geometric
latent spaces support generation beyond appearance alone.
For embodied world modeling [1, 5, 13, 25, 34, 59, 75, 81],
these representations must also preserve the robot and scene
structure needed for interaction.
General-purpose video
models provide a strong starting point, but their com-
pression objectives and computational budgets are not tai-
lored to these requirements.
This raises two questions:
how should pretrained video representations be adapted to
embodied 4D dynamics, and how can prediction improve
within a fixed parameter budget?
We begin with the strengths and limitations of general
video VAEs [35, 53, 56]. Their pretrained representations
offer both compact compression and reusable visual pri-
ors. However, compression can introduce artifacts around
robotic arms, and RGB reconstruction objectives do not
explicitly supervise metric geometry.
We find that pre-
serving pretrained RGB priors is essential when adapting
a VAE to geometry. This observation motivates selective
adaptation: retain pretrained visual priors while learning
the geometric representations and predictive capabilities re-
quired by embodied tasks. Existing 4D generation mod-
els [8, 14, 38, 43, 47, 48, 52, 67] often rely on large back-
bones, motivating us to improve generation quality with
fewer parameters. Building on our RGB and geometry la-
tents, we introduce a Dual-Stream Looped Diffusion Trans-
former that increases effective depth through shared-weight
recurrence while preserving cross-modal interactions. This
design achieves generation quality comparable to larger
models without increasing backbone parameters.
Guided by this analysis, we introduce Loop4D, com-
bining a 4D VAE tailored to embodied dynamics with
a Dual-Stream Looped Diffusion Transformer. Our 4D
VAE compresses appearance and geometry into compact
spatiotemporal latents. The RGB VAE combines geometry-
guided supervision with reconstruction and prediction ob-
jectives [78] to reduce compression-induced robot-arm ar-
tifacts. The geometry VAE uses residual adapters and geo-
metric constraints to adapt our pretrained RGB VAE while
preserving its pretrained backbone and visual priors.
Building on these representations, our Dual-Stream
Looped Diffusion Transformer maintains separate RGB and
XYZ hidden states with shared Transformer weights and
cross-modal connections. However, directly applying ex-
isting looping strategies [10, 33, 44] to our 4D generation
setting does not reliably improve prediction quality. Addi-
tional loops can degrade robot-region predictions, while ap-
pearance and geometry may require different update mag-
nitudes.
We therefore introduce two adaptations.
First,
per-loop prediction supervision and a robot-region non-
degradation constraint encourage successive loops to re-
fine manipulation-relevant regions. Second, a lightweight
shared gate uses the current hidden states and candidate in-
crements to adapt update magnitudes separately for RGB
and XYZ. The model repeatedly applies shared middle lay-
ers within each denoising call, increasing effective depth
without replicating backbone parameters.
These adapta-
tions enable models with fewer parameters to use additional
computation to approach the generation quality of larger
models. Our contributions are summarized as follows:
• We introduce Loop4D, a framework that combines a
high-fidelity 4D VAE tailored to embodied dynamics with
a recurrent generative backbone.
• We develop a 4D VAE that combines geometry-guided
predictive reconstruction for preserving robot motion
with adapter-based geometry adaptation that retains pre-
trained RGB priors.
• We propose a Dual-Stream Looped Diffusion Trans-
former with robot-region refinement constraints and
adaptive modality updates, improving generation through
recurrent computation without replicating parameters.
2. Related Work
2.1. Generative Latent Representations
Latent diffusion models scale image and video generation
through compact representations [3, 45, 49, 66]. Beyond
reconstruction fidelity, representation alignment and latent
regularization can improve downstream generation [27, 30,
51, 68, 70]. Video autoencoders also explore compatibil-
ity with pretrained generators, generation-aware compres-
sion, and predictive learning [23, 76, 78]. Geometric codecs
and geometry-grounded video representations extend these
ideas to point maps, motion, and 3D-consistent genera-
tion [16, 39, 61, 82]. These studies motivate evaluating la-
tents through both reconstruction and generation. Loop4D
combines predictive RGB reconstruction and geometry-
guided robot-region supervision with residual XYZ adap-
tation that preserves pretrained RGB priors.
2.2. Embodied 4D World Modeling
Dynamic scene methods reconstruct observed motion with
factorized fields or deformable Gaussians [4, 15, 58, 65],
while generative approaches synthesize content across time
and viewpoints [8, 60, 74]. Embodied world models pre-
dict future observations conditioned on instructions or ac-
tions [63, 64, 79, 80].
For manipulation, these predic-
tions must preserve the robot’s appearance and the spatial
structure needed for interaction. Robot4DGen [37] gener-
ates geometrically consistent RGB-D futures for manipu-
lation, whereas PointWorld [21] models action-conditioned

2.3. Looped Generative Computation
Shared-layer recurrence [12, 17, 28, 31, 42, 62] increases
the effective depth while reusing parameters [11, 18]. In
diffusion models, inference-time scaling also includes noise
search and feedback-based refinement [32, 41]. Looped-
DiT [10] repeats Transformer blocks within each denoising
call, using intermediate supervision to support iterative re-
finement. Unlike additional sampling steps, these loops re-
fine hidden states at a fixed noise level. Loop4D extends
this design to coupled RGB and XYZ streams, introduc-
ing robot-region non-degradation constraints and adaptive
modality updates. We examine how these mechanisms co-
ordinate appearance and geometry, and evaluate the trade-
off between generation quality and recurrent computation at
a fixed backbone parameter count.
3. Method
Given an initial RGB-D observation and a text instruction,
Loop4D predicts future RGB frames I ∈RT ×H×W ×3 and
aligned camera-space XYZ maps G of the same shape [37].
As shown in Fig. 2, our 4D VAE uses RGB (EI, DI) and
geometry (EG, DG) codecs to produce aligned spatiotem-
poral latents zI and zG. We first train the RGB VAE, then
initialize and adapt the geometry VAE while preserving its
RGB priors.
With both VAEs frozen, we train a Dual-
Stream Looped Diffusion Transformer to jointly generate
the two latent sequences for decoding.

3.2. Loop4D: Geometry VAE
Pretrained RGB VAEs provide visual priors that we aim to
retain when learning geometry. Rescaling XYZ maps to the
RGB input range [−1, 1] makes them compatible with the
input interface, but does not eliminate the statistical differ-

tuning may weaken these priors, motivating lightweight
adaptation that preserves the RGB backbone while learning
geometric corrections.

3.3. Dual-Stream Looped Diffusion Transformer
Compact models require less memory for their weights,
making them attractive for practical embodied applications.
We use shared-layer recurrence to increase effective depth
at a fixed parameter count, allowing iterative refinement
of internal representations without explicit reasoning to-
kens. For 4D generation, this refinement must also coor-
dinate RGB appearance and XYZ geometry, as both de-
scribe the same evolving scene.
We therefore introduce
a Dual-Stream Looped Diffusion Transformer that cou-
ples modality-specific hidden states throughout the recur-
rent computation as shown in Fig 3.

4. Experiments
We evaluate Loop4D in three main settings: (i) RGB and
geometry reconstruction with the Loop4D VAE; (ii) future
4D video generation on simulated and real-world tasks; and
(iii) simulated grasping using generated videos, following
Robot4DGen [36]. We further examine how VAE adapta-
tion affects generation and how looped refinement affects
quality and computation.
4.1. Experimental Setup
Dataset. We use the Robot4DGen dataset [36], which con-
tains multi-view RGB-D demonstrations of three simulated
and four real-world robot manipulation tasks. We generate
videos at a resolution of 640 × 480.
Metrics. We report rFVD [55], SSIM, and PSNR (dB) for
RGB reconstruction. For video generation, we report gFVD
and gFID [20], excluding the conditioning frame from gen-
eration metrics. Geometry is evaluated using depth AbsRel
and threshold accuracy δ1 (1.25). Simulated grasping is
evaluated by task success rate.
Baselines. For reconstruction, we compare Loop4D with
MotionCrafter [82], HunyuanVideo [26], CogVideoX [66],
LTX-2.3 [35], Wan2.1 [53], Wan2.2 [56], and Robot4DGen
VAEs [36] . For generation, we compare with video gener-
ators Wan2.1 [53], Wan2.2 [56], and LTX-2.3 [35], and 4D
generators 4DNeX [8] and Robot4DGen [36]. We addition-
ally include 4D Gaussian [57] as a reconstruction reference.
For simulated grasping, we compare with Diffusion Policy
(DP) [9], and Robot4DGen [36].

5. Conclusion
We presented Loop4D, combining a 4D VAE tailored to
embodied dynamics with a Dual-Stream Looped Diffusion
Transformer. The VAE preserves motion details and pre-
trained RGB priors, while Loop refinement constraints and
adaptive modality updates guide recurrent refinement with-
out replicating backbone parameters. Experiments demon-
strate high-quality 4D generation and validate Loop4D in
simulated manipulation, highlighting the value of adapting
latent representations and generative computation.
