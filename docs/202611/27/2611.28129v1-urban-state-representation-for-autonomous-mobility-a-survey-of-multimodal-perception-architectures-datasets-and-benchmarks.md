## Abstract
Cities are sensed by many platforms at once: on-board cameras, LiDAR and radar on autonomous vehicles, roadside units at intersections, drones and satellites overhead, and the GNSS traces of millions of trips. Yet most learned representations are built for one platform, one city, and one task. We survey this fragmented landscape through a single lens, the Urban State Representation (USR): a georeferenced, layered, time-varying, uncertainty-aware state that any sensing platform can write to and any downstream task can read from. We (i) formalize USR and five desiderata for it, and relate it to bird's-eye-view (BEV) features, occupancy, vectorized maps, neural fields and 3D Gaussians, scene graphs, road-network graphs, language tokens, and world-model latents; (ii) organize sensing platforms, cross-view (ground-aerial) matching, fusion operators, 3D geometric foundation models, end-to-end and vision-language-action models, and generative world models and data engines in one taxonomy that spans sensors to decisions; (iii) compile 26 urban datasets and simulators across ego, cooperative (V2X), aerial, trajectory, and language settings, with a metric glossary, a cross-benchmark results snapshot, a trend analysis, and a platform-coverage gap analysis; and (iv) review applications and propose, as an outlook, USR-5, a five-test protocol with explicit references and one aggregate score for measuring whether a state is shared, together with a research agenda of six open problems covering cross-city and cross-sensor transfer, uncertainty, efficiency, sim-to-real evaluation, and governance. This survey is an entry point for computer vision, autonomous driving, transportation, and urban computing researchers; the paper list, datasets, and USR-5 code are at https://github.com/binayksingh/Awesome-Urban-State-Representation.
