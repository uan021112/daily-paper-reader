A B S T R A C T
Lightweight 3D building models are crucial for urban spatial analysis, yet existing pipelines typically
involve costly dense scene reconstruction, building extraction, and mesh simplification. While 3DGS
reconstructs scenes more efficiently than conventional multi-view stereo (MVS) pipelines, existing
large-scale methods lack semantic awareness and support for parallel instance-level training, and
Gaussian-based lightweight building modeling remains largely unexplored. In this paper, we propose
SemLite-GS, a building-centric Gaussian splatting framework, which combines semantic selection,
parallel training, and plane-constrained mesh reconstruction to generate lightweight building models
from large-scale UAV scenarios. First, a semantic orthographic-view reference map is generated to
establish globally consistent building identities. These identities are propagated to the input oblique
images to extract camera–instance pairs and corresponding regions of interest (ROIs) for instance-
level optimization. Second, a semantic-selective parallel PGSR rasterizer processes camera–instance
pairs from multiple buildings and views concurrently while restricting computation to relevant image
tiles and associated Gaussian primitives. Surrounding contextual Gaussians are retained for visibility
consistency but excluded from parameter optimization, thereby saving unnecessary computation.
Third, we propose a plane-guided SDF (Signed Distance Function) refinement strategy for lightweight
mesh extraction. Architectural planes, extracted from rendered depth, opacity, and normal maps, guide
SDF optimization to directly generate lightweight meshes that preserve sharp architectural boundaries.
Furthermore, polygon density is adaptively allocated according to geometric complexity, minimizing
redundant tessellation in planar regions while preserving sharp architectural transitions. Experiments
on multiple large-scale UAV datasets demonstrate that our SemLite-GS substantially reduces building
reconstruction time compared with representative full-scene 3DGS methods. With comparable
polygon budgets, SemLite-GS achieves superior preservation of dominant planar structures and
sharp architectural transitions compared with conventional dense reconstruction followed by mesh
decimation. The source code is available at https://github.com/IRIS-LAB-whu/SemLite-GS.
1. Introduction
Lightweight 3D building models, represented as com-
pact, low-poly meshes typically comprising several hundred
faces (Liu et al., 2025b), are essential for efficient urban-
scale representation and spatial analysis (Peters et al., 2022).
Serving as foundational 3D primitives, they support a wide
range of applications, such as digital twins, geographic
information systems, and large-scale visualization (Biljecki
et al., 2015; Schuegraf et al., 2025). Compared to dense
point clouds and highly tessellated meshes, lightweight 3D
building models mainly capture dominant building struc-
tures using far fewer vertices and faces, thereby significantly
reducing storage, transmission, and rendering costs. Over the
past few decades, UAV oblique photogrammetry has been
widely studied and has enabled detailed 3D reconstruction
of large-scale urban environments. Despite these advances,
many practical applications require only a specific subset of
target buildings rather than complete scene models. Con-
sequently, efficiently reconstructing these target buildings
from large UAV image blocks remains a critical challenge.
Conventional photogrammetric workflows typically ad-
dress lightweight building modeling through a sequential
pipeline: dense scene reconstruction, followed by building

In this paper, we propose SemLite-GS, a semantic-
selective parallel Gaussian Splatting framework featuring
structure-aware lightweight mesh reconstruction for large
UAV oblique image blocks. Starting from registered multi-
view UAV images with known camera parameters, SemLite-
GS rapidly initializes a shared scene-level Gaussian rep-
resentation, constructs an orthographic building-instance
map, and formulates each building identity as an explicit
reconstruction target. The orthographic semantic informa-
tion is propagated to the oblique views to establish cam-
era–instance correspondences and define regions of interest
(ROIs) for optimization. Based on these correspondences,
a semantic-selective parallel PGSR optimization strategy
is introduced, wherein multiple camera–instance pairs are
processed in parallel to refine the building instances through
ROI-constrained rasterization and target-selective parameter
updates. While target Gaussians receive direct optimization
signals, contextual Gaussians are retained solely for visibil-
ity consistency. This mechanism enables efficient parallel
reconstruction without the need to repeatedly optimize the
entire scene. Furthermore, architectural planes extracted
from the optimized Gaussian representation are incorporated
as geometric priors into SDF refinement, facilitating the di-
rect generation of lightweight meshes with adaptive polygon
allocation and preserved structural transitions. By integrat-
ing semantic selection during Gaussian optimization and
structural guidance during mesh reconstruction, SemLite-
GS effectively bypasses dense scene reconstruction followed
by generic mesh decimation.
The main contributions of this work are summarized as
follows:
• Semantic-selective Gaussian reconstruction for target-
oriented building modeling: A semantic-selective
Gaussian Splatting framework is proposed to formu-
late individual buildings as explicit reconstruction tar-
gets. By propagating orthographic semantic identities
to oblique views, the framework establishes a camera–
instance observation graph and enables building-
specific Gaussian optimization.
• ROI-aware parallel PGSR optimization with con-
textual visibility preservation: An efficient instance-
level Gaussian training strategy is introduced to pro-
cess multiple camera–instance pairs in parallel within
a shared GPU workload. ROI-local rasterization and
target-conditioned Gaussian updates reduce unnec-
essary computation while retaining fixed contextual
Gaussians for visibility reasoning.
• Plane-guided Gaussian-to-mesh reconstruction for
lightweight structural modeling: A plane-guided
mesh generation strategy is developed by incorpo-
rating architectural planes into SDF refinement. The
proposed strategy enables adaptive polygon allocation
and preserves essential architectural structures during
lightweight model generation.
2. Related Work
2.1. Lightweight Building Modeling
Lightweight building modeling aims to generate com-
pact geometric representations while preserving dominant
architectural structures under constrained polygon budgets.
Existing approaches can be broadly categorized into three
main streams: dense-mesh simplification, primitive-based
reconstruction, and learning-based polygon generation.
Dense-mesh simplification, a widely adopted strategy,
typically reconstructs detailed geometry through structure-
from-motion, multi-view stereo, point-cloud fusion, and sur-
face meshing, followed by polygon reduction (Schönberger
and Frahm, 2016; Schönberger et al., 2016). Classical meth-
ods, such as quadric-error metric optimization (Garland and
Heckbert, 1997) and variational geometric proxies (Cohen-
Steiner et al., 2004), simplify meshes by minimizing geomet-
ric approximation errors. Recent studies further incorporate
structural constraints, including planar adjacency preserva-
tion (Bouzas et al., 2020) and structure-aware edge collapse
(Li et al., 2024), to better retain architectural features. How-
ever, these approaches inherently rely on the availability
and quality of dense geometric reconstruction; consequently,
geometric errors introduced prior to simplification cannot be
recovered during the polygon reduction phase.
Primitive-based methods directly construct compact
building models from geometric abstractions, such as planes,
lines, contours, or spatial cells. Manhattan-world models
represent regular buildings using axis-aligned primitives (Li
et al., 2016), while PolyFit and TopoLAP exploit planar
and linear relationships to generate watertight polygonal
models (Nan and Wonka, 2017; Liu et al., 2019). More
recent approaches extend primitive-based reconstruction to
complex urban scenes through adaptive plane arrangements,
hypothesis selection, and spatial partitioning (Bauchet and
Lafarge, 2020; Fang and Lafarge, 2020; Huang et al., 2022;
Chen et al., 2022; Sulzer and Lafarge, 2024; Bauchet et al.,
2024). Although these methods can generate compact and
structurally meaningful models, they generally require reli-
able geometric observations and explicit primitive extraction
as prerequisites for model generation.
Learning-based methods infer compact building struc-
tures directly from geometric observations. For instance,
Point2Roof predicts roof structures from airborne point
clouds (Li et al., 2022), while Point2Building autoregres-
sively generates polygonal vertices and faces (Liu et al.,
2024b). While learning-based approaches reduce depen-
dence on manually designed geometric rules, they usually
require instance-level geometric inputs and may suffer from
domain gaps when generalizing from training data to real-
world UAV scenes.
Overall, existing lightweight building reconstruction
paradigms predominantly focus on simplifying or generating
compact geometry after suitable building observations have

have already been isolated or that reliable point clouds and
meshes are available. Therefore, they do not directly address

2.2. Gaussian-Based Surface Reconstruction
Recent advancements in Gaussian-based surface recon-
struction have primarily focused on improving geometric
fidelity and recovering accurate surfaces from Gaussian
representations. Existing studies enhance Gaussian-based
geometry by introducing surface-aware representations, geo-
metric regularization, and additional geometric priors, or by
extracting explicit surfaces from optimized Gaussian repre-
sentations. These advances have substantially improved the
capability of Gaussian models, enabling high-quality surface
modeling beyond the original view-synthesis-oriented 3D
Gaussian Splatting.
Representative methods enhance Gaussian geometry
from diverse perspectives. SuGaR encourages Gaussians
to align with underlying scene surfaces and subsequently
applies Poisson reconstruction for mesh extraction (Guédon
and Lepetit, 2024). Gaussian Surfels introduces flattened
elliptical primitives for local surface representation (Dai
et al., 2024), while PGSR flattens anisotropic 3D Gaussians
toward local planes, renders plane-distance and normal
maps for depth recovery, and uses single-view geometric
and multi-view photometric and geometric constraints to
improve surface reconstruction across varying viewpoints
(Chen et al., 2025). Further studies incorporate additional
geometric priors, including depth and normal supervision
(Turkulainen et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2024), to enhance
geometric consistency and surface recovery quality. Beyond
refining Gaussian geometry, several methods have investi-
gated explicit surface or mesh reconstruction from Gaussian
representations. Gaussian Opacity Fields extracts surfaces
through level-set extraction from continuous opacity fields
(Yu et al., 2024), while 3DGSR jointly optimizes Gaus-
sian representations and signed distance fields for surface
reconstruction (Lyu et al., 2024). Geometry Field Splat-
ting reconstructs surfaces from Gaussian-induced geometry
fields (Jiang et al., 2025), and GS2Mesh generates explicit
meshes by rendering and fusing stereo depth observations
(Wolf et al., 2024). These methods significantly improve
Gaussian-based geometric reconstruction and demonstrate
the potential of Gaussian representations for explicit surface
modeling. However, their primary objective remains high-
fidelity surface recovery, focusing on preserving detailed
geometry rather than generating lightweight models with
structure-aware simplification and explicit polygon budget
control. Nevertheless, their success has further motivated
the application of Gaussian representations to structured
environments, particularly architectural reconstruction sce-
narios.
Building upon these foundational advances, recent stud-
ies have extended Gaussian-based reconstruction specifi-
cally to architectural modeling. Gaussian Building Mesh
combines semantic masks with Gaussian geometry for build-
ing mesh generation (Gao et al., 2025a), while GS4Buildings
introduces LoD2-derived depth and normal priors to im-
prove building reconstruction (Zhang et al., 2025). GS2Poly
integrates Gaussian-derived planar primitives with opacity-
field-guided spatial partitioning (Liu et al., 2025b), and SF-
Recon selects informative Gaussians for edge pruning and
Delaunay triangulation (Li et al., 2025). These methods
demonstrate the feasibility of Gaussian-based architectural
reconstruction. However, they generally assume that Gaus-
sian representations for target regions are already available
and primarily focus on geometric recovery or mesh extrac-
tion. The problem of jointly selecting target buildings from
large UAV scenes and generating lightweight meshes with
structure-aware polygon allocation remains insufficiently ex-
plored.
2.3. Scalable Gaussian Splatting
Scalable Gaussian Splatting methods aim to mitigate
primitive redundancy, memory consumption, and optimiza-
tion costs when extending 3DGS to large-scale scenes. Ex-
isting approaches mainly improve scalability through struc-
tured or compressed representations and scene decomposi-
tion strategies.
Structured representation methods organize Gaussians
according to spatial locations or levels of detail. Scaffold-
GS generates view-adaptive Gaussians from anchor points
(Lu et al., 2024), while Octree-GS introduces an octree-
based level-of-detail hierarchy for multiscale representation
(Ren et al., 2025). Hierarchical 3D Gaussians combine in-
dependently optimized scene chunks into a multiresolution
representation (Kerbl et al., 2024). Other approaches directly
reduce representation redundancy. LightGaussian combines
pruning, distillation, and quantization to compress Gaussian
representations (Fan et al., 2024); CompGS encodes Gaus-
sian parameters using compact codebooks (Navaneet et al.,
2024); and EfficientGS controls primitive growth through
selective densification and pruning (Liu et al., 2025a). Al-
though these methods effectively reduce representation or
rendering costs, they generally retain the complete scene as
the reconstruction domain.
Scene-decomposition methods enhance scalability by
dividing large scenes into manageable reconstruction units.
VastGaussian partitions scenes into visibility-aware cells
(Lin et al., 2024), while CityGaussian combines block-wise
training with level-of-detail representations for large-scale
urban reconstruction (Liu et al., 2024a). CityGaussianV2
and CityGS-X further improve parallel optimization and ge-
ometric supervision for city-scale scenes (Liu et al., 2025c;
Gao et al., 2025b). However, this parallelism is organized
according to spatial coverage rather than semantic building
identities, such that a single reconstruction unit may contain
both target buildings and irrelevant surroundings.
To introduce semantic awareness, recent approaches
have incorporated building masks to guide Gaussian op-
timization (Wang et al., 2025; Cheng et al., 2027). For

targeted, instance-level parallel optimization while strictly
preserving contextual visibility consistency.

4.2.1. Orthographic Semantic Anchoring
FastGS (Ren et al., 2026) is trained on the registered
UAV images to efficiently initialize the scene-level Gaussian
representation 0. The imagery provides broad and relatively
uniform coverage for subsequent DOM generation. For sub-
sequent PGSR training, the initialized representation retains
each Gaussian’s center, rotation, three scale parameters,
opacity, and appearance attributes.
Assuming that the world 𝑧-axis represents the vertical
direction, a downward-looking virtual camera is defined in

4.3. Semantic-Selective Parallel PGSR
Building on the camera–instance observation graph,
which provides valid multi-view observations, masks, and
ROIs for the target buildings, SemLite-GS refines multiple
building instances concurrently within a shared Gaussian
representation. Figure 4 illustrates the optimization loop,
comprising three stages. Pair-conditioned Gaussian com-
paction first constructs a compact active set containing the
target and contextual Gaussians required for each sampled
pair. ROI-constrained parallel rasterization then organizes
the Gaussian–tile entries into a shared pair–tile work queue,
enabling concurrent rendering across buildings and cameras
while restricting tile construction, sorting, and pixel evalua-
tion to the corresponding ROIs. Finally, masked supervision
combines the PGSR photometric and geometric terms over
verified target observations with frozen-context protection
at reliable context pixels near building boundaries. Parallel
backpropagation computes pair-local gradients, and only
target gradients are written back to the corresponding scene-
level indices for selective parameter updates. The updated
shared representation is used in the next iteration, enabling
parallel training while preserving contextual visibility.

5. Experiments
5.1. Experimental Setup
5.1.1. Datasets
The evaluation encompasses six large-scale UAV scenes
drawn from GauScene (Xiong et al., 2024), Skylume (Li
et al., 2026), and Tianjin_Xiqing (Lin et al., 2025). LiDAR
reference point clouds are available for the five GauScene
and Skylume scenes, enabling quantitative assessment; Tian-
jin_Xiqing is included only in the qualitative comparisons.
Table 1 summarizes the image count, resolution, and number
of target building instances for each scene. All analyses
are based on the registered multi-view UAV images and
associated camera parameters provided by the datasets.
5.1.2. Implementation Details
All experiments were conducted on a workstation equipped
with a single NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU. For image-based
pipelines, input images from GauScene and Skylume were
downsampled by a factor of eight, whereas those from Tian-
jin_Xiqing were downsampled by a factor of four. SemLite-
GS performs shared scene-level initialization followed by
semantic-selective parallel PGSR training of the selected
buildings.

measured using Chamfer distance (CD), while mesh com-

faces.

5.2. Results and Analysis
The evaluation comprises three parts. First, SemLite-
GS is compared with CityGS-X, CityGaussianV2, PG-
SAG, and Metashape to assess computational efficiency and
lightweight mesh quality. Second, the proposed mesh recon-
struction module is compared with alternative lightweight
reconstruction methods using fixed instance-level Gaussian
representations optimized by SemLite-GS as their geometric
input. Third, ablation studies examine the efficiency and
cross-view consistency of instance-mask propagation, the
contribution of the frozen-context protection loss, and the
effect of parallel camera–instance pair training.

6. Discussion
SemLite-GS depends on the Gaussian representation ini-
tialized by FastGS to render the orthophoto from which glob-
ally consistent building identities are established. Incom-
plete geometry or rendering errors at this stage may leave
buildings undetected or produce incomplete instance masks.
Such errors can propagate to Gaussian labels and camera–
instance observations. Because subsequent semantic-selective
PGSR training operates only on identified instances, build-
ings omitted at initialization cannot be recovered down-
stream. Future work should explore rapid scene-initialization
strategies tailored to orthographic rendering that improve
building coverage and instance-mask reliability while pre-
serving computational efficiency.
Architectural fidelity also remains difficult to assess from
LiDAR point clouds alone. CD quantifies average geomet-
ric proximity but does not explicitly measure planar con-
sistency, boundary straightness, ridge localization, or the
integrity of wall intersections. Consequently, a mesh may
attain a low CD despite fragmented planes or distorted
architectural edges. Future benchmarks should incorporate
expert-constructed lightweight building meshes as structural
references and supplement distance-based evaluation with
measures of planarity, edge and intersection-line deviation,
topological correctness, and mesh compactness. Such a pro-
tocol would provide a more complete assessment of geomet-
ric accuracy and architectural structure preservation.
7. Conclusion
This study presents SemLite-GS, a novel building-centric
Gaussian splatting framework for lightweight building re-
construction from large-scale UAV oblique imagery. SemLite-
GS establishes globally consistent building identities through
orthographically anchored instance propagation. Guided by
these identities, the framework performs semantic-selective
parallel PGSR training while preserving contextual vis-
ibility and generates lightweight meshes through plane-
constrained reconstruction with SDF optimization. Exten-
sive experiments on multiple large-scale UAV datasets
demonstrate that the proposed framework improves cross-
view instance consistency, substantially reduces measured
computational runtime compared to complete-scene 3DGS

Page 22 of 24


pipelines, and achieves a favorable balance among geomet-
ric fidelity, architectural structure preservation, and mesh
compactness under comparable polygon budgets. Ablation
results support the contributions of orthographic anchor-
ing, frozen-context protection, and parallel camera–instance
pair training. The reported results collectively indicate that
incorporating semantic target selection into Gaussian opti-
mization and architectural constraints into mesh generation
provides an effective alternative to dense full-scene mesh
extraction followed by post-hoc mesh simplification for
on-demand lightweight building modeling. In the future,
we will investigate rapid scene-initialization strategies for
more reliable orthographic instance discovery and develop
structural reference meshes and metrics to evaluate the
architectural fidelity of lightweight building reconstructions
more comprehensively.
