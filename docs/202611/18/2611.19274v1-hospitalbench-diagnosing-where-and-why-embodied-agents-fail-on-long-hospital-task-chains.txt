## Abstract 

Long-horizon embodied tasks fail in ways that end-task suc-cess cannot see. HospitalBench is a diagnostic benchmark of ten hospital chores in NVIDIA Isaac Sim, each two un-scored warm-up steps followed by a causal chain of five scored sub-tasks in one continuous simulation: the state one step leaves (an object pose, a choice, a fact seen once) determines what the next step must do, unlike benchmarks that place sub-tasks side by side. Vision-language agents act through skill-level tools from camera images. A scripted robot that makes only the calls a model gets is the solvabil-

1arXiv:2611.19274v1 [cs.RO] 14 Nov 2026 ity check of a chore. From each trajectory the benchmark reads where a chain breaks, what the agent did after a failed call, and how far its own position estimate drifts from the true one. This version reports first results only: 15 of 60 planned runs (three models at high effort, one run each on chores T1 and T10 from both start states and T3 from one), with scripted-robot proofs on three of the ten chores. Of the 15 runs, 2 passed all five sub-tasks. Runs still going after sub-tasks 1 to 5 were 15, 8, 4, 2, 2. Of 189 failed calls, 168 were followed by a different call and 111 by a call that did not fail. These are first results and not a full benchmark. We will release the scenes, task chains, scripted robot, evalua-tion harness and trajectories. 

## 1. Introduction 

Where along a chain of dependent chores does an embod-ied agent’s performance degrade, and why? Long-horizon benchmarks report that success falls as tasks get longer [22, 28], and recent work traces failures to the seams be-tween skills [24, 35], but one end-task score cannot say at which step, at which link or by which kind of failure a chain breaks. A chain shows degradation by carrying only if each step needs the actual result of the one before, or a fact seen earlier: a trolley parked a few centimetres off its mark moves every grasp that follows, whereas a card seen once names the forceps to take and is gone by the time they are needed. HospitalBench is built for this diagnosis: ten chores of five dependent sub-tasks (50 sub-tasks, 40 links) in six simulated hospital areas (Sec. 3, Tab. 2), each link de-pending on the step before it in one of four ways (Fig. 2). A model sends every robot call and a controller executes it. A walk is one model at one effort tier (the reasoning-effort setting of its API call) running the sub-tasks of one chore in order, with no reset (a chain ), from one start state (the ini-tial scene). The scene state is the poses of the robot, objects and people, which every pass term and harm event reads. A walk ends at its first failed sub-task. The number passed before it is its passed in a row score. The scripted robot is a hand-written program that makes only the calls a model makes. The calibration model is the model whose walks were used to choose the version of a chore (Opus 5.5 high), so its results are not held out. Held-out models (GPT-6.1 Terra, GPT-6.1 Sol) were not used for that choice (Sec. 4). 

What we find so far (Sec. 5). This version reports first results only. We ran Opus 5.5 high, GPT-6.1 Sol high and GPT-6.1 Terra high once on each of five cells (chores T1 and T10 from both start states, T3 from start 0), 15 runs of the 60 planned, outside the staged protocol of Sec. 4. The scripted robot has a proof on those chores and starts, and on none of the other seven chores. Two runs passed all five sub-tasks, both on T1 (GPT-6.1 Terra from start 0, GPT-6.1 Sol from start 1). Runs still going after sub-tasks 1 to 5 were 15, 8, 4, 2, 2. Every other run ended at its first failed sub-task, and in 7 of those 13 runs this was the first free sub-task T#.02, never the guided T#.01. On T1, 3 of the 6 runs that reached T1.02 failed it. Two took the forceps that a card shown only in the first picture of T1.01 named to keep, and one never picked either forceps. After a failed call the next call differed in 168 of 189 cases and did not fail in 111. With one run per cell there is no interval and no ranking of models, and these are first results, not a full benchmark. The other seven chores have no model run. 

Failure modes and harm events. We read four failure modes from a trajectory (Sec. 4): compounding errors per link, recovery , state drift and tool-use breakdowns . This version reports the last three from the 15 runs (Sec. 5.1). Compounding by landing position and harm events are de-fined but not analysed here. Harm events (an instrument left off the tray, the robot closing in on a person) are simulator proxies and not injuries. 

Honest scope. No single task is new: every kind of ac-tion in our tasks is in a task prompt of seven existing bench-marks, and five further 2026 benchmarks are in the same comparison (Tab. 1). The design property we add is link dependence, tested link by link (the link test , Sec. 3.2) and through a fact shown once. The link test has been run on 1 of the 40 links, for the scripted robot on an earlier T1 version. The rest have only a paper check on assumed num-bers (Sec. 3.2). Only T1, T10 and T3 have model runs. No method has been trained on HospitalBench, and we do not claim it works as a training environment. 

Contributions. (1) A diagnostic benchmark of ten hos-pital chores, each five dependent sub-tasks in one reset-free simulation, with the kind of each of the 40 links stated, a link kind for a fact shown once, and 36 named harm events (Sec. 3). (2) A staged protocol (stage 0 calibration, an alone check M1, held-out stages 1 to 3) that reports the calibration model apart from held-out models, and the first 15 runs, made outside it (Sec. 4). (3) First results on three chores with per-run records of where a chain ended, what followed each failed call and how far the robot’s position estimate drifted, and a read of the T1.01 →T1.02 link (Sec. 5.1). 

## 2. Related work 

The skills are not new. Every kind of action in our tasks appears in at least one of 983 task prompts we collected ver-batim from seven benchmarks: LIBERO-10 [20], CALVIN [21], EmbodiedBench [34], VLN-MME [40], VLABench [39], BEHAVIOR-1K [17] and LADDER [22] (counts in Tab. 1). Five CVPR 2026 benchmarks are in the table from their first pages only: MANSION [8] (multi-floor scenes), LMEE-Bench [30] (multi-goal navigation, then questions on what was seen), AGENTSAFE [37] (hazardous instruc-tions), LIBERO-Plus [11] (perturbed LIBERO tasks) and CapNav [26] (capability-conditioned navigation). Hospital, surgical and care settings are not new. 

NVIDIA Isaac for Healthcare and its Project Rheo blueprint provide hospital and operating-room digital twins with surgical tray pick-and-place, case cart pushing and tro-car assembly [1, 2, 42]. Rheo’s benchmark snapshot (GR00T-based policies, four scenes of one task) reports no moving people and no frontier-model scores, as does ORBIT-Surgical (14 surgical tasks [38]). SurRoL [32] and SurgVLA-Bench [27] cover surgical-robot and laparoscopic tasks. RCareWorld [36] and Assistive Gym [10] simulate caregiving. Difference: our chores cover hospital logistics and nursing-support work, never touch a patient, and chain five dependent sub-tasks. We make no clinician-shortage claim [42]. 

The stated limits are not new. Moving people are in PARTNR [7], Habitat 3.0 [23] and HA-VLN 2.0 [9]. Scene-changing events are in HAZARD [43], orientation limits in VLMbench [41] and goals with checked tolerances in ARNOLD [13]. We use such limits only as task conditions. How they were chosen is a selection effect (Sec. 7). 

Dependence has been studied. Perturb-and-replay is published [14]. Preceding skills shift the observations of later ones [35]. Skills that succeed from clean snapshots of-ten stall from chained states [24]. Deviation states can be rebuilt by replaying action prefixes [18]. Task-state depen-dencies can be compiled into graphs [29]. Dependent API workflows lose success as chains grow [28]. Difference: we apply perturb-and-replay per link of a benchmark’s chain and add a link that carries a fact seen once. 

Other long-horizon agent benchmarks (LHAW [25], Long-Horizon-Terminal-Bench [19], AutoLab [33], Deep-SWE [15]) evaluate text and code agents on files and out-puts. The scene state a step leaves for the next, and the drift of a robot’s reported pose, are outside what they measure. 

## 3. The benchmark 

Rooms. Each of the ten chores has its own room, built with Isaac Lab-Arena [4] (commit 3e2c70b) and furnished with 74 distinct NVIDIA SimReady assets [6] (of 2,595 asset folders, each at its pinned revision, card revision 4763fbf). 44 keep their real joints (instrument hinges, drawers, doors, brakes, casters), so a step can fail through a moving part, for example forceps opening when gripped away from the joint. Three missing items (hazardous-drug cabinet, sharps container, waste-bag holder) are flagged stand-ins (supple-ment Sec. D, rooms: Fig. S1). Every item to pick fits the 8 cm gripper. 

Robot. A wheeled mobile manipulator (Ridgeback base, 7-DoF Franka arm, parallel gripper opening to 8 cm, a head camera and a wrist camera) in NVIDIA Isaac Sim 6.1.0 [5], build 6.1.0-rc.26, a release candidate: results are tied to this build, and we have not rerun on another. The model sees the sub-task instruction and, at every call, four pictures (head RGB, head depth, arm RGB, arm depth) with the base pose and gripper width. 3.1. Link kinds 

Each link from sub-task k to k+1 is one of three kinds, and may also carry a fourth, and has a stated reason in the task file: 15 of the 16 C links name a hospital rule ( e.g . each re-turned instrument goes to its own outline, in any order), and the 24 V and P links name a physical reason (the forceps lie on the trolley, so they move with every slip of the park). The other C link, T1.04 →T1.05, has no hospital rule: the task file gives only the geometric reason that the scissors outlines beside the straight and the curved forceps outline are 22 cm apart on the count mat. The reasons are in the supplement (Sec. B). V Cart pose. Sub-task k parks a cart, trolley, pole or jack within a band of 8 to 10 cm. k+1 picks or places on it within a re-grip band (the tolerance of its own grasp or push on the result) at most a third of k’s band, so the grasp moves with every slip of the park. C Choice. 

Sub-task k offers two or more valid options and the option taken sets the target of k+1 . The targets are at least 20 cm apart or different objects. P Placement. Sub-task k places within a band of at least 3 cm. k+1 acts relative to the re-sult within a re-grip band of 1 cm or less and a yaw band. 

F Fact shown once. A fact that k+1 needs is shown to the model only during an earlier sub-task, in one picture, and is never restated. The grader reads the choice. In T1, a KEEP card on the Mayo trolley shows which forceps the surgeon keeps, in the first picture of T1.01 only. T1.02 must take the other one. In the runs of Sec. 5 the pictures of earlier calls are not resent, each is replaced by the text [picture from call N not resent] , while the text turns (the model’s own replies and the call results) stay in its history. The KEEP card is therefore visible only at the first call of T1.01, and a model carries the fact to T1.02 only if it wrote it down in a text reply when it saw it. Whether it does is the test. An F link is also a V, C or P link (T1.01 →T1.02 is 

VF ), so the 14, 16 and 10 above are unchanged. Today only T1.01 →T1.02 has such a link. Over the ten tasks the 40 links (four per task) are 14 V, 16 C and 10 P. The per-task strings are in the supplement (Tab. S1). 

3.2. The link test 

For each link from sub-task k to k+1 , the scripted robot completes sub-tasks 1, . . . , k on one start state of the task. Then: • V and P links (a parked cart, a placed item): k’s result is moved inside k’s own pass band in every planar direction (eight for a round band, two for a one-axis band) at two sizes fixed in advance (50% and 80% of the room left after the scripted robot’s landing error), and turned both ways, alone and with the shift. • C links : k takes the other valid option. The link is marked measured only if the fixed form (a replay of the scripted plan for k+1 ) fails in every displaced run, the adaptive form (the scripted robot re-reading the scene) 

passes in every one, and three undisplaced control replays pass. A displaced run counts only if its measured displace-ment matches the written one. Measured: T1.01 →T1.02 has been tested from shoved starts for the scripted robot, over phases of the people schedule, two sizes and all pla-nar directions, on an earlier T1 version. The test has not been repeated on the current version. Paper check only: the other links pass an arithmetic check (the fixed replay misses 

k+1 ’s re-grip band by at least 0.2 cm, or 0.5° for a turn, at both slip sizes) that uses assumed landing, heading, yaw and control-miss numbers (supplement Tab. S15). A paper check is not a measurement. 

## 4. Evaluation protocol 

Walks and start states. A walk runs the sub-tasks of one chore in order in one simulation, from one start state with 5a fixed people schedule, with no redo: it ends at its first failed sub-task (supplement Fig. S2: observations, prompt context, one call, execution, grading). A sub-task passes when the scene state meets its numeric pass terms. Its call cap bounds it. After each passed sub-task the model is told “passed” and nothing else about the grade. Each chore has two public start states (the only ones used for reported walks). A hidden test split is planned and not built. Warm-ups. Two unscored warm-up sub-tasks, W1 and W2, run before T#.01: the model answers a lamp question by press-ing a YES or NO panel, and a right answer to W1 unlocks W2. A warm-up miss makes the run invalid and never a failure. It is re-run once on the same seed, and a second miss is excluded from end-task success. Guided first sub-task. The prompt of T#.01 spells out the scripted robot’s calls and its odometry stop points, so that every model can pass it. Position 1 is therefore reported as guided, and the first free sub-task is T#.02. An API failure makes a walk invalid. It is re-run, never scored as a fail. Passed in a row 

(0 to 5) counts T#.01 and later and is the only score below end-task success. 

Stages. A chore version is chosen and tested in order, and each stage names the model it uses, so that no held-out model shapes the version it is scored on. 

Stage 0 (calibration). Opus 5.5 high, three walks per start state. A version is accepted when the median passed in a row is 1 to 2 of 5 across the start states and the scripted robot passes it on both. The aim is one knob per change be-tween versions, and the ledger shows the rows where several changed at once. The ledger is in supplement Sec. I. These walks are not counted. The calibration model is reported apart from held-out models. 

M1 (alone). Each sub-task that fails in stage 0 is run alone, from the scripted robot’s saved state, with its carried facts stated in one sentence. It must pass for GPT-6.1 Terra or GPT-6.1 Sol at high effort. A sub-task that fails alone is a hard sub-task and not a broken link, and its prompt gets a few facts from the scripted robot’s log (goal values and key waypoints, never the full list of calls) until it passes. Alone runs of the calibration model and Terra set the hint level, so Terra is not independent of M1. 

Stage 1 and 2 (held-out). GPT-6.1 Terra at high effort (stage 1) and GPT-6.1 Sol at high effort (stage 2), one walk per start state at present, on the frozen version. These are the counted walks of the protocol. 

Stage 3 (later). Further models, planned. No open-weight, VLA or GR00T model has run. 

The runs of this paper. The 15 runs of Sec. 5 belong to none of these stages. They are uncounted runs of Opus 5.5 high, GPT-6.1 Sol high and GPT-6.1 Terra high with the same settings for all three models, made under an exception to the stage rule for one set of results. Each is valid: its record is complete and no API failure ended it. A run that ended on an API failure is re-run and never scored as a fail. Only Opus 5.5, GPT-6.1 Terra and GPT-6.1 Sol have run on current versions. A counted walk of the protocol is one that ran to its end on a frozen version from a public start state with a valid record. 

End-task success and intervals. The headline score is the share of counted walks, per model and chore, in which all five sub-tasks pass, printed as x/n . Every interval in this paper is an exact (Clopper–Pearson) 95% interval, and at x = 0 the one-sided bound 1 − 0.05 1/n is used. Every walk logs the harm events of its chore. This version has one run per cell, so a chore and start cell prints a count and no interval, and only counts pooled over a model or over all runs carry an interval. 

Failure measures. Four failure modes and a per-position curve are defined for each trajectory: compound-ing (the pass rate of k+1 given k, by the half of k’s band the result landed in), recovery , state drift and tool-use breakdowns . This version reports the last three and the per-position curve, read from each run’s call log by 

walk analytics.py and collected in the run registry. A failed call is one whose result carries an error, ok false, or a stop that is not arrived or done . Recovery asks what the next call in the same sub-task was: a different call, and whether it failed in turn. A call counts as repeated when its tool and arguments equal an earlier call of the same sub-task. Unstuck is a failed call whose next call did not fail and whose sub-task passed in the end. State drift is the distance between the true base position and the robot’s own odom-etry estimate at the last call. Tool-use breakdowns are the failed calls, counted by tool. The first failed sub-task and the failed grade terms are read from the grader’s log with 

chain grades.py .

Planned and not run. Continue after a fail (the scene is reset to the scripted robot’s end state of the failed sub-task and the walk goes on, as clean-start data for later positions) and a clean-start control (each T#.02 to T#.05 alone beside the chained walk, same model).

## 7. Limitations and impact 

Coverage and statistics. This version holds 15 runs of the 60 planned: three models, one run per cell, on three of the ten chores (T1 and T10 from both start states, T3 from start 0). The other seven chores have no model run, so claims about the ten chores are design claims. No cell has more than one run, so no cell has an interval, and pooled counts such as 2 of 15 end-task passes have wide descriptive inter-vals (1.7 to 40.5%). The runs are uncounted by the proto-col’s rules and were made under an exception to its stage rule. A repeat of a cell may give another result. 

The calibration model is not held out. The T1 version was chosen by running Opus 5.5 high on candidate versions of the ledger (26 versions in all), and Opus 5.5 is also the coding agent that built the benchmark. Its results are re-ported apart in the design and pooled with the other two models only in the counts above. The build ran many pilots and walks of earlier versions that this paper does not report, the T1 and T10 versions and the T10.03 grip rule changed after model walks, and the current T1 was selected among the ledger’s versions, so a result on it is optimistic for an un-related new version. GPT-6.1 Terra is held out of the choice of version, but earlier alone runs of it helped set the hint level of M1. 

Dependence is measured on one link. Of the 40 links only T1.01 →T1.02 has a shoved-start test, only for the scripted robot and on an earlier T1 version. The paper check of the other 39 rests on assumed numbers (supple-ment Tab. S15). The reading of T1.02 in Sec. 5.1 rests on 6 runs, three of which failed the same grade term (two by taking the kept forceps, one by picking neither). It has 8no same-model control run alone and no mitigation exper-iment. Four of the five picks at T1.02 were the curved for-ceps on both starts, so a fixed preference for it would give the same pattern and the runs cannot tell a carried fact from such a preference. 

Alone checks are not reported. A clean control, which runs a sub-task alone beside the chained walk, and continue-after-fail walks are designed and have no data in this ver-sion. 

Baselines and the scripted robot. There is no null agent (done at once, random calls), human or teleoperated base-line. The scripted robot has a proof of the current version on T1 and T10 from both start states and on T3 from start 0, and on no other chore. The T10 proof ran with the route facts given to the robot privately, so it shows that T10 is doable when they are known (supplement Sec. G). 

Graders and thresholds. Harm events are simulator proxies. The frozen T1 grader does not log “instrument off the tray”. Its person rule adds a step’s closing only when the robot’s fastest point moved more than 0.5 mm in that step, so slow approaches add nothing (the T2–T10 worlds use a windowed rule). T5’s vial-break threshold is not calibrated by drop tests. No human has audited a sample of grader verdicts. 

Scope, people and misuse. A controller executes each call, so low-level control is not measured and a miss may come from the controller. The intended pose of a call is not logged, so a model error and a controller error cannot be told apart. No trained VLA policy is scored. T1.01 is guided and a passed sub-task is announced, which a real robot would not get. No task involves a patient. No human subjects took part. Every patient name and record number on a label (T3, T10) is synthetic. A high score is not evi-dence that a robot is safe near staff or patients. Deployment needs clinical validation and regulatory review.

This supplement backs the main paper section by section: the ten tasks, their link strings and the five sub-tasks and link bands of each task (Sec. B), the harm events (Sec. C), the stand-ins (Sec. D), the arithmetic of the paper check (Sec. E), the release block with per-asset licenses (Sec. F), and the per-run records, tool-use counts, scripted-robot proofs and models of the runs of main Sec. 5 (Sec. G). Pic-tures of the built rooms are in Fig. S1 (Sec. B). 

Glossary. Chore (also written task in the T1 to T10 la-bels and task files): one hospital role of five sub-tasks. Sub-task T#.0 k: one graded step. Link : the dependence of sub-task k+1 on k (kinds V, C, P, F). Chain : the five sub-tasks of a chore in order with no reset. Walk : one model’s run through a chain from one start state. Stage 0, M1, stages 1 to 3 : main Sec. 4. Scripted robot : a hand-written pro-gram. The state-reading chain reads simulator state, the 

parity robot uses only a model’s prompt, pictures and his-tory. 

Current versions and what is measured. The findings of main Sec. 5 use the current versions: T1 (vqsnd, a KEEP card in the first T1.01 picture only, T1.02 takes the forceps not kept, two unscored warm-ups before T1.01, the T1.01 prompt guided), T10 and T3. The 26 T1 versions tried are in Sec. I. Walks of earlier versions are not reported in this version of the paper, and no number in it is computed from them. The 15 runs are listed in Tab. S17. They are un-counted runs of one model and one cell each, made un-der an exception to the stage rule (main Sec. 4). The run registry also holds two runs that ended INVALID, both of GPT-6.1 Sol high on T1 start 0 ( m2cal sol t1 s0 r101 

and its re-run r101x ). They are not scored. r101 ended on an OpenAI HTTP 429, the project spend limit. r101x 

crashed in hist run.py on an empty call log after that 429, a fault since fixed. A third run of that cell ( r102 ) is the valid one. It ran on an explicit decision of the authors after the limit was raised, and not under the one-re-run rule of main Sec. 4. Of the 40 links, only T1.01 →T1.02 was measured from shoved starts, for the scripted robot on an earlier T1 version. Every other link check below is arith-metic on stated bands (1 link measured, 39 by paper check only). Scene versions. A task’s scene version ends at the last change to its scene files (UTC, month-day hour:minute): T1 10-31 19:40, T2 11-01 21:35, T3 11-01 21:35, T4 11-01 21:35, T5 11-01 21:35, T6 11-02 10:57, T7 11-01 21:35, T8 11-02 04:18, T9 11-01 21:35, T10 11-01 21:35. A scene edited after its last commit takes the time of the edit, so a cut-off can lie close to the day of writing. The versions of T1, T10 and T3 used for main Sec. 5 are frozen (task hashes 1a930e024e8b, f5f8e55c49a5 and d168bd98e111 of the run set), and the scenes of the other seven chores are not frozen. Time. No sub-task or harm event sets a time limit or a deadline for the agent. The time windows and speeds that tests read from the scene are listed in Sec. C, the other time quantities of the harness in main Sec. 3 and Sec. H. A guard applies to each API call: after four tries a call without an answer makes the walk invalid, which is re-run and never scored as a model failure.

## B. Tasks, sub-tasks and link bands 

Table S1 lists the ten tasks with one letter per link. Ta-bles S2–S11 give, for each task, the five sub-tasks in order, the kind of the link from sub-task k to k+1 and its link band. Two bands are kept apart. A pass band is the tolerance a sub-task’s own text states and its grade tests (T1.04: 3 cm). The re-grip band of k+1 is the tolerance of the grasp, push or read that k+1 makes on the result k left, which the link test must still meet after that result has moved inside k’s pass band. It is tighter and is not k+1 ’s pass band (T1.04 grips the forceps where they lie: 0.8 cm, 2°). The link band of a V or P link is k’s pass band (centre distance in cm, heading in degrees where the item has one) → the re-grip band of k+1 . For a C link it is how far apart the option targets are and the pass band of k+1 . Each link rests on the rule stated below.

Four tests read a time window or a speed from the scene: T1 “instrument off the tray” (an instrument at rest outside for 0.5 s), T5 “vial tipped” (over 30 degrees for 0.5 s), T5 “chemo vial break” (impact speed at first contact) and the person rule (robot motion per physics step, below). The still check of the pass terms is 0.2 s at under 1 cm/s at ev-ery physics step, a maximum and never a mean. The person rule (harm event robot closes on a person ) has two forms in the grader code. (i) The harness rule, checked at ev-ery physics tick of every sub-task (so its sub-task column reads 1–5): the sub-task fails when the robot closes the gap to a person inside 30 cm, defined as more than 5 mm of the robot’s own motion toward her while the gap is under 30 cm (motion counted only on physics steps where a robot point moves more than 0.5 mm, that is 3 cm/s at 60 Hz, so slower motion adds nothing, the gap is measured to the sur-face of a capsule of radius 28 cm, and a closer gap with less robot motion is logged as person-caused and does not fail this rule). (ii) The sub-task grades of the T1.01 push, T10.01 and T10.04 also require the smallest gap over the sub-task to be at least 30 cm whatever the cause. For T1.01 this any-cause term is a design fact of T1, frozen with it: a person who walks into the robot during the push fails T1.01 although form (i) logs that closing as person-caused. The drive call itself starts to brake the base when a robot part is within 0.5 m of a person, at the accel the model sent for that call (0.05 to 2.0 m/s 2, default 0.3), not instantly: from the 0.6 m/s speed cap at the default accel the stop takes 0.6 m, more than the 0.5 m trigger, so the model’s speed and accel decide whether it stops in time. ## E. Link arithmetic used by the paper check 

For a V or P link from k to k+1 , let bk be the position band of sub-task k, ek the landing error of the robot’s action that ends k, bk+1 the re-grip band of k+1 , e′ 

> k+1

the landing error of the replay’s own action at k+1 and m the control miss. A fixed replay of the reference plan for k+1 is aimed at the intended pose of k’s result. The result is moved inside 

k’s own band by s = ρ (bk − ek) with ρ ∈ { 0.5, 0.8} in every direction ( bk − ek is the room left in the band after the landing error ek). The replay then misses k+1 ’s re-grip band by s − bk+1 − e′ 

> k+1

− m, and the link passes the paper check only if that margin is at least 0.2 cm at both sizes. The two landing errors are different quantities: ek limits the shove, e′ 

> k+1

is the error of the replay’s own action. Tab. S15 uses the same value for both (the cart value for a V link, the place value for a P link), so ek appears twice in the numbers but not in the notation. Worked example, V link at 

ρ = 0 .5: 0.5 (8 − 0.7) − 2 − 0.7 − 0.1 = 0 .85 cm. P link: 

0.5 (3 − 0.2) − 0.8 − 0.2 − 0.1 = 0 .3 cm. Turns are checked the same way with heading bands in degrees and a margin of at least 0.5°. For a C link the margin is the separation of the two option targets minus e′

> k+1

, minus m = 0 .1 cm, 17 minus bk+1 , again at least 0.2 cm. The numbers used for all tasks are in Tab. S15. The shoved starts exist only in the link test of the scripted robot. The start states of every model walk are the unshoved public start states.

The heading and yaw landing errors and both control misses are assumptions, to be replaced by measurements when each link is tested. The paper check rests on these assumed numbers and is not a measurement. 

With these numbers all 40 links pass the paper check (14 

V, 16 C, 10 P). For a C link the margin is large by construc-tion (option targets 22 to 300 cm apart), so its paper check only confirms that the options are distinct. Whether the next sub-task depends on the choice is decided by the measured test alone. A paper check is not a certificate: Sec. 3.2 of the main paper describes the measured link test that marks a link measured . One link has it (T1.01 →T1.02, for the scripted robot on an earlier T1 version). All links are re-leased with their mark (Sec. F). 

Is the target of k+1 nominal or recomputed from the landing of k? The text is nominal, the grader’s target is actual. (i) The instruction of every sub-task is the prompt 

string of the task file, read once into a table when the harness starts and sent unchanged when k+1 opens. Its numbers are bands about a mark or an object. (ii) After a passed k the model is told only “Sub-task k was graded: passed”. Afailed sub-task ends the walk, so a “NOT passed” comes only with the end and is never acted on. No landing error is returned. (iii) The grader reads the target from the scene as 

k left it: for example the T1.03 target is the outline centre on the count mat, which follows its trolley (the scripted robot’s tools shift the target by the carrier’s displacement), and the T10.03 grade takes the desk and bed that the model chose in T10.01–T10.02. The model therefore gets no recomputed coordinate. To pass k+1 it must act on what lies in the scene.

Hidden test split (planned and not built). Further start states per task would be kept on a scoring server and never released. The number is not fixed. An entry is an agent that answers the same calls. The server runs its walks and returns x/n per task with the exact bound and the harm events. If a hidden start state leaks, it is retired and replaced, and affected scores are marked. 

Canary and scoring-server terms. Every released file carries one canary string so that its presence in training data can be detected. The scoring-server terms bar training or tuning on the benchmark’s start states, logs or hidden split, and require each entry to state its model version and set-tings. 

Datasheet summary. Motivation: test whether agents that drive a robot act on where the previous step actually left things. Composition: 10 tasks × 5 sub-tasks, 40 links, 36 harm events, 74 SimReady assets plus robot and people models, two public start states per task (a hidden split is planned). No personal data (names and MRNs on labels are synthetic). Collection: scenes built with a floor-plan builder and Isaac Lab-Arena, and walk logs recorded by the harness in simulation. Uses: intended for evaluating and comparing agents in simulation. It is not for certifying robots, staffing decisions or clinical claims. Distribution and maintenance: 

below. 

Maintenance. A version is frozen when its scoring server opens. A fix that changes any grade ships as a new version, and scoring-server rows name the version they were scored on. Bugs are reported through the repository’s issue tracker. The maintainers keep the scoring server run-ning and announce the retirement of a version in advance. 

Misuse. The main risk is misreading: a high score in a simulated hospital is not evidence that a robot is safe near staff or patients, and must not be presented as such. Harm events are proxies on scene state and are no safety certifica-tion.

Time quantities (main Sec. 3). The harness has these and no others: a wait of at most 20 s and a drive to of at most 5 s of simulated time per call, the people’s sched-ules and door cycles (scene properties in simulated time, T1: surgeon at the table’s west side, stepping 0.5 m every 20 s, and circulating nurse crossing the trolley lane for about 6 s every 30 s), the 60 Hz physics rate, a guard on each API call (four tries without an answer make the walk invalid), and windows that tests read on the scene: the still check of the pass terms (0.2 s at under 1 cm/s at every physics step), a 0.5 s settling window (T1 “instrument off the tray”), a 0.5 s tilt window (T5 “vial tipped”), an impact speed (T5 “vial break”) and the per-step motion of the person rule. None limits the time the agent takes: a sub-task is bounded by its call cap, and the API-call guard makes a walk invalid, which is re-run. It never scores a fail. 

Picture lag (main Sec. 3). Under GPU load the render products can return the previous frame or a black one. Since 2 Nov 2026 07:50 UTC the harness re-reads such a picture up to three times and logs each re-read ( FRAME RETRY ). The runs of main Sec. 5 all ran after that date.
