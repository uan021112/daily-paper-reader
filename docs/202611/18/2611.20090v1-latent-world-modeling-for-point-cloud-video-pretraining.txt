## Abstract 

Masked modeling is the dominant self-supervised paradigm for point-cloud videos. However, it leaks the information it is meant to predict, whether it reconstructs masked points as in MAE-style methods or predicts their latent features. Because point-cloud tokens are anchored at points sam-pled from each frame, querying a target at its own posi-tion reveals where its geometry has moved, in reconstruc-tion and latent prediction alike. For latent predictors, ran-dom spatio-temporal masks introduce a further shortcut, as visible frames on both sides of each target allow interpo-lation rather than forecasting. The pretext task thus re-duces to completion. Once the target positions are with-held, a masked latent predictor performs only slightly bet-ter than copying the last frame. To remove this leakage, we propose PointWM, a latent world model that turns com-pletion into forecasting by predicting the features and ge-ometry of future frames from strictly earlier context. Each target is queried only with its last-seen position and times-tamp, while a latent action learned without annotations is meant to capture the part of the future that the past leaves undetermined. On HOI4D action segmentation with a P4Transformer backbone, PointWM improves F1@50 over training from scratch by 24.9 offline and 29.1 under strictly causal inference. These results show the promise of la-tent world models as a leakage-free pretraining paradigm for point-cloud videos. Our code is publicly available at 

https://github.com/InitalZ/PointWM .

On point-cloud videos, however, this positional query turns prediction into completion. In RGB video, patch po-sitions lie on a fixed grid, so the query for a future patch is the same whatever the future holds (Figure 1a). By contrast, point-cloud tokens are anchored at points sampled from the observed surfaces, so a target token’s position is computed from the target frame itself and reveals where its geometry has moved (Figure 1b). We observe this query design in existing latent predictors for point-cloud videos [14]. The resulting trade-off is counterintuitive because a more infor-mative query makes the pretext task easier while weakening the pressure to learn temporal dynamics. Even withholding target positions is insufficient because random target sam-pling across the clip leaves visible context on both sides of a target, allowing interpolation rather than forecasting. Aleak-free objective should therefore predict frames that fol-low the context, with a predictor that knows when it is asked to predict but must infer where the target geometry will be (Figure 1c). This perspective also clarifies existing approaches to self-supervised learning on point-cloud videos (Figure 2). By recovering masked point tubes together with motion proxies such as cardinality differences, trajectories, or lo-cal rotations [11, 23, 29, 33], reconstruction-based meth-ods shape the representation through a hand-designed proxy rather than through the motion itself. Because their de-coders also receive the true positions of masked tubes, they inherit positional leakage [11, 23, 33]. Contrastive methods instead relate clips, frames, or points [16, 24, 39], or con-trast predicted and observed features of later segments [25, 26], but temporal redundancy makes adjacent samples near-duplicates and many negatives ambiguous [26]. Although JEPA-style predictors have reached point-cloud videos [14], they query masked tubes by their true 4D centers and use random masks, inheriting positional leakage and enabling completion rather than forecasting. To our knowledge, no existing objective for self-supervised learning on point-cloud videos forecasts both the features and the geometry of future frames without access to their positions. We introduce PointWM (Point-cloud latent World 

Modeling), a latent world model that forecasts what future frames of a point-cloud video contain and where their ge-ometry moves. To account for what the past leaves un-determined, forecasting is conditioned on a latent action. PointWM predicts a masked, contiguous window of com-plete future frames from strictly earlier context, leaving a one-frame gap so that the tokenizer’s temporal receptive field does not expose the first target frame. From queries that combine last-seen anchor positions with target times-tamps, a shared predictor outputs both the target features 2and residual anchor displacements from the last-seen posi-tions. Because anchors are resampled in every frame, we supervise the geometric output as a set with a symmetric Chamfer objective, without assuming persistent point iden-tities. Withholding target positions makes the task predictive but also ill-posed because the same past can be followed by several futures. A deterministic predictor therefore re-gresses toward their average. To address this ambiguity, PointWM conditions forecasting on a compact latent ac-tion (Figure 3). In this formulation, a posterior infers the action from the change between frame-pooled student fea-tures, while a prior that sees only the context and the fore-cast horizon learns to imagine it. The prior limits what the posterior may encode and is in turn trained to match it, which encourages the action to carry what the past does not determine. Finally, removing the completion objective also removes the implicit regularization it provides. We find that a purely causal objective is prone to collapse, which we counter with variance–covariance regularization on frame-pooled student features. Our main contributions are as follows. • We identify how existing self-supervised paradigms for point-cloud videos rely on hand-designed proxies, am-biguous negatives, or completion under positional leak-age to learn motion without forecasting it. Leak-free fore-casting must model how the scene evolves. • We introduce PointWM, the first latent world model for self-supervised learning on point-cloud videos, which forecasts both the features and the anchor geometry of future frames from strictly earlier context, without access to target positions. • To address the ambiguity of forecasting, we introduce an annotation-free latent action whose context-only prior regularizes the posterior toward what the past does not determine. • Experiments on HOI4D action segmentation and MSR-Action3D action recognition show that withholding tar-get positions turns latent prediction into forecasting. PointWM consistently outperforms training from scratch and leaky masked latent prediction, with a 29.1 F1@50 gain over the former in strictly causal online segmenta-tion. 

## 2. Related Work 

Self-supervised learning on point-cloud videos. 

Reconstruction-based methods extend masked autoen-coding to point tubes with hand-designed temporal proxies such as cardinality differences, motion trajectories, or local rotations [11, 12, 23, 33], but their decoders receive the true positions of masked tubes, leaking target geometry [25]. Whereas contrastive methods discriminate clips, frames, or points [24, 31, 39], contrastive predictive methods go fur-ther by using an autoregressor to predict the latent features of a clip’s last segment from its earlier segments [25, 26]. The proxies used by reconstruction methods shape the representation instead of the motion itself, while temporal redundancy makes the negatives on which contrastive objectives depend near-duplicate and ambiguous [26]. For CPR, embedding the prediction token at the mean position of the target segment also gives its predictions access to the target’s centroid [25]. By comparison, PointWM regresses the latent features and anchor geometry of several future frames without proxies or negatives, using only last-seen anchors and target timestamps to query them. 

Latent prediction and target-position information. 

Joint-embedding predictive architectures predict target rep-resentations from visible context [1, 4] and have been adapted to static point clouds [21, 37] and point-cloud videos. For video, these methods either predict masked point-tube features [14] or distill dense targets from frozen image or video foundation models [20]. Their predictors use position-based queries to locate each target. PointTube-JEPA queries masked tubes by their 4D centers [14], and PointCMP’s regressor likewise receives the positions of masked tokens [24]. Within masked reconstruction of point-cloud videos, DiMP identifies that leaked centers re-duce decoding to local geometric retrieval [38]. It noises the centers and points of masked tubes and predicts the clean centers from visible context, allowing its decoder to query targets with predicted rather than true positions. Yet po-sitional queries leak target geometry to latent predictors, and DiMP only noises the leaked information rather than withholding it. At low noise levels, the noised centers and points still reveal the targets almost exactly (Table 5). Ran-dom tube masking across the clip creates another short-cut for latent predictors such as PointTube-JEPA [14] be-cause visible context surrounding each target in time allows them to recover masked features through completion rather than forecasting. PointWM instead withholds target posi-tions entirely, predicting only frames that follow the con-text and forecasting where their geometry moves. Because its queries use only last-seen anchors and target timestamps, no part of them is computed from the target. 

World models and latent actions. Developed for pixels and control [2, 10], latent world models predict future states conditioned on actions. When action annotations are un-available, latent actions can be inferred from consecutive observations [5, 6, 22, 30]. These world models, however, operate on images or videos whose tokens lie on a fixed grid with persistent identities. Both assumptions break in point-cloud videos, where target positions leak future ge-ometry and anchors are resampled in every frame. 4.1. Experimental Setup 

Datasets. HOI4D [17] has 2,971 training and 892 test egocentric hand–object videos of 150 frames, labeled per frame with 19 actions. We hold out 10% of the training videos for model selection and pretrain on the rest with-out labels, so no test video is seen in pretraining. MSR-Action3D [15] has 567 videos of 20 full-body actions, split by subject into 270 training and 297 test videos. 

Implementation. We pretrain on 32-frame clips for 100 epochs with AdamW [19], batch size 64, and weight decay 0.05. The learning rate warms up over 15 epochs to 5×10 −4

and cosine-decays to 10 −6. Fine-tuning runs end to end for 30 epochs. The backbone is P4Transformer [7] unless noted, and all models are trained on four A800 GPUs. 

Baselines. Scratch is the same backbone without pretrain-ing. Controlled variants differ from PointWM only in the pretext objective. Block trains with block masks and Leak 

adds causal masks, both querying targets at their true posi-tions. Last keeps both mask types but queries every target with the anchors of its nearest preceding visible frame, re-moving only the leakage.

## 5. Conclusion 

In this paper, we present PointWM, the first latent world model for self-supervised learning on point-cloud videos, which turns masked latent prediction into forecasting. Querying future frames with last-seen anchor positions and target timestamps instead of their true coordinates, PointWM predicts their latent features and anchor geom-etry from strictly earlier context, so the predictor must infer where the scene moves rather than read it off the query. A key enabler is the annotation-free latent ac-tion, whose context-only prior encourages the posterior to encode what the past leaves undetermined. On HOI4D and MSR-Action3D, PointWM substantially improves over training from scratch and over leaky masked latent predic-tion in offline and strictly causal online action segmentation 8and in cross-dataset action recognition. One limitation lies in the learned prior, whose forecasts still fall well short of those from the oracle posterior, so the ambiguity of an un-observed future is only partly resolved. As an initial explo-ration of latent world modeling for point-cloud videos, we expect PointWM to serve as a baseline for forecasting-based representation learning.

## A. Implementation Details 

A.1. Architecture and Pretraining 

Unless noted otherwise, our models use a P4Transformer backbone [7]. They use a P4DConv point-tube tokenizer followed by a transformer over (x, y, z, t ) tokens. Table S1 lists the complete pretraining configuration. PointWM is pretrained on unlabeled HOI4D sequences. No video of the official HOI4D test split is used in pretraining. Pretrain-ing clips are centered by the clip-wide centroid and scaled by the clip-wide maximum radius before tokenization. On-line segmentation (Table 4) uses the raw HOI4D coordi-nates without this normalization. During pretraining, each frame is resampled to 2048 points. The normalized clip is then augmented with a single isotropic scale factor drawn from [0 .9, 1.1] , a rotation about the vertical axis drawn from 

[−15 ◦, 15 ◦], and per-point Gaussian jitter with σ = 0 .005 ,clipped to ±0.02 . The tokenizer repeats boundary frames for one-step temporal padding, preserving T ′ = 32 anchor frames. All target-sampling indices below are zero-based. The latent action is linearly projected and added to every target query of its anchor (Eq. 2). The IDM qψ applies a LayerNorm and a two-layer MLP (width 256, GELU) to δt.Two linear heads then output the mean and log-variance of a diagonal Gaussian over at ∈ Rda , with the log-variance clamped to [−8, 4] . The prior pω applies a LayerNorm and a linear layer of width 256 to sc, the layer-normed and frame-pooled context feature of the last context anchor tc. It adds a learned embedding of the horizon k = t−tc, with one entry per integer k. A GELU, a linear layer, and a second GELU follow, then mean and log-variance heads like those of the IDM. These heads are zero-initialized, so the prior starts at 

N (0, I). The prior term Lp has no free-bit threshold and does not send gradients into the posterior. The prediction loss can still update the prior on clips whose action is re-placed by the prior mean. Algorithm S1 summarizes one pretraining step.

## B. Evaluation Protocols and Metrics 

B.1. Forecasting Probe 

Each frozen pretrained model is probed on the HOI4D test split. The probe uses 200 unaugmented 32-frame clips. They are taken at evenly spaced positions in the list of all windows cut from the test videos with stride 16. The predic-tor receives the first 25 anchor frames as context and fore-casts the six frames after a one-frame gap, queried with last-seen anchors. Models with a latent action are conditioned on the prior mean μpt . Targets are each model’s own full-clip teacher features ht,j (Eq. 1), paired by slot.

It reports the Chamfer distance between forecast and true anchors, which, unlike the feature metrics, measures physical displacement. 

C.2. Seed Variance of the Ablations 

Table S8 repeats Table 5 with the standard deviation over three seeds. The gains from removing leakage and prevent-ing collapse are large relative to the seed spread. Differ-ences in effective rank and MSR-Action3D accuracy be-tween nearby rows overlap within one standard deviation and should be read as descriptive trends rather than estab-lished significance. 

C.3. Hyperparameter Sensitivity 

Table S6 varies one hyperparameter of PointWM at a time. Intermediate horizons and a compact action space give the strongest overall trade-off: K = 4 remains close to 

K = 6 , while shorter or longer target windows reduce trans-fer. Raising da beyond 8 brings no consistent benefit, with 

da = 16 slightly improving Seg 10% but lowering skill and online F1@50. A modest prior-mean replacement proba-bility helps; removing it weakens the context-only forecast. Loss weights within a factor of two retain most of the gain, whereas excessive frame regularization degrades transfer. Small differences in this sweep do not establish an optimal setting. 

C.4. Label Efficiency and Controlled Comparison 

Table S7 varies the fraction of labeled HOI4D training videos for offline segmentation. Table S9 completes the controlled comparison of Tables 2 and 3 with all variants on P4Transformer. PointWM leads at every label fraction, and its 10%-label F1@50 of 69.2 exceeds Scratch with all labels by 11.0 points. With all labels, Last improves on both completion objectives across the downstream metrics, and PointWM adds a further 4.7 F1@50 over Last.

Sec. 4.5 reports that normalizing pretraining clips with context-only statistics barely changes forecast skill. Ta-ble S10 adds the downstream metrics. 

## D. Limitations 

Prior–posterior gap. At t+6 , the forecast skill with the context-only prior is 0.45, against 0.61 with the oracle pos-terior (Table 6). The prior thus resolves only part of the ambiguity of an unobserved future. 

Feature-space evaluation. Skill and ∆R2 are measured within each model’s own teacher space, so they compare forecasting across models rather than physical motion. Ta-ble S5 complements them with anchor geometry. 

Approximate correspondence. Because FPS is rerun in every frame, slot j follows a surface region only approx-imately. Llat pairs slots by index and tolerates this drift, whereas geometry is supervised as a set. Fast motion may break the approximation. 

Pretraining data. PointWM is pretrained only on HOI4D, which contains egocentric hand–object interac-tions. Scaling to larger and more diverse point-cloud videos is left for future work. 15
