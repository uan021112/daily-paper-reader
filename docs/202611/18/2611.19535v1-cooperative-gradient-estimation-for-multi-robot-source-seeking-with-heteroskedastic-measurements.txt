# Cooperative Gradient Estimation for Multi-Robot Source Seeking with Heteroskedastic Measurements 

Alessandro Renzaglia a, Lara Briñón-Arranz b

Abstract 

This paper proposes a novel approach for localizing the source of a three-dimensional scalar field using a team of heterogeneous cooperative mobile robots. Each robot measures the field intensity at its location and shares the information within the team. Measurement noise is explicitly considered, and heterogeneity arises from robot-dependent sensing accuracies, leading to heteroskedastic data. The pro-posed framework combines a weighted centroidal Voronoi tessellation-based for-mation strategy, defined on a spherical surface and parameterized by sensor noise variances, with a Weighted Least Squares estimator to compute the field gradient at the formation center. By explicitly accounting for sensor heterogeneity in both formation design and estimation, the method achieves robust gradient estimation under noisy measurements. In particular, we show analytically that this construc-tion is nearly optimal. The covariance of the gradient estimate is derived in closed form, providing a lower bound on the attainable estimation variance for any for-mation on the sphere, and the proposed tessellation is shown to asymptotically satisfy the conditions under which this bound is attained. The estimated gradient is then exploited within a gradient-ascent strategy to guide the team toward the source location. Simulation results demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed approach compared to alternative strategies and highlight its intrinsic ability to re-configure online in response to robot failures, thereby enhancing its adaptability. 

Keywords: Sensor Networks, Gradient Estimation, Multi-Robot Systems, Source Seeking 

1. Introduction 

Cooperative source seeking is a critical challenge in multi-robot applications due to its importance in various scenarios, including search and rescue missions, arXiv:2611.19535v1 [cs.RO] 14 Nov 2026 environmental monitoring, and pollution source detection [1], [2], [3]. In this context, an unknown source generates a spatially distributed signal field. A team of autonomous robots, equipped with sensors capable of measuring the signal in-tensity at their locations, works together to localize the source by leveraging the collected information. Despite being a widely studied problem in the literature, efficient and accurate source localization based only on sparse and noisy field in-tensity measurements remains a challenging problem. As discussed in more detail in the following section, most existing solutions focus on two-dimensional envi-ronments and often do not analyze the impact of measurement noise. When noise is considered, it is always assumed that the measurements collected by the robots have equal variance, i.e., the collected data are homoscedastic. This is not always the case in many real-world applications, where heterogeneous robots may be equipped with sensors of different quality and characteristics. For example, a few robots equipped with a high-quality, high-value sensor that provides very accurate measurements can be deployed together with robots equipped with cheaper, less accurate sensors. In this paper, we translate the source localization task into the problem of accu-rately estimating the gradient of the generated scalar field from sparse and noisy measurements. Since the reliability of the collected heteroskedastic data varies across sensors, this heterogeneity can significantly affect the quality of the esti-mated gradient and, consequently, the accuracy of the final source localization. Therefore, such information must be taken into account not only in the estima-tor design but also during the robots’ planning phase. In particular, determining where each robot should collect its measurements becomes a key decision for maximizing the information gathered at the team level. To this end, this work incorporates sensor heterogeneity directly into the design of the measurement lo-cations and the coordination strategy. More precisely, we aim to answer the fol-lowing question: How should a heterogeneous team of robots be coordinated so that their sensing capabilities are exploited to achieve reliable configurations for efficient and robust source localization? The main contribution of this paper is to present a new approach to address this issue and to perform the source search within a 3D signal field with a multi-robot system collecting heteroskedastic measurements. The main novelty of this solution is to propose a coordinated measurement strategy based on multi-robot formations obtained via weighted centroidal Voronoi tessellations on spherical surfaces. The weights, which are related to the noise variance of each robot, allow the formation to incorporate the heteroskedasticity of the data and reflect it in the geometric properties of the multi-robot configuration. As a result, the formation 2presents a different density of robots depending on sensor accuracy, providing higher spatial coverage near less accurate, and therefore less reliable, sensors. The data collected from this formation are then used to obtain a gradient estimate using a weighted least squares method. Furthermore, we provide an analytical justification of the resulting design in terms of estimation accuracy. We derive a closed-form expression for the covariance of the gradient estimate and a cor-responding lower bound on the estimation variance attainable by any formation on the spherical surface, together with the two geometric conditions under which this bound is reached. We then prove that the weighted tessellation satisfies both conditions, up to an approximation error that vanishes as the team size grows, and verify numerically that the resulting formation lies within a few percent of the bound already for small teams. Additionally, a key feature of the proposed frame-work is its intrinsic reconfiguration capability. Changes in team composition or sensor variances are directly reflected in the weighting function defining the CVT, leading to a modified set of target measurement locations. As a result, the sens-ing formation is automatically reshaped through the formation controller without requiring explicit fault handling or redesign. 

1.1. Related Work 

Source seeking with autonomous robots has been extensively studied in recent years, with numerous solutions proposed for both single and multi-robot systems. However, the majority of these approaches are limited to 2D search spaces, while solutions for 3D environments are still scarce. Existing solutions can be broadly categorized into two classes: gradient-free and gradient-based approaches. Gradient-free methods have gained popularity due to their simplicity and ro-bustness in environments where gradient information is difficult to obtain, for instance, due to discontinuities in the signal. In this context, a significant body of work has focused on Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) algorithms. For in-stance, [4] and [5] proposed PSO-based strategies for efficient source localization in 2D environments. [6] introduced an adapted version of the Quantum Particle Swarm Optimization (QPSO) algorithm, which proposed leading-following be-haviors in a swarm-based approach to improve search efficiency in 2D spaces. PSO-based solutions, however, can become unstable when individual field mea-surements are unreliable due to high levels of noise, especially with small-sized multi-robot teams. Single outliers can indeed corrupt personal and global best positions, misleading the entire team and potentially causing the optimization to stagnate or induce significant oscillations. Other gradient-free approaches draw inspiration from biological systems. A method inspired by schools of fish, where 3robots adjust their speed based on environmental cues to locate darker areas, is presented in [7]. Similarly, in [8], a novel flocking control strategy is proposed for multi-robot exploration and gas field mapping within a 2D framework. Building on this idea, [9] presents a collaborative gas source localization strategy employ-ing networked nano-drones operating in unknown and cluttered environments, achieved through hybrid integration of Adaptive Robotic Particle Swarm Opti-mization (ARPSO) with Bidirectional Brain Emotional Learning (BBEL). The use of multi-robot formations is then treated in [10], which proposed a gradient-free approach addressing source localization and formation control for ground robots. Communication can also be a crucial aspect in multi-robot strategies, and [11] introduced a consensus-like perception algorithm that allows estimating the source location without explicit communication. The second class of solutions leverages an explicit gradient estimation of the sensed field to guide robots toward the source. A key challenge in these ap-proaches is obtaining a reliable estimation from sparse and noisy measurements. To do so, two main, often interconnected, problems usually arise: i) how to get the most accurate estimate of the gradient given a set of sparse noisy measurements at a given time, and ii) where to locate the robots to obtain the set of measurements that will provide the best gradient approximation, given the estimation method. Several techniques have been proposed to address this issue, including consensus-based algorithms [12], cooperative Kalman filters [13], and Least Squares meth-ods [14]. However, these solutions often focus on the estimation problem, over-looking the study and optimization of sensor placement, or present approaches whose use is limited by strict requirements on specific robot formations, which are only defined for a limited number of robots, as in [12], hence reducing their applicability and robustness to robot failures. To address these limitations, some works have proposed multi-robot forma-tions with specific geometric constraints to ensure reliable gradient estimation with proved guarantees [15, 16]. In [17], the authors showed that more generic swarm configurations can be adopted to estimate an ascending direction instead of the exact gradient. While most of these works focus on 2D environments, a few have explored 3D scenarios. In [16], we introduced 3D symmetric multi-robot formations that enable the estimation of both the gradient and the Hessian of the field using simple averages of robot measurements and their relative positions. However, this approach requires an even number of robots and a centralized archi-tecture. In a more recent work [18], we overcome these limitations by proposing a new class of formations based on Voronoi partitions over a spherical surface, enabling more flexible and scalable solutions. However, this solution remains 4suitable only for homogeneous sensing noise. A third, more recent line of work replaces the explicit estimation step with a learned one. Reinforcement learning has been applied to derive source-seeking policies directly from sequences of measurements [19], while Gaussian-process regression and Bayesian optimization have been used to build a probabilistic model of the field and to select informative sampling locations [20, 21]. Neural-network field approximation has also been combined with formation control for multi-robot source localization [22], although this line of work assumes a deterministic field with bounded approximation error rather than a stochastic, heteroskedastic measurement model. These approaches are attractive precisely when the structure of the field or the statistics of the measurement noise are unknown a priori, since they do not require an explicit model of either. The counterpart is the need for training data or for an exploration phase, and the loss of the closed-form error characterization that a model-based estimator provides. The approach proposed here represents a complementary situation, where the noise statistics are assumed known, and this knowledge is exploited to obtain both an analytically character-ized estimator and a formation design that is provably close to optimal. The work presented in this paper represents, to the best of our knowledge, the first solution that explicitly considers a heterogeneous system collecting het-eroskedastic measurements in a 3D environment and proposes a novel integra-tion of a robust gradient estimation scheme with a formation control strategy that adapts to the differences in measurement noise variance.

Voronoi-based Gradient Estimation 

A standard approach to locate the signal source based on noisy measurements of its intensity is to first cooperatively estimate the gradient of the field at a given point c. The gradient estimate, denoted by b∇σ (c), thanks to Assumption 1, can indeed be used to guide the multi-robot formation via a classic gradient-ascent (GA) method [14], [24], [15]. By estimating the gradient at the center of our multi-robot formation, we can define its motion as: 

c(k + 1) = c(k) + ε b∇σ (c(k)) (2) where c(k) ∈ R3 represents the formation center at iteration k and ε > 0 is the step size of the GA algorithm. It could be argued that, in real-world scenarios, Assumption 1 might not al-ways be valid, and local irregularities in the field could prevent the existence of a well-defined gradient at all times. However, these local effects can be mitigated, for instance, with the inclusion of an inertia term that could allow the formation center to maintain its motion in the absence of a gradient estimate, whether due to missing measurements or abrupt fluctuations. This inertia, or an exploratory motion as done in [25], would help overcome short-term instabilities until new, stable gradient information becomes available. In this section, we present the proposed approaches to obtain a robust gradient estimate b∇σ given a set of sparse heteroskedastic measurements and to define suitable multi-robot formations to collect the required data from suitable locations that take into account the heterogeneity in the sensors’ noise.

2.2.2. Weighted Voronoi-based Multi-Robot Formation Generator 

The following problem to solve is how robots decide where to collect measure-ments to feed the gradient estimation module. In our scenario, this is equivalent to deciding how to define the multi-robot formation. Several works in the literature 8studied optimal N-robot configurations, trying to minimize the estimation error. Even though an optimal solution is, in general, too hard to obtain, especially in 3D dimensions, it has been shown that uniformly distributed formations with robots placed at a fixed distance from a formation center are suitable solutions to obtain robust estimates, such as in [24], [16], [12]. However, most of these solutions either work only for a specific number of robots or are built on heuristics that ob-tain a good spatial distribution, but without being based on any formally defined criteria to optimize. 4. Discussion 

This section discusses the implications of the proposed approach, analyzes the observed performance, and highlights its limitations and potential extensions. 

4.1. Impact of Heteroskedastic Measurements on Gradient Estimation 

The simulation results presented in the previous section confirm that explic-itly accounting for heteroskedastic measurement noise significantly improves the accuracy and robustness of gradient estimation. By weighting both the formation design and the estimation process according to sensor-dependent noise variances, 27 the proposed approach prioritizes reliable measurements while reducing the in-fluence of noisier sensors. This effect is particularly evident when comparing the proposed strategy with unweighted or homogeneous formations, where estimation accuracy degrades noticeably in the presence of heterogeneous sensing capabili-ties. In particular, we showed how the gap to the uniform formation increases with sensor disparity (Table 2) and is most pronounced for small teams, while it decreases as the team grows, since the weighted moments of any well-distributed large formation progressively average out. These observations support the central premise of this paper: in multi-robot source-seeking problems, estimation quality and robot deployment must be jointly considered rather than treated as independent components. The Weighted-CVT provides an adaptive mechanism to couple estimation objectives with formation control. Unlike fixed or heuristic formations, the proposed method adapts the spatial configuration of the robots to the statistical properties of the sensors, re-sulting in measurement locations that are better suited for gradient estimation. More specifically, as shown in Section 2.2.3, the resulting formations satisfy the optimality conditions derived in Proposition 2 as a structural consequence of their construction, and Section 3.1 confirmed numerically that they remain within a few percent above the theoretical lower bound of eq. (20), against excess values of above 15% for the uniform formation under the same conditions (Table 1). These considerations can also be applied to different cooperative, sensing-based, multi-robot strategies. Although noise models are typically used to design improved estimation approaches, they are rarely leveraged explicitly for the coordination and control of robots. 

4.2. Reconfiguration and Adaptability to Robot Failures 

One of the key strengths of the proposed framework is its intrinsic ability to reconfigure online in response to changes in team composition, such as robot failures. When a robot becomes unavailable, the formation automatically adapts through the same weighted CVT mechanism without requiring explicit fault de-tection, task reassignment, or redesign of the control architecture. The presented failure scenarios demonstrate that, after a transient phase, the formation converges to a new equilibrium configuration and the gradient estima-tion accuracy recovers sufficiently to resume effective source-seeking behavior. This highlights an important property of the approach: the ability to adapt emerges naturally from the formulation rather than being enforced through additional su-pervisory layers. 28 4.3. Assumptions on Sensor Noise Modeling 

In conclusion, it is important to note that the proposed framework assumes the noise variance associated with each sensor is known a priori and remains con-stant during operation. Such variances can be obtained through prior calibration or manufacturer specifications. However, sensor noise characteristics may evolve over time due to environmental conditions, hardware degradation, or partial sen-sor failures. In such cases, a mismatch between the assumed and actual noise variances may reduce the effectiveness of the weighting strategy and lead to sub-optimal formation configurations or biased gradient estimates. Therefore, an important extension of this work would be to incorporate online estimation or adaptation of the sensor noise variances. For instance, residual-based approaches could be employed to detect inconsistencies between predicted and observed measurements, enabling the weights used in the formation and esti-mation stages to be updated online. Such mechanisms would allow the framework to adapt not only to changes in team composition but also to evolving sensor reli-ability. 

5. Conclusions 

This paper presented a novel solution for locating the source of a signal prop-agating in a three-dimensional environment using a heterogeneous team of robots equipped with signal strength sensors affected by Gaussian noise with different variances. The proposed multi-robot formation strategy leverages a centroidal Voronoi partition based on distances weighted by measurement noise, allowing the system to self-organize into a non-uniform configuration that incorporates sen-sor heterogeneity. To demonstrate the advantages of these formations, we derived a closed-form expression for the covariance of the resulting gradient estimate, to-gether with a lower bound, valid for any formation on the sphere, on the total variance of that estimate. We then showed that the proposed Weighted-CVT for-mation satisfies the associated optimality conditions up to an error that vanishes with the team size, a result then confirmed numerically even for small teams. Data collected from this optimized formation are then fed into a Weighted Least-Squares (WLS) estimation method, resulting in a robust and reliable estimation of the signal gradient at the center of the formation, which is then used to guide the robots toward the source. Simulation results demonstrated that the proposed approach successfully drives the multi-robot system to locate the source of the field. Furthermore, a compara-tive analysis showed how this solution significantly outperforms alternative multi-29 robot formation strategies in both convergence speed and accuracy. We finally demonstrate the applicability of our solution for robotics systems by presenting a realistic simulation incorporating the dynamic model and a controller designed specifically for a group of UAVs. These results also showed the intrinsic ability of the proposed formation control strategy to reconfigure online in response to unforeseen situations. For future work, we aim to improve our solution by incorporating a strategy that recursively estimates the confidence weights in the WLS problem to indi-rectly capture variations in the sensor variances, which could be caused by sensor deterioration, malfunction, or model errors. This would also trigger dynamic re-configurations of the multi-robot formation, as in the case of a robot’s failure shown in Section 3.4.
