## Abstract 

Open-vocabulary 3D understanding aims to localize and segment arbitrary objects beyond predefined categories. However, existing approaches based on Gaussian Splatting often rely on expensive preprocessing and lengthy train-ing, limiting their applicability when fast and lightweight solutions are desired. We propose a lightweight, training-free framework for query-driven 3D instance segmentation on Gaussian Splatting. Instead of conventional mask-wise CLIP embeddings, we use dense pixel-level CLIP features to preserve fine-grained semantic information and enable flexible text-driven localization without relying on prede-fined instance proposals. Given a text query, we identify a high-quality target region in a carefully selected pivot view and propagate it across views using a video segmen-tor, providing consistent and comprehensive coverage of the target instance. We then lift the resulting 2D tracklets to 3D Gaussians through a novel visibility voting strategy, yielding accurate 3D segmentation masks with clean ob-ject boundaries. Extensive experiments demonstrate state-of-the-art performance on LERF-OVS, ScanNet, and Scan-Net++, competitive results on 3D-OVS, and significant speedups over existing open-vocabulary 3D segmentation methods. These results highlight the effectiveness of our framework for efficient open-vocabulary 3D understanding without per-scene training.

While this approach sig-nificantly accelerates scene-level understanding, the lifting process itself still requires over 10 minutes per scene to fuse features across the entire scene, even when only specific objects or regions are of interest. This leads to redundant computation and memory overhead. In real-world applications where users demand iterative interaction with a specific set of objects of interest, each query must be processed efficiently to provide immediate feedback. Users often need to identify or interact with ob-jects right after reconstruction, rather than waiting through hours of preprocessing or optimization. To characterize this latency, we introduce the concept of Time-to-First-Query (TTFQ) – the elapsed time between loading a re-constructed vanilla 3D scene and enabling the first open-vocabulary query and segmentation results. A low TTFQ is critical for responsive, scalable, and user-centric 3D sys-tems, where fast turnaround directly translates to better us-ability and deployment feasibility. Motivated by the need to minimize TTFQ for instant responsiveness, we propose a training-free query-wise framework for open-vocabulary 3D segmentation on Gaus-sian Splatting scenes. Designed with high flexibility archi-tecture, the framework can seamlessly integrate different vision-language and video segmentation backbones without retraining, making it adaptable to evolving vision founda-tion models without structural modification. Given a re-constructed 3D scene represented by Gaussian primitives, we perform 3D instance segmentation from a text query without requiring any additional training. Particularly, our method directly localizes and propagates relevant 2D re-gions associated to the text query across camera views of the scene. These propagated masks are then lifted into 3D through a lightweight visibility voting mechanism, pro-ducing precise and complete 3D segmentation with clean boundaries under one second per query. This lightweight, training-free design circumvents the heavy preprocessing required by previous pipelines and enables interactive open-vocabulary 3D segmentation on resource-limited use cases. More importantly, we demonstrate that such query process-ing leads to state-of-the-art segmentation accuracy while preserving low query latency. Our contributions are: • A novel query-wise approach for open-vocabulary 3D in-stance segmentation in Gaussian Splatting scenes, shift-ing from heavy precomputing scene-level semantics to lightweight precomputing and focusing on on-demand query-driven segmentation. • A dense pixel-level CLIP features that preserve fine-grained semantics beyond mask-level embeddings, en-abling flexible localization of arbitrary object regions without predefined masks or feature distillation. • A novel and robust multi-view 2D instance localization strategy that maps text queries to image regions via a con-volutional flood-fill algorithm and propagates them across views for geometrically consistent, spatially complete 3D segmentation. 2• A lightweight visibility voting mechanism that aggregates multi-view 2D instance regions to construct an occlusion-aware 3D instance mask, enabling real-time rendering while maintaining high-quality segmentation with mini-mal computational overhead. 

## 2. Related Work 

Visual Foundation Models (VFMs) have greatly ad-vanced open-vocabulary recognition and dense perception. CLIP [39] provides strong open-vocabulary understand-ing, while DINO [4] improves dense visual representa-tions and Grounding DINO [28] combines them for phrase-level grounding. Other works [9, 32] extend VLMs to de-tection and reasoning. However, many such methods al-ter CLIP during pretraining, reducing compatibility with existing open-vocabulary systems. ProxyCLIP [23] and DeCLIP [45] instead improve dense prediction while pre-serving CLIP compatibility. For video object segmenta-tion, SAM [22], SAM 2 [40], and HQ-SAM [17] pro-vide strong baselines for robust segmentation and track-ing. Efficiency-focused variants [48, 54, 55] further accel-erate SAM-based tracking with lightweight designs. Lever-aging advances in vision–language models and video seg-mentation, we build a framework that integrates efficient open-vocabulary models with tracking-based segmentors and 3D cues to achieve precise and temporally consistent 2D instance localization. 

Open-Vocabulary 3D Instance Segmentation was first explored using dense point clouds [10, 11, 34–36, 44]. These approaches typically operate on RGB-D data and have demonstrated strong open-vocabulary understanding capabilities. However, the growing adoption of radiance-field-based scene representations has motivated the devel-opment of methods that leverage multi-view RGB images to achieve richer visual appearance and more efficient scene reconstruction. Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) [1, 2, 31, 33] and Gaussian Splatting [12, 15, 18, 41, 49] enable high-quality 3D reconstruction from multi-view RGB im-ages. However, their radiance-field-based representations differ substantially from point clouds, introducing a dis-tribution gap that motivates dedicated methods for open-vocabulary 3D understanding in these reconstruction frame-works. Early NeRF- and Gaussian-based approaches [3, 19, 20, 24, 27, 37, 38, 43, 47] formulate segmentation as a scene-level optimization problem, performing instance and language feature learning by aggregating information from thousands of 2D masks into 3D primitives, requir-ing hours of preprocessing and long training cycles. Ef-forts to reduce training cost include Dr. Splat [21], which proposes training-free feature registration but still incurs substantial preprocessing overhead. VALA [46] introduces visibility-aware feature aggregation to accelerate optimiza-tion but still requires costly scene-level semantic prepro-cessing. LUDVIG [30] extracts patch-wise CLIP fea-tures and lifts them via global optimization, resulting in time-to-first-query above 10 minutes and suboptimal masks due to the lack of clean supervision. B3Seg [16] uses ac-tive view selection and iterative Beta–Bernoulli updates, but its reliance on instance position for camera sampling limits open-vocabulary flexibility, while its 12-second query la-tency hinders real-time use. Open-vocabulary 3D instance segmentation methods on neural primitives can be broadly categorized into pixel-based and point-based approaches. Pixel-based methods [3, 19, 20, 24, 38, 43] operate in image space, where fea-tures become meaningful only after rasterization to a target view. In contrast, point-based methods [21, 27, 29, 30, 47, 51, 56] represent instance or language features directly in 3D space, enabling explicit instances and interactive ma-nipulation. The query mechanisms of the two paradigms also differ. Pixel-based methods must re-query instances for each viewpoint, whereas point-based methods query in-stances directly in 3D and render them efficiently via Gaus-sian rasterization. In scenarios where the primary objective is interactive 3D manipulation and fast query or inference, particularly as the number of frames increases, pixel-based methods become less efficient than point-based approaches. Another research direction focuses on object-aware re-construction, segmenting instances before optimizing Gaus-sian scenes [29, 51, 56]. These methods group point clouds using 2D instance segments and avoid mask-wise language feature extraction, enabling minutes of prepro-cessing. Yet, they require over two hours of training per scene and lack true open-vocabulary support, as their re-liance on single-view Grounded SAM [42] struggles with occlusions and limits general 3D applicability. Recogniz-ing these limitations, our method follows the point-based paradigm, eliminating both heavy preprocessing and addi-tional training while preserving high segmentation quality and low query latency. Furthermore, it supports full open-vocabulary querying, making it well suited for interactive 3D applications and resource-limited platforms.

3.1. Query-driven 2D Instance Localization with Dense CLIP Feature 

Given multi-view images and a text query, our goal is to effi-ciently extract the 2D pivot segment that best aligns with the query. Unlike prior methods that rely on SAM-based mask representations and are therefore constrained by predefined object proposals, we notice that dense pixel-level represen-tations enable flexible query-driven localization of arbitrary regions, ranging from object parts and subparts to complete object instances. We further observe that, by operating di-rectly in the image space, these representations preserve the original pixel-level vision–language alignment of CLIP fea-tures while avoiding the semantic degradation that can arise when features are distilled, compressed, or lifted into scene-level 3D representations. Moreover, our approach is inher-ently image-wise: feature extraction is performed only once per image, after which arbitrary queries can be evaluated di-rectly on the dense feature maps. This is substantially more efficient than mask-wise pipelines [21, 24, 27, 38, 43, 47], which require processing and scoring a large number of mask proposals for every image. We begin by computing dense pixel-level CLIP fea-tures for each image using ProxyCLIP [23], a training-free method that aligns CLIP embeddings with spatial VFM features for dense vision–language correspondence. The text query is encoded into a CLIP text embedding, after which we compute cosine similarity between the image features Fimg ∈ RN ×H×W ×D and the query embedding

3.2. Occlusion-aware 3D Instance Mask Formation via Visibility Voting 

The localized 2D pivot region from Sec. 3.1 serves as the initial instance hypothesis. We propagate it across visible frames using a tracking segmentor to recover the complete object, enabling geometrically consistent and spatially com-plete 3D segmentation. 

Budget-aware frame selection and ordering. Selecting a 4strong pivot view is crucial for high-quality 3D segmenta-tion. The pivot region, identified as the most query-relevant region in the most visually prominent view, provides the richest semantic and spatial cues for the target object. Prop-agating this region to all views is unnecessary and ineffi-cient, we propose a lightweight view-selection protocol that filters out irrelevant frames while preserving complete cov-erage of the target instance. We denote the 3D Gaussians whose projections lie within the pivot region as pivot Gaus-sians Gpivot , as they encode reliable geometric and semantic cues from the object’s most representative viewpoint. Lifting to 3D via Visibility Voting. A straightforward ap-proach to construct a 3D instance mask is projection map-ping, assigning a Gaussian to the instance if its projection falls within the tracked 2D masks. However, the unstruc-tured nature of Gaussian primitives and inevitable track-ing errors make this approach highly sensitive to incor-rect tracklets. To address this sensitivity, we introduce a visibility-aware voting mechanism that aggregates evidence across views to robustly decide each Gaussian’s instance membership. The key idea is to treat erroneous segments as outliers: even if a Gaussian is misassigned in one view, consistent assignments in other views can correct it through multi-view voting. For a video sequence I, we project 3D Gaussians into each view i ∈ I and evaluate them against the propagated 2D instance tracklets M to enforce cross-view consistency. At each frame i, we derive the mask mi ∈ M and the Gaus-sian visibility indicator Vi ∈ { 0, 1}N , where N is the to-tal number of Gaussians. The visibility indicator specifies whether each Gaussian is visible or occluded in the current frame. In each view where it is visible, a Gaussian casts a foreground vote if its projection lies within the mask mi,and a background vote otherwise. ## 5. Conclusion 

We presented a practical and scalable framework for open-vocabulary 3D instance segmentation that balances accu-racy and efficiency. Our query-wise, training-free approach combines dense vision-language features, robust 2D local-ization, and visibility voting to construct occlusion-aware 3D mask to achieve state-of-the-art accuracy with low computational overhead and substantially reduced TTFQ. Comprehensive experiments across diverse and challenging benchmarks demonstrate strong generalization and compat-ibility with consumer-grade hardware.
