Abstract
Video world models generate plausible novel views but
often lack persistent memory, failing to preserve a place’s
geometry and appearance when an agent revisits a scene.
A central challenge is how previous views should be
effectively used for creating memory.
Reconstructing
them independently underutilizes geometric constraints
between nearby views, while repeatedly reconstructing a
growing history can propagate later prediction errors into
earlier geometry. We introduce RecallWorld, a persistent
embodied video world model that jointly reconstructs short
multi-view episodes into Scenelets while keeping geometry
independent across episodes. Starting from a single source
image, camera-aware ray lifting places perspective and
native fisheye images in a common world frame, retaining
wide-field content for perspective generation. Each gener-
ated segment extends the Scenelet memory and conditions
subsequent generation.
Coverage-based retrieval selects
complementary Scenelets from the full history,
while
target-view visibility is resolved geometrically before ap-
pearance fusion, providing consistent memory conditioning
for guiding subsequent generation. Across four datasets,
RecallWorld improves revisit PSNR over the strongest
evaluated baseline by 2.6 dB on partial revisits and 3.9 dB
on full cycles, demonstrating substantially stronger preser-
vation of scene identity across exploration and return.
1. Introduction
Video world models generate future visual observations
conditioned on actions or camera motion, supporting
robotic interaction and manipulation [50, 53, 68], au-
tonomous driving [11, 39, 65], and synthetic training-data
generation [21, 33, 56].
However, for embodied explo-
ration, plausible next frames are not enough: the model
must preserve a place’s geometry and appearance after its
observations leave the recent context as an agent may later
approach the same location from a new direction. Persistent
video generation therefore requires long-term access to ear-
lier observations without reconstructing or conditioning on
the full history.
No prior video world model combines these capabili-
ties. Camera-controlled generators produce locally coher-
ent video [2, 14, 38, 43, 70] but do not guarantee consistent
revisits. Memory-based methods retrieve earlier views or

We present RecallWorld, a persistent embodied video
world model with episodic Scenelet memory.
Firstly,
camera-aware ray lifting unifies perspective and native fish-
eye observations in a common world frame [36], retaining
surfaces outside the perspective image used for generation.
Next, each completed segment forms a Scenelet: overlap-
ping views are reconstructed jointly, and geometry remains
independent across episodes.
Then, coverage-based re-
trieval utilizes complementary Scenelets under a fixed con-
ditioning budget, and target-view geometry resolves visibil-
ity before appearance fusion. This design separates local re-
construction from long-term retention while keeping earlier
Scenelets accessible. Figure 2 illustrates our pipeline.
We evaluate RecallWorld on real robot-navigation tra-
jectories from GrandTour [44] and M2DGR [58], synthetic
trajectories from TartanGround [35], and real scene cap-
tures from DL3DV [30]. On exploration-and-return trajec-
tories starting from the same source image, RecallWorld’s
generated revisits preserve the structure and appearance of
previously observed places, whereas camera-controlled and
global-memory baselines alter scene content. Our ablation
studies show that Scenelets improve revisit fidelity over in-
dependent views as memory and reduce peak reconstruction
memory relative to global memory. Natively using fish-
eye images further expands the surface coverage retained
in memory. We also measure camera adherence and video
coherence throughout each rollout. Together, these results
show that RecallWorld preserves place identity across ex-
ploration and partial or full return cycles.
Our main contributions are:
• Episodic Scenelet memory. We show that how observa-
tions are grouped into memory is important for persistent
video generation. We introduce episodic Scenelets, which
jointly reconstruct nearby views while keeping geometry
independent across different episodes.
• Persistent Retrieval and visibility-aware conditioning.
We retrieve Scenelets from the full history under a fixed
memory budget and use their geometry to determine
which surfaces are visible in the target view before com-
bining appearance for generation.
• Camera general persistent representation.

2. Related Work
Camera Conditioned Video Generation. Video diffusion
models synthesize temporally coherent sequences [3, 16].
Video world models predict future observations from ac-
tions or camera motion. Action-conditioned models support
interactive navigation [2, 4, 50], while pose-conditioned dif-
fusion and explicit 3D guidance enable broader camera con-
trol [5, 13, 14]. SANA-WM generates minute-scale videos
along continuous camera trajectories [70], SEVA synthe-
sizes image-conditioned novel views [67], and GEN3C uses
a 3D cache for world-consistent camera control [37]. Cam-
era control, however, does not ensure a consistent revisit
once a place leaves the model’s recent context.
Feed Forward Reconstruction.
Beyond camera con-
trol, feed-forward models recover geometry and correspon-
dences from images [26, 46], while generative reconstruc-
tion jointly models geometry and appearance to ground
synthesized views in observed structure [6, 42, 64]. Most
generative models assume pinhole imagery, and rectifying
wide field-of-view inputs discards peripheral coverage [9].
UniK3D instead estimates rays and radial range for per-
spective, fisheye, and panoramic cameras [36]. Fisheye3R
and Cam3R generalize feed-forward reconstruction [9, 12],
while ray-based generators extend camera control across
lens geometries [18, 23, 61]. RecallWorld uses π3X [47]
for joint multi-view reconstruction, Depth Anything 3 [29]
for perspective initialization, and UniK3D [36] for native
fisheye initialization. Together, these components provide
camera-aware geometry for episodic memory.
Memory Aware Generation.
For persistence over long
horizons, video models retain spatial evidence as retrieved
images indexed by pose, time or field-of-view overlap [52,

3. RecallWorld
A persistent video world model must preserve observed ge-
ometry and appearance after a place leaves its recent con-
text.
RecallWorld generates video autoregressively and
consolidates each completed segment into an independent
Scenelet. Nearby keyframes share a joint reconstruction,
while stored Scenelets remain fixed, preventing later predic-
tion errors from rewriting earlier geometry. Camera-aware
ray lifting places perspective and fisheye observations in
a common world-frame representation. Before generating
each segment, RecallWorld retrieves a complementary set
of Scenelets covering the target views, resolves visibility
among their projected surfaces, and conditions the gener-
ator on the resulting appearance. This separates local re-
construction from long-term retention while keeping active
conditioning bounded. Figure 2 illustrates our framework.

3.2. Camera Aware Ray Lifting
Persistent memory must retain the geometry and appear-

than being limited to the narrower perspective view used

same world space, including surfaces outside the rectified
perspective view.

3.3. Episodic Scenelet Memory
Beyond locating observed surfaces in 3D, persistent mem-
ory depends on consolidation granularity applied to mem-
ory, which determines how nearby views share a reconstruc-
tion. Per-view reconstruction discards constraints among
nearby frames, whereas repeatedly reconstructing the grow-
ing history allows newly generated content to revise ear-
lier geometry. RecallWorld avoids both extremes by group-
ing each segment into a bounded Scenelet whose views
share a local reconstruction that remains independent across
episodes.
For each Scenelet Sg with g ≥1, Jg indexes five evenly
spaced keyframes from the corresponding completed seg-
ment. π3X [47] jointly reconstructs the keyframes using
their intrinsics and target poses. A similarity transform then
aligns the predicted cameras to those poses, using Kab-
sch [24] for rotation and the camera centers for scale and
translation.
The resulting depths are converted to radial
range and lifted (see Equation 2), ensuring that stored ge-
ometry matches the generator’s camera conditioning. For

6. Conclusion
We introduced RecallWorld, a persistent embodied video
world model that separates local reconstruction from
long-term retention. Episodic Scenelets jointly reconstruct
nearby views while keeping geometry independent across
episodes, so later predictions do not rewrite earlier recon-
structions. Complementary retrieval and geometry-first vis-
ibility resolution recall relevant history within a fixed con-
ditioning budget. Camera-aware ray lifting unifies perspec-
tive and native fisheye observations in memory, retaining
wide-field content for later perspective generation. Across
four benchmarks, RecallWorld improves revisit PSNR over
the strongest evaluated baseline by 2.6 dB on partial revisits
and 3.9 dB on full cycles, demonstrating that it more faith-
fully preserves place identity across exploration and return.
Limitations and future work.
Long-horizon autoregres-
sive generation can compound prediction errors, motivat-
ing uncertainty-aware consolidation and cross-episode ver-
ification of Scenelet memory. Extending static Scenelets
with time-indexed geometry and appearance could track
moving actors, object changes, and illumination across re-
8


visits while preserving enduring scene structure.
Native
fisheye generation could further carry wide-field coverage
from retained observations into generated views. Together,
these directions would extend persistent world models from
remembering where an agent has been to modeling how
places evolve.
