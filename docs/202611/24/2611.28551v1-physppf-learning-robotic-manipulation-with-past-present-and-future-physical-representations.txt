## Abstract 

Vision-language-action (VLA) models have advanced robotic manipulation, yet complex tasks requiring both pre-cise 3D contact and long-horizon coordination remain chal-lenging. Most existing studies enhance spatial perception or temporal reasoning in VLAs separately. Spatial meth-ods primarily inject geometric or affordance priors into current-frame perception without tracking multi-step tran-sitions, while history-aware and world-model VLAs typi-cally capture past context or future dynamics in 2D feature or pixel spaces, either lacking explicit 3D contact cues or incurring high generation overhead. To address this, we propose PhysPPF , a VLA framework that performs uni-fied spatiotemporal modeling in a compact physical token space. Spatially, WWH (What-Where-How ) distills pre-trained perception teachers exclusively during training into compact token groups capturing what 3D structures occupy the scene, where to interact, and how the gripper should ap-proach, without test-time 3D encoders. ## 1. Introduction 

Enabling robots to perform diverse manipulation skills in physical environments is a central goal in robot learn-ing [5–7, 11, 13, 30, 34, 46, 51]. Recent vision-language-action (VLA) models advance this direction by leverag-ing pretrained vision-language (VL) backbones to gener-ate actions from visual observations and language instruc-tions [3, 4, 15, 16, 29, 36, 49, 54]. Despite this progress, re-liable execution remains challenging for complex tasks re-quiring both precise physical contact and long-horizon co-ordination. Consider the instruction “open the drawer and place the object inside” [20, 28]: the robot must first align its gripper with the handle to pull the drawer open, then pick up the object while remembering that the drawer is al-ready open, and finally place the object inside without col-lision. However, conventional VLAs pass single-frame 2D features from the VL backbone directly to the action expert (Fig. 1(a), left), lacking both 3D spatial cues for accurate contact and temporal context for tracking stage progress. In response, recent works augment VLAs with spatial cues or temporal context between the VL backbone and the action expert (Fig. 1(a), right), yet both dimensions are typically studied separately and exhibit inherent lim-itations when applied individually. From a spatial per-spective, existing methods incorporate 3D point and depth priors [19, 32, 37, 50] or spatial grounding and affor-dance cues [25, 44] to enhance spatial awareness. How-ever, they typically model these priors in isolation with-out local contact geometry for gripper alignment, while those relying on test-time 3D encoders further incur infer-ence latency. From a temporal perspective, history-aware policies leverage multi-frame contexts, perceptual memo-ries, or past motion cues [18, 20, 26, 33, 35, 45], but re-main confined to past observations without foresight for up-coming contacts and mostly rely on 2D historical features rather than explicit 3D contact geometry. To provide such lookahead, world-model VLAs either generate future visual frames [14, 17, 38] or predict latent visual and motion rep-resentations [23, 40, 43, 52]. Nevertheless, frame-level gen-eration spends substantial computation on visual synthesis and incurs high inference latency, whereas 2D latent or mo-tion predictions still lack fine-grained 3D contact cues for precise manipulation. These limitations highlight that spatial perception and temporal reasoning are inherently coupled in robotic ma-nipulation: each action relies on 3D physical cues for ac-curate contact, as well as past memory and future foresight to coordinate transitions across evolving 3D states. On one hand, reliable contact control requires a unified 3D repre-sentation that jointly captures global scene geometry, task-relevant interaction regions, and local approach cues rather than isolated spatial priors. On the other hand, evolving past, present, and future states directly over compact 3D tokens, rather than raw RGB pixels, provides explicit phys-ical grounding across time steps while avoiding redundant visual computation and high inference latency. Motivated by this insight, we propose PhysPPF , a VLA framework that performs unified spatiotemporal modeling in a compact physical token space (Fig. 1(b)). For spa-tial modeling , we introduce WWH (What-Where-How ) to factorize 3D perception into three compact physical to-ken groups: scene-level point and depth geometry captur-ing what 3D structures occupy the workspace, instruction-conditioned affordances localizing where to interact, and metric depth and local surface normals constraining how 

the gripper should approach. These tokens are supervised by pretrained perception teachers via lightweight decoders only during training, with both teachers and decoders dis-carded at inference to avoid test-time 3D encoding over-head. For temporal modeling , we develop PPF (Past-Present-Future ) to model causal state transitions directly in this WWH space without pixel reconstruction. Specifi-cally, PPF consolidates post-execution states into a bounded 

past physical memory via similarity-based merging, and employs a spatiotemporal world model conditioned on this memory and VL features to jointly estimate present and pre-dict future physical tokens under asymmetric attention. Fi-nally, parallel gated residual cross-attention branches inject past, present, and future tokens into a flow-matching ac-tion expert to avoid cross-temporal interference, achieving strong manipulation performance with modest latency and parameter overhead (Fig. 1(c, d)). Our contributions are as follows: • We propose PhysPPF , a VLA framework that performs unified spatiotemporal modeling in a compact physi-cal token space. By integrating 3D spatial perception and temporal state evolution, it guides action generation across contact-rich and long-horizon tasks. • We introduce WWH to factorize 3D workspace structure, task affordances, and local approach cues into compact physical tokens via training-only teacher supervision. In 2this space, PPF maintains a bounded past memory, infers present and future states via an asymmetric world model, and conditions actions through parallel gated branches. • Extensive experiments across SimplerEnv, CALVIN, and LIBERO show that PhysPPF consistently outperforms existing spatial, history-aware, and world-model VLAs with modest latency and parameter overhead, improv-ing over the StarVLA baseline by +17.2 points on Sim-plerEnv, +29.3 points in 5-subtask success on CALVIN, and +3.5 points on LIBERO. 

## 2. Related Work 

Spatial Perception in VLA Models. Recent VLA mod-els encode visual observations and language instructions with pretrained VL backbones to guide action genera-tion [3, 4, 15, 16, 29, 36, 54]. Although some studies re-fine visual perception via stronger vision encoders or ac-tive attention [41, 48], they still rely on 2D image features without explicit 3D cues for contact-rich manipulation. To enhance spatial awareness, one line of work incorporates 3D geometric priors, either injecting 3D positional and spa-tial tokens at test time [32, 50] or aligning VL features with 3D foundation models during training [19, 37]. An-other line introduces spatial grounding and affordance cues via localization-guided training [44] or progress-aware af-fordance prediction [25]. However, these methods mostly model scene geometry or 2D affordances separately without local surface cues for gripper alignment, while those using test-time 3D encoders add latency. Moreover, most spatial VLAs are confined to single-frame perception rather than multi-step 3D evolution. In contrast, WWH jointly encodes workspace geometry ( what ), task affordances ( where ), and local approach cues ( how ) in a shared token space via training-only distillation, avoiding test-time 3D encoders while supporting multi-step physical modeling. 

Temporal and World Modeling in VLA Models. Be-yond single-frame perception, long-horizon manipulation benefits from both retaining history to track stage progress and predicting future states to guide upcoming interactions. History-aware VLAs capture past context via multi-frame inputs, streaming tokens, or motion cues [18, 20, 26, 45], while retrieval-based policies query perceptual tokens or experiences [33, 35]. In parallel, world models fore-cast future dynamics by synthesizing dense or structured frames [14, 17, 38], or by aligning and predicting 2D vi-sual latents and motion cues [2, 23, 40, 43, 52]. Recent studies further model 3D spatiotemporal dynamics via cal-ibrated RGB-D pretraining [47], training-only 3D flow [8], or depth-lifted 3D motion [42]. However, most methods model past memory and future prediction separately, lack-ing either contact foresight or consolidated long-horizon memory. Moreover, frame generation incurs high latency, most history and latent methods operate in 2D spaces with-out explicit 3D contact cues, and recent 3D models either re-quire calibrated depth inputs or lack test-time 3D foresight. To bridge this gap, PPF evolves states directly in the com-pact WWH space from RGB inputs without pixel recon-struction, unifying bounded retrieval-free memory, present perception, and future prediction via an asymmetric world model and parallel gated branches.

## 5. Conclusion 

In this paper, we present PhysPPF , a VLA framework for unified spatiotemporal modeling in a compact physical to-ken space. Spatially, WWH distills frozen teachers exclu-sively during training into compact tokens capturing scene geometry ( what ), task affordances ( where ), and local ap-proach cues ( how ), adding no test-time overhead. Tem-porally, operating in this WWH space without pixel re-construction, PPF maintains a past physical memory and jointly infers present and future tokens via an asymmetric world model to guide a flow-matching action expert through parallel gated branches. Experiments across SimplerEnv, CALVIN, and LIBERO show that PhysPPF consistently outperforms generalist, spatial, history-aware, and world-model VLAs with modest parameter and latency overhead.
