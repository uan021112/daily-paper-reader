Unified 3D understanding and generation require compact sequences preserving spatial structure.
Dense grids yield long sequences, while serialization can separate spatial neighbors. We propose
Sparse Proximity Aware Compression (SPAC), a shared sparse SDF vocabulary combining sparse
encoding with reversible spatial merging. A Sparse SDF VQ-VAE quantizes near-surface features
while retaining explicit coordinates, using reconstruction supervision for surface fidelity and detail.
3D Byte-Pair Encoding (3D-BPE) merges recurring face-adjacent patterns using relative offsets
before serialization. Macro tokens expand exactly into leaf tokens and coordinates. After Morton
serialization, a shared LLM reads geometry for captioning and predicts tokens that decode into meshes
for text-conditioned generation. With fixed geometry, architecture, and band width in voxels, sparse
encoder cost grows approximately quadratically with resolution. 3D-BPE reduces mean complete
LLM sequence length by 45.4 percent, including coordinates, text, and control tokens. SPAC achieves
strong results in both 3D captioning and text-conditioned mesh generation. Ablations show gains
over leaf tokens and Morton 1D-BPE in both tasks.

1. Introduction
Autoregressive next-token prediction offers large language models (LLMs) a common interface for
understanding and generation (Brown et al., 2020). Models such as Emu3 (Wang et al., 2024a),
Chameleon (Team, 2024), and Transfusion (Zhou et al., 2025) extend this interface to visual content
through discrete or hybrid representations. For 3D, this requires a geometry vocabulary that keeps
sequences compact while retaining spatial structure for decoding. Such a vocabulary must serve both
directions: the model should interpret geometric structure when describing an object and produce
decodable geometry when conditioned on text. This makes token design central to unification, because
the same sequence determines what the language model observes and what the geometric decoder
can reconstruct. A useful representation must therefore balance sequence length, spatial organization,
and recoverability.
LLaMA-Mesh (Wang et al., 2024c) and ShapeLLM-Omni (Ye et al., 2025) unify generation and
understanding through textual meshes and quantized voxels. XCube (Ren et al., 2024), Direct3D-
S2 (Wu et al., 2025), and TRELLIS (Xiang et al., 2025b) concentrate computation near surfaces,
typically using diffusion or rectified flow for generation. A compact shared vocabulary must address
three issues. First, dense voxel token counts grow cubically with resolution, so increasing geometric
detail also increases the sequence the LLM must process. Restricting encoding to a surface band
addresses spatial occupancy, but does not remove repeated local patterns among active sites. Second,
serialization can separate spatial neighbors: tokens that form one geometric structure need not
be adjacent in a one-dimensional sequence. Compression based only on sequence proximity may
therefore overlook recurring 3D patterns. Third, implicit coordinates tie geometry to sequence
positions, complicating variable-size token composition. Merging such tokens must also preserve the
positions needed to reconstruct their constituent geometry. These issues are coupled. A short sequence
is useful only if its tokens still identify where geometry lies and how local parts fit together. Combining
sparsity with compression therefore requires a representation that exposes spatial relations to the
tokenizer and retains a defined route back to the original quantized geometry. These constraints
motivate sparse encoding, explicit coordinates, and reversible spatial merging.
We propose Sparse Proximity Aware Compression (SPAC), which combines sparse encoding with
reversible spatial merging. A Sparse SDF VQ-VAE quantizes near-surface latent features into tokens
while retaining explicit coordinates. Its reconstruction objective balances surface fidelity and fine
geometric detail. For a fixed surface and band width in voxel units, the number of active sites grows
approximately quadratically with resolution. Explicit coordinates also make neighborhood relations
available to the compression stage without depending on a chosen serialization. Sparsity reduces
the number of encoded locations, but recurring local patterns remain. We introduce 3D Byte-Pair
Encoding (3D-BPE) to learn merges on the face adjacency graph using relative offsets. Merging before
serialization captures recurring spatial patterns without requiring their tokens to be consecutive in a
sequence. Each resulting macro token expands exactly into its constituent leaf tokens and coordinates.
Relative offsets record how constituent patterns are placed, allowing a learned merge to be reused
across translations. This exact recovery preserves the quantized coordinate–token mapping, while
the VQ-VAE determines surface reconstruction through its codebook and decoder.
After Morton serialization, a shared LLM reads geometry tokens for captioning and predicts
them for text-conditioned generation. Predicted macro tokens expand into leaf tokens, the VQ-VAE
reconstructs a sparse SDF, and Marching Cubes extracts the mesh directly. Both tasks consequently
share geometric units, and generation follows a defined path from language-model predictions to a
mesh. Experiments support favorable sparse encoder scaling. 3D-BPE reduces mean complete LLM
sequence length by 45.4 percent, including coordinates, text, and control tokens. Among evaluated
models, SPAC leads all five captioning metrics and achieves strong mesh generation results. Ablations

2. Related Work
Discrete 3D Representations and Autoregressive Generation. Classical implicit and hybrid repre-
sentations provide continuous geometric priors (Mildenhall et al., 2022; Shen et al., 2021; Zhang
et al., 2023) but do not directly support discrete sequence modeling. VQ tokenizers (Esser et al.,
2021; Razavi et al., 2019; van den Oord et al., 2017) bridge this gap by quantizing geometry for
decoder-only Transformers. MeshGPT (Siddiqui et al., 2024), MeshAnything (Chen et al., 2025a,c),
and MeshXL (Chen et al., 2024a) use face-, vertex-, or coordinate-based sequences, enabling au-
toregressive synthesis but suffering from long sequences and ordering sensitivity at high resolutions.
AutoSDF (Mittal et al., 2022) learns an autoregressive prior over quantized SDF grids, while Shape-
Former (Yan et al., 2022) uses sparse quantized implicit codes for shape completion. BPT (Weng et al.,
2025) compresses explicit mesh sequences via block indexing and patch aggregation; FreeMesh (Liu
et al., 2025) rearranges and merges frequent coordinate patterns. MeshMosaic (Xu et al., 2026b)
assembles autoregressively generated mesh patches, and MeshWeaver (Xu et al., 2026a) uses sparse
voxels to guide vertex prediction. Sparse volumetric methods, including XCube (Ren et al., 2024),
Direct3D-S2 (Wu et al., 2025), Sparc3D (Li et al., 2025b), TRELLIS (Xiang et al., 2025b), and
O-Voxel (Xiang et al., 2025a), concentrate computation near occupied or surface regions, enabling
higher resolutions. Their generative stages typically use diffusion or rectified flow. Other routes
include diffusion-guided optimization (Lin et al., 2023; Poole et al., 2022), diffusion-based view
synthesis (Liu et al., 2023b; Long et al., 2024), feed-forward reconstruction (Hong et al., 2024; Xu
et al., 2024a), and latent diffusion (Li et al., 2025a; Wang et al., 2023; Yang et al., 2024; Zhang
et al., 2024; Zhao et al., 2023). SPAC combines sparse SDF codes with reversible, offset-conditioned
merging on the 3D face-adjacency graph to form a compact geometry vocabulary.
Multimodal Large Language Models for 3D. Building on vision–language alignment (Alayrac et al.,
2022; Chen et al., 2024b; Dai et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2023a; Radford et al., 2021), 3D-LLM (Hong
et al., 2023), PointLLM (Xu et al., 2024b), and ShapeLLM (Qi et al., 2024) inject 3D features into
LLMs for understanding. SAR3D (Chen et al., 2025b) uses multi-scale triplane codes for next-scale
generation and a separately fine-tuned LLM for understanding. Recent work moves toward unified
understanding and generation: ShapeLLM-Omni (Ye et al., 2025) incorporates 3D VQ tokens for
generative and discriminative tasks, LLaMA-Mesh (Wang et al., 2024c) represents meshes as UTF-8 text,
and EVA01 (Yang et al., 2026) uses specialized experts to unify 3D understanding, generation, and
editing. ShapeLLM-Omni also describes a rectified-flow route from voxels to high-quality meshes (Ye
et al., 2025). SPAC follows the unified modeling direction with sparse SDF tokens that decode directly
into mesh geometry.
Long-Sequence Modeling and Compression. BPE (Sennrich et al., 2016) merges frequent adjacent

3. Method
SPAC uses a shared sparse SDF vocabulary for 3D understanding and generation, combining reversible
spatial compression with autoregressive modeling.

4. Empirical Results
4.1. Implementation Details
VQ-VAE Training. The Sparse SDF VQ-VAE uses SDF samples from 50k ShapeNet+Objaverse meshes
for geometry reconstruction and codebook learning. As detailed in Sec. 3.2, its first stage freezes the
pretrained encoder–decoder to learn the codebook, and its second stage jointly fine-tunes the full
VQ-VAE for sparse SDF reconstruction.
LLM Training. The LLM uses text pairs for the same 50k meshes, training on 8 A100 GPUs with batch
size 2, learning rate 5×10−5, and cosine decay. The Qwen3-4B decoder-only backbone adds 8192
distance, 8192 macro, and 32768 Morton-coordinate tokens. Stage 1 freezes the VQ-VAE encoder and
LLM backbone, training only new 3D-token embeddings and the output projection to align geometric
symbols with the LLM’s semantic space. Stage 2 keeps the encoder frozen and fully fine-tunes the LLM
on text-paired 3D assets mixed with UltraChat for language retention, using AdamW, 10% warmup,
DeepSpeed ZeRO-2, and mixed precision for LLM optimization.

5. Conclusion
SPAC uses a shared sparse SDF vocabulary learned by a Sparse SDF VQ-VAE to unify 3D understanding
and generation in a single autoregressive LLM. Reversible 3D-BPE compresses recurring spatial
patterns, and the predicted tokens decode directly into mesh geometry. Experiments show favorable
sparse encoder scaling, shorter complete LLM sequences, and gains in both captioning and generation
over leaf tokens and Morton 1D-BPE. Exact expansion preserves the quantized coordinate–token
mapping; SDF sampling, the codebook, and the decoder determine surface detail. SPAC models
geometry and text, generating untextured meshes from text prompts. Future work includes image
conditioning and surface appearance, broader training coverage across shapes and descriptions, and
refined tokenization for thin structures and less common shapes.

F. Limitations and Future Directions
Modalities and Outputs. SPAC models geometry and text and generates untextured meshes. Image
conditioning and appearance require added representations. CLIP (Radford et al., 2021) could supply
image features to condition the LLM alongside the existing sparse geometry-token representation.
Representation and Reconstruction. SDF resolution, codebook, and decoder capacity determine
geometric detail. Exact 3D-BPE expansion preserves quantized leaves, while reconstruction depends
on the tokenizer. Examples G20 and G24 guide future work on surface fidelity, codebook use, and
preservation of small structures.
Sequence Length and Computation. Sequence length follows the number of active sites and
autoregressively predicted coordinate–token pairs. Near-quadratic encoder scaling assumes a fixed

regular surface, voxel bandwidth, architecture, and local neighborhoods. Surface complexity, channels,
and neighborhood size alter cost.
Data and Generalization. The 50k meshes provide training coverage across their shapes and
descriptions. Qualitative examples from these training objects show variation in part coverage and
caption attributes. Broader mixed 2D–3D data could improve robustness. Dynamic or 4D coordinate–
token maps additionally require temporal representations and consistency.
G. Ethics and Societal Impact
Broader Impacts. Efficient 3D modeling supports gaming, film, virtual and augmented reality, design,
education, and cultural preservation. Misuse includes counterfeiting, unauthorized copying, and
deceptive or harmful content. Release licenses will prohibit malicious use. We encourage respect
for data and design rights, transparent documentation, provenance tracking, watermarking, and
safeguards tailored to each application.
Use of Large Language Models. LLMs assisted with language editing, literature organization, consis-
tency checks, and routine technical tasks. Authors remain responsible for methodology, experiments,
and verification of mathematical claims and results.
