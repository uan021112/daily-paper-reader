## Abstract
Accurate road surface reconstruction is essential for autonomous-driving mapping and simulation, but existing methods trade reconstruction quality for cross-scene inference. Optimization-based methods recover detailed surfaces through costly per-scene optimization, while feed-forward methods lack road-specific constraints and often produce large elevation errors. We propose \textbf{BReD}, a feed-forward framework for reconstructing road elevation, appearance, and semantics from calibrated six-camera sequences. BReD first estimates coarse elevation through LiDAR-supervised depth learning and BEV lifting, using only cameras at inference. A lightweight BEV residual diffusion model then conditions elevation refinement on a camera-derived depth--elevation discrepancy and elevation-aware image features. Diffusion-indexed perturbations expose the model to different discrepancy corruption levels during training, while inference performs one deterministic refinement from the clean discrepancy. The refined elevations are temporally aggregated to anchor road-aligned Gaussians, whose attributes are predicted by a geometry-prior-guided decoder without test-time optimization. On four held-out nuScenes scenes, BReD achieves 7.34 cm elevation RMSE and 90.92\% semantic mIoU, compared with 14.43 cm for RoGS without test-time LiDAR and 8.88 cm for its test-time-LiDAR-assisted variant; both RoGS variants require per-scene optimization.
