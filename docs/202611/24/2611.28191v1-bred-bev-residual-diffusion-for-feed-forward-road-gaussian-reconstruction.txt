# BReD: BEV Residual Diffusion for Feed-Forward Road Gaussian Reconstruction 

Baizhi Wang Sun Yat-sen University 

Abstract 

Accurate road surface reconstruction is essential for autonomous-driving mapping and simulation, but existing methods trade reconstruction quality for cross-scene infer-ence. Optimization-based methods recover detailed sur-faces through costly per-scene optimization, while feed-forward methods lack road-specific constraints and of-ten produce large elevation errors. We propose BReD ,a feed-forward framework for reconstructing road ele-vation, appearance, and semantics from calibrated six-camera sequences. BReD first estimates coarse elevation through LiDAR-supervised depth learning and BEV lift-ing, using only cameras at inference. A lightweight BEV residual diffusion model then conditions elevation refine-ment on a camera-derived depth–elevation discrepancy and elevation-aware image features. Diffusion-indexed pertur-bations expose the model to different discrepancy corrup-tion levels during training, while inference performs one deterministic refinement from the clean discrepancy. The re-fined elevations are temporally aggregated to anchor road-aligned Gaussians, whose attributes are predicted by a geometry-prior-guided decoder without test-time optimiza-tion. On four held-out nuScenes scenes, BReD achieves 7.34 cm elevation RMSE and 90.92% semantic mIoU, com-pared with 14.43 cm for RoGS without test-time LiDAR and 8.88 cm for its test-time-LiDAR-assisted variant; both RoGS variants require per-scene optimization. 

1. Introduction 

Accurate road surface reconstruction is fundamental to au-tonomous driving, supporting localization, mapping, sim-ulation, and closed-loop planning through a joint repre-sentation of metric geometry, appearance, and semantics. Road elevation is particularly important for distinguish-ing slopes, curbs, intersections, and subtle structures lost in planar bird’s-eye-view (BEV) representations. Conven-tional high-definition (HD) map production typically re-lies on dedicated survey vehicles equipped with costly Li-DAR systems, followed by substantial processing or anno-tation of road elements. A transferable camera-only infer-ence pipeline could instead lower the sensing cost and en-able scalable map collection from production vehicles or crowdsourced fleets, while jointly reconstructed semantics could serve as pre-annotations for downstream map produc-tion. Although surround-view cameras offer such a scal-able and low-cost sensing setup, recovering renderable road surfaces from images remains difficult because perspec-tive observations provide indirect geometry, distant regions contain limited visual evidence, and dynamic objects fre-quently occlude the road. Effective reconstruction must therefore infer fine-grained elevation while preserving ap-pearance and semantics across the camera surround. Exist-ing methods mainly follow two paradigms. Optimization-based approaches represent roads with meshes or Gaussian primitives and optimize their geometry and attributes per sequence [5, 14, 15, 19]. They can produce detailed re-constructions, especially with dense LiDAR, but incur sub-stantial test-time cost and cannot directly transfer learned road priors across scenes. Feed-forward geometry mod-els instead support cross-scene inference [7, 17, 18], but lack road-specific metric and topological constraints, of-ten causing large vertical errors and rendering artifacts on non-planar roads. Recent road-specific feed-forward meth-ods mainly target monocular or sparse-view inputs [8, 20]. Joint feed-forward reconstruction of elevation, RGB, and semantics from six-camera observations remains insuffi-ciently explored. This setting presents three challenges. First, depth-based BEV lifting unifies surround-view fea-tures into a metric surface, but discretized frustum depths introduce quantization artifacts and lose centimeter-level height variations. Second, refining the coarse surface re-quires sampling image evidence near its estimated 3D loca-tion, where elevation errors can cause feature misalignment. Such refinement must exploit perspective cues and imper-fect camera-derived geometry without ground-truth eleva-tion at inference. Third, the BEV elevation must be con-verted into dense, renderable primitives with multi-view-consistent geometry, appearance, opacity, orientation, scale, and semantics, without resorting to per-scene optimization. We address these challenges with BReD , a BEV arXiv:2611.28191v1 [cs.CV] 20 Nov 2026 Re sidual Diffusion framework for feed-forward road Gaus-sian reconstruction. BReD first predicts frame-wise coarse elevation through depth-supervised BEV lifting. It then introduces a conditional residual diffusion module that refines the coarse elevation rather than generating abso-lute elevation from pure noise. Specifically, the discrep-ancy between the coarse elevation and a camera-depth-derived elevation serves as an observable geometric con-dition, while elevation-aware image features provide local perspective evidence. Diffusion-indexed perturbations ex-pose a timestep-conditioned ConvGRU to different discrep-ancy corruption levels during training; inference uses the clean discrepancy at t = 0 for one deterministic refinement step. The frame-wise refined elevations are transformed into a common reference frame and temporally aggregated to anchor dense road-aligned Gaussians. A geometry-prior-guided MLP predicts bounded residuals for elevation, color, opacity, scale, rotation, and semantics, producing a render-able sequence-level representation without test-time gradi-ent optimization. The elevation generator and Gaussian de-coder are trained in two stages. We evaluate BReD on four held-out scenes from the official nuScenes validation split. Although trained with LiDAR-derived depth and elevation supervision, BReD uses only calibrated cameras and poses at inference and requires no scene-specific optimization. It achieves a mean elevation RMSE of 7.34 cm , compared with 14 .43 cm for RoGS without LiDAR and 8.88 cm for its LiDAR-assisted variant, together with 90 .92% semantic mIoU and 24 .15 dB PSNR. These results indicate the poten-tial of transferable camera-only inference for scalable geo-metric map reconstruction and semantic pre-annotation. On 

scene-0523 , the elevation generation and Gaussian re-finement stages progressively reduce RMSE from 19 .60 to 

9.47 and 5.07 cm , respectively. Our contributions are sum-marized as follows: • We introduce BReD, a feed-forward framework for scal-able joint road elevation, appearance, and semantic recon-struction from calibrated six-camera sequences, requiring only cameras and no scene-specific optimization at infer-ence. • We formulate elevation refinement as conditional BEV residual diffusion, where a camera-derived depth– elevation discrepancy provides an observable residual condition, elevation-aware image features provide local perspective evidence, and diffusion-indexed perturbations expose a timestep-conditioned ConvGRU to different dis-crepancy corruption levels during training. • We develop a geometry-prior-guided Gaussian decoder that converts temporally aggregated BEV surfaces into renderable road Gaussians; on four held-out nuScenes scenes, BReD achieves 7.34 cm elevation RMSE and 

90 .92% mIoU. 

2. Related Work 

2.1. BEV Perception and Elevation Estimation 

Camera-based BEV perception transforms perspective-view features into a metric top-down representation. Lift-Splat-Shoot [16] predicts categorical depth distributions, lifts image features into camera frustums, and aggregates them on a BEV grid. BEVDepth [12] introduces explicit LiDAR depth supervision and camera-aware depth pre-diction, while BEVStereo [11] uses temporal stereo cues to improve depth estimation. Transformer-based meth-ods such as BEVFormer [13] instead construct spatial and temporal BEV features through attention. These archi-tectures have supported 3D detection, occupancy predic-tion, and semantic mapping [21, 24]. RoadBEV [22] reconstructs road elevation and RGB appearance from monocular or stereo images, while FastRSR [23] improves this setting through depth-aware multi-scale projection and elevation-distribution prediction. Their released methods and evaluation protocols are designed for monocular or stereo front-view inputs and cannot be directly applied to the more complex six-camera surround-view setting of autonomous-driving datasets such as nuScenes. In contrast, our method directly processes surround-view observations from autonomous-driving scenes and jointly reconstructs road elevation, RGB appearance, and semantics. 

2.2. Optimization-Based Road Reconstruction 

Road surface reconstruction represents road geome-try together with appearance or map-related attributes. RoMe [14] models large road regions using an explicit tri-angular mesh, a learned elevation function, and per-vertex color and semantic attributes. EMIE-MAP [19] combines an explicit mesh with implicit elevation and appearance en-coding to model roads observed by surround-view cam-eras. RoGS [5] places 2D Gaussian surfels on a road-aligned meshgrid and optimizes their geometry, color, opac-ity, scale, rotation, and semantic attributes. Related driving-scene methods extend NeRF or Gaussian Splatting to street reconstruction, including static and dynamic scene com-ponents [9, 25, 26]. MagicRoad [15] further incorporates semantic-guided obstacle inpainting and appearance en-hancement into road reconstruction. These approaches ob-tain scene-specific road representations through optimiza-tion over the observations of each target sequence. They establish effective road representations and rendering objec-tives, while feed-forward approaches seek to reuse learned road priors across scenes. 

2.3. Feed-Forward Geometry and Gaussian Recon-struction 

Feed-forward reconstruction predicts scene geometry or renderable primitives directly from input images. Pixel-Splat [1] predicts pixel-aligned Gaussians from posed im-age pairs, while MVSplat [2] constructs Gaussian repre-sentations from sparse multi-view features and plane-sweep correlations. DrivingForward [17] extends this paradigm to flexible surround-view inputs for driving-scene novel-view synthesis. GaussianFormer [6] uses Gaussian prim-itives for semantic occupancy prediction rather than sur-face rendering. Geometry foundation models provide a complementary representation: VGGT [18] jointly predicts camera parameters, depth, point maps, and tracks, and DriveVGGT [7] incorporates temporal and calibrated multi-camera constraints for driving sequences. Road-specific feed-forward methods combine direct prediction with struc-tured surface representations. BEV-GS [20] predicts road elevation and texture from a single image and constructs grid Gaussians for real-time rendering in a compact front-road region. RoadVGGT [8] uses a geometric foundation model to predict dense road Gaussians and consolidates them through confidence-weighted road-plane fusion, with category-aware processing around road structures. Its se-mantic maps are provided as inputs and associated with the reconstructed Gaussians, whereas its learned Gaussian attributes cover geometry, appearance, opacity, scale, and rotation. BReD follows the feed-forward road Gaussian setting but centers its design on surround-view BEV ele-vation generation: a residual diffusion module refines the coarse elevation, and a geometry-prior-guided residual head jointly predicts Gaussian geometry, appearance, opacity, and semantic logits. This design connects depth-supervised BEV lifting, fine-grained elevation refinement, and render-able road reconstruction within one cross-scene inference pipeline. 

3. Methodology

FFGS temporally aggregates refined surfaces and predicts bounded Gaussian attribute residuals without test-time gra-dient optimization.

3.3. Feed-Forward Road Gaussian 

In FFGS , frame-wise refined elevations are transformed into a common reference frame and temporally aggregated to initialize road Gaussians, whose attribute residuals are pre-dicted by a geometry-prior-guided MLP without test-time gradient optimization. 

Gaussian and Geometry Prior Initialization. We dis-cretize the road corridor (within 8m of the vehicle trajec-tory) into a 0.05 m BEV grid of M vertices with fixed hor-izontal coordinates μxy = ( x0, y 0). 4. Experiments 

4.1. Experimental Setup 

Datasets. We train on the official 700 -scene nuScenes training split ( 28 ,130 keyframes) and evaluate on four held-out validation scenes. These include scene-0523 ,used by RoMe and RoGS, two urban intersection scenes (scene-0035 , scene-0519 ), and one rainy sharp-turn scene ( scene-0905 ). Following RoMe [14] and RoGS [5], all methods use the same evaluation scenes and seven-class road masks and semantic pseudo-labels gener-ated by an off-the-shelf Mask2Former [3]. 

Baselines. We compare with RoMe [14], RoGS [5], VGGT-Long [4, 18], and DriveVGGT [7]. RoMe and RoGS are run using their official implementations and optimized in-dependently on each target sequence for two epochs, us-ing the same camera frames, poses, and Mask2Former road masks as BReD. RoGS is evaluated both with and without target-sequence LiDAR; only RoGS w/ LiDAR uses LiDAR at inference. VGGT-Long uses the pretrained VGGT model with chunk acceleration, and DriveVGGT uses its official nuScenes-trained checkpoint; both receive the same cali-brated camera sequences and perform feed-forward infer-ence without per-scene optimization. BReD is trained with LiDAR supervision but uses only calibrated cameras and poses at inference, without target-sequence optimization.

On the same evaluation scenes, valid support, and metrics, it also obtains lower error than the per-scene-optimized RoGS variant using target-sequence Li-DAR (8.88 cm).

4.4. Discussion 

Reconstruction Quality over Extreme Scenarios. Fig. 5 compares reconstruction at a curved intersection with dense crosswalks and multiple camera seams. Both methods re-cover comparable RGB appearance and semantic struc-tures, including crosswalks, lane lines, road edges, and side-walks. However, RoGS w/o LiDAR produces an almost uniform surface with limited transverse variation, whereas BReD preserves distinct elevations for the lane crown, curb, and sidewalk. Accordingly, BReD produces sparse resid-uals mainly near object boundaries, while RoGS shows widespread errors along the outer road. This comparison attributes BReD’s advantage in this challenging scene pri-marily to elevation accuracy rather than photometric or se-mantic quality.

5. Conclusion 

We presented BReD , a feed-forward framework for sequence-level road reconstruction from calibrated surround-view cameras. BReD combines LiDAR-supervised BEV lifting during training, single-step residual denoising, temporal surface aggregation, and geometry-prior-guided Gaussian decoding. It uses camera-only inference without scene-specific optimization and achieves 

7.34 cm elevation RMSE and 90 .92% mIoU on four held-out nuScenes scenes, providing an effective alternative to per-scene road reconstruction.
