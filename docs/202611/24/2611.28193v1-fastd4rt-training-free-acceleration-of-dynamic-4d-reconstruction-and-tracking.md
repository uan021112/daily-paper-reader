## Abstract
Query-based 4D reconstruction models such as D4RT encode a video once into a global scene representation and obtain outputs by decoding independent point queries. However, inference is often influenced by dense outputs and long videos. Although D4RT separates encoding from decoding, a dense output still requires one decoder query for every pixel of every frame, and a long video is still encoded as many overlapping segments. In this work, we analyze the inference cost of D4RT and show that a large part of it is redundant. In particular, we find that the later decoder blocks attend to a small set of tokens shared by all queries. Based on these findings, we propose FastD4RT, a training-free framework that accelerates D4RT. By exploiting the smoothness of the outputs over time and across pixels, FastD4RT encodes and decodes only at sparse anchors and recovers the remaining outputs via interpolation. It decodes the remaining queries against a shared token set selected once per segment. We further characterize the errors introduced by both components. Experimental results show that FastD4RT accelerates D4RT by about 5 times on average across all its tasks with competitive accuracy, and further improves accuracy on long videos. Our code is available at https://github.com/Lycheemeng/fastd4rt.
