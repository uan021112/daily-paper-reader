Abstract — Dexterous functional grasping is crucial for robotic tool use, yet today’s dexterous hands exhibit substantial morphological diversity. This hardware heterogeneity makes cross-embodiment functional grasping a fundamental but largely unexplored problem. Existing functional grasping methods typically use retargeted hand poses as guidance, which ties the task representation to a specific embodiment and does not explicitly capture object-centric functional contact intent, limiting generalization across hands. To address this, we propose FunGrasp-Zero, a reinforcement learning framework for zero-shot cross-embodiment functional grasping. Instead of relying on embodiment-specific joint configuration, FunGrasp-Zero cap-tures functional intent with an object-centric, cross-embodiment-consistent contact map, which is adapted to heterogeneous hands via morphological mapping. We construct a hand-object potential field from the mapped contact regions, yielding attractive and repulsive guidance vectors. A Field-Conditioned Graph Attention Network (FC-GAT) leverages these vectors to coordinate multi-finger motions across hands, while a potential field reward guides fingers toward target regions and penalizes non-target collisions to enforce functional alignment and stability. We further establish X-FunBench for cross-embodiment functional grasping that evaluates both physical grasp success and functional intent preservation across hands. Evaluated on X-FunBench, our unified policy, co-trained on four embodiments, achieves an 86% average grasp success rate across five unseen hands on novel objects. Deployed onto four unseen physical hands (Leap, Inspire, Sharpa, and Wuji) to grasp novel objects, the policy achieves an 80% average success rate without fine-tuning. To our knowledge, FunGrasp-Zero is the first RL-based framework to achieve zero-shot cross-embodiment functional grasping across unseen hands in both simulation and the real world. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

The rapid growth of dexterous hand platforms has intro-duced substantial diversity in hand morphology and kinemat-ics, making cross-embodiment generalization an important capability for dexterous manipulation [1]–[7]. For robotic tool use, however, functional grasping is not defined by a specific joint configuration, but by task-relevant contacts between particular hand parts and object regions [8]–[11]. Therefore, cross-embodiment transfer should preserve functional con-tact intent, which each hand can realize through its own morphology and kinematics rather than specific joint poses. Existing work has largely studied functional grasping and cross-embodiment dexterous manipulation separately. Conversely, recent cross-embodiment frameworks learn transferable representations or control interfaces across different hands, but mainly address generic grasping [2]–[6], [15], [16], reaching or in-hand reorientation [4], [17] without explicitly modeling functional contact intent. Thus, preserving functional grasp semantics while enabling a unified closed-loop policy to execute them across heterogeneous hands remains an open challenge. To our knowledge, no prior RL-based method has demonstrated zero-shot functional grasping generalization to unseen heterogeneous dexterous hands. To address both limitations, we seek a representation that preserves functional intent while remaining independent of embodiment-specific configurations. An object-centric poten-tial field provides such a representation. We therefore propose 

FunGrasp-Zero , a reinforcement learning framework for zero-shot cross-embodiment functional grasping. Instead of relying on embodiment-specific joint configurations, FunGrasp-Zero arXiv:2611.29195v1 [cs.RO] 22 Nov 2026 captures functional intent through an object-centric functional contact map, which is transferred to heterogeneous hands via morphological mapping. Grounded in the mapped contact prior, we construct a hand-object potential field that provides node-wise spatial guidance and dense rewards. It attracts des-ignated phalanges toward functional regions while repelling non-target contacts across different hands. Specifically, from a single human demonstration, we partition the MANO hand [18] mesh into phalangeal seg-ments and construct a canonical functional contact map on the object surface. Morphological mapping then transfers these regions to heterogeneous robotic hands according to their kinematic structures. We represent each hand as a morphology-aligned graph. For each hand node, we locate target contact points and non-target repulsive points within the mapped regions to derive spatial guidance vectors, thereby establishing a spatial potential field. Together with physical contact feedback, these guidance vectors form node features. Then a Field-Conditioned Graph Attention Network (FC-GAT) processes them to coordinate multi-finger motions across hands. Meanwhile, this potential field provides dense reinforcement learning rewards, where attractive terms guide fingers toward functional target regions and repulsive terms penalize unintended contacts. To evaluate zero-shot cross-embodiment functional grasp-ing, we establish X-FunBench , which combines diverse func-tional demonstrations with an evaluation protocol that mea-sures not only grasping success, but also whether functional contact intent is preserved across hands. On X-FunBench, our unified policy co-trained on four hands achieves a 94% success rate on seen objects with human-like functional grasps, and maintains 92% on unseen objects. Crucially, it directly generalizes zero-shot across five heterogeneous hands on novel objects with an 86% average success rate in simulation. Furthermore, deployed zero-shot onto four physical hands (Leap, Inspire, Wuji, and Sharpa), the policy achieves an 80% average success rate in the real world. To the best of our knowledge, we are the first closed-loop RL-based method to achieve zero-shot functional grasping generalization across unseen heterogeneous dexterous hands in both simulation and the real world. A. Learning-Based Dexterous and Functional Grasping 

Functional grasping targets task-relevant contacts while maintaining grasp stability for downstream manipulation [12], [19]. Learning-based methods have explored functional grasp synthesis from human demonstrations and contact-based representations [11], [20]–[22], but their open-loop execution is less robust to disturbances. In parallel, reinforcement learning methods learn closed-loop functional grasping poli-cies from demonstrations [13], [14], but typically use hand-centric retargeted grasp poses as guidance. This pose-based guidance is embodiment-specific and can lead to contact misalignment across different hand morphologies. Extending closed-loop functional grasping to unseen heterogeneous hand embodiments remains an open challenge. In contrast, our object-centric functional potential field represents functional intent without prescribing a specific hand joint configuration and provides spatial guidance for closed-loop execution across heterogeneous hands. 

B. Cross-Embodiment Dexterous Grasping 

Cross-embodiment dexterous grasping seeks representa-tions and control interfaces that generalize across hands with different morphologies and kinematic structures. Existing methods explore unified action spaces and kinematic map-pings [1]–[5], [16], human-like contact representations [11], morphology-aligned hand graphs [15], graph-structured multi-embodiment grasp generation [23], and hardware-agnostic interaction representations [8], [24]. These advances improve cross-embodiment grasp generation and control, but most focus on grasp synthesis or generic grasp execution rather than closed-loop task-specific functional interaction. FunGrasp-Zero bridges this gap by combining an object-centric func-tional potential field with a morphology-aligned hand graph, enabling a shared policy to execute consistent functional contacts across unseen heterogeneous hands. B. Topology-Aligned Functional Potential Field 

Embodiment-specific hand poses are not a natural represen-tation of cross-embodiment functional intent, since different hands may realize the same task-relevant contacts with different joint configurations. Therefore, we draw inspiration from artificial potential fields [25] to formulate functional grasping as a contact-matching potential field. Defined over topological functional regions, this field exerts attraction toward intended contacts and repulsion from non-target surfaces, steering heterogeneous hands to fulfill consistent functional intents while respecting their unique kinematics.

Morphological mapping. To project the canonical contact map CMANO (o) onto diverse robotic morphologies, we define a topological mapping τh : FMANO → F h governed by four rules (Fig. 3): 1) standard phalanges map one-to-one (e.g., Shadow); 2) hands lacking an independent little-finger metacarpal omit that region (e.g., Wuji); 3) fingers with fewer phalanges merge proximal and intermediate canonical MANO segments into a single robotic link (e.g., Brainco); and 4) four-fingered hands omit the entire little finger (e.g., Leap). Cross-hand functional grasping requires evaluating not only grasp success, but also whether the same functional intent is preserved across different hand morphologies. Existing benchmarks lack a unified protocol for such cross-hand func-tional evaluation. We therefore establish X-FunBench , which combines functional grasp demonstrations, embodiment-specific contact annotations and cross-hand evaluation metrics. 

Data. X-FunBench provides canonical functional contact annotations that can be mapped to different robotic hands, enabling consistent evaluation across hands. We curate training demonstrations from OakInk [26] and construct the entire test set from newly captured human demonstrations on metric-scale physical tools. Each instance provides the MANO hand pose, metric 6D object pose, and aligned 3D object mesh. In total, X-FunBench contains 100 functional grasp demonstrations across 33 object categories and multiple object scales, split into 50 training objects and 50 test objects. 

Evaluation Protocol. X-FunBench evaluates generalization along both object and hand dimensions. The 50-object test set spans 20 categories, including 8 unseen categories during training and novel object instances from 12 seen categories. For cross-embodiment evaluation, policies are co-trained on four hands (Shadow, Ability, Schunk, and BrainCo) and evaluated zero-shot on five unseen hands (Inspire, Sharpa, Wuji, LEAP, and PaXini). We conduct 20 randomized trials per object-hand pair without embodiment-specific fine-tuning. 

Evaluation Metrics. X-FunBench measures three comple-mentary aspects of functional grasping: (1) Success Rate (SR) ↑: the percentage of trials in which the object is lifted by more than 0.2 m and held for at least 20 consecutive time steps. (2) Functional Contact Distance (FCD, cm) 

↓: the average distance from each contacting fingertip to its assigned target functional region. Because target regions are mapped from the same canonical annotations, FCD measures whether functional intent is preserved across heterogeneous hands. (3) Relative Wobble Displacement (RWD, mm) ↓:

the mean inter-step object displacement relative to the wrist frame during lifting, measuring grasp stability. FCD (cm)

V. E XPERIMENTS 

We conduct benchmark evaluations on X-FunBench in simulation alongside real-world deployments across physical robotic hands, aiming to answer the following five questions: 

Q1: What zero-shot success rate does our method achieve across unseen hand embodiments and novel objects? Q2: Does the trained policy achieve functional intent alignment with human demonstrations? Q3: How does the single-hand suc-cess rate of FunGrasp-Zero compare against prior methods relying on kinematic retargeting or fixed grasp patterns? Q4: 

How do embodiment quantity and morphological diversity influence zero-shot cross-embodiment generalization? Q5: 

How reliably does our method perform when deployed on physical robotic systems in the real world?

B. Implementation Details 

Policies are trained and evaluated using PPO [30] in the RaiSim [31] simulation with an NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU. The FC-GAT backbone is implemented as a two-layer GAT. Prior to execution, the initial wrist pose is retracted from the reference demonstration along the palm normal with added spatial perturbations. Each episode comprises a 130-step exploration phase followed by a 50-step lifting phase. Empirically, the spatial decay rates are set to κatt = 10 .0 and 

κrep = 20 .0, node weights to wtip = 3 .0 and wbase = 1 .0,and scaling factors to λatt = 1 .0, λrep = 0 .05 , λmat = 40 .0,

λmis = 4 .0, λimp = 16 .0, and λpen = 2 .0.

E. Impact of Embodiment Diversity and Composition 

To answer Q4, we evaluate how embodiment diversity and quantity affect zero-shot cross-hand generalization. In Ta-ble III, co-training on two structurally similar high-DoF hands, Shadow and Schunk, yields an average zero-shot success rate of only 0.55 on unseen hands. In contrast, pairing the high-DoF Shadow hand with the low-DoF Ability hand improves the unseen success rate to 0.76. These results suggest that morphological diversity in the training set improves zero-shot transfer by exposing the policy to a broader range of kinematic structures. Building on this observation, our four-hand training setup combines two low-DoF hands with two high-DoF hands, establishing a balanced morphological distribution. As shown in Table I, extending to this four-hand configuration raises the unseen hand success rate to 0.86, achieving the best overall performance across both seen and unseen embodiments. The results suggest that both morphological diversity and quantity of training embodiments contribute to stronger cross-embodiment generalization.

G. Real-World Experiments 

As shown in Table V, the unified four-hand co-trained policy achieves an 80% average success rate across four unseen physical hands on novel tools, compared with 85% for dedicated single-hand policies. A trial is successful only when all three independent evaluators agree that the grasp preserves the demonstrated functional intent, demonstrating effective zero-shot sim-to-real transfer. VI. C ONCLUSION 

In this paper, we proposed FunGrasp-Zero , a reinforcement learning framework for zero-shot cross-embodiment dexterous functional grasping. By constructing a functional poten-tial field across different hands, FunGrasp-Zero uses field-conditioned node features and functional rewards to guide FC-GAT in coordinating multi-finger motions. Evaluations on X-FunBench and zero-shot deployments across four unseen physical hands demonstrate robust generalization and functional intent alignment across unseen hands and objects.
