## Abstract 

Event-supervised Gaussian reconstruction combines the high temporal resolution and wide dynamic range of event cameras with efficient explicit rendering. However, re-covering a continuous surface remains difficult: changes in rendered appearance can compensate for depth errors while preserving the predicted brightness changes. We in-troduce Event-Anchored Surface Transport (EAST), which couples an event-constrained signed distance field to Gaus-sian rendering. Triangulation-supported candidates anchor the zero set, while normal flow measured from event timing constrains ray–surface intersection depths. Gaussian cen-ters receive bounded corrections from the descriptor differ-ence between the live field and a frozen initial reference. The correction is zero at initialization, yet transmits im-age gradients to the live field. Experiments on PAEv3D and NeRF Synthetic show improved surface accuracy and reference-view rendering over Gaussian baselines, together with efficient mesh extraction from the learned field. 

## 1. Introduction 

Event cameras record asynchronous brightness changes with high temporal resolution and a wide dynamic range [3], supporting reconstruction under rapid motion and challenging illumination. Event-supervised Gaussian meth-ods fit these measurements through differentiable rasteri-zation and render the learned scene efficiently [6, 8, 11, 28]. Recovering a mesh requires the primitives that ex-plain image changes to also determine a consistent three-dimensional surface [5, 7]. RGB images directly constrain intensity and texture at each observed viewpoint. Events instead record thresholded changes in log intensity, leaving absolute appearance unob-served [3]. Event-based radiance fields recover appearance and density by explaining this temporal signal [13, 15, 19]. Surface reconstruction must additionally determine where along the moving rays those changes originate. This difference makes appearance–depth compensation particularly relevant to event supervision. Under local trans-lation, the predicted brightness-change response is propor-tional to image-gradient magnitude and inverse depth. Afarther edge with a stronger gradient can produce the same first-order change as a nearer, weaker edge. Gaussian ap-pearance, opacity, and projected footprint can adjust this gradient without correcting depth. Without direct inten-sity and texture observations to constrain these appearance adjustments, event-only fitting requires geometric evidence beyond brightness-change agreement. Figure 1(a,c,d) connects the local ambiguity to a con-trolled rendering diagnostic. Scaling Gaussian centers about a fixed camera and scaling their covariances accord-ingly preserves projected footprints under unchanged visi-bility, while a translated view changes the rendered image. This diagnostic motivates constraints that connect surface location across viewpoints. Triangulation and normal flow provide complementary geometric evidence. Triangulation tests whether rays from distinct camera poses support a common spatial location, supplying positional constraints across views [18, 32]. Nor-mal flow describes how observed edge structure moves in the image over time. Through calibrated camera motion, it constrains depth along viewing rays without using the im-age gradient chosen by the renderer [9]. Its information is directional and depends on motion relative to the edge, so it complements spatial ray agreement. An implicit field supplies a shared surface [20, 22, 24], allowing measure-ments from different positions, times, and edge orientations to constrain the same boundary. An SDF alone does not resolve appearance–depth com-pensation; accurate surface placement requires geometric constraints and effective rendering–surface coupling [20, 25]. Their coupling must therefore express geometric refinement relative to the initial field (Fig. 1(b)), so that surface changes affect the images with-out imposing an initial relocation. Returning image resid-uals through the same correction lets rendering refine the shared boundary. We propose Event-Anchored Surface Transport (EAST). Calibrated event rays provide three-dimensional candi-dates, and local event timing provides edge-normal motion. These observations constrain a signed distance field (SDF) through soft zero-set anchors and ray-intersection depths. To couple the field to rendering, EAST evaluates the live field and a frozen initial reference at each current Gaussian center. Their descriptor difference produces a bounded cor-rection that is initially zero but remains differentiable with respect to the live field. Geometric observations constrain the SDF directly, and event-image residuals refine it through the transported centers. Our contributions are: • A local analysis of appearance-gradient–depth compensa-tion that explains how measured event motion adds depth information to brightness-change fitting. • Reference-relative surface transport that combines sup-ported SDF anchors and measured normal flow with Gaussian rendering, enabling geometry and image resid-uals to refine a shared surface. • Improved surface accuracy and rendering over event-supervised Gaussian baselines on PAEv3D and NeRF Synthetic, with efficient native surface extraction. 

## 2. Related Work 

Event-based fields and geometry. Event multi-view stereo locates scene structure through agreement among calibrated event rays. EMVS accumulates back-projected rays and identifies regions of high ray density [18], while stereo event reconstruction exploits observations from two event cameras [32]. Contrast maximization estimates mo-tion, depth, and optical flow by aligning warped events [2]. These geometric constructions recover structure around event-generating edges. Implicit fields combine such lo-calized evidence across views. Neural rendering learns a scene representation whose predicted brightness changes explain the event stream. EventNeRF [19], E-NeRF [13], and Robust e-NeRF [15] learn radiance and density fields, enabling novel-view synthesis from event supervision. Ra-diance fields integrate along rays; surface fields locate a shared boundary. IDR [24] jointly learns an implicit sur-face and its appearance from multi-view images. NeuS [22] connects an SDF to volume rendering, and EventNeuS [20] combines SDF and density-field learning under event super-vision. A zero set supplies a continuous surface represen-tation; calibrated ray agreement and measured edge motion supply observations of its location. 

Event-supervised Gaussian reconstruction. Event-3DGS [6] and E-3DGS [29] adapt explicit Gaussian rendering to event observations, with E-3DGS addressing 2large and unbounded scenes. IncEventGS [8] reconstructs incrementally through tracking and mapping: the cur-rent Gaussian scene supports camera-motion estimation, and mapping jointly refines the scene and trajectory. EventSplat [28] uses event-to-video initialization and spline-interpolated poses to reconstruct from moving event cameras. EvGGS studies generalizable Gaussian reconstruction [21], Elite-EvGS distills event-to-video priors [31], and E2EGS addresses pose-free reconstruc-tion [12]. GPERT [14] further separates per-event depth ray tracing from snapshot intensity rendering, using warped events to connect geometry and photometry at different temporal resolutions. EAST couples this image-fitting capacity to an event-constrained surface. 

Interfaces between Gaussians and surfaces. Gaussian surface methods differ in how primitives represent geom-etry and how they exchange information with an implicit field. SuGaR [5] regularizes Gaussians toward surface alignment for mesh extraction. 2D Gaussian Splatting [7] uses oriented planar disks and perspective-correct splatting, giving the primitives an explicit surface interpretation. Ge-ometry Field Splatting [10] parameterizes a geometry field with Gaussian surfels and converts it to density for ren-dering. Gaussian Opacity Fields [27] extracts surfaces as opacity level sets with adaptive tetrahedral meshing. Hy-brid methods retain distinct Gaussian and implicit represen-tations. NeuSG [1] uses regularized Gaussian geometry to guide implicit reconstruction, with surface normals helping refine the Gaussian points. 3DGSR [16] links SDF values to Gaussian opacity and aligns rendered geometric attributes. GSDF [25] uses mutual guidance between Gaussian and SDF branches, while SurfaceSplat [4] initializes Gaussians from an SDF mesh and uses rendered novel views to refine the implicit surface. EAST instead transports Gaussian cen-ters through bounded descriptor changes relative to a frozen field. This preserves the initialized renderer and carries im-age gradients to the event-constrained SDF.

## 4. Experiments 

4.1. Experimental Setup 

We evaluate EAST on six PAEv3D objects and five NeRF Synthetic scenes: Chair, Drums, Hotdog, Lego, and Mic [17, 20]. Baselines include EventNeRF [19], Event-3DGS [6], IncEventGS [8], and EventNeuS [20]. All meth-ods use events, calibration, and supplied poses, without ref-erence RGB supervision or pretrained depth priors. 

Initialization and training. Gaussians and the SDF each undergo 10K initialization updates, followed by 20K joint updates. Gaussian initialization fits event-derived inten-sities and event counts; SDF initialization establishes the reference field. Joint training optimizes the live SDF and Gaussians with the reference field, Gaussian count, support weights, and transport radii fixed. Checkpoints are selected by training event loss. Ablations share the same initializa-tion and training schedule. Architecture, hyperparameters, and sampling details appear in the supplement. 

Evaluation. Accuracy (Acc.) and completeness (Comp.) measure reconstructed-to-reference and reference-to-reconstructed surface distances; CDsum is their sum. Surfaces are extracted on a common 300 3 grid and com-pared using 100K area samples in the supplied world coordinates. EAST and EventNeuS extract SDF zero sets, EventNeRF extracts a density isosurface, and Gaussian baselines use depth fusion. Full-frame PSNR, SSIM [23], and LPIPS [30] are evaluated over 21 reference views per PAEv3D object and seven per Synthetic scene.

Relative transport. Absolute projection slightly im-proves completeness but worsens accuracy and CDsum: greater proximity to the reference surface coexists with misplaced predicted geometry. Measured motion and coor-dinated gradients therefore complement supported anchor-ing and rendering feedback in refining the shared zero set.

## 5. Conclusion 

EAST combines supported event-ray anchors and measured normal flow with Gaussian rendering through bounded reference-relative transport. The shared SDF improves sur-face accuracy and image quality over Gaussian baselines on PAEv3D and NeRF Synthetic, with efficient mesh extrac-tion. 

Limitations and future work. EAST relies on supplied calibration and camera poses, which determine both trian-gulation and normal-flow constraints. Normal flow provides 8depth information only when projected translation has a nonzero component along the measured edge normal; pure rotation and tangential motion must rely on other geomet-ric evidence. The coupling also fixes each Gaussian’s sup-port weight and transport radius at initialization, so these bindings cannot adapt as surface estimates change. Joint pose–surface refinement is a promising direction for uncer-tain trajectories. Adaptive support could follow evolving geometry and incorporate previously unbound Gaussians as new depth evidence becomes available.
