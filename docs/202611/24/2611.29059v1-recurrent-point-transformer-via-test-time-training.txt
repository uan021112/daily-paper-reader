# Abstract 

Real-world point clouds are not naturally bounded: 3D scans grow as sensors move through rooms, LiDAR sur-veys extend across forest plots, and 4D captures can span thousands of frames. Yet most point-cloud encoders are de-signed for fixed-size inputs, forcing inference-time cropping or tiling that loses long-range context and requires ad-hoc output stitching. We formulate arbitrary-extent point-cloud segmentation as recurrent sequence-of-chunks processing and introduce PoinTTT, a recurrent point-cloud encoder built on Point Transformer V3 (PTv3). PoinTTT augments PTv3 with residual Large Chunk Test-Time Training (LaCT) layers in both the encoder and decoder. Each LaCT layer maintains compact fast weights as recurrent state: tokens from each chunk update the fast weights, which are then ap-plied to produce chunk features and passed to the next chunk. This design allows PoinTTT to train on fixed-size chunks while processing scenes of arbitrary spatial or temporal ex-tent, with activation memory bounded by the chunk size. A pilot study on ScanNet and nuScenes shows that recurrent TTT improves segmentation over PTv3 baselines. On FOR-instanceV2, PoinTTT achieves state-of-the-art forest seman-tic segmentation of 87 .9 mIoU with nearly constant memory. On HOI4D, online full-sequence recurrent inference carries state across clips rather than resetting at benchmark bound-aries, reaching 42 .6 mIoU; a matched full-sequence Mamba control reaches approximately 39 .9. These results suggest that test-time training is a practical primitive for scalable spatial and temporal point-cloud understanding. 

# 1. Introduction 

Modern 3D perception increasingly operates on observations that are unbounded in space, time, or both. As shown in Fig. 1a, indoor scans may span multiple rooms [1, 6, 10, 57], 4D captures grow as frames arrive [28], and dense LiDAR surveys can cover broad outdoor plots [3, 4, 17, 40, 44, 53]. Point clouds naturally represent such expanding observa-tions, yet most point-cloud backbones assume bounded in-puts [5, 9, 33, 34, 43, 46, 49–51, 58, 59, 61]. This creates a mismatch for tasks like semantic segmentation: every point requires a label, but the needed evidence may lie outside the current crop, tile, or clip. Partitioning large scenes there-fore limits context and forces the model to reset at artificial boundaries, rather than accumulating information across the 1arXiv:2611.29059v1 [cs.CV] 21 Nov 2026 full scene or sequence. We study a different interface for point cloud understand-ing: rather than treating cropping or tiling as an external inference procedure, we make chunking part of the model. An arbitrary-extent scene is represented as a sequence of fixed-size chunks processed by a recurrent encoder. The model sees one bounded chunk at a time, keeping training and activation memory controlled, while carrying a compact state across chunks so inference can scale with the spatial or temporal extent of the observation. For point clouds, this interface is practical: the same model can consume indoor scans, forest plots, and 4D frame streams without a memory footprint that grows with the number of points. The key design question is where recurrence should be introduced and what form the recurrent state should take. A strong local backbone such as PTv3 [50] already pro-vides effective geometric ordering and neighborhood rea-soning within each chunk, but it has no explicit mechanism for carrying information across chunks. Recent state-based sequence models, including linear attention [20, 42, 55], Mamba [11, 16, 22], and Test-time Training [41, 60], show that compact recurrent states can replace explicit all-pair context and improve efficiency. Our goal is complemen-tary: rather than replacing the point-cloud backbone, we add a cross-chunk memory module to it. Test-time training provides a natural mechanism for this memory by storing context in fast weights that are updated by each chunk and reused by subsequent chunks. We instantiate this idea as PoinTTT , a recurrent point-cloud encoder built upon PTv3 [50]. PoinTTT augments both encoder and decoder of PTv3 [50] with residual LaCT [60] layers. Each LaCT layer maintains a small fast-weight network as recurrent state. Given a chunk, its tokens update the fast weights; the updated weights are then applied to produce chunk features and carried forward to the next chunk. The residual is gated from zero, so PoinTTT retains PTv3 at initialization and learns to add cross-chunk compu-tation during training. The same recurrent encoder supports both offline and online settings: when the whole sequence is available, PoinTTT uses a bidirectional update-and-apply pass for full-scene spatial inference; for online 4D input, it propagates the same fast-weight state causally through the temporal stream, predicting each arriving chunk without future chunks (Sec. 4.3). We first conduct a pilot study on indoor ScanNet [10] and outdoor nuScenes [4]. On ScanNet, we find that feeding chunks bottlenecks the network, regardless of whether the network is trained on entire ScanNet scenes or trained chunk-wise. This supports our hypothesis that partial observation is suboptimal for point-cloud understanding and highlights the necessity of using context from the entire scene. We further compare PointMamba [26] and PoinTTT, and find that PoinTTT outperforms PointMamba by a large margin.

# 3. Related Work 

## 3.1. Point-Cloud Segmentation Backbones 

Point-cloud segmentation has progressed from permutation-invariant set encoders to architectures with increasingly rich local geometry and context. PointNet [33] and Point-Net++ [34] introduced direct neural processing of point sets, while DGCNN [46] and KPConv [43] improved local inter-action modeling. MinkowskiNet [9] made large voxelized scenes practical, and Point Transformer [61], PTv2 [49], Swin3D [56] and PTv3 [50] introduced attention-based ar-chitectures for 3D segmentation. Recent systems further scale this line through point prompt training in PPT [51], multi-dataset pretraining in Point-MoE [7], lighter trans-former design in LitePT [59], and vanilla transformer vari-ants in VOLT [58]. PointMamba [26] explores state-space sequence modeling for point-cloud analysis. These models provide strong backbones for bounded point sets, voxel sets, windows, or scenes. PoinTTT complements this progress by retaining the backbone as a local point-cloud encoder while introducing a compact recurrent state that carries informa-tion across chunk boundaries. This allows computation to extend beyond individual chunks and supports spatially or temporally extended inputs. 

## 3.2. Large-Scale Point-Cloud Understanding 

Large-scale point-cloud benchmarks make the bounded-input assumption visible. Indoor datasets such as S3DIS [1], Matterport3D [6], ScanNet [10], and ScanNet++ [57], together with outdoor LiDAR datasets such as Seman-tic3D [17], SemanticKITTI [3], nuScenes [4], Waymo [40], DALES [44], and FOR-instanceV2 [53], span diverse en-vironments. Yet mainstream backbones, including point-based methods [33, 34], voxel and sparse convolutional methods [8, 15, 23, 39], and transformer-based meth-ods [49, 50, 56, 61], are in practice trained and evaluated on sampled blocks, sliding windows or tiles. Dynamic point cloud datasets such as MSR-Action3D [24], NTU RGB+D [37], Synthia 4D [9], and HOI4D [28], together with the temporal extensions of outdoor LiDAR benchmarks above, add a temporal version of the same scaling issue. A line of work models point-cloud sequences via spatiotemporal neighborhoods, 4D convolutions, transformer attention, or primitive-level hierarchies [9, 12–14, 25, 27, 28, 30, 47, 48], but the common protocols still operate on short clips or reset state at clip boundaries. PoinTTT uses a shared recurrent chunk interface for large spatial scenes and full temporal sequences, carrying state across chunk boundaries. 

## 3.3. State Recurrence and Test-Time Training 

State recurrence has re-emerged as a scalable alternative to explicit all-pair attention. Linear attention [20, 35, 36, 42, 54, 55] can be viewed as a recurrent accumulation of key-value statistics, state-space models such as Mamba [11, 16, 22] use compact causal state, and TTT layers [41] make the state an inner-loop-updated network. LaCT [60] improves the efficiency and capacity of this view by updating large chunks with expressive fast-weight MLPs. In 3D, closely related reconstruction systems use TTT or LaCT-like states to compress long image sequences into compact scene rep-resentations, including tttLRM [45], ZipMap [19], and Fast Spatial Memory [31]. A separate line of work brings test-time learning to point clouds for distribution shift rather than long-range memory: MATE [32] uses masked autoencoding as an online test-time objective for point cloud classification, SMART-PC [2] adapts robust skeletal representations un-der point-cloud corruptions, and PointMAC [18] uses meta-learned auxiliary objectives for test-time point-cloud com-pletion. These methods adapt a model to a sample or target distribution, whereas PoinTTT uses fast weights that store context across chunks of the same large scene or temporal sequence, and its update-and-apply recipe applies for cross-chunk recurrence while respecting the unordered nature of points within each chunk. 

# 4. Method 

PoinTTT processes arbitrary-extent point clouds as se-quences of bounded chunks. Its core mechanism is a recur-rent fast-weight state, implemented with residual LaCT [60] layers, that is updated by each chunk and reused by subse-quent chunks. The underlying point-cloud backbone pro-vides local feature extraction within each chunk, while LaCT carries information across chunks. This separates local ge-ometry processing from global context accumulation, allow-ing one model to handle both large static scenes and stream-ing 4D inputs while keeping activation memory bounded by the size of each chunk.

## 4.3. PoinTTT Architecture 

PoinTTT augments a point-cloud backbone with residual LaCT [60] layers. The backbone extracts local geometric features inside each chunk, while LaCT carries informa-4tion across chunks through the recurrent fast-weight state in Eq. (1). We insert LaCT layers in both the encoder and decoder, allowing both geometric and semantic features to accumulate cross-chunk context. Each LaCT layer is inserted as a gated residual branch,

Here Ot is the projected LaCT output from Eq. (2), Ht de-notes the backbone features for chunk Pt, and λ is initialized to zero. Thus, PoinTTT starts from the original backbone behavior and learns to add cross-chunk computation during training. Spatial and temporal inference use the same resid-ual LaCT block, with fast weights propagated according to the chosen inference mode. The recurrent interface has two modes, depending on whether all chunks are available before prediction. 

Offline spatial inference. For static scenes, all chunks are available before prediction. PoinTTT first scans the chunks in forward order under no_grad , using Eq. (1): 

Wt = Update( Wt−1, H t), W0 = Winit .

This pass initializes the fast weights with full-scene context without storing full-scene activations. PoinTTT then runs an apply pass over the sequence in reverse order, starting from the final state WT , and produces the chunk features eHt and logits ˆYt,

eHt = Ht + tanh( λ) Apply( WT , H t),

ˆYt = Decode( eHt).

The reverse apply pass is differentiable with respect to both the LaCT layers and the surrounding backbone parameters during training. Gradients are accumulated across the apply sequence and backpropagated after the full pass completes. At inference time, this update-and-apply procedure predicts each chunk using state informed by the full spatial sequence, while keeping activation memory bounded by chunk size. 

Online temporal inference. For dynamic scenes, future observations are unavailable when the current chunk must be predicted. PoinTTT therefore uses causal recurrence: 

Wt = Update( Wt−1, H t),

eHt = Ht + tanh( λ) Apply( Wt, H t),

ˆYt = Decode( eHt).

The prediction at step t depends only on chunks observed up to time t, making the model online in the standard stream-ing sense. The fast weights act as recurrent memory over the 4D sequence. During training, gradients are accumu-lated across the sequence and backpropagated after the full sequence has been processed.

# 5. Experiments 

We evaluate whether PoinTTT scales beyond the ScanNet and nuScenes pilot study (Sec. 2) to arbitrary-extent spatial and temporal inputs. We study two settings: forest seg-mentation , where the number of spatial chunks grows with scan size, and online 4D segmentation , where chunks arrive over time. Unlike standard scan- or clip-based inference, PoinTTT initializes its fast-weight state once per test scan or video and processes the entire input recurrently before computing the final metric. 

## 5.1. Setup 

Datasets and metrics. FOR-instanceV2 [53] contains large forest LiDAR plots with ground, wood, and leaf labels; indi-vidual scenes range from millions of points to roughly 4×10 8

points. We report class IoU and mIoU. HOI4D [28] con-tains dynamic hand–object point-cloud sequences; we report semantic segmentation mIoU over the temporal stream. 

Training. For FOR-instanceV2 and HOI4D, we first pretrain the PTv3 [50] backbone on fixed-size point-cloud chunks using the standard point-wise cross-entropy segmentation loss. This stage learns local geometric and semantic features for the chunk encoder. We then insert residual LaCT [60] layers and finetune PoinTTT end-to-end, updating both the backbone and recurrent layers with the update-and-apply recurrence active. Forest pretraining and finetuning use 100 and 200 epochs, respectively; HOI4D uses 50 epochs for each stage. Both stages use AdamW with OneCycleLR schedules. Forest training uses a single A100 GPU in both stages. HOI4D pretraining runs on one A100 GPU, while finetuning uses eight A100 GPUs. Inference for both bench-marks is performed on a single A100 GPU. See the appendix for batch sizes, parameter-group learning rates, and sched-uler settings for pretraining and finetuning. 

Mamba baselines. For spatial modeling, we adapt Point-Mamba [26] to scene-level semantic segmentation using the same PTv3-style scene processing protocol as our baselines, while preserving its original tokenization and serialization. For the PTv3+Mamba control in Sec. 5.3, we use the tempo-ral Mamba implementation from PointNet4D [30].

## 5.2. Forest Spatial Segmentation 

Tab. 2 compares PoinTTT with prior forest segmentation methods on FOR-instanceV2 [53]. PoinTTT is trained only on fixed-size chunks, but at test time it is applied to each forest plot as a full-scene recurrent sequence over all chunks. Thus, the model keeps the same bounded-memory interface used during training while allowing information to be car-ried across the entire plot. PoinTTT reaches 87.9 mIoU, improving over PTv3 [50] by 1.4 points and over Forest-Former3D [53] by 1.7 points. The largest gain appears on the wood class, where stems and branches often extend across local partitions. This sug-gests that full-plot context is useful for point-cloud under-standing, and that fast-weight recurrence provides an effec-tive memory mechanism for aggregating such cross-chunk information. Qualitative results in Fig. 3(b) further show that PoinTTT better preserves connected tree structures during offline spatial inference. 

## 5.3. Online 4D Temporal Segmentation 

Streaming inference beyond the local window. PoinTTT processes each HOI4D video as a stream of three-frame chunks, each serving as the input window jointly encoded at one step. The fast-weight state is initialized once per video and carried across chunks; predictions depend only on observations received so far. The context horizon grows with the observed video prefix, reaching approximately 300 frames at the end of a video. The recurrence has no preset video-length limit: processing can continue with the same chunk budget and fixed-size state. The reported experiment tests this capability over complete HOI4D videos. 

Published protocols and matched memory mechanisms. 

Prior HOI4D baselines use short-clip protocols, typically three frames, with ten-frame variants also reported [30]. Our PTv3 baseline follows this local-window setup and is trained and evaluated on three-frame clips. Tab. 3 reports the pub-lished three-frame results, distinguishing the offline super-vised results in PointNet4D [30], Table 9, from its online P4Mamba result in Table 7. To compare recurrent memory mechanisms on the same long sequences, we construct a PTv3+Mamba baseline by replacing LaCT’s fast-weight memory with Mamba selec-tive state-space layers. We retain the same PTv3 backbone, three-frame input windows, training setup, parameter budget, and full-video streaming protocol. Both models carry their respective recurrent states across the complete video. Tab. 3 reports 39.9 mIoU for PTv3+Mamba and 42.6 for PoinTTT. Complementary HOI4D approaches include LeaF [29] and 4DMAP, the frame-wise masked autoregressive pretrain-ing scheme introduced with PointNet4D [30]. Our com-parison here focuses on 4D point-cloud encoder backbones under supervised semantic segmentation training. 

## 5.4. Qualitative Comparison 

On ScanNet (Fig. 3), PTv3 exhibits block-shaped and diago-nal artifacts introduced by Hilbert-order chunk partitioning; PoinTTT reduces these artifacts and produces smoother se-mantic boundaries. On FOR-instanceV2, PTv3 struggles with tilted trunks and irregular branches that deviate from dominant vertical structures, while PoinTTT better preserves their geometric continuity. On HOI4D, PTv3 is more prone to frame-local mistakes, such as confusing moving objects with nearby background regions. Temporal context across frames helps PoinTTT make more stable predictions and distinguish moving objects from the background.

Architecture design. Joint encoder–decoder insertion out-performs either placement alone (Tab. 4c), suggesting that scene context benefits both feature extraction and point-wise reconstruction. Each inserted layer maintains its own fast weights and forms associations from the features at that stage, allowing different resolutions to maintain different represen-tations of the scene. Chunk size. Chunk size controls both the geometry jointly available to the backbone and the granularity of recurrent updates. For a fixed scene, larger chunks produce fewer state updates, each incorporating more points; the fast-weight state capacity remains unchanged. Update steps. Each inner update writes key–value associa-tions from the current chunk into the fast weights. Repeating the update revisits the same observations; it does not intro-duce additional points or extend the observed scene. More updates per chunk do not improve segmentation accuracy (Tab. 4g). The associative update objective and the seg-mentation objective serve different purposes, so more inner optimization need not yield better predictions. This also dif-fers from adding recurrent layers in Tab. 4f, where each layer writes and reads features from a different stage of the back-bone. We use one update per chunk, which gives the best result in this sweep without the additional inner iterations. 

Input budgets and memory scaling. Tab. 4h extends the pi-lot study to test whether the advantage of recurrence persists across different local input budgets. On ScanNet, both mod-els benefit from larger evaluation chunks, while PoinTTT outperforms independently chunked PTv3 at every matched point budget. Both methods eventually process all points in a scene. However, merging independently predicted chunks does not let an individual prediction use information from outside its own chunk. PoinTTT can condition that pre-diction on information accumulated in the recurrent state. Its advantage at larger budgets suggests that local geomet-ric coverage and cross-chunk context offer complementary benefits: a larger chunk strengthens the local input, while recurrence supplies information beyond it. Tab. 4i compares full-scene PTv3 with chunked PoinTTT on the same scene, using different per-forward point bud-gets. Full-scene processing keeps activations for the entire input, whereas PoinTTT processes a bounded chunk and retains scene information in its fast weights. These weights have a fixed size per layer, independent of the total point count, and are not a growing cache of point-wise features. Compressing context into this state allows information to persist across chunks without keeping full-scene activations, accounting for the lower peak VRAM of chunked execution. The appendix provides the per-forward point budgets and the memory required to store the fast weights. 

# 6. Conclusion 

We argue that arbitrary-extent point-cloud segmentation should be treated as a recurrent inference problem, rather than as independent crop or clip prediction. Chunking pro-vides the necessary bounded-memory interface, but our Scan-Net [10] pilot shows that chunking alone creates a context bottleneck: predictions made from local chunks cannot fully recover scene-level reasoning. Thus, we introduce PoinTTT to address this bottleneck by carrying compact LaCT [60] fast weights across the chunk sequence. These fast weights act as recurrent scene memory, allowing the model to accumulate context while keeping acti-vation memory tied to the chunk size. The same mechanism supports both offline spatial inference over large static scenes and online temporal inference over 4D streams. Across Scan-Net, FOR-instanceV2 [53], HOI4D [28], and nuScenes [4], PoinTTT improves segmentation under fixed-memory in-8ference and demonstrates that fast-weight TTT recurrence is a practical way to connect local point-cloud chunks into coherent full-scene or full-sequence predictions.

## A.2. Evaluation Pipeline Details 

For static forest plots, PoinTTT resets the fast-weight state at the beginning of each test plot. The plot is partitioned into a sequence of fixed-size chunks, the offline update pass processes all chunks to build a plot-level state, and the apply pass decodes chunk predictions using that state. Per-chunk predictions are then mapped back to the original points, and class IoU and mIoU are computed over the full plot. For 4D sequences, PoinTTT resets the fast-weight state only at the beginning of each video. At step t, the model re-ceives the current three-frame chunk, updates the state, emits point-wise predictions, and carries the updated state to t+1 .Future chunks are not used. This differs from window-based HOI4D evaluation, where methods typically process short windows and reset the backbone state at clip boundaries. 

## A.3. Additional Evaluation Details 

For the ScanNet memory measurements in Tab. 4i, PTv3 processes the full scene (about 5.2 million points) in one forward pass. PoinTTT processes up to 10 ,240 points per pass. Its recurrent state holds about 280 ,000 fast weights and occupies approximately 1 MB. The published PTv3 score of 80.4 mIoU on nuScenes uses test-time augmentation. Our nuScenes results in Tab. 1 use a single pass without augmentation. 

## A.4. Limitations and Future Work 

PoinTTT provides a memory-bounded interface for arbitrary-extent point-cloud segmentation, and several directions re-main for future work. First, because the fast-weight state is propagated recurrently, inference is naturally sequential along the chunk axis. This keeps activation memory bounded by chunk size, but the total runtime still grows with the number of chunks. Future work could improve inference efficiency with parallel, hierarchical, or cached state propa-gation while preserving the recurrent context mechanism. Second, our experiments cover four complementary set-tings: ScanNet for indoor segmentation, FOR-instanceV2 for large forest plots, and HOI4D for online 4D segmen-tation, together with nuScenes for outdoor driving LiDAR. Due to compute constraints, we do not scale the evaluation to a larger collection of datasets or longer training sched-ules. Extending PoinTTT to more domains, sensors, and scene types would further test the generality of fast-weight recurrence for point-cloud understanding. Finally, PoinTTT is trained in a supervised setting and does not yet use large-scale point-cloud pretraining. We expect pretraining on diverse 3D and 4D data to improve robustness, cross-domain transfer, and long-range context ag-gregation. This is a natural next step, especially for settings with domain shift such as forests captured under different sensors, acquisition conditions, or vegetation distributions. We leave this as future work. 12
