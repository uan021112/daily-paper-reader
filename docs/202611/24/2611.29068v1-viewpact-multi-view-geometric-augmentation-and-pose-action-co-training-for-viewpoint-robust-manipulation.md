## Abstract
Image policies are scalable but remain brittle to camera viewpoint changes. On LIBERO, we identify proprioception as an input shortcut: masking robot states sharply degrades several policies, while retraining from images alone recovers much of the loss. We introduce ViewPACT, an image policy trained with privileged geometric supervision. Auxiliary objectives recover end-effector and task-relevant object poses in the camera frame, while deployment requires only RGB observations. Joint geometric supervision improves mean simulation success from 37.4% to 78.4% under unseen external-camera viewpoints, with more modest gains under separately evaluated object, scene, and camera-intrinsic shifts. Across four real-robot tasks, combining simulated and real-image geometric supervision achieves 68.1% mean success, versus 3.1% for fixed-view behavior cloning and 30.6% for the strongest evaluated alternative. These results support geometric supervision for viewpoint robustness in the evaluated fixed-view demonstration setting.
