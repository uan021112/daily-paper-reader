# GAAFormer: Geometry-Anchored Attention Transformer for Joint Point Cloud Denoising and Upsampling 

## Haoxi Tian School of Civil Engineering and Geomatics Southwest Petroleum University 

## Abstract 

Point cloud denoising and upsampling require samples that accurately recover the underlying surface from noisy or sparse observations. A central challenge in both tasks is preserving surface coverage while refining point locations. We introduce GAAFormer (Geometry-Anchored Attention Transformer), a unified generative octree transformer that performs both tasks with a shared network. Its decoder anchors attention neighborhoods to geometry, providing spatial context for occupancy prediction and point genera-tion. Fixed voxel windows and alternating shifts preserve the grouping of surviving nodes as the output tree changes. The design retains the encoder, positional encoding, prediction heads, and parameter count of a serialized-attention back-bone. We establish a deletion-consistency property of the neighborhood rule and evaluate both tasks using the same model. On PUNet, GAAFormer achieves a mean Chamfer distance (CD) of 6.0643 × 10 −5, reducing CD and Haus-dorff distance by 3.35% and 19.34%, respectively, relative to the serialized backbone. For upsampling, it achieves CD values of 0.8475 , 1.7057 , and 1.0147 (×10 −5) on PU-GAN, PU1K, and Sketchfab, respectively, with improvements in CD, Hausdorff distance, and point-to-surface distance over the same backbone on PU-GAN and Sketchfab. These results establish a single architecture for point cloud denoising and upsampling. Within the shared encoder–decoder, the same geometry-anchored attention module improves both noise removal and dense point generation without task-specific redesign or additional parameters. 

## 1. Introduction 

Point cloud denoising removes geometric noise, while point cloud upsampling generates dense samples from sparse ob-servations. Both tasks require accurate recovery of the un-derlying surface: predicted points must lie near the surface and cover its geometry. A unified network must therefore suppress noise and generate new samples while preserving geometric detail and surface coverage. Figure 1 contrasts task-specific pipelines with our unified architecture, in which the same geometric attention module and network weights support both tasks. Generative octree decoders provide a shared represen-tation for these tasks. They predict occupied cells from coarse to fine and place a point in each retained leaf, al-lowing the output support and cardinality to differ from the input [16, 17]. Transformer backbones add feature interac-tions over groups of octree nodes. Serialized neighborhoods make these interactions efficient [15, 19], but their member-ship depends on positions in a compact node sequence. In a generative decoder, this sequence is rebuilt as predicted occupancy changes. The central problem is preserving reliable geometric con-text while the decoder changes its own set of active cells. Pruning a branch can shift surviving nodes across serial-ized group boundaries even when their coordinates are un-changed, altering the context used to predict occupancy and point locations. An incorrect coarse occupancy decision re-moves all descendant samples, so finer coordinate refinement cannot restore the excluded region. This creates a shared difficulty: denoising must suppress perturbations while re-taining valid surface regions, whereas upsampling must ex-tend geometric evidence into sparsely sampled regions. Both tasks therefore require spatially consistent attention neigh-borhoods throughout tree generation. To address this problem, we introduce GAAFormer (Geometry-Anchored Attention Transformer), a unified gen-erative octree transformer whose decoder anchors attention groups to spatial coordinates. Each cell is assigned to a fixed 

4 × 4 × 4 voxel window, and consecutive blocks alternate the window shift to exchange information across boundaries. At a fixed depth and shift, group assignment depends only on coordinates, so removing nodes does not regroup the sur-vivors. One encoder–decoder, shared prediction heads, and a single checkpoint serve both denoising and upsampling. We evaluate GAAFormer on PUNet for denoising and on PU-GAN, PU1K, and Sketchfab for upsampling. ## 2. Related Work 

Point cloud denoising and upsampling. Learning-based upsampling constructs dense samples from sparse obser-vations. PU-Net [22] expands learned point features, PU-GAN [7] introduces adversarial distribution learning, and PU-GCN [12] uses graph convolutions and NodeShuffle. MPU [21] and PU-CRN [2] refine points progressively, while PU-Transformer [13] explores transformer feature extraction. Neural Points [3] and iPUNet [18] learn continuous local sur-faces; Grad-PU [4] refines interpolated points using a learned distance field. For denoising, PointCleanNet [14] learns out-lier removal and point correction; differentiable manifold reconstruction [10] reconstructs local surfaces before resam-pling; and score-based denoising [11] iteratively updates points using an estimated score field. PointFilter [23] pre-dicts point displacements, PCDNF [9] couples denoising with normal filtering, and IterativePFN [1] learns successive filtering stages. Our focus is the representation of context inside a hierarchical decoder that generates output support. 

Octree representations and generation. O-CNN [16] makes high-resolution 3D convolution practical by orga-nizing features in an octree. Output-guided skip connec-tions [17] align encoder information to a generated output tree, enabling shape and scene completion. The same output-guided formulation has been applied to joint point cloud upsampling and cleaning [6]. We retain its tree construction, scale transitions, aligned skip connections, occupancy heads, and leaf coordinate prediction. Our contribution concerns the decoder’s attention neighborhoods, which must remain spatially meaningful as the generated support changes. 

Attention neighborhoods for 3D data. Point Trans-former [24] develops attention over local point neighbor-hoods. Stratified Transformer [5] combines dense nearby keys with sparse distant keys. OctFormer [15] partitions sorted octree keys into fixed-cardinality groups, while Point Transformer V3 [19] further demonstrates the scalability of serialization. Spatial windows and shifts originate in Swin Transformer [8]; Swin3D [20] extends window-based pro-cessing to sparse 3D representations. Thus, spatial windows themselves are an established primitive. We study their particular role when the network generates and prunes the domain on which its attention operates, and give an explicit membership property for that changing domain. 

Position encoding and neighborhood construction. Rel-ative positional information can distinguish locations within an attention group, while convolutional positional encoding introduces local features [5, 15]. These operations comple-ment the choice of which keys enter a group. In GAAFormer, the original positional encoding and projection parameters remain in place; coordinate anchoring changes the support of the attention operator. This separation enables direct initial-ization from the serialized backbone and direct replacement of its grouping rule. 23. Method 

3.1. Motivating Observations 

In both denoising and upsampling, the generated points should recover the geometry and coverage of the underlying surface. We examine how octree generation and attention neighborhoods affect this shared requirement. 

Surface loss from coarse pruning. Let a decoder expand occupied octree cells from depth d0 to depth D. If a cell at depth d is rejected, none of its descendants can appear in the generated tree. A local occupancy error therefore has a spatial extent that depends on its depth. Coarse decisions determine the spatial support available for subsequent re-finement, while the finest coordinate head positions samples inside the retained leaves. Geometric context should there-fore support occupancy prediction throughout the hierarchy. 

Neighborhood sensitivity to tree updates. Figure 3 sum-marizes a perturbation study of the grouping rule. We delete 1% of depth-five branches in six training geometries and in-spect 4,096 queries at each of depths six through eight. After removing deleted neighbors from the comparison, the mean neighborhood Jaccard is 0.608 for regular Morton groups and 0.546 for dilated groups. Coordinate windows preserve a Jaccard of 1.000. These observations motivate spatially bounded attention neighborhoods whose membership fol-lows the coordinates of the generated cells as the output support changes.

3.4. Deletion Consistency and Computational Cost 

Proposition 1 (Deletion-consistent membership) . Fix an octree depth, a shift, and the coordinates and batch indices of the nodes. For any removed set R ⊆ U d and surviving query i ∈ U d \ R,

Ns(i; Ud \ R) = Ns(i; Ud) \ R. (9) 

Proof. Membership in Equation (7) depends only on the query and key coordinates and their batch indices. Removing other nodes changes none of these quantities. The remaining members are therefore exactly the original members outside 

R.This property rules out regrouping caused solely by com-paction of the active node list. It concerns the attention graph at one layer; attention weights can still change when a key in the same window is deleted, and the full network can change features through its other operators. For comparison, a fixed-cardinality serialized partition can move a surviv-ing node across a group boundary after an earlier entry is removed. The proposition formalizes the membership dis-tinction without imposing assumptions on learned feature values. For Gd nonempty windows and channel width C, the at-tention score and value operations cost O(Gd 64 2C), and the padded feature buffer has 64 Gd entries. Since the window ca-pacity is fixed and Gd ≤ |U d|, this component scales linearly in the number of cells, with a padding-dependent constant. Coordinate grouping is an additional implementation cost; the expression does not assert a linear-time sorting routine. The new grouping adds no learned tensors. ## 4. Experiments 

4.1. Experimental Setup 

Tasks and data. We evaluate denoising on 20 PUNet test geometries under six conditions: 10k or 50k input points with 1%, 2%, or 2.5% noise, giving 120 inputs. Upsampling uses 27 PU-GAN, 100 PU1K, and 14 Sketchfab inputs, giv-ing 141 inputs. The same GAAFormer checkpoint handles all 261 denoising and upsampling inputs. 

Implementation. We optimize the models for 1,024 steps using AdamW with learning rate 10 −5, weight decay 0.05, and batch size two, and evaluate the final checkpoints. 

Metrics. CD is the sum of the two mean squared nearest-neighbor distances. HD is the maximum squared nearest-neighbor distance over both directions. P2F is mean Eu-clidean distance to the reference mesh. We report CD ×10 5,HD ×10 3, and P2F ×10 3; lower is better. Farthest-point sampling (FPS) caps predictions at 10k or 50k points for denoising and 80k for upsampling. Predictions already be-low the cap are unchanged. Raw evaluations retain every generated point. 

Comparison methods. We compare with representative denoising and upsampling methods, including OUNet [6] as a representative octree-convolutional network. External com-parison values in Tabs. 1 and 2 are taken from its reported benchmark. We denote the counterpart with serialized de-coder attention by Serial-OUFormer. GAAFormer replaces its decoder grouping while retaining the encoder, positional encodings, and prediction heads. Missing entries are shown as dashes; HD values are converted to the display units above.

4.6. Inference Cost 

We measure inference on an RTX 4090 for a 10k denois-ing input, a 50k denoising input, and a PU-GAN upsam-pling input. Latency is the median of three synchronized repeats after two warm-ups. Relative to Serial-OUFormer, GAAFormer changes latency from 131.02 to 135.79 ms, from 251.40 to 259.94 ms, and from 113.68 to 124.82 ms, respectively. Peak allocated memory decreases from 1,273.7 to 1,043.3 MiB, from 2,341.8 to 2,057.8 MiB, and from 1,342.9 to 1,091.9 MiB. The two architectures each contain 10,623,997 parameters. 

## 5. Conclusion 

We presented GAAFormer, a unified generative octree trans-former for point cloud denoising and upsampling. Its geometry-anchored attention module combines fixed win-dows and alternating shifts to preserve surviving-cell neigh-borhood membership and exchange context across bound-aries. The module serves both tasks within one encoder– decoder with shared prediction heads and a single check-point, without additional learned parameters. On PUNet, the module reduces mean denoising CD and HD by 3.35% and 19.34% over the serialized backbone. The same module improves upsampling CD, HD, and P2F on PU-GAN and Sketchfab. Ablations identify spatial grouping as the cen-tral source of denoising gains, and inference measurements show lower peak memory.
