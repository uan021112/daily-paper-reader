# Abstract 

Text-to-3D solid retrieval faces a fundamental representation gap: while natural language describes intrinsic shapes, Boundary Representation (B-rep) entangles precise geometry with arbitrary variations in pose, scale, and boundary encoding conventions. Existing methods either discretize B-reps at the cost of analytic precision, or learn features from topological graphs without explicit invariance guarantees. We propose a text-to-B-rep retrieval framework based on geometric equivalence classes and spectral invariants, mathematically eliminating representation ambiguities prior to cross-modal alignment. Formalizing translation, rotation, reflection, uniform scaling, and contour re-indexing via group actions, we exploit the planar-profile-and-extrusion structure of engineering solids to construct a unified spectral descriptor across cross-sectional regions, contour boundaries, and extrusion depths. On public benchmarks, our method achieves state-of-the-art recall performance across all evaluation metrics. 1arXiv:2610.33484v2 [cs.CV] 27 Oct 2026 × Text-to-B-rep Retrieval

# 1 Introduction 

Text-to-3D retrieval identifies models that match a natural-language description. As 3D design collections grow, retrieval is extending from generic objects to engineering models [1–4]. Boundary Representation (B-rep) describes solid boundaries through parametric curves, surfaces, and their topological relations, preserving analytic geometry that discrete point clouds, voxels, and meshes do not represent explicitly [5–7]. Engineering retrieval therefore requires aligning language with this precise geometric representation. The difficulty is that language describes shape, whereas B-rep encodings also record arbitrary representation choices. For the bearing support in Figure 1(a), changing the coordinate frame, contour starting point, or traversal direction leaves the shaft bore and slotted mounting foot unchanged. The same description still applies, although the numerical encoding changes. Discretizing B-reps into point clouds [8–10] or meshes [11, 12] introduces sampling sensitivity and loses analytic precision. Graph-based encoders [6, 7] retain boundary topology but must learn to separate geometry from its encoding conventions. We instead remove these variations before cross-modal alignment.

Our starting point is the geometric equivalence class illustrated in Figure 1(b): shape-preserving changes share a description, while removing the bore changes the geometry. We formalize translation, rotation, reflection, uniform scaling, and contour re-indexing as group actions, then construct descriptors on the resulting equivalence classes. Spectral geometry provides coordinate-independent shape information [13–15]. 3× Text-to-B-rep Retrieval 

For a straight extrusion, its planar-profile-and-depth structure also permits separation of variables in the 3D Laplacian. We combine the regional Dirichlet spectrum, boundary turning Fourier magnitudes, and extrusion depth to describe these complementary geometric components without a volumetric eigensolve. This equivalence-class perspective also informs cross-modal training. Standard single-positive contrastive learning treats each model–text pair independently, so geometrically identical solids with different con-struction records can become false negatives. Descriptor-consistent multi-positive sets allow equivalent solids to share textual supervision. We evaluate the resulting alignment on Text2CAD [16], recovering the corresponding B-rep solids from construction records, and on native STEP models from MMCAD-B. 

## 1.1 Contributions 

• A Quotient-Space Perspective on B-rep Representation. We identify that a core bottleneck in Text-to-B-rep retrieval is the entanglement of intrinsic geometry with extrinsic boundary encoding conventions. By formulating geometric invariance via group actions and equivalence classes, we shift the paradigm from data-driven invariance learning to mathematically guaranteed invariant representations. • Analytic Spectral Descriptors for B-rep Solids. Exploiting the parallel between extruded solid geometry and separation of variables, we construct a unified spectral descriptor combining Dirichlet eigenvalues and boundary Fourier magnitudes. It captures regional, boundary, and extrusion geometry directly from B-reps without discretization losses. 

# 2 Related Work 

Text-to-3D retrieval. Cross-modal retrieval between text and 3D shapes has been explored through point clouds [17–19], meshes, and rendered images [20, 21]. ShapeGlot [22] learns joint embeddings between lan-guage and 3D shapes via contrastive objectives. Text2Shape [19] aligns text with colored point clouds. More recent methods leverage pretrained vision-language models [18, 21, 23–25]: ULIP [17] and OpenShape [26] align point-cloud encoders with CLIP embeddings. However, these methods target mesh or point-cloud representations and do not address parametric CAD models with their construction-sequence structure. 

CAD representation learning. DeepCAD [27] pioneered autoregressive generation of CAD construction sequences. SkexGen [28] disentangles sketch and extrusion generation. CAD-SIGNet [29] learns signatures over construction graphs. Text2CAD [16] provides multi-level text descriptions for DeepCAD and generates parametric CAD construction sequences from text. CADIR [30] introduces an executable CAD intermediate representation and supports text- and image-based retrieval of construction graphs. Broader representation-learning and multimodal applications are discussed in Appendix H. Published results for BRepCLIP [31], Duoduo CLIP [32], CLIP-GS [33], CADGCL [34], and BRT [35] are summarized in Appendix I.

## 3.2 Structural Priors and Equivalence Relations 

Equivalence transformations factored out by Φ. To ensure invariance, Φ must factor out extrinsic sim-ilarity transformations and intrinsic recording conventions (contour starts, traversal directions, and unit indices) [36]. Denoting this equivalence as B′ ∼ B , Φ must satisfy: 

B′ ∼ B =⇒ Φ(B′) = Φ(B), (3) while remaining sensitive to substantive geometric changes (Appendices A and E). 

Product structure of extruded B-reps. Exploiting the sketch-and-extrusion paradigm, engineering B-reps naturally admit a Cartesian product decomposition for each straight-extrusion unit: 

P = Ω × (0, h), (4) where Ω ⊂ R2 is the cross-section and h > 0 is the depth (Figure 3). This 2D-1D product structure decomposes the 3D invariance problem into tractable regional and boundary subproblems (Appendix A). Figure 3 separates the roles of the planar region, its boundary, and extrusion depth. We construct a descriptor for each extrusion unit from these components. # 4 Method 

## 4.1 Constructing Orthogonal Spectral Invariants 

Equation (4) identifies three geometric components: the planar region Ω, extrusion depth h, and boundary 

∂Ω. We derive regional and extrusion spectra first, then use boundary turning harmonics to capture local contour structure. The resulting features are assembled into the model-level descriptor Φ(Bi).

Regional Dirichlet spectrum. We first consider the planar cross section Ω (including internal holes) and extract shape features that do not change under 2D coordinate translation or rotation. The eigenvalues of the Laplace operator are invariant under isometries, meaning that the resonant frequencies of a domain depend only on its intrinsic geometry rather than its placement in the plane [37, 38]. Accordingly, we solve the Dirichlet Laplacian eigenvalue problem on Ω:

−∆uk = μkuk in Ω, uk = 0 on ∂Ω,0 < μ1 ≤ μ2 ≤ · · · . (5) The eigenvalue sequence {μk} is invariant to planar translation, rotation, and reflection [13]. Since scaling the domain uniformly by a factor s scales μk by s−2, multiplying μk by the cross-sectional area |Ω| removes the scale factor and gives a dimensionless 2D regional spectrum: 

bμk = μk|Ω|. (6) 

Extrusion spectrum. The 2D regional spectrum bμk describes only the base cross section Ω and cannot distinguish a thin washer from a long cylinder with the same base. Solving for 3D eigenvalues directly on 

P via tetrahedral meshing would introduce high computational cost and volumetric discretization error. Because P = Ω × (0, h) in Eq. (4) is a Cartesian product, the 3D Dirichlet Laplacian admits exact separation of variables (∆P = ∆Ω + ∂zz ). Thus, without 3D meshing, we can combine the 2D eigenvalues μm with the depth h algebraically to obtain the exact 3D eigenvalues of the extrusion unit:

## 4.2 Equivalence-Aware Cross-Modal Alignment 

Given the invariant descriptor di = Φ(Bi), we map geometry and text into a shared D-dimensional space using a dual-encoder architecture [17, 24, 43, 44]. Specifically, a residual MLP fB encodes di, while a text encoder fT encodes the text Ti:

zBi = Norm ( fB(di)) , zTi = Norm ( fT (Ti)) , (10) where cross-modal similarity is measured by the cosine inner product Sij = ⟨zTi , zBj ⟩.

# 6 Conclusion 

In this paper, we introduced a principled approach for text-to-CAD retrieval anchored in spectral geometry and group theory, effectively addressing the fundamental coordinate-leakage limitation inherent in prior coordinate-dependent paradigms. By formulating a canonical-invariant representation that strictly preserves similarity and construction-gauge symmetries, and further pairing it with spectral equivalence classes to tackle multi-positive contrastive ambiguity, our method achieves state-of-the-art retrieval performance (e.g., 11 × Text-to-B-rep Retrieval 

0.886 R@5 on Text2CAD). Crucially, these gains are realized using strictly less supervisory information than coordinate-based counterparts. Ultimately, our findings suggest a conceptual shift in 3D CAD understanding: moving away from brittle coordinate reconciliation toward robust, geometry-grounded invariance paves a more reliable path for cross-modal shape retrieval and generative engineering design.

# Appendix A From Construction Records to B-rep Geometry 

## A.1 Geometric Basis of the Sketch–Extrusion Representation 

A.1.1 Product Geometry and Boundary Decomposition 

The B-rep models considered in this work are constructed from planar sketches, straight extrusion operations, and their Boolean combinations. This class admits a particularly explicit geometric structure that separates the cross-sectional shape from its extension along the third dimension. Let Ω ⊂ R2 denote the planar region enclosed by the sketch contours, including possible inner contours corresponding to holes, and let h > 0 be the extrusion depth. In a local coordinate system aligned with the sketch plane, a straight extrusion preserves the same cross-section along the extrusion direction. Following the notation of the main text, its interior can therefore be written as 

P = Ω × (0, h). (11) For the closure of the solid, the boundary decomposes as

A.1.2 Geometric Roles of the Descriptor Components 

This provides the geometric motivation for the three components used in the descriptor. The region Ω

represents the global structure of the cross-section, ∂Ω exposes local contour information such as corners, arcs, holes, and turning patterns, and h controls the extension along the third dimension. The regional Dirichlet spectrum, boundary turning spectrum, and extrusion spectrum are constructed from these three complementary views. The representation’s discriminative capacity is evaluated through descriptor distinctness and self-retrieval in Appendix D. These empirical checks assess retrieval utility but do not establish geometric uniqueness [46].

## A.3 Equivariance of Reconstruction 

A.3.1 Similarity Transformations and Discretization 

Let

Fg·ct (g · B) = g · F ct (B). (18) Sketch embedding and straight extrusion satisfy this relation under the corresponding parameter trans-formations. ## C.1 Per-Unit Feature Composition 

C.1.1 Spectral Features 

For each extrusion unit, we retain 12 normalized Dirichlet eigenvalues, 12 normalized extrusion eigenvalues, and 16 boundary turning Fourier magnitudes, giving a 40-dimensional spectral representation.

## C.2 Model-Level Aggregation and Layout 

C.2.1 Geometric Ordering of Extrusion Units 

A complete B-rep may contain a variable number of extrusion units. Since their original construction indices are not intrinsic geometric quantities, the units are organized by normalized cross-sectional area, with isoperimetric ratio and aspect ratio used as secondary ordering keys. 

C.2.2 Global Aggregation and Descriptor Dimension 

The first four units retain their complete local descriptors. When the number of units m < 4, a fixed-dimensional layout requires a fixed padding convention inde-pendent of the input record. When m > 4, statistics of the remaining units must be insensitive to their order. 

C.3.2 Invariant Attributes and Deterministic Ordering 

Invariance of the complete model representation additionally requires the attributes, sorting keys, and global block to obey the same invariances. Topological counts are unchanged by similarities; length-, area-, and volume-dependent attributes require dimensionless forms, and aspect ratio requires a rotation-invariant definition. When sorting keys coincide, exact reindexing invariance requires either identical local descriptors or an index-independent tie-breaking rule. Appending the full invariant local descriptor as a final lexicographic key is one sufficient construction. Under these conditions, an admissible geometric transformation changes neither the local vectors, their ordering, nor the aggregate block, and unit reindexing changes only the input labels. Their concatenation is therefore unchanged. This argument separates the established spectral invariances from the conditions required by the complete descriptor assembly.

# F Training and Supervision Details 

## F.1 Optimization and Model Configuration 

The main retrieval model uses Qwen3-Embedding-4B with LoRA adaptation. We train for 30 epochs (18,210 steps) on 8 GPUs with a batch size of 128 per GPU (global batch size 1,024). We use OneCycleLR with a backbone learning rate of 10 −4 and a prediction-head learning rate of 5 × 10 −4, gradient checkpointing, and mixed bf16/fp32 precision. The model has 77.4M trainable parameters: 70.3M in text LoRA and 7.1M in the CAD tower. 

## F.2 Auxiliary Geometric Targets 

Specifically, we attach several geometric-attribute prediction heads to the shared textual features [48]. The five classification targets are rotational symmetry order, the first Betti number b1 (hole topology), circular-contour count, extrusion-unit count, and face count. The regression targets include slenderness ratio, isoperimetric ratio, fill ratio, aspect ratio, and depth dispersion. All these attributes are computed automatically from the B-rep geometry and require no additional manual annotation.

# H Extended Related Work 

Representation learning and multimodal applications. The broader representation-learning literature studies contrastive sentence and multimodal embeddings [49–52], incomplete multi-view graph clus-tering [53], spectral distribution matching for dataset distillation [54], and supervision under class im-balance [55, 56]. Complementary retrieval and evidence-gathering settings include open-domain video search [57, 58], audio-driven multimodal search [59], and search-grounded video misinformation detec-tion [60]. Multimodal applications extend to long-video understanding and video world models [61–63], video emotion recognition [64–66], video question answering and model collaboration [67, 68], and image editing [69–71]. At the system level, foundation-model research examines scientific and web agents [72–74], multi-agent workflows and academic presentation generation [75, 76], reasoning-model alignment and policy optimization [77–79], vision-language model routing [80], and social-bias evaluation of game agents [81].
