Efficient Multi-View Generation with Iterative Global-Local Proxy
Attention
Wei Tan, Kunming Luo, Hongyu Yan, Yu Zhang, Zihao Zhang, Xiatian Zhu, and Lei Xie
Abstract—Generating consistent multi-view RGB images and
normal maps from a single input image provides complementary
appearance and geometric cues for 3D reconstruction. How-
ever, maintaining cross-view consistency requires information ex-
change across viewpoints, and dense cross-view attention becomes
computationally expensive as the number of views and spatial
resolution increase. In this paper, we propose Global-Local Proxy
Attention (GLPA) to reduce cross-view communication costs
while preserving fine-grained within-view processing. Specifically,
GLPA retains full-resolution interactions within each view and
uses compact global proxies for cross-view communication. To
further leverage the exchanged information, GLPA incorporates
iterative proxy refinement, which rebuilds the proxies from
updated features and reuses the same attention parameters
for subsequent interactions within a single denoising step. We
evaluate GLPA on two structurally different diffusion backbones,
FLUX and Stable Diffusion. Experiments on FLUX show that
GLPA achieves a 2.65× speedup in attention computation per
denoising step over dense attention, while improving reconstruc-
tion IoU from 0.6849 to 0.7019 and reducing NVS LPIPS from
0.086 to 0.076. Results on Stable Diffusion further demonstrate
the effectiveness of GLPA, with a 1.80× attention speedup and
improvements across all five quality metrics.
Index Terms—single-image 3D reconstruction, diffusion mod-
els, efficient cross-view attention.
I. INTRODUCTION
I
MMERSIVE multimedia applications, including virtual and
augmented reality and interactive games, rely on 3D assets
that can be viewed from different perspectives [1]. Creating
such assets often requires labor-intensive manual modeling or
dedicated multi-view capture. Single-image 3D reconstruction
offers an alternative by inferring object geometry and appear-
ance from a single input image [2]–[4]. The reconstructed
assets should preserve fine geometric and appearance details
and maintain consistency across viewpoints [5]. Achieving
these properties efficiently remains an important challenge for
single-image 3D content generation.

generative priors to novel-view synthesis [6]. Building upon
this idea, multi-view diffusion models jointly generate images
from multiple viewpoints before reconstructing the underlying
3D object [7], [8]. Wonder3D further couples RGB images
with normal maps, providing complementary appearance and
geometric cues for downstream reconstruction [9]. These
approaches have established multi-view generation as an ef-
fective intermediate stage in single-image 3D reconstruction,
where reconstruction quality strongly depends on both the
fidelity of individual views and their consistency across view-

Maintaining such consistency typically requires explicit
information exchange among generated views. Dense cross-
view attention allows every generated token to interact with
tokens from other viewpoints, but its computational cost grows
quadratically with the total number of tokens. As spatial
resolution and the number of views increase, this all-to-all
interaction quickly becomes a major computational bottle-
neck. Recent methods therefore seek more efficient cross-
view interaction. Era3D replaces dense cross-view attention
with row-wise attention under a canonical orthographic camera
configuration, substantially reducing the interaction space [10].
However, this efficiency is obtained by imposing structured
correspondence and camera constraints on the generated
views, which limits the flexibility of cross-view interaction.
CTR3D removes this restriction by reducing redundant multi-
view tokens before cross-view attention and recovering the full
token set afterward, enabling dense and high-resolution multi-
view generation without imposing the same row-wise camera
constraint [11]. However, its cross-view interaction is per-
formed on the reduced representation, with the original token
resolution recovered only after attention. This reduction-and-
recovery formulation does not explicitly separate fine-grained
local processing from compact cross-view communication.
In contrast, these two interactions naturally serve different
roles: within-view processing preserves spatial details, whereas
cross-view communication mainly conveys information re-
quired for consistency across viewpoints.

Proxy Attention (GLPA), which preserves full-resolution
within-view interaction while compressing cross-view com-
munication through compact global proxies. As illustrated in
Fig. 1, this design enables consistent multi-view RGB and

reducing the cost of dense cross-view attention. Specifically,

information exchange across viewpoints, while the original
full-resolution features remain available for detailed within-
view interaction. In this way, cross-view communication is
reduced without compressing the feature representation used
for fine-grained local processing. A compact proxy representa-
tion constructed only once, however, may not fully reflect the
feature state after cross-view interaction. The initial proxies
are formed before information from other viewpoints has been
incorporated into the full-resolution features. After the first
interaction, these features contain newly exchanged multi-

the previous state. To further enhance this mechanism, GLPA
incorporates iterative proxy refinement, which reconstructs the
global proxies from the updated full-resolution features and
performs a subsequent interaction using the same attention
parameters. This allows the proxy representation to evolve
together with the feature state without introducing additional
network layers or diffusion sampling steps.
The overall multi-view generation pipeline incorporating
GLPA is illustrated in Fig. 2. GLPA combines global-local
proxy interaction with bounded iterative proxy refinement
within a unified attention design. We instantiate GLPA on
a transformer-based FLUX backbone and further adapt the
same mechanism to a UNet-based Stable Diffusion backbone
to demonstrate its applicability across structurally different
diffusion architectures.
Extensive experiments show that a single round of global-
local proxy interaction reduces attention runtime, whereas
iterative proxy refinement improves generation and recon-
struction quality at additional computational cost. On the
FLUX backbone, this single-round interaction reduces the
attention runtime per denoising step from 1.637 s to 0.390 s
compared with dense attention. With iterative refinement, the
full GLPA configuration requires 0.617 s per denoising step,
achieving a 2.65× attention speedup over dense attention. The
generated RGB images and normal maps further support high-
quality downstream 3D reconstruction on the GSO benchmark.
Results on the SD-based implementation additionally show
that the proposed design can be applied to structurally different
diffusion backbones.
Our contributions are summarized as follows: (i) we
propose GLPA, a unified attention mechanism combining
full-resolution within-view interactions, compact proxy-based
cross-view communication, and bounded iterative proxy re-
finement with shared attention parameters; (ii) we implement
GLPA on transformer-based FLUX and UNet-based Stable
Diffusion to examine its applicability across diffusion ar-
chitectures; and (iii) we conduct extensive experiments on
multi-view generation and 3D reconstruction, demonstrating
that GLPA achieves a favorable balance among generation
and reconstruction quality, attention runtime, and memory
consumption.
II. RELATED WORK
Recent single-image 3D generation methods can be broadly
divided into approaches that directly predict or generate
3D representations and methods that first synthesize consis-
tent multi-view images for subsequent reconstruction. Re-
cent works such as One-2-3-45++ [12], LGM [13], and
CraftsMan3D [14] have explored different combinations of
multi-view generation, feed-forward reconstruction, and 3D-
native generative models. Our work follows the multi-view
generation paradigm and focuses on improving the efficiency
and consistency of cross-view interaction in diffusion-based
multi-view synthesis. In this section, we first review recent

A. Multi-view diffusion
Building on the generative priors of pretrained 2D diffusion
models, novel-view synthesis has expanded from camera-
conditioned single-view prediction to joint multi-view gen-
eration [6], [7], [15]–[17]. Cross-view feature interaction is
widely adopted during joint denoising to maintain structural
and appearance consistency across generated views. Spatial
geometry also provides useful cues for cross-view communi-
cation. Existing methods employ pixel correspondences and
epipolar constraints to guide feature interaction [18], [19],
or associate information across viewpoints through 3D-aware
feature aggregation and depth-based reprojection [8], [20],
[21]. Geometric correspondences have also been used to
supervise attention maps for improved spatial consistency [22].
For downstream 3D reconstruction, generated multi-view ob-
servations need to capture both appearance and geometry
consistently. Recent approaches combine RGB synthesis with
surface normal or depth prediction to provide complementary
geometric cues for reconstruction [9], [23]. Video diffusion
priors have also been employed to synthesize object views
along camera trajectories [24]–[26]. As the number of views
and spatial resolution increase, cross-view communication
becomes computationally expensive, motivating more efficient
approaches to high-resolution and dense multi-view genera-
tion [10], [11].
B. Efficient cross-view interaction
Reducing redundant token computations is a common strat-
egy for improving attention efficiency in Transformers [27].
Token merging shortens the sequence involved in attention
by combining similar features [28], and has been applied to
Stable Diffusion-based image generation and zero-shot video
editing [29], [30]. Compressing key and value representa-
tions while retaining the original queries can also reduce
attention costs [31], [32], while EDiT combines spatial key-
value compression with linear attention for efficient diffu-
sion generation [33]. Attention can also operate at different
spatial granularities, combining fine-grained local interactions
with coarser long-range information aggregation [34], [35]. In
multi-view diffusion, adjustments to the interaction range and
feature representations are further reflected in the design of
cross-view communication. Era3D employs a canonical ortho-
graphic camera configuration to restrict cross-view attention
to corresponding image rows, whereas CTR3D uses token
reduction and recovery to perform cross-view interactions
on compressed multi-view representations [10], [11]. Global
information exchange can also be mediated by a small set
of intermediate tokens, reducing dense interactions among the
original features. Agent Attention and DAM employ interme-
diate representations to reduce the interaction scale between
queries and keys/values, with DAM introducing dynamic at-
tention mediators into diffusion Transformers [36], [37]. PT-
DiT constructs proxies from features aggregated over spatial
or spatiotemporal windows, establishes global dependencies
through attention among proxies, and employs window and
shifted-window attention to process local features. This de-
sign has been applied to text-conditioned image, video, and
multi-view generation [38]. For single-image-conditioned joint
multi-view RGB/normal generation, a key question is how to
reduce cross-view communication costs while preserving fine-
grained within-view interactions. GLPA addresses this through
global-local proxy interaction, which retains full-resolution
within-view attention and uses compact global proxies for
cross-view communication.

C. Looped Transformers
Shared-parameter computation allows Transformer modules
to be applied repeatedly to updated features without requiring a
complete set of layer parameters for each iteration [39]. Depth-
specific low-rank adaptation and loop-level hyper-connections
provide additional flexibility in feature transformation and
state propagation when Transformer modules are reused [40],
[41]. Hyper-SET develops a recurrent-depth Transformer
based on hyperspherical energy minimization, where feature
states are updated through iterative optimization [42]. Per-
ceiver repeatedly reads the input to update compact latent
representations, allowing subsequent attention operations to
build on previously aggregated information [43]. For visual
generation, ELT reuses Transformer modules across iterations
for image and video synthesis, using intra-loop self-distillation
to supervise intermediate predictions [44]. For proxy-based
communication in multi-view diffusion, a relevant question is
how features updated by an initial interaction can contribute to
subsequent cross-view information exchange. Within a single
diffusion denoising step, GLPA updates the full-resolution
working state through residual feedback with a bounded coef-
ficient, reconstructs global proxies from the updated features,
and performs subsequent interactions with the same attention
layer, sharing its parameters across interaction rounds.
III. METHOD
We integrate GLPA into a multi-view diffusion model for
high-quality multi-view generation from a single input image,
as illustrated in Fig. 2. Given an input image, the diffusion
model generates multi-view RGB images and normal maps,
which are then used to reconstruct a textured 3D mesh with
an existing method such as NeuS [45]. GLPA reduces dense
cross-view computation by preserving full-resolution within-
view interaction and compressing cross-view communication
through global proxies.
Specifically, GLPA consists of two main components:
global-local proxy interaction (Sec. III-B) and iterative proxy
refinement (Sec. III-C), as outlined in Alg. 1. During a global-
local proxy interaction, each view preserves full-resolution
local attention, while pooled proxy tokens summarize infor-
mation from different views and enable efficient cross-view
communication. Based on the updated features, the global
proxies are further refined in the subsequent interaction round,
allowing cross-view information to be progressively updated
and propagated.

B. Global-local proxy interaction
Our design assigns different information roles to cross-
view and within-view interaction. Cross-view communication
exchanges information shared across viewpoints, while within-
view interaction processes spatially detailed features such as
silhouettes, normal discontinuities, and textures. We therefore
retain full-resolution within-view processing and use compact
spatial summaries for cross-view communication.
1) Local interactions: Generated tokens are organized into
local groups by viewpoint, each group containing the RGB
and normal tokens of a single view. Tokens within a group
interact at their original resolution, and no direct full-resolution
connection is formed across views. The proxy pathway does
not spatially compress or replace the full-resolution within-
view token path.

C. Iterative proxy refinement
In the single-round interaction described above, global prox-
ies are constructed from the input features of the current layer.
After the first interaction, the updated tokens already contain
information propagated from other views. We therefore rebuild
the global proxies from these updated features and perform a
second interaction, allowing the cross-view representation to
be further refined based on the newly exchanged information.

The two rounds share the same Q/K/V projections, attention-

cable, without introducing round-specific attention weights.
Gradients are propagated through the feedback path, and each
refined attention layer introduces only one learnable scalar α,
while the fixed scale γ is shared as a hyperparameter. The
pooling operation itself remains parameter-free. Refinement is
applied only to attention layers that use global-local proxy
interaction. The entire refinement process is performed within
a single denoising step by reusing the same attention layer,
without adding extra sampling steps or network layers. Inter-
mediate outputs provide feedback to the subsequent round, and
the output of the final executed round is used as the attention
result.
D. Instantiation on a UNet-Based Backbone
On the SD UNet backbone, GLPA retains the same proxy
construction and iterative refinement as the FLUX implemen-
tation. Local attention groups follow the UNet’s factorized
view and domain structure, with each group containing tokens
from a single view and domain. Global proxies aggregate
information across views and domains and incorporate the
corresponding view and domain embeddings. The SD attention
projections and readout parameters are shared across refine-
ment rounds.

B. Metrics
Our experiments cover novel view synthesis (NVS) and 3D
reconstruction. NVS quality is measured with Learned Per-
ceptual Image Patch Similarity (LPIPS) [52], which captures
perceptual similarity between synthesized and reference views
while tolerating slight spatial misalignment. For 3D recon-
struction, Chamfer Distance (CD) and Volume Intersection
over Union (IoU) are employed to measure the geometric
agreement between reconstructed meshes and ground-truth
shapes.
C. Implementation details
We implement GLPA on two diffusion backbones, SD2.1-
UnCLIP [53] and FLUX.1-dev. For the SD2.1 configuration,
we generate 12 RGB and normal views. The model is trained
on 8 A100 GPUs for 40,000 training steps with a total batch
size of 128. We initialize the learning rate at 1 × 10−4 and
decrease it to 5 × 10−5 after 5,000 training steps. During
inference, we use DDIM [54] with 40 denoising steps and a
guidance scale of 3.0. For the FLUX.1-dev configuration, we
generate 8 RGB and normal views. We train the model on 8
A100 GPUs for 10,000 steps with a total batch size of 32. The

E. Ablation Study
1) Iterative Refinement: To evaluate global-local proxy
interaction and iterative proxy refinement on both FLUX and
SD backbones, we compare dense attention, GLPA without
refinement, GLPA with the first-round proxies reused in the
second round, and the full model. As shown in Table II, GLPA
without refinement substantially reduces attention runtime
but yields lower generation and reconstruction quality than
dense attention on the FLUX backbone. Iterative refinement
improves performance on both tasks, enabling the full model to
achieve better reconstruction accuracy and competitive novel
view synthesis quality relative to dense attention. Compared
with the proxy-reuse variant, the full model reduces CD from
0.0254 to 0.0185 and increases IoU from 0.6246 to 0.7019,
while also improving all three novel view synthesis metrics.
These results support the effectiveness of updating proxies
during refinement. With the additional computation from re-
finement, GLPA retains a 2.65× speedup in attention compu-
tation per denoising step over dense attention on the FLUX
backbone. On the SD backbone, GLPA without refinement
improves reconstruction metrics but yields lower NVS quality
than dense attention. The full model improves all five quality
metrics over dense attention and both ablated variants, while
retaining a 1.80× speedup. These results support combining
compact cross-view communication with recurrent updates for
high-quality multi-view generation and reconstruction at lower
computational cost.

V. CONCLUSIONS
In this paper, we have presented GLPA, an efficient attention
mechanism for multi-view generation in single-image 3D
reconstruction. GLPA preserves full-resolution within-view in-
teractions while enabling efficient cross-view communication
through compact global proxies. GLPA further incorporates
iterative proxy refinement to improve cross-view consistency
among generated views. This single-round global-local proxy
interaction reduces attention runtime compared with dense
attention. With iterative proxy refinement, the full GLPA con-
figuration improves generation and reconstruction quality over
the single-round variant. Extensive experiments on novel view
synthesis and 3D reconstruction demonstrate the effectiveness
of our method, producing consistent multi-view observations
and complete 3D models.
