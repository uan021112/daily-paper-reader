Abstract
Abstracting structured buildings from limited observations
is essential for 3D urban modeling and digital twins. Partial
point clouds and overhead images of the same building pro-
vide incomplete 3D surface measurements and visual cues
to its footprint, roof geometry, and rooftop structures, re-
spectively. This motivates a uniﬁed framework that aligns
geometric and visual cues in 3D and reconstructs buildings
from either or both modalities. Building on this connec-
tion, we present Any2Building, a uniﬁed model for Level of
Detail 2 (LoD2) building reconstruction from partial point
clouds, overhead images, or both, with optional coarse geo-
metric priors. At its core is an occupancy-grounded shared

1. Introduction
Structured 3D building models support urban digital
twins [2] and applications such as urban understanding [43]
and visual localization [44]. Level of Detail 2 (LoD2) mod-
eling [17] captures a building’s spatial organization in a
compact, structured representation that preserves the ge-
ometry and spatial relationships of its roof sections and
detailed rooftop structures.
Building reconstruction of-
ten relies on limited observations, including partial point
clouds [13, 15, 16, 22] and overhead images [18, 30]. Point
clouds provide partial geometric measurements, while over-
head images reveal appearance and layout but leave height
and hidden surfaces ambiguous.
Although these hetero-
geneous observations can originate from the same build-
ing, point cloud based building reconstruction and image-
based building generation are still largely treated as sepa-
rate tasks. This common origin motivates a uniﬁed recon-
struction framework that recovers LoD2 building from ei-
ther modality independently or their combination.
Recent building reconstruction methods have made

BuildAny-
Point [13] reconstructs structured buildings from partial
point clouds, but its voxel-aligned conditioning does not
readily incorporate image evidence as a spatially grounded
3D condition. In prior-assisted image-based building gener-
ation [18], extending noise-based prior injection to partial
point clouds can obscure ﬁne structural cues. Meanwhile,
general-purpose 3D generators offer useful inspiration
through their efforts to unify heterogeneous conditions
within a shared framework [6, 11, 28, 42]. Axolotl3D [11]
and Hunyuan3D-Omni [28] concatenate visual and geomet-
ric tokens for attention-based conditioning, but rely on the
generator to resolve cross-modal structural relationships
while inferring missing geometry—a challenging joint
inference problem under incomplete observations.
This direct coupling is particularly challenging in build-
ing reconstruction, where low-resolution orthographic im-
agery provides limited height and facade cues, yet reveals
roof layouts and footprint boundaries essential for LoD2 re-
construction. However, direct feature fusion does not read-
ily align these visual cues with the ﬁne-grained geometric
evidence in partial point clouds, as illustrated by the direct-
fusion example in Fig. 1(b). The remaining challenge is to
establish consistent geometric correspondence between het-
erogeneous observations and complete building structure,
enabling modality alignment and fusion as well as task uni-
ﬁcation to promote positive transfer across reconstruction
tasks.
To address this challenge, we introduce Any2Building,
a uniﬁed LoD2 building reconstruction model that supports
point cloud input and remote sensing image inputs, together
with height or box priors. Our main technical innovation
is a set of shared queries initialized on a low-resolution 3D
voxel grid. The available inputs are encoded as conditioning
tokens, and shared spatial queries aggregate their features at
common 3D locations. Supervision with complete building
surface voxels encourages the queries to capture the over-
all building structure, including unobserved regions, before
they condition the generator. By relating different obser-
vations to the same underlying geometry, the queries fur-
ther guide the generator, enabling shared structural learning
across point-cloud completion and image-based generation,
together with optional coarse geometric priors. The gener-
ated structure latent is decoded into a voxel grid. We ﬁnd
that these voxels alone sufﬁce for continuous mesh recon-
struction by a trained surface generator, without additional
point or image conditioning. Low-poly remeshing and tex-
turing then produce compact Lod2 building assets.
To support learning across observation settings, we
introduce BuildingWorld-Image, an image extension of
BuildingWorld [17]. It comprises approximately one mil-
lion aligned triplets of partial point clouds, overhead im-
ages, and LoD2 building models across 18 cities. These
observations share the same geometric targets, enabling
structural learning and evaluation across input modalities.
Extensive experiments on multiple public datasets and
BuildingWorld-Image dataset demonstrate that our method
not only achieves superior performance on individual tasks
but also beneﬁts from complementary modalities when ob-
servations from point cloud modality are limited.
Our contributions are summarized as follows.
• We propose Any2Building, a uniﬁed model for LoD2
building reconstruction from partial point clouds, over-
head images, or both, with optional coarse geometric
priors.
The model combines complementary observa-
tions and promotes positive transfer across reconstruction
tasks.
• We design a spatial conditioning module based on shared
3D queries.
Supervised by complete building vox-
els, these queries align heterogeneous observations with
building geometry and provide spatial guidance for gen-
rator.
• We introduce BuildingWorld-Image, a dataset contain-
ing approximately one million aligned triplets of partial
point clouds, real overhead images, and LoD2 building
models across 18 cities, supporting training and evalua-
tion across observation settings.

2. Related Work
Point cloud completion for building reconstruction.
General point cloud completion methods recover missing
geometry [9] through global feature encoding [37, 40], hier-
archical decoding [27, 33], voxelized representations [34],
and geometry-aware transformers [3, 38, 39].
Multi-
modal methods, including EGIInet [36], DuInNet [21],
and MAENet [20], further incorporate paired images [10].
These methods output point sets, requiring subsequent pro-
cessing for structured mesh reconstruction.
To recover coherent architectural structure, PolyFit [23]
and City3D [15] assemble planar primitives into compact
meshes through constrained face selection. ArcPro [16] pre-
dicts architectural programs encoding building layers and
their hierarchy, while Point2Building [22] autoregressively
generates mesh vertices and faces from point clouds. For
existing meshes, RoLoPM [4] combines offset surface ex-
traction, simpliﬁcation, and geometric ﬂow to produce low-
poly models while preserving visual ﬁdelity.
BuildAny-
Point [13] combines point-conditioned diffusion with au-
toregressive mesh generation, while UniCo [5] uses learn-
able primitive proxies to jointly predict point geometry and
assembly-ready primitives non-autoregressively. Both rely
on observed 3D geometry through voxelized point condi-
tioning and shared point cloud features. Incorporating im-
ages therefore requires an additional mechanism to spatially
ground 2D appearance.
Image-based building generation.
Recent image-to-3D
models, including TRELLIS, TRELLIS.2, and SAM 3D [1,
31, 32], provide strong generative priors for recovering ge-
ometry from visual observations.
At the building level,
BuilDiff [30] generates point clouds through single-image-
conditioned diffusion, while SatSkylines [18] introduces ge-
ometric control by interpolating coarse-prior latents with
Gaussian noise. However, extending this noise-space injec-
tion to partial point clouds can obscure ﬁne geometric cues,
while coupling geometric guidance with noise strength lim-
its independent control over local structure. Related urban
generation methods synthesize larger scenes through com-
positional modeling [19, 35] and satellite-conditioned gen-
eration [12, 14, 24, 29]. Overhead appearance nevertheless
leaves height and hidden surfaces ambiguous, while coarse
priors do not specify detailed architectural geometry.
Geometrically conditioned 3D generation.
Geometric
conditioning enables more explicit control over generated
shapes. SDFusion [6] supports generation from partial ge-
ometry and images, while CLAY [42] accommodates di-
verse controls, including point clouds, voxels, and bound-
ing boxes. SpaceControl [7] introduces spatial constraints
at test time without additional training.
Hunyuan3D-
Omni [28] and Axolotl3D [11] concatenate visual and ge-
ometric tokens for attention-based conditioning, leaving the
generator to infer how these heterogeneous cues jointly con-
strain missing geometry. This calls for a shared represen-
tation that spatially organizes complementary observations
and relates them to complete architectural structure across
input settings.
3. Method
Any2Building generates LoD2 buildings from a partial
point cloud P, an overhead image I, or both, with an op-
tional coarse geometric prior R. Its central component is a
set of shared spatial queries that aggregate observations on
a low-resolution 3D grid. Supervised by complete build-
ing occupancy, these queries form an intermediate repre-
sentation of overall structure before conditioning the gen-
erator. As illustrated in Fig. 2, the query features guide
a ﬂow-matching DiT through query-enhanced (QE) tokens
and spatial prompts. The generated structure is decoded
into a 643 surface-voxel grid and converted into a compact
LoD2 mesh. All geometric inputs, spatial queries, and out-
put voxel grids share a normalized building coordinate sys-
tem linked to the georeferenced overhead imagery. This al-
lows generated buildings to be aligned with the source im-
agery and mapped back to their real-world locations, even
in the image-only setting.

Evaluation settings and metrics.
Within each input set-
ting, methods share the evaluation frame and input prepro-
cessing. Building-PCC follows the metric deﬁnitions and
reporting scale of BuildAnyPoint [13]. On BuildingWorld-
Image, we report CDL1, CDL2, surface F-score at a normal-
ized threshold of 0.02, Earth Mover’s Distance (EMD), uni-
formity (Uni.), and density-aware Chamfer distance (DCD).
Both CD variants and EMD are scaled by 103. The main ex-
perimental tables report F-scores as percentages. PSNR and
SSIM evaluate appearance on the textured-reference subset.
Voxel-support studies use voxel F1. Detailed metric deﬁni-
tions and alignment procedures are provided in the Appen-
dices B.
Training and inference.
Our model is trained for 200k it-
erations on eight NVIDIA A800 GPUs. Inference uses the
same GPU type, with 12 ﬂow-sampling steps and a guid-
ance scale of 3.0. Learned 3D generation baselines are ﬁne-
tuned on the corresponding training split for 200k optimizer
updates per independently trained generative stage.

5. Conclusion
We presented Any2Building, a uniﬁed model for LoD2
building reconstruction from partial point clouds, overhead
images, or both.
The model also supports image-based
reconstruction with coarse geometric priors.
Supervised
by complete-building surface occupancy, our shared spa-
tial queries aggregate input features on a common 3D grid
and condition the same structure generator across input set-
tings. We generate surfaces from the predicted voxels alone
and remesh them into compact LoD2 models. We also in-
troduced BuildingWorld-Image, comprising approximately
one million aligned triplets of partial point clouds, overhead
images, and LoD2 building meshes across 18 cities. Ex-
periments show improved reconstruction accuracy across
unimodal and multimodal point-cloud completion, single-
image generation, and prior-assisted generation. Ablations
show that joint training improves reconstruction from either
modality alone, and that adding imagery improves comple-
tion from sparse or spatially incomplete point clouds.

A. BuildingWorld-Image:
Construction and
Evaluation
BuildingWorld-Image extends an existing geometric collec-
tion with geographically grounded visual observations. Its
purpose is to connect building geometry, partial point mea-
surements, and real overhead imagery within a common
instance-level representation. This section details the data
composition, construction and reﬁnement procedures, parti-
tioning, image-based evaluation protocols, and representa-
tive cases, including source textured meshes and instance-
level correspondences (Fig. S3).
A.1. Composition and Geographic Coverage
Table S1 reports the tabulated BuildingWorld data pool be-
fore train–test partitioning. It contains 1,583,024 unique
city-qualiﬁed geometric targets, including 1,011,196 targets
paired with real overhead imagery after quality ﬁltering.
This tabulated image subset spans 18 cities on four conti-
nents and includes residential, industrial, and dense urban
buildings. Perth contributes 3,843 image–mesh pairs.
The image count is a subset of the geometry count, so the
two columns are not additive. Counts refer to source build-
ing targets rather than augmented image views, point-cloud
realizations, or prior variants. Some source targets contain
grouped structures. The paired subset connects visual ob-
servations to geometry; the broader geometry pool provides
additional architectural examples.

Retrieving overhead evidence.
A building’s geographic
extent deﬁnes a context window in an orthophoto or remote-
sensing mosaic. We retain the image-to-world transforma-
tion and available source metadata. Municipal orthopho-
tos and mixed aerial/satellite basemaps are both used; con-
sequently, we describe the collection as overhead remote-
sensing imagery rather than assuming a uniform satellite
sensor. The context preserves neighboring structures, while
an instance mask focuses attention on the target.
Instance masks and paired conditions.
We project the
source building geometry into the geographic window to
initialize an instance mask. RGB context, mask, and cutout
are stored as related observations of the same target. Partial
point clouds use available measurements or source-derived
simulations according to their source pool.
Coarse geo-
metric conditions include bounding volumes and footprint-
derived prisms. A prior that uses target-derived height is
distinguished from one derived entirely from external obser-
vations; it is not treated as free information in image-only
reconstruction. The projected instance mask is not supplied
as an additional condition in the single-image setting.
13


For quantitative evaluation, each target building is local-
ized using a 2D bounding box obtained by projecting its ref-
erence geometry into the georeferenced image. The same
bounding-box crop is used for all compared methods. In
the single-image setting, background pixels inside the crop
are retained. The projected instance mask and its bound-
ing box are distinct annotations: storing a mask does not
imply that background removal is applied in every input set-
ting. Our image-based evaluation therefore assumes ground-
truth-derived instance localization and measures reconstruc-
tion given an instance crop, rather than end-to-end build-
ing detection and reconstruction. Although this is sufﬁcient
for single-building generation, we are also exploring, at this
stage, the use of agents to automate the entire city-scale gen-
eration pipeline.
Geometric preparation.
We remove invalid or empty
geometric assets, standardize orientation, and encode the
valid surfaces into sparse-structure and shape representa-
tions. Base-plane handling follows the source’s conventions
and the open-bottom training target; it is not a general water-
tightness repair. Geometry is normalized consistently with
its associated point observations.
A.3. Quality Screening and Reﬁnement
Native image information.
Quality screening checks na-
tive resolution, foreground coverage, exposure, texture, and
orientation. Resolution tiers use the source image before
resampling; enlarging a crop does not add evidence or im-
prove its tier. Context and cutout variants share the same
image window, padding, and display scale, with only the
instance mask differing.
Coordinate, unit, and scale calibration.
Systematic
scale disagreement is ﬁrst diagnosed in the coordinate con-
version, rather than absorbed by a per-building image ﬁt. In-
correct map projection, confusion between meters and feet,
or a stale pixel-to-world scale after crop resizing can af-
fect every target from a source. Our Boston repair restores
the local offset and the source survey-foot convention. The
inspected Cambridge export additionally requires a source-
speciﬁc scale factor; it is not a universal correction for Cam-
bridge data. These corrections belong to the shared source-
to-world transform and are checked against geographic con-
text before local alignment.
Different boundaries and height parallax.
A visible
roof boundary, an eave outline, and a ground footprint need
not coincide. Projecting a source mesh into an image can
also disagree with visible edges because an oblique acquisi-
tion displaces elevated surfaces relative to the ground, with
different roof levels shifting differently. Occlusion and dif-
ferences in acquisition date introduce further discrepancies.
A single isotropic scale cannot generally resolve these ef-
fects. We therefore distinguish coordinate calibration from
correspondence reﬁnement, retain the meaning of each con-
tour, and ﬂag ambiguous roof–footprint matches instead of
treating every residual as a scale error.
Shape-preserving
boundary
reﬁnement.
After
geo-
graphic calibration, local reﬁnement addresses residual con-
tour displacement where the projected and visible bound-
aries are compatible. Our reﬁnement proposal searches a
bounded translation and isotropic scale around the regis-
tered contour. For contour point p and its center c, the can-
didate transform is T(p) = s(p −c) + c + t. The objec-
tive combines orientation-compatible image-edge distance
with penalties on translation t and scale s, preserving the
source polygon rather than reclassifying foreground pixels.
The transformation preserves polygon components, vertex
ordering, and corners, avoiding the erosion of roof structure
that unconstrained image segmentation can introduce. We
retain a proposal only when it improves the edge objective
and remains inside the image; otherwise the registered prior
is kept. Successive reﬁnement passes record their cumula-
tive scale, so a bound relative to an intermediate contour is
not confused with a bound relative to the source geometry.
Figure S2 shows examples.
Reﬁnement preserves vector-contour structure and re-
tains the registered prior when a proposed update fails the
acceptance criteria. No independent pixel annotations are
available for this reﬁnement, so the examples do not estab-
lish a mask-IoU improvement. Difﬁcult cases involving oc-
clusion, multiple roof levels, building groups, or an incor-
rect source contour remain subject to review.
A.4. Splits and Representative Cases
The evaluation split contains 2,000 unique building targets
in two groups. The unseen-city group contains 250 build-
ings from each of Greater Geelong, Philadelphia, Perth, and
Toronto, for 1,000 targets. These four cities are excluded
from training across images, point clouds, geometry, and
derived observations.
The unseen-region group contains 100 buildings from
each of Calgary, Wellington, Tokyo, Montreal, Hong Kong,
Cape Town, Edmonton, Mississauga, New York, and Mel-
bourne, for another 1,000 targets. Contiguous geographic
regions are reserved before selecting the test buildings. All
buildings in these regions are excluded from training, not
only the selected test targets. Spatial buffers cover at least
the image-crop extent to prevent overlap between training
and test imagery.
The four held-out cities account for 10,753 image–mesh
pairs in Table S1, leaving 1,000,443 pairs before region and
buffer exclusions. The resulting training set contains ap-
proximately one million image–mesh pairs. Training uses

A.5. Image-Based Evaluation and Rendering
The main-text image-generation table reports two input set-
tings with different image preprocessing protocols.
The
single-image comparison uses real remote-sensing instance
crops with the background retained. It evaluates building-
ﬁne-tuned TRELLIS, TRELLIS.2, SAM 3D Objects, and
Any2Building using the same input image and crop. The
prior-assisted setting instead uses background-removed in-
stance crops from orthophotos, reﬂecting OSM-assisted re-
construction scenarios with available building footprints.
Training in this setting uses the corresponding background-
removed images. The comparison evaluates SpaceControl,
Hunyuan3D-Omni, SatSkylines, and Any2Building using
the same image crop and coarse geometric prior. Within
each setting, all methods share the input preprocessing, ref-
erence geometry, and evaluation frame.
Texture generation.
All compared methods follow a
reference-image-assisted texturing workﬂow. For each pre-
dicted untextured mesh, appearance-reference images are
synthesized and used to condition the corresponding texture-
generation module. Each baseline retains its own texture
generator. The synthesized appearance references used for
conditioning are distinct from the ground-truth renderings
used for evaluation. Accordingly, appearance scores eval-
uate the complete geometry-and-texturing pipelines, rather
than isolating the contribution of the geometry generator.
Appearance evaluation.
PSNR and SSIM are evaluated
on the subset with textured reference geometry. Following
the four-view camera conﬁguration of SatSkylines, we ren-
der each predicted textured mesh and its reference from yaw
angles {0◦, 90◦, 180◦, 270◦}, with a ﬁxed pitch of 30◦, ﬁeld
of view of 40◦, and camera radius of 2 in the normalized
rendering frame. Predictions and references use identical
camera poses, rendering resolution, background, and light-
ing.
We compute PSNR and SSIM between corresponding
predicted and reference renderings, average the four view
scores for each building, and then average across build-
ings. RGB values are normalized to [0, 1]. These metrics
compare rendered textured meshes rather than renderings
against the input remote-sensing image, and therefore re-
ﬂect both geometric and appearance differences.

B. Implementation, Evaluation, and Recon-
struction
B.1. Architecture, Training, and Inference
The point encoder uses four EdgeConv stages with 16-
neighbor aggregation and farthest-point reductions to 2,048
and 1,024 centers.
It returns 1,024 spatially indexed to-
kens of width 1,024.
The frozen DINOv3 encoder pro-
cesses images at 512 × 512. The spatial-query lattice con-
tains 163 locations.
Query features are projected into a
30-block diffusion transformer of hidden width 1,536. Spa-
tial prompts enter blocks 0, 10, 20, and 29 (zero-based in-
dices), while query-enhanced condition tokens supply cross-
attention throughout the transformer.
The shared training conﬁguration samples point-only (P),
image-only (I), point–image (P+I), and image-plus-prior
(I+R) modes with equal probability. Point degradation pre-
cedes resampling to the encoder input size; repeated in-
put rows do not constitute additional observations.
The
query occupancy loss has weight 0.1.
Voxel-level geo-
metric supervision uses the frozen sparse-structure decoder
with a BCE/Dice auxiliary loss weighted by 0.02. Query-
enhanced tokens and spatial prompts are dropped together
with probability 0.1 for classiﬁer-free guidance.
At inference, the surface generator operates at the pre-
dicted occupied voxel coordinates, without additional im-
age or point-cloud features. It is therefore conditioned on
the predicted structure, even though direct observation con-
ditioning is removed.
Baseline adaptation.
We initialize the learned 3D gener-
ation baselines from their released pretrained checkpoints
and ﬁne-tune them on the training split corresponding to
each experiment.
Each independently trained generative
stage receives 200,000 optimizer updates, using the task-
speciﬁc input preprocessing described in Appendix A.5.
For Hunyuan3D-Omni, we ﬁne-tune the shape diffusion
model and uniﬁed control encoder jointly as a single shape-
generation stage.
The update budget refers to optimizer
steps rather than inference sampling steps.
Baseline availability.
BuilDiff and Axolotl3D are dis-
cussed as related work but are excluded from the quantita-
tive comparisons because we could not obtain runnable ofﬁ-
cial implementations as of November 15, 2026. BuilDiff’s
ofﬁcial repository contained only a README and license,
while the Axolotl3D project page did not provide a public
implementation or checkpoint download.

B.3. Ablation Protocols
Evaluation scope.
All three studies in Table 4 use the
full 2,000-building BuildingWorld-Image test set. Geomet-
ric metrics are evaluated on the ﬁnal low-poly meshes pro-
duced by the reconstruction procedure speciﬁed for each
study, using the shared evaluation frame and metric deﬁni-
tions in Appendix B.2. F-score uses a normalized distance
threshold of 0.02, and CDL1 is reported with a scale factor
of 103. Component variants are compared within each input
setting in panel (a); the paired contribution of imagery is
evaluated separately in panel (b). In the surface-generation
study, both the structure-only and surface-stage variants are
converted to low-poly meshes using the indicated backend
before evaluation.
Spatial-query variants.
The condition concat baseline
replaces the spatial-query conditioning design with direct
concatenation of the available input tokens. Point and image
tokens are included according to the input setting, and en-
coded prior tokens are also concatenated in the I+R setting.
The resulting sequence directly conditions the DiT, without
query read-back or spatial-prompt injection. This baseline
therefore compares the complete spatial-query conditioning
design with direct token conditioning while retaining access
to the available input modalities and priors.
The remaining variants remove one component at a time.
The w/o occupancy supervision variant omits the auxiliary
query occupancy loss while retaining the query representa-
tion and the remaining generation objectives. The w/o query
read-back variant uses the base condition tokens H directly,
i.e., HQE = H, while retaining query occupancy supervi-
sion and spatial prompts. The w/o spatial prompts variant
removes the aligned residual injection into the DiT while
retaining query-enhanced condition tokens and occupancy
supervision. Each variant is retrained using the same train-
ing protocol; the components are not simply disabled in the
trained full model at inference.
Joint and single-task training.
We train one specialist
for each of P, I, P+I, and I+R, using the corresponding ar-
chitecture and inference inputs. Each specialist and the joint
model receive the same number of optimizer updates. The
joint model samples the four input modes with equal proba-
bility, whereas each specialist uses only its designated mode.
The comparison therefore matches the total update count
per model rather than the number of training exposures to
each mode. At evaluation, the joint model and the corre-
sponding specialist receive the same type of input; joint
training does not introduce extra inference modalities into
the P-only or I-only comparisons.
Paired point degradation.
The image-contribution study
uses the same trained checkpoint for P and P+I inference.
The None setting uses the original 2,048-point partial ob-
servation without additional degradation; it does not denote
a complete building point cloud. Random degradation re-
tains 205 or 41 points from this observation. Spatial degra-
dation adapts the ShapeNet-55 cropping scheme used by
AdaPoinTr [39]. A randomly selected spatial region is ﬁrst
removed, after which the remaining observations are sub-
sampled to 41 points. Thus, spatial removal precedes sub-
sampling, and the 41-point input is not obtained by directly
cropping away all but 41 points. Comparing this setting
with random subsampling to the same count examines the
role of spatial coverage under equally sparse observations.
For every comparison, P and P+I use identical retained
points, normalization, and generation noise; P+I addition-
ally receives the paired overhead image.
Degradation is
applied before resampling to the encoder input size, so re-
peated samples do not add geometric observations. The re-
ported point counts refer to the retained observations rather
than the length of the resampled encoder input.
Surface-stage variants and their point-conditioning com-
parison are described in Appendix B.5; conversion and tim-
ing settings are speciﬁed in Appendix B.4.

C. Additional Ablations and Analysis
This section examines point-cloud encoding, query resolu-
tion, and geometric priors, and provides qualitative compo-
nent comparisons and textured visualizations. The encoder
ablation uses Building-PCC; the query-resolution experi-
ment uses the full BuildingWorld-Image test set. The paired
point-degradation protocol is described in Appendix B.3.
C.1. Point-Cloud Encoder Ablation
We compare different point-cloud conditioning encoders on
Building-PCC while keeping the spatial-query module and
generation pipeline ﬁxed. Speciﬁcally, we replace Edge-
Conv with a pointwise MLP, a Point Transformer, or the
TRELLIS.2 VAE encoder [32]. The point-based variants
use the same sampled centers, whereas the VAE encoder
extracts conditioning features from voxelized partial obser-
vations. All encoder outputs are adapted to the same token
count and feature width. We additionally remove the ad-
ditive positional embedding from the EdgeConv tokens to
isolate its contribution while retaining local neighborhood
aggregation. All variants follow the same training split, op-
timization budget, and evaluation protocol. Table S3 com-
pares point-cloud completion and downstream low-poly re-

C.3. Image-Conditioned Geometric Priors
Prior-assisted generation on Skylines-50K.
Following
the 500-asset evaluation setting of SatSkylines [18], we re-
produce the compared methods on Skylines-50K and eval-
uate LoD0 and LoD1 priors separately. As shown in Ta-
ble S5, Any2Building achieves the best results across all
geometry and appearance metrics under both prior settings,
demonstrating effective building reconstruction with geo-
metric constraints of varying detail.
A rough prior constrains the feasible building volume
while leaving the generator to infer roof structure and fa-
cade articulation. Figure S4 compares image-only gener-
ation with prior-assisted generation on selected examples.
The examples show roof structure beyond the coarse prior,
but do not quantify a mean improvement over a matched
image-only baseline.
Prior quality and provenance matter:
target-derived
heights constitute extra supervision at inference, and incor-
rect footprints or heights can bias the result. We therefore
distinguish the prior-conditioned setting from image-only
reconstruction throughout.
