Abstract— Underwater visual systems often suffer
from blurry textures and low color contrast due to the
inevitable light propagation. These issues can signif-
icantly degrade the perception of stable robotic oper-
ations. In this paper, we introduce a novel learning-
based sensing system that tackles the multidimen-
sional vision tasks in underwater; concretely, we deal
with image enhancement, depth estimation, and un-
certainty for 3-D visual systems. Also, we propose
a TRIDENT model in a fast and lightweight manner;
TRIDENT consists of three parallelized decoders and
one backbone structure for efficient feature sharing.
In addition, it is designed to be trained to express
complex parameterization. In experimental evalua-
tion on several standard datasets, we demonstrate
that TRIDENT significantly outperforms other existing
methods on image enhancement and depth estima-
tion. Despite performing three tasks, our model has
better efficiency than the others for both memory
size and inference time. Finally, our joint learning ap-
proach demonstrates robustness in feature matching
and seamlessly extends from 2-D to 3-D vision tasks.

I. INTRODUCTION
As environmental issues such as marine debris and global
warming increase in severity, the exploration and under-
standing of underwater environments through robotics have
become imperative for various applications, including under-
water scene analysis [1], underwater mapping [2], and 3-D
object detection [3]. However, the underwater environment
poses significant challenges due to distortion and variable
water depth, leading to color contrast loss and degradation
of scene quality.
Notably, some studies [4, 5] have highlighted the limita-
tions of range sensors, such as Light Detection and Ranging
(LiDAR), in underwater settings due to the inherent properties
of water. To ensure robust and accurate geometric operations
in turbid underwater conditions, a multi-factor perception
approach is imperative. This approach should encompass com-
pact algorithm designs, address indistinct sensory data, and

approach. Even when an integrated system is offered, compu-

considered. For example, several researchers [8, 9, 14] have
introduced systems with low computational demand for tasks
such as restoration and depth estimation, leveraging learning-
based approaches. However, these models often suffer from
reduced perceptual accuracy or lower-resolution outputs. On
the other hand, methodologies [7, 10, 12, 15, 16, 17] that
prioritize performance metrics tend to disregard computational
and memory constraints, rendering them unsuitable for field

highlights the need for a holistic approach that harmoniously
balances performance and computational efficiency in under-
water perception research. In response to this gap, this paper
introduces TRIDENT—a novel integrated network designed to
perform three critical tasks simultaneously: Underwater Image

II. RELATED WORKS
1) Underwater Image Enhancement: This is a critical area
of research for reliable underwater vision processes. Model-
based methods perform color correction using traditional im-
age processing techniques or by estimating depth based on the
amount of blurring. For example, Galdran et al. [18] enhanced
contrast by restoring short-wavelength colors, while Berman
et al. [19] employed a haze line-based approach to estimate
ambient color and transmission in color-biased environments.
But, these models frequently face criticism for inadequate
perceptual fidelity. In response, learning-based methods have
significantly advanced the field. Peng et al. [7] designed a
transformer model called U-Shape with new network modules,
employing a loss function across RGB, LAB, and LCH color
spaces. Similarly, Li et al. [20] proposed a learning pipeline
that integrates features from different color spaces, such as
HSV and LAB, in a network called UColor to enrich the
diversity of feature representations. UIEC2 [12] also showed
powerful performance by using both RGB and HSV space
to consider the image hue. UIE-Dal [10] was designed with
fully connected layers and a Generative Adversarial Net-
works (GAN) structure to achieve SOTA performance. Despite
these advances, these models primarily focus on natural color
restoration without adequately addressing lightweight design
and inference time. Some approaches, such as UGAN [9],
employ a GAN architecture with a gradient difference loss
function to enhance UIE performance, while FUnIE-GAN
[8] demonstrated fast inference times, making it suitable for
embedded devices. However, these methods do not incorporate
3-D information.
2) Monocular Depth Estimation: Dense depth maps are
highly useful for geometric tasks such as navigation and 3-D
vision. For this reason, monocular depth estimation has been
extensively studied. Recently, Zhang et al. [21] attempted to
train depth estimation models using diffusion to generate syn-
thetic datasets, showing that these models generally perform
better than traditional methodologies, but the improvement is
marginal. In [17], Local Planar Guidance Block (LPG) was
used to determine whether specific pixels are on the same
plane, extending the process to multiple scales to capture both
local and global features. Yuan et al. [15] used a transformer as
an encoder and window Fully Connected-CRFs as a decoder
to capture correlations between neighboring pixels. Kim et al.
[16] highlighted the robust capabilities of their Global-Local
Path Networks Architecture that uses transformers and learns
from local and global depth features. Recent studies [22, 23]
have attempted to perform monocular depth estimation on
large-scale datasets using diffusion or foundation models,
achieving high generalization performance. However, most
of them were studied in terrestrial environments and did
not consider the degree of hazing and low color contrast in
estimating depth. Furthermore, they did not consider model
computation, which is important in robotics.
On the other hand, UDepth [14] used pre-processed data
to minimize the impact of water properties degradation, pro-
viding robust depth estimation. In addition, UDepth showed a
fast inference time for robotics tasks.
3) Multi-Task Learning: There have been learning-based
studies [6, 13, 24] that attempted to perform multiple tasks
for robust computer vision tasks in underwater. DeepSESR
[13] proposed a robust learning-based vision framework that
efficiently performs simultaneous image enhancement, super-

resolution, and saliency detection. Similarly, DeepWave [6]
performed UIE and super-resolution tasks simultaneously.
Both methods did not consider depth information which is
highly related to the level of image degradation. Similar to
our proposal, Joint-ID [24] performs depth estimation and
image enhancement simultaneously. However, the model is
unsuitable for real-time sensing due to the model size and
only considers two tasks.
III. PROBLEM FORMULATION
A. Preliminary
Light attenuation and backscattering significantly influ-
ence underwater image degradation, typically modeled using
McGlamery’s model [25] as follows:
Ic(x) = Jc(x) · Tc(x) + Bc · (1 −Tc(x))
(1)
, where c ∈{r, g, b} is the wavelength component, Ic(x) and
Jc(x) represent the observed and original images, respectively.
Bc is the homogeneous ambient light, and Tc(x) is the medium
transmission ratio. For a homogeneous medium, Tc(x) is
definable as follows:
Tc(x) = e−βc · d(x)
(2)
Here, βc is the attenuation coefficient with respect to the
ambient medium, which is an invariant constant value, and
d(x) represents scene depth. Tc(x) denotes the degree to
which color intensity is transferred from the clean image.
Consequently, Equation (1) indicates that a complete image
restoration is feasible only if we simultaneously obtain values
for Tc(x) and ambient light Bc. The restoration process using
the inverse distortion model is defined as follows:
Jc(x) = Ic(x)

theory is provided in IV-A.4.
B. Training Pipeline
The approach focuses on generating enhanced images from
distorted inputs and estimating distance from the camera
origin. We formulate the problem as learning a pixel-to-pixel
mapping from a source domain X (of distorted images) to
target domains E (of enhanced images), D (of depth maps),
and U (of depth uncertainty), representing this mapping as
a generating function G : X →{E, D, U}. To address
inaccurate depth estimation and edge instability, we also
estimate the depth uncertainty.

5) Depth Extraction Block (DEB) - (e): DEB focuses on
generating a comprehensive depth map from the feature di
inputs. This involves concatenating depth features at different
scales and applying convolutional operations and sigmoid
activation, ultimately producing a normalized depth image.
The normalized depth image is multiplied by the maximum
depth value to get the final depth map. The multi-scaled DF
features are shown locally and globally in Fig. 4.

tainty is widely used to assess data reliability, as demonstrated

context of our model, which operates within limited param-

instability and reduced performance under full capacity. To
address this, we propose a novel uncertainty estimation method
designed to mitigate these challenges and maintain model
robustness. Information about depth and hazing can be used
implicitly to estimate the degree of difficulty in recognition.
UEB is introduced for 3-task training or scenarios requiring
uncertainty analysis. The process uses the inverse transmission
embedding vector and encoder features to estimate the region

A. Implementation Details
As mentioned in III-B, TRIDENT training is supervised
by paired data of the form ({X}, {E, D, U}) in Joint-ID’s
synthetic large training set. We used the Adam optimizer with
a learning rate of 2 × 10−4 and let TRIDENT converge with
40 epochs and 32 batch sizes. Our hardware system consists
of a Nvidia RTX 3090 Ti graphics and a CPU 11th Intel i7
on Ubuntu 22.04.

• USOD [36]: The dataset is the first large-scale dataset

tection.
• USR-248 [37]: A dataset designed for underwater sin-
gle image super-resolution tasks. It consists of high-
resolution real underwater images and their correspond-
ing low-resolution pairs.
• Jamaica [38]: The Jamaica dataset, also known as the
Port Royal, Jamaica dataset, is part of a collection of
underwater image datasets aimed at object detection.
1) Quantitative performance comparison: Supervised met-

other models on the joint-ID test set, which exhibits severe
hazing conditions. It also attains the best performance for the
metrics Abs Rel and Sq Rel on the pseudo-reference L400 test
set. However, the results indicate that ours does not perform
best on PSNR, SSIM, and RMSE. This is because the ground
truth is not a fully undistorted image; specifically, distant
regions are not properly calibrated. In Fig. 12, other models,
including the ground truth, show a decline in performance
with increasing distance on the L400 test set. Additionally, our
model performs best at larger distances for the unsupervised
metrics UIQM [34] and UCIQE [35]. This explains why
we did not achieve a high score on the L400 test set. The

intensities through the depth uncertainty. < value means the

B. Salient Object Localization
Our proposed system combines robust image enhancement
and depth estimation tasks with depth uncertainty. In this
subsection, we show that the simultaneous extraction of this
information enhances multiple 3-D vision tasks in both speed
and accuracy; in the yellow dotted box in Fig. 17(a), the
original salient object is partially erased, while our image
with high color contrast retains the object’s intact shape.
This improvement in 2-D visual performance extends to 3-D
vision tasks by utilizing depth information. For example, the
extracted salient object can be extended to 3-D salience local-
ization by recovering 3-axis coordinate information. Specif-
ically, an example process of 3-D saliency localization is


12
shown in Fig. 17(b); i) image enhancement is performed to
make saliency detection robust, ii) 3-D reconstruction of the
entire scene is performed using the distance obtained through
depth estimation, iii) finally, the entire reconstructed scene
is filtered through salient object mask and depth uncertainty.
The reason for filtering through depth uncertainty is that the
depth confidence of the edge of the object is low. This 3-D
vision task can be greatly useful in autonomous navigation in
underwater environments.

VII. CONCLUSION
In this paper, we propose a new training pipeline to achieve
the three objectives and prove that the proposed TRIDENT has
robustness in perceptual restoring, depth estimation, and uncer-
tainty at the SOTA; we showed it outperforms in quantitative
and qualitative UIE, UMDE evaluation. We also confirmed
that the overall depth estimation and image enhancement
accuracy can be increased through uncertainty filtering. we
demonstrated it is light-weight and fast compared to other
UMDE and UIE models, despite performing two or three tasks.
In addition, the depth-wise image restoration approach has
proven to be advantageous in obtaining rich correspondences
in feature matching. Finally, we demonstrated that our mecha-
nism is not limited to extending from 2-D to 3-D robot vision
systems. However, this study has a limitation: the depth scale
is unknown. It can create a scale problem for large-scale 3-
D reconstruction or SLAM. In the future, we would like to
solve this problem through depth completion or sonar sensor
fusion.
