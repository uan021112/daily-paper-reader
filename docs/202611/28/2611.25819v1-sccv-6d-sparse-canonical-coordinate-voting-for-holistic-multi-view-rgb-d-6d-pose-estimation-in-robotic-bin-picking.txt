6D Pose Estimation in Robotic Bin Picking

Abstract
Industrial bin picking requires pose estimates that are ac-
curate under clutter, partial occlusion, and the reflec-
tive, texture-less nature of manufactured parts. Most re-
cent approaches to 6D pose estimation follow multi-stage
pipelines. These achieve high accuracy, but incur mem-
ory and runtime footprints that do not cope with the op-
erational constraints of many real-world industrial deploy-
ments. Holistic pipelines estimate poses in a single pass,
with cost controlled through indiscriminate subsampling
or lowered resolution.
Continuing this line, we present
SCCV-6D, a fully sparse multi-view RGB-D estimator. We
reconstruct the 3D space at millimeter precision, prune
background via a staged heatmap mechanism, and predict
class, translation offset and rotation at highest resolution
for an arbitrary number of objects simultaneously. Rota-
tion is formulated as canonical coordinate voting. Points
vote for their canonical object coordinates with an associ-
ated confidence, enabling closed-form pose recovery with
a symmetry-aware weighted Procrustes solve.
Along an
in-cluster point recovery induced by offset votes, this re-
moves time-consuming post-processing stages.
We reuse
the task-supervised attention priors from the heatmap for-
mulation for a novel attention-gated distillation objective
that reweights teacher–student feature alignment. On the
IPD test split we report competitive 0.826 AP at 1.13 s per
scene, significantly faster than the leading entries. On MV-
YCB our approach reaches 96.1 ADD-S, and remains com-
petitive even on the densely packed bins of ROBI.

These challenges motivate multi-view acquisition. Surfaces
missing in one view are regularly recovered in another, and
fusing several calibrated views produces a more consistent
and reliable reconstruction [10, 11, 27, 32, 52]. Yet, espe-
cially multi-view reconstruction introduces a computational
bottleneck. At resolutions sufficient for reliable instance
separation, reconstructing and processing the 3D volume
incurs substantial computational and memory costs. The
reconstruction volume covers the entire workspace while
discriminative geometry is localized in small areas, so the
task-relevant occupancy is highly sparse. Two paradigms,
multi-stage and holistic pipelines, have emerged to handle
high-resolution reconstruction differently.
Multi-stage pipelines obtain object hypotheses from an up-
stream, decoupled detector or segmenter, estimate the 6D
pose per crop, and append refinement stages [5, 14, 26–
28, 35]. These methods currently lead the industrial track

of the BOP challenge [40] and achieve the highest reported
accuracies. However, they are ensembles of independently
trained networks. Memory and training-data requirements
accumulate across the sub-modules. Inference proceeds se-

repeats on the other BOP-industrial dataset, XYZ-IBD [23]
and ITODD-MV [9]. We attribute the coupling to the se-
quential structure. Accuracy in such pipelines is gained by
appending stages, and the cost is incurred anew for every
hypothesis. The fast and accurate regime is unoccupied.
Holistic pipelines instead estimate all poses in a single for-
ward pass [3, 10, 11, 16, 17, 32, 34].
These methods
are considerably smaller and faster, where the computa-
tion and memory demand is controlled by indiscriminate
scene-reduction strategies to a fixed cardinality [10, 11, 17,
34] or coarse-resolution processing [3]. Accuracy degrades
accordingly [40].
The authors of [32] proposed to make this reduction step
task-dependent via a staged heatmap formulation. A coarse
voxelized region-of-interest stage predicts where target ob-
jects are plausible, a finer objectness stage focuses towards
foreground candidates.
Only the retained candidates are
forwarded to the 6D pose heads. Allocating computation
on task-relevant evidence rather than indiscriminate sam-
pling strategies appears to be a promising way out of the
trade-off. We adopt this line of research and address one
structural limitation. [32] consumes depth-only, and geo-
metrically degenerate parts are reported as dominant fail-
ure mode. We fuse color and depth directly during scene
reconstruction. Synchronized RGB-D views are integrated
into a sparse truncated signed distance field [56], and the
colored surface points from the zero-crossing band are the
input to our network. The staged heatmap strategy is ap-
plied on the sparse voxelization of that cloud, after which
foreground voxel candidates are lifted back to point level
through an exact grid lookup. A shared Point Transformer
trunk [51] feeds three heads: classification, translation and
rotation. To the best of our knowledge, we present the first
fully sparse holistic RGB-D framework for 6D pose estima-
tion.
Conventional holistic designs recover the pose in a sub-
sequent object-wise aggregation, commonly a mean-shift
clustering of keypoint votes followed by a least-squares fit
[10, 11, 16, 17, 34]. Depth-based pipelines frequently ap-
pend an ICP refinement that dominates the post-processing
budget [14, 32]. We remove this time-consuming stage in
three steps. First, translation offsets are voted per point,
which naturally forms clusters [32].
A single clustering
pass over the scene replaces the per-object mode.
Only
points voting into a cluster are retained, which sharpens the
point cloud segments beyond the heatmap accuracy. Sec-
ond, every point of a cluster predicts its location in the class-
conditioned canonical frame together with a learned confi-
dence. Thus, a point and its predicted coordinate form a
3D-3D correspondence. Third, the pose follows in closed
form as the confidence-weighted Procrustes solution [20]
over the correspondence set that is over-determined by or-
ders of magnitude relative to keypoint voting, batched over
all objects of a scene. Assignment and reliability are both
predicted rather than inferred from the current pose esti-
mate, so neither correspondence search nor outlier rejection
is iterated.
Additionally, we introduce heatmap-guided feature distilla-
tion, a novel scheme that improves efficiency while reduc-
ing memory consumption. The staged heatmap formulation
provides a sparse, task-supervised measure of relevance.
We reweight the teacher-student feature alignment by the
attention priors assigned by the teacher. The teacher and
student share grid construction and network components but
differ in capacity. We summarize our contributions as fol-
lows.
• A holistic, fully sparse multi-view RGB-D framework for
6D pose estimation that fuses appearance and geometry
before the first network stage, and recovers poses scene-
adaptively in 1.13 s, outperforming methods in the same
latency regime by a wide margin.
• A symmetry-aware canonical coordinate voting head that
replaces rotation regression by per-point correspondence
votes with learned confidences and a weighted Procrustes
solve, yielding poses for an arbitrary number of objects in
closed form and without iterative refinement.
• A heatmap-guided feature distillation scheme that reuses
the attention priors of the pipeline as transfer weights.
2. Related Work
Industrial 6D pose estimation.
6D pose estimation in in-
dustrial contexts generally falls into two categories, distin-
guished by how objects are localized. Multi-stage pipelines
rely on image or point cloud crops from an upstream detec-
tor. CosyPose [27] detects per view, estimates a pose per
detection and reconciles the candidates by bundle adjust-
ment, while [28, 49] render and compare against normal-
ized crops. SAM-6D [35] and FreeZe [5] pair promptable
segmentation or frozen foundation descriptors with a regis-
tration step. AlignPose [38] chains a detector, a segmenter,
a single-view estimator and two refiners before multi-view
aggregation. 3PT [26] unifies detection, segmentation and
pose in a jointly trained transformer pair that tops eleven
2


BOP tracks. Yet, all of these architectures incur structural
training and memory overheads from multiple networks.
Sequential candidate processing makes runtime scale with
object and view count, and refinement crop size. Holistic
pipelines avoid the upstream dependency and estimate all
poses in one forward pass. PVN3D [16] and FFB6D [17]
vote for object keypoints from fused appearance and geom-
etry and solve a least-squares fit per object. MV6D [10] and
SyMFM6D [11] extend point-wise voting to several RGB-
D views with learned point-to-pixel fusion and, in the latter
case, symmetry-aware keypoint targets. SwinDePose [34]
follows the same scheme from depth-only. The respective
fusion modules expect an input of fixed cardinality, which
forces a random or farthest-point reduction. Methods that
consume cropped segments [14, 37, 45] avoid this by an
instance-level assumption.
We address this with an ap-
proach that keeps the single-pass property of the second line
while adopting a task-centric reduction strategy [32].
Sparse encoding for 3D perception.
Sparse processing
is the de-facto standard in long-range perception.
Ap-
proaches differ mainly in how they enlarge the receptive
field without densifying, by attending inside cubic win-
dows [13, 29], by partitioning space along the sensor geom-
etry [30], or by removing dense stages from detection [6].
Point-based operators are sparse by construction. Yet, pre-
vious models were dominated by the cost of neighborhood
queries [43, 50]. Point Transformer V3 [51] replaces those
queries by serializing points along space-filling curves, so
that a patch is a contiguous slice of a sorted array rather than
a gathered neighborhood, and its attention runs over a fixed
token count per patch. The receptive field is therefore set
and controllable by the grid size and grows through pool-
ing at constant attention cost. The model takes point sets
of varying cardinality without padding and scales with the
retained points rather than the workspace volume, which is
what a scene-adaptive pipeline requires and why we adopt
PTv3 as the encoding blocks of the pose heads.
Correspondence-based 6D pose recovery.
A network
can regress the parameters of SE(3) directly. Rotations have
no smooth low-dimensional parametrization, so nearby ori-
entations can map to distant target values, and symmetry
leaves the target multimodal [57]. Correspondence-based
predictors delegate pose recovery to a geometric solver,
which keeps the learning problem local. That route has been
developed primarily on the image lattice, where object co-
ordinates are regressed per pixel [2, 41, 46]. NOCS normal-
izes the object frame across a category before a closed-form
alignment [47]. Symmetry is resolved once per class, since
a crop is not associated with an individual object before the
solve. We transfer the idea to the 3D domain, where our
translation offset votes group points into object-centric seg-
ments that approximate the canonical model up to a rigid
transform. Predicting the canonical coordinate of each point
makes the correspondence 3D to 3D. The residual is then a
Euclidean distance in the metric frame of the reconstruc-
tion. The solver is the closed-form weighted Procrustes so-
lution [7, 20, 24], and the symmetry group is selected per
detected object.
Knowledge distillation for point clouds.
Knowledge
distillation uses a pretrained teacher to guide a smaller stu-
dent and has proven effective for training compact models
with minimal accuracy loss [18, 21, 53, 55]. Yet, distil-
lation on point clouds remains structurally underexplored.
The unordered, sparse nature prevents index-aligned feature
matching, and background dominates the active sites, so a
uniformly averaged feature loss is spent almost entirely on
regions that carry no task-relevant information. The lim-
ited local structure of a single point further weakens the
supervision a feature-matching loss can transfer. Existing
schemes therefore guide the transfer. Yang et al. [53] re-
strict the loss to positions selected by the teacher’s classifi-
cation response, PVKD [21] distills point-to-voxel relations
for LiDAR segmentation, and PointDistiller [55] combines
graph-based local distillation with an occupancy-weighting
strategy. We believe that this proxy does not carry over to
bin picking. Densely reconstructed regions, e.g. bin walls,
produce higher occupancy weights than the small, reflective
and frequently occluded target objects. We weight instead
by the task-supervised, object-centric attention priors of the
heatmap stages, available at no additional cost.

3.6. Heatmap-Guided Feature Distillation
The heatmap formulation produces attention priors that are
supervised for the pose task, which we exploit in an on-top
distillation module. Teacher T and student S share the grid
construction, the staged network design, and differ only in
capacity. The student uses shallower sparse U-Nets in the
heatmap stages, and fewer transformer blocks with reduced
channel dimensions in the encoders.
Both models prune adaptively, so their active sites do not
coincide, and a feature loss cannot be indexed by position.

4.3. Distillation Study
We probe the student performance on the IPD valida-
tion split.
Teacher and students hold 126.7 M, 95.1 M
and 82.3 M parameters.
A student with 25% fewer pa-
rameters trained under a uniformly averaged feature loss
drops 11.8 AP relative to the teacher (Table 5). The loss
arises in the heatmap stages, where foreground–background
imbalance weakens relevance prediction.
Downstream
heads cannot recover foreground that was never retained.
Voxel-occupancy reweighting following PointDistiller [55]
closes about one third of the gap.
Our novel weight-
ing strategy recovers 98%, leaving the student 1.4 AP
short and increasing the margin over [55] from 5.9
to 6.3 AP at 35% reduction.
Our heatmaps remove
most background before point-level processing, so occu-
pancy is computed on an object-enriched but still noisy
high-resolution set.
That noise is deliberate, carried by
the Gaussian-smoothed heatmap targets to retain contextual
cues for cluttered objects, and it becomes overweighted un-
der the occupancy-based scheme and likely reduces the ef-
fectiveness.
4.4. Component Study
Table 4 isolates the individual design decisions. Replac-
ing canonical voting by the per-voxel Chamfer regression
with batched ICP of [32], inside our otherwise unchanged
pipeline, costs 11.4 points AP and is by a wide margin the
largest single effect. Restricting the heads to depth reduces
accuracy by 5.7AP, the second largest effect. The loss re-
flects weaker separation rather than detection. The heatmap
stages prune conservatively to retain contextual informa-
tion.
Appearance helps the classification and translation
heads to distinguish retained background from clutter with
similar geometry, producing sharper clusters and cleaner
correspondences. Predicting on the voxel grid instead of
the restored points reduces accuracy by 1.4 points AP and
by 2.6 points APmm
MSSD. This pattern is consistent with an
encoding error that distorts the pose estimate rather than the
detection stage, and therefore impacts millimeter-level met-
rics more strongly. The replacement of the two encoders
of each head by a single one costs 1.2 AP, and the removal
of the confidence weighting a further 1.0 AP. Confidence
weighting contributes largely at the millimeter thresholds,
where down-weighting unreliable correspondences matters
most.
Limitations.
The method assumes a CAD model per
class and a known, finite symmetry group, which suits in-
dustrial parts but excludes unseen objects. Continuous sym-
metries enter only through a discretization of that group,
and a misspecified group is not detected at inference. Re-
covery further presumes extrinsics accurate relative to ϑ,
since an error of that order displaces the fused surface and
the correspondences with it. Thresholds are set per dataset
and scale with capture volume, as none of them is learned.
5. Conclusion
We presented SCCV-6D, a fully sparse multi-view RGB-D
6D pose estimator that fuses color and depth into a sparse
TSDF. Staged heatmaps focuses high-potential foreground
regions in a scene-adaptive manner. The attention priors
that drive the pruning are reused as transfer weights for fea-
ture distillation, which yields compact students that outper-
form density-based reweighting in this domain. SCCV-6D
predicts class, translation and rotation for an arbitrary num-
ber of objects in a single forward pass. Rotation is cast
as canonical coordinate voting, so every pose follows in
closed form from a symmetry-aware weighted Procrustes
solve. This removes the object-wise post-processing that
otherwise scales with bin occupancy. Across complemen-
tary regimes, we reported accuracy within reach of the lead-
ing multi-stage ensembles at significantly lower latency.
