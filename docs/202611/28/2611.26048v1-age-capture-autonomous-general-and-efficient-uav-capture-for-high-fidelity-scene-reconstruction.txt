informative observations and determine task completion under
different sensing configurations and limited flight resources.
We present AGE-Capture, an autonomous, sensor-adaptive, and
efficient active-capture framework for real UAVs. Building on
renderability, the framework uses a lightweight online state to
evaluate the utility of candidate views, assess task progress, and
guide flight execution. By decoupling geometric exploration from
reconstruction observations and establishing a unified camera-
ray interface, the framework allows different range sensors and
RGB camera models to share a reconstruction-driven acqui-
sition pipeline. Reconstruction-frontier viewpoints characterize
residual acquisition value for region screening and autonomous
termination; dynamics-aware hierarchical planning combined
with asynchronous event-driven capture reduces unnecessary
inter-region motion, time spent waiting for plans, and repeated
observations. Across ten indoor and outdoor simulation scenes,
AGE-Capture achieves a mean peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR)
3.30–3.53 dB higher than that of the online baselines while using
58.2% of the common mission-time budget on average. Its mean
peak GPU memory usage is 91.0%–93.3% lower than that of the
same baselines. Experiments with different sensor configurations
validate the adaptability of the shared acquisition pipeline, while
flights in nine real-world scenes further demonstrate onboard
closed-loop operation under practical computational and motion
constraints.
I. INTRODUCTION
Neural rendering and Gaussian Splatting have advanced
high-fidelity 3D reconstruction and novel-view synthesis [1],

(Xiaofeng Jin): xiaofeng.jin@polimi.it.
[2]. Yet reconstruction quality remains strongly dependent on
the spatial and angular coverage of the acquired observations.
Active reconstruction addresses this dependence by selecting
observation positions and viewing directions that improve
the current scene representation under limited acquisition
resources. For large-scale real-world scenes, UAVs provide
a practical platform because they can reach viewpoints at
different elevations and headings (Fig. 1).
Existing active-capture methods primarily address where
to observe next. Traditional next-best-view (NBV) methods
evaluate candidate viewpoints using geometric uncertainty,
information gain, coverage, or visibility [3]. More recent ap-
proaches estimate the potential value of candidate observations
through neural-field uncertainty, radiance-field feedback, or
related reconstruction-aware signals, thereby relating observa-
tion utility more directly to the reconstruction objective [4],
[5], [6].
Identifying valuable candidate viewpoints, however, is only
one part of a deployable UAV active-reconstruction system.
The system must also accommodate heterogeneous sensing
configurations and coordinate acquisition decisions with exe-
cution under limited flight resources. Existing methods com-
monly evaluate reconstruction under a fixed view count or
time budget. These limits constrain capture but do not indicate
whether further observations would improve the reconstruc-
tion. The system must therefore assess residual reconstruction
value online and terminate capture when further acquisition is
no longer warranted.
Renderability-based reconstruction gain provides a state rep-
resentation suitable for this online decision process. R3CON

The main contributions of this work are as follows:
• A unified active reconstruction architecture for het-
erogeneous sensing configurations. We decouple ge-
ometric sensing from reconstruction observations and
construct a common observation interface using camera
rays, allowing RGB camera models with calibrated ray
mappings to share the same reconstruction-aware active
capture pipeline, while the geometric exploration module
handles depth-map and LiDAR inputs.
• A reconstruction-frontier mechanism for autonomous
task completion. We define reconstruction-frontier view-
points at the position level and use seed-based region
screening to assess task progress and residual acquisi-
tion value, allowing the system to determine whether to
continue capture under the defined reconstruction-value
threshold.
• An efficient active capture system under limited
flight resources. Hierarchical planning coordinates re-
gion scheduling with local view selection. Asynchronous
perception, state updates, planning, and execution reduce
the time spent waiting for plans.
II. RELATED WORK
A. Viewpoint Utility for Active Reconstruction
Early active perception and NBV planning primarily fo-
cused on geometric mapping and exploration of unknown
space. Frontier-based methods prioritize the boundary between
known and unknown space, whereas information-driven meth-
ods rank candidate viewpoints using criteria such as expected
entropy reduction, voxel visibility, or surface coverage [8],
[9], [10], [11], [3]. These works established the basic active-
observation paradigm: estimating the utility of candidate ob-
servations from the current scene state and selecting the view
with the highest expected return.
With high-fidelity scene representations such as neural ra-
diance fields and Gaussian Splatting entering online mapping,
the focus has further shifted from geometric coverage to ob-
servation value that is more directly associated with final ren-
dering or reconstruction quality. ActiveNeRF and NeurAR use
neural-field uncertainty to assess the utility of candidate obser-
vations [12], [13], while FisherRF uses Fisher information to
characterize the potential contribution of new observations to
radiance-field parameter estimation [14]. NVF and NARUTO
further combine visibility, uncertainty, and target observation
quality for active mapping and reconstruction [6], [15]. Sub-
sequent Gaussian Splatting methods construct reconstruction-
aware gains from Gaussian statistics, rendering feedback,
localization–exploration coupling, and multi-level information
value to improve geometric and appearance reconstruction
quality [16], [17], [18], [19], [20], [21], [22].
B. Motion and Mission Planning
Beyond candidate-view valuation, active reconstruction
must plan feasible motions to reach observation goals and
acquire the corresponding measurements. NeurAR integrates
uncertainty-driven view selection with online reconstruction,
while EVPP combines implicit and volumetric representations
with graph-search-based view-path planning [13], [23]. Other
work incorporates frontier exploration into task progression
for implicit reconstruction [24]. Together, these methods move
reconstruction-aware planning beyond candidate-view ranking
toward embodied acquisition coupled with motion, paths, and
online reconstruction.
For large-scale tasks, making an individual viewpoint ex-
ecutable is insufficient; the system must also organize con-
tinuous observations and mission progress. TARE combines
detailed local planning with sparse global planning [25].
Informative UAV path planning combines information value,
topology, and path cost [26], while kinodynamic exploration
incorporates vehicle constraints into target and trajectory eval-
uation [27]. TRACE directly optimizes sensor-footprint-aware
trajectories in a dynamic information field [28], extending dis-
crete NBV selection to trajectory-level active reconstruction.
Their tasks are commonly bounded by a prescribed view
count, path length, time budget, or exploration condition.
C. Requirements for Autonomous UAV Capture
Reconstruction-aware methods determine which observa-
tions can improve reconstruction, but their scores are often
tied to a particular representation. Embodied planners orga-
nize visits to candidate viewpoints, but commonly optimize
paths or trajectories under a given sensing setup and mission
boundary. Autonomous UAV active reconstruction additionally
requires a common reconstruction-oriented state that accepts
heterogeneous RGB camera rays and supports observation-
value queries, regional task progress, and completion deci-
sions. Planning must also coordinate online state updates with
trajectory execution to maintain continuous acquisition.
R3CON represents online reconstruction value with a
lightweight, explicit renderability state that supports efficient
queries and updates [7]. We use this state as feedback for
closed-loop UAV capture.
III. METHOD OVERVIEW
We formulate active reconstruction as a closed-loop capture
process for a UAV. The system centers on a persistent ren-
derability state that summarizes reconstruction support from
acquired observations and provides residual observation value
for subsequent capture. The active-reconstruction node queries
this state to select an executable next-best view (NBV);
executing the NBV produces the observation that updates the
state and closes the loop.

V. HIERARCHICAL ACTIVE CAPTURE PLANNING
A. Global Region Management
Large scenes contain non-uniform free space and recon-
structable surfaces. Searching the entire scene for every NBV
expands the candidate set and increases cross-region motion.
We partition reachable free space into bounded regions, each
represented by a seed, to provide a controlled search scope for
local NBV planning, as illustrated in Fig. 5.
From the free-space map provided by geometric exploration,
we retain voxels that satisfy the clearance constraint and are
connected to the UAV’s current position. Local maxima of the
free-space distance field form seed candidates, and a spatial-
separation constraint selects the final seeds. Each seed serves
as a query location for its region.
We compute seed-to-voxel connectivity distances on the
reachable free-space graph and assign each voxel to its near-
est seed. Compared with Euclidean nearest-seed assignment,
graph-distance partitioning follows free-space connectivity
across obstacles and narrow passages. Connectivity distances
from the current UAV position define the initial visit order.
We determine task completion from the reconstruction gain
expected from further acquisition. To estimate the remaining
gain online, we query sampled positions in the current region
and the seeds of regions awaiting a visit. At each queried
position pi, we compute the renderability gains for the surfaces
visible from that position:

C. Continuous Event-Driven Capture
As shown in Fig. 6, continuous event-driven capture oper-
ates the consumer, planner, and executor asynchronously: the
consumer processes observation events, the planner generates
subsequent decisions while the current trajectory is executed,
and the executor follows successive trajectory segments.
1) Timestamped Event-Driven Observation: During explo-
ration, timestamps are published at 1 Hz to record periodic
observations. During reconstruction, the planner uses the time-
parameterized trajectory to predict when the UAV will reach
the selected NBV and publishes the corresponding timestamp
to the event queue. The update front end continuously matches
incoming synchronized RGB–pose data to queued timestamps;
only matched observations update the persistent renderability
state.

1) Continuous Capture: Continuous-capture ablations sep-
arate two sources of inefficiency in closed-loop capture: wait-

driven completion criterion, disabling early replanning in-

(Fig. 19(a)). Early replanning completes the subsequent de-
cision during execution of the active trajectory, overlapping
planning and motion.
The pseudo-update ablation reveals the effect of stale
state on capture decisions. Across the three scenes, disabling
pseudo-update increases the total number of matched RGB–
pose observations written to the reconstruction backend by
27.3% and total mission time by 16.7% (Fig. 19). Before the
preceding target produces a real observation, its expected sur-
face support is absent from the persistent state, so subsequent
planning can select overlapping observations. Pseudo-update
incorporates this expected support into prospective scoring,
reducing repeated capture between successive decisions.

VII. DISCUSSION
To fit each real-world mission within a single flight, we
limit the capture region and relax the completion criterion,
leaving some distant surfaces with insufficient observations. In
the threshold ablation, stricter completion criteria require more
capture time (Fig. 20). R3CON shows that renderability-driven
capture can achieve high-quality reconstruction with greater
freedom in viewpoint selection [7]. Reconstructing details such
as reflections also depends on the backend’s ability to represent
view-dependent appearance.
In the Workshop scene, we use the same 98 event frames
for scene-specific 3DGS optimization and reconstruction with
Atlas3. The exported Atlas model uses only degree-zero
spherical harmonics, limiting its ability to reproduce view-
dependent reflections. Missing reflected details and overall
color shifts relative to the reference images (Fig. 18) are
consistent with Atlas scoring lower than scene-specific 3DGS
on both PSNR and SSIM. Atlas nevertheless produces more
coherent textures and object contours in the illustrated regions,
and the scene remains recognizable in all four extrapolated
views. The different reconstructions obtained from the same
observations motivate calibrating the completion threshold to
the reconstruction backend and the target quality.
For a UAV with limited attitude control, a wider field
of view can reduce the need to reposition by making more
viewing directions available from a single pose (Table VI).

Compared with ground mobile platforms, UAV deployment

demonstrate that a reconstruction-driven autonomous capture
loop can operate under these constraints.
