Abstract
Recovering globally consistent 3D human motion from
monocular videos remains challenging under dynamic cam-
era motion.
Although SLAM and human mesh recov-
ery (HMR) provide complementary camera and body es-
timates, directly combining them often leads to inconsis-
tent orientations and accumulated trajectory drift.
We
present GravHMR, a training-free, plug-and-play frame-
work that couples SLAM and HMR through a shared
gravity-aligned reference.
By allowing gravity to be es-
timated independently of the HMR backbone, GravHMR
accommodates world-grounded HMR models with or with-
out explicit gravity-view representations. Specifically, Yaw-
Aware Orientation Optimization (YAOO) resolves heading
inconsistencies by correcting body and velocity orienta-
tions about the camera-frame gravity axis.
Conditioned
on these corrected orientations, Gravity-Aware Trajectory
Optimization (GATO) anchors root trajectories in the met-
ric SLAM world by jointly leveraging 2D reprojection, the
metric scale implicit in the human body, and the body’s
own motion. On EMDB-2, GravHMR achieves state-of-the-
art performance with 154.0 mm W-MPJPE100 using ViPE
and 189.8 mm using masked DROID-SLAM, outperform-
ing TRAM (222.4 mm) under the same masked DROID-
SLAM camera estimates. It further reduces W-MPJPE100
by 44.3% over GVHMR (276.5 mm) and by 20.0% over
HTD-Refine (192.4 mm) with the same HMR backbone.

The central challenge is therefore not merely to re-

transferred between SLAM and HMR. Directly adopting
the full camera-derived body orientation may discard use-

accumulated drift. Moreover, these discrepancies interact:
an incorrect heading biases the motion prior and geometric
constraints used for root trajectory recovery. This motivates
a selective and ordered coupling strategy: first correcting
the horizontal headings of the body and its motion about the
shared gravity axis, and then recovering root displacements
under the corrected body orientations.
Our main contributions are:
• We introduce GravHMR, a training-free framework that
couples independently estimated SLAM camera geome-
try and HMR motion priors through a shared gravity ref-
erence, enabling consistent world-grounded human mo-
tion recovery without retraining the underlying HMR
backbone.
• We propose a gravity-constrained geometric coupling
strategy with two complementary modules: Yaw-Aware
Orientation Optimization (YAOO) and Gravity-Aware
Trajectory Optimization (GATO). YAOO reconciles body
and motion orientations within the shared gravity refer-
ence for better human orientation, while GATO anchors
root trajectories in the metric SLAM world using geo-
metric observations and gravity-referenced motion priors.
Together, they improve body tilt and vertical alignment,
travel direction and human root trajectory.
• GravHMR improves the global trajectories of its HMR
backbone.

2. Related Work
2.1. Monocular Human Motion Recovery and Pose
Estimation
Monocular human motion recovery builds on parametric
human body models [19, 21] to estimate pose and shape
from images. HMR [8] directly regresses body parameters
from a single image with an adversarial prior. SMPLify [2]
instead fits SMPL to 2D keypoints through iterative opti-
mization. SPIN [14] integrates model fitting into the train-
ing loop to improve regression through optimized body es-
timates. CLIFF [17] incorporates bounding-box location
and full-frame reprojection supervision to improve camera-
relative orientation and pose estimation, while SPEC [12]
and CameraHMR [20] further condition the regression on
estimated camera intrinsics.
Part-aware designs such as
PARE [11] and analytical inverse-kinematics models such
as HybrIK [15] improve robustness under occlusion and ex-
treme poses. HMR 2.0 [5] adopts a vision transformer and
a transformer decoder for robust single-image human mesh
recovery, and ReFit [28] iteratively refines its predictions
through recurrent feedback. Multi-HMR [1] extends feed-
forward recovery to multiple whole-body meshes in a single
image. PromptHMR [30] conditions full-image mesh re-
covery on spatial and semantic prompts, supporting partial
observations and person–person interactions.
For video inputs, temporal modeling provides additional
evidence for coherent motion reconstruction. VIBE [10]
combines temporal regression with an adversarial motion
discriminator trained using unpaired motion-capture data.
TCMR [4] emphasizes temporal context to improve motion
consistency while preserving per-frame accuracy.
HTD-
Refine [31] estimates image-conditioned 3D joint velocities
and accelerations and uses them to refine existing motion
reconstructions. These advances improve body reconstruc-
tion and temporal dynamics, while recovering global trajec-
tories under moving cameras additionally requires resolving
camera–human motion ambiguity.
2.2. Global Human Motion Recovery from Moving-
Camera Videos
Recovering world-space human motion requires separat-
ing human movement from camera ego-motion, which
in turn relies on robust visual SLAM such as DROID-
SLAM [25] and DPVO [26] for camera-pose initialization.
GLAMR [34] predicts global human trajectories from lo-
cal body motion and jointly optimizes human trajectories
and camera poses against image observations. TRACE [24]
regresses world-space 5D trajectories directly from in-the-
wild video. SLAHMR [33] combines SLAM-derived cam-
era motion with learned human motion priors to resolve
scale ambiguity and recover global human trajectories.
PACE [13] tightly couples human motion priors and back-
ground scene features in a bundle-adjustment-inspired op-
timization of human and camera motion.
WHAM [23]
combines human motion features with SLAM-estimated
camera angular velocity to recover world-grounded motion.
GVHMR [22] predicts body orientation in gravity-view co-
ordinates and combines it with camera rotation and local
root velocity to reconstruct global motion.
TRAM [29]
combines masked SLAM and background-derived metric
2


scale with a video-based human motion regressor to re-
cover world-space trajectories, and GENMO [16] further
casts global motion recovery as a conditional generative
problem over a large motion prior. PromptHMR-Vid [30]
extends promptable mesh recovery to video and combines
its predictions with TRAM’s metric SLAM for global mo-
tion estimation. Humans as Checkerboards [32] uses HMR-
derived human–scene contact depths to calibrate monocular
SLAM scale without iterative optimization, and [3] simi-
larly treats the human body as a dynamic calibration pattern
for multi-camera scene reconstruction. Joint human–scene
optimization has also been explored by JOSH [18] and Hu-
manMM [36] for multi-shot videos.
Despite these advances, independently estimated cam-
era geometry and human motion predictions can remain
inconsistent. Integrating predicted root velocities may ac-
cumulate trajectory drift, while camera-space lifting alone
does not explicitly reconcile image observations with the
temporal motion prior. GravHMR addresses this inconsis-
tency through decoupled orientation and trajectory recov-
ery: camera geometry provides the world-space reference,
2D pose observations anchor root positions, and gravity-
referenced displacements constrain motion consistency.

3.3. Temporal Consistency Recovery
GATO and higher-order motion cues are complementary
in frequency.
GATO anchors the root to absolute posi-
tions through per-frame reprojection, which removes low-
frequency heading and trajectory drift but leaves each frame
weakly coupled to its neighbors, so residual high-frequency
jitter can remain. Image-derived kinematic cues show the
opposite behavior. The joint velocities and accelerations
predicted by PVA-Net [31] are locally accurate and tem-
porally smooth, but integrating them accumulates drift and
therefore cannot determine the global trajectory on their
own.
Since GATO already provides a drift-free trajectory,
these cues no longer need to recover global position; they
only need to regularize local temporal derivatives around
a well-anchored solution. We therefore add a Flow-Aware
Temporal Consistency (FATC) stage that reuses the refine-
ment formulation of HTD-Refine [31]: starting from the
GATO reconstruction, we jointly refine body pose and root
motion so that their temporal derivatives agree with the ve-
locities and accelerations predicted by PVA-Net. FATC is
not designed to recover the global trajectory; it inherits the
trajectory of GATO and mainly removes high-frequency jit-
ter and foot sliding. Sec. 4.2 reports its effect separately.
4. Experiments
4.1. Experimental Setup
Datasets. We evaluate world-grounded motion on EMDB-
2 [9], 25 in-the-wild sequences captured by a moving hand-
held camera, and on the RICH [6] test set, 191 views
recorded by static cameras.
For the ablations we addi-
tionally use EgoBody [35] (17 recordings), whose head-
mounted camera frequently truncates the body.
Metrics. Following GVHMR [22], we report W-MPJPE100
and WA-MPJPE100 for global joint accuracy over 100-
frame segments, using alignment based on the first two
frames and the entire segment, respectively. RTE measures
trajectory error normalized by ground-truth travel distance.
For camera-space reconstruction, we report PA-MPJPE,
MPJPE, and PVE. Accel, Jitter, and Foot-Sliding (FS) mea-
sure motion smoothness and foot stability, and ERVE and
W-RVE evaluate root-velocity accuracy. Lower is better for
all metrics.

4.4. Comparison under Identical Camera Inputs
Does better SLAM yield better global HMR perfor-
mance? Improved camera estimates benefit global human
motion recovery, but the reconstruction pipeline also mat-
ters. Using the same masked DROID-SLAM camera es-
timates and ground-truth intrinsics as TRAM, GravHMR
achieves 189.8 mm W-MPJPE100 on EMDB-2, compared
with 222.4 mm for TRAM and 204.9 mm for TRAM +
HTD-Refine, reducing error by 14.7% and 7.4%, respec-
tively. With ViPE camera poses and estimated intrinsics,
GravHMR further reduces the error to 154.0 mm. These re-
sults demonstrate gains under identical camera inputs and
additional benefits from the ViPE camera configuration.

Coupling of Orientation and Trajectory Recovery.
YAOO and GATO interact: the body orientation determines
the root-relative joints used for reprojection and the world-
space direction of the motion prior, and GATO recovers
the root under this orientation. Together they reduce W-
MPJPE100 from 262.8 to 155.3 mm relative to the GVHMR
rollout, before FATC (Tab. 3), and from 203.5 to 123.3 mm
(−39.4%) on EgoBody. The orientation columns separate
their roles: YAOO only rotates about gravity, so Etilt stays
within 0.05◦of the rollout on EMDB-2 while Eyaw falls
from 7.33◦to 6.69◦; Edir, governed by GATO, falls from
11.8◦to 5.4◦. With VIMO [29], which has no gravity-view
head, GravHMR changes W-MPJPE100 only from 197.9 to
196.3 mm (Tab. 3). VIMO’s world-space body is already
the camera-frame orientation lifted by the SLAM rotation
and its root is already camera-anchored, so YAOO has no
rollout heading to correct and GATO little drift to remove.
The large gains on GVHMR therefore come from recon-
ciling a gravity-view rollout with the camera, which is the
setting GravHMR targets.
5. Conclusion
We presented GravHMR, a training-free framework that
couples SLAM camera geometry and HMR motion priors
through a shared gravity-aligned reference. Our approach
selectively corrects body and velocity headings while re-
taining gravity-referenced body tilt. It further uses 2D pose
8


observations to refine pose and orientation before recov-
ering global root trajectories, reducing inconsistencies be-
tween human motion priors and camera geometry without
retraining the HMR backbone.

A.2. Angular Consistency Between YAOO and

GATO (Sec. 3.2.2) uses the body orientation in two places:
the reprojection term of Eq. (7) places the root-relative
joints with Rt, and the dynamic term of Eq. (9) compares
the root displacement with dt. Both come from Eq. (4) and
are rotated by the same yaw ψt, so the two terms see one
heading and cannot pull the root toward different directions.

sequence, and (v) re-solve Eq. (A1). Three iterations suffice
for the weights to stabilize; the procedure adds a negligi-
ble cost relative to the trajectory optimization. When fewer
than three joints are valid in a frame, the solve of Eq. (A1)
becomes ill-posed; we then fall back to all joints with a
small floor weight (10−3) so that the frame keeps a defined
root, which is subsequently constrained by Lhuman-scale and
Ldynamic.
Effect of truncation (Tab. A7).
Tab. A7 was run with
the depth of the per-frame 2D root solve (2D body extent
in Tab. 5), which makes GATO sensitive to which joints
are trusted; its detector-confidence row equals that row of
Tab. 5.
On EMDB-2, where the subject is mostly fully
visible, all strategies lie within 1.4 mm W-MPJPE100. Un-
der frequent truncation (EgoBody), uniform weights give
232.3 mm and the detector confidence 205.0 mm; the dy-
namic weight lowers this to 144.9 mm, and combining it
with the structural prior to 136.1 mm, consistent with the
per-joint degradation in Fig. A1. With the HMR depth of the
main configuration, GravHMR reaches 123.3 mm on Ego-
Body with the detector confidence alone (Tab. 5).
B. Generalization to HMR Backbones
GVHMR predicts the body orientation in a gravity-view
frame and integrates a world-space rollout from it. Other
HMR backbones, such as VIMO [29], predict only a
camera-frame orientation and translation.
Source of the gravity reference.
GravHMR takes grav-
ity from the images with GeoCalib [27] and expresses the
SLAM cameras in the resulting gravity frame (Sec. 3.1), so
this step does not depend on the backbone.
Backbones without a gravity view.
For such a backbone,
the world-space body is the camera-frame orientation lifted
by the SLAM rotation, so Rroll
t
= eRt in Eq. (3), ψt = 0,
and YAOO leaves the body and the motion prior unchanged;
the world-space root is likewise the camera-frame root lifted
by the SLAM camera. GATO is applied without change.
With VIMO, GravHMR changes W-MPJPE100 only from
197.9 to 196.3 mm on EMDB-2 (Tab. 3): there is no rollout
heading to correct and little drift to remove. The large gains
on GVHMR come from reconciling a gravity-view rollout
with the camera, which is the setting GravHMR targets.
C. Additional Results
Camera-space accuracy.
GravHMR corrects the world-
space orientation and trajectory and refines body pose only
through FATC. Tab. A1 checks that this does not cost
camera-space accuracy:
on RICH, GravHMR improves
PA-MPJPE from 39.5 to 35.5 mm and Accel from 4.1 to
3.35 m/s2 over GVHMR.
Camera intrinsics.
ViPE estimates the focal length more
accurately than TRAM on all 25 EMDB-2 sequences: the
mean absolute error is 0.6% against 7.7%, and the maxi-
mum is 1.2% against 34.6% (Tab. A3). At this accuracy
the remaining error no longer matters: replacing the ViPE
intrinsics with ground truth changes W-MPJPE100 from
154.0 to 154.1 mm. TRAM’s intrinsics, in contrast, raise its
W-MPJPE100 from 222.2 to 304.9 mm. GravHMR needs
good intrinsics as well: with masked DROID-SLAM and
TRAM’s intrinsics, it degrades from 189.8 to 270.4 mm.
Gravity source.
Tab. A4 replaces the vertical of the
body orientation, which YAOO keeps from the HMR roll-
out (Sec. 3.2.1), with GeoCalib or ground-truth gravity.
GeoCalib matches the rollout vertical on W-MPJPE100
(both 155.3 mm) and ground-truth gravity is slightly worse
(160.0 mm), so the rollout vertical is not the limiting factor
on EMDB-2.
