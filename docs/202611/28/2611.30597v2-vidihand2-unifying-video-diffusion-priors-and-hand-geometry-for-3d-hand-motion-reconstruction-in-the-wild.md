## Abstract
Learning robot manipulation from egocentric human videos requires accurate metric 3D hand motion. Recent methods leverage pretrained video diffusion model (VDM) priors to recover occluded hands directly from full-frame videos, but their appearance-based depth regression generalizes poorly to unseen cameras. In this work, we propose ViDiHand2, a geometry-driven framework that reformulates VDM-based hand depth estimation from direct regression into fine-grained geometric constraint solving. Specifically, we introduce Fine-Grained Geometric Lifting and Clip-Level Ray Fitting, which recover metric depth by enforcing geometric consistency between pixel-aligned 2D joints and the metric structure of MANO hands, while leveraging temporal observations across the clip. Since the lifting relies on these 2D joints, to improve robustness under occlusion and image truncation, we further develop Confidence-Gated Fusion to adaptively combine geometrically lifted joints with MANO predictions according to localization confidence. Finally, we enhance the VDM backbone to better capture spatial-temporal representations and produce the per-frame 2D joint and MANO predictions that both steps rely on. Extensive experiments on seven public datasets demonstrate that ViDiHand2 consistently outperforms 18 baselines, reducing in-the-wild absolute joint error by 42% and detection failures by 8.82x compared with the best baseline on each metric, while running at 57 FPS. The project page is available at https://vidihand2.github.io/.
