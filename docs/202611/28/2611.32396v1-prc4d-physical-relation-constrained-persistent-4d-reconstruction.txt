Abstract
A faithful 4D reconstruction should capture both the
shape and motion of objects, even when they disappear from
view. Existing methods preserve previously observed ge-
ometry and extend motion through occlusion using local
spatial and motion associations. However, treating short-
term contact or shared motion as sufficient evidence of per-
sistent rigidity can introduce incorrect motion constraints
and distort overall reconstruction. To address this limita-
tion, we present PRC4D, a physical relation-constrained

them to maintain spatial consistency through occlusion.
PRC4D combines 3D contact and relative motion consis-

figurations under changing visibility.

1. Introduction
Monocular videos provide only partial observations of dy-
namic scenes, leaving the motion of occluded objects un-
certain.
Recent advances in monocular 4D reconstruc-
tion have improved geometric recovery and motion estima-
tion through visual correspondences [3, 11, 36] and struc-
tured motion representations [17, 40, 42]. In particular, 4D
Primitive-Mˆach´e (4DPM) [22] accumulates previously ob-
served geometry as 3D primitives and extends their mo-
tion through occlusion, enabling persistent reconstruction
beyond the currently visible scene.
However, approaches that infer occluded motion from
local spatial and motion associations can mistake transient
interactions for persistent rigid couplings. Components may
remain in contact while undergoing relative motion, as in
articulated structures [9, 18, 27], or briefly move together
before separating. Treating these associations as fixed rigid
relations can introduce incorrect motion constraints and dis-
tort relative object poses. The central challenge is therefore
not simply to propagate object motion, but to determine
which physical relations provide reliable constraints over
time.
To address this limitation, we present PRC4D, a physi-
cal relation-constrained framework for persistent 4D recon-
struction. We infer candidate rigid couplings from 3D con-
tact and relative motion consistency, then verify their persis-
tence using sequence-level evidence and graph-level con-
sistency. The verified relations form a temporal physical-
relation graph that identifies which reconstructed compo-
nents remain rigidly coupled.
The verified graph constrains both observed and oc-
cluded motion. For observed components, we refine their
poses to reduce deviations from the relative configurations
of their rigid groups.
When a component becomes oc-
cluded, we propagate motion from a visible member of the
same group, maintaining the relative configuration as visi-
bility changes (Fig. 1).
We evaluate PRC4D on diverse real-world multi-object
sequences and demonstrate improved reconstruction accu-
racy.
To further assess its robustness to occlusion, we
construct OmniGibson-Occlusion, a controlled synthetic
benchmark that provides ground-truth geometry and mo-
tion throughout both visible and occluded intervals. PRC4D
achieves the highest mean reconstruction F-score among the
compared methods on both benchmarks, with controlled oc-
clusion experiments additionally showing reduced relative
pose errors and more consistent object configurations.
Our contributions are summarized as follows:
• We introduce a temporal physical-relation graph that dis-
tinguishes persistent rigid couplings from transient in-
teractions by combining geometric and motion evidence
with sequence-level verification.
• We develop a relation-constrained reconstruction frame-
work that uses the same verified relations to refine ob-
served component poses and propagate motion through
occlusion.
• We construct OmniGibson-Occlusion, a synthetic bench-
mark with ground-truth geometry and motion throughout
occlusion, enabling controlled evaluation of reconstruc-
tion robustness under changing visibility.
• We demonstrate improved reconstruction accuracy on
real-world and synthetic benchmarks, with controlled oc-
clusion experiments showing reduced relative pose errors
and more consistent object configurations.
2. Related Work
Monocular 4D Reconstruction.
Recent advances in
monocular 4D reconstruction have improved the recov-
ery of dynamic scene geometry and motion through ge-
ometric priors [14, 23, 29, 33, 37], temporal correspon-
dences [3, 7, 11, 30], and structured motion represen-
tations [1, 21, 25, 26, 40, 43].
Optimization-based ap-
proaches, such as Shape of Motion [31] and C4D [34],
integrate long-range correspondences and geometric con-
straints to recover coherent 3D motion. Meanwhile, feed-
forward methods, including POMATO [41], St4RTrack [4],
and D4RT [39], jointly estimate spatiotemporal geometry
and motion from video.
However, temporal correspon-
dences and motion regularization do not necessarily enforce
constant relative transformations between distinct compo-
nents that move rigidly together. Our method addresses this
by inferring persistent rigid relations from reconstructed
geometry and motion, and using them as explicit cross-
component SE(3) constraints for pose refinement.
Occlusion-Aware and Persistent 4D Reconstruction.
Occlusion-aware 4D reconstruction has been explored
through spatiotemporal reasoning, geometric completion,
and persistent scene representations [8, 20, 32]. Recent ap-
proaches, such as Complet4R [35] and COM4D [5], lever-
age information across observations to reconstruct geome-
try beyond the current view and recover compositional 4D
scenes. 4DPM [22] further enables persistent reconstruction
by accumulating previously observed geometry and extend-
ing object motion through occlusion using spatial contact
and motion similarity. However, such local associations do
not necessarily establish persistent rigid couplings across
a sequence. Our method addresses this limitation through
sequence-level relation verification and relation-constrained
pose refinement and motion propagation.
Rigid Motion Constraints and Relational Reconstruc-
tion.
Geometric and relational constraints provide addi-
tional structure for reconstructing dynamic scenes from am-
biguous visual observations [2, 12, 24]. MoSca [13] repre-

Geometric Contact Evidence.
We first identify potential
rigid couplings using the accumulated 3D geometry of re-
constructed components. Candidate pairs are selected based

measured by the proportion of surface voxels within a spa-

teresis aggregates noisy observations into continuous con-
tact intervals. These intervals provide evidence of physical
contact, but do not by themselves establish rigid coupling.

3.4. Graph-Guided Motion Propagation
Occlusion-Aware Relation Activation.
The inferred per-
sistent rigid relations enable motion propagation from vis-
ible components to their occluded rigid partners. At each
keyframe, we construct active rigid groups from the veri-
fied physical-relation graph. A relation remains active dur-
ing occlusion if its most recent joint observation supports
rigid coupling, while contradictory reobservations prevent
further propagation through that relation. For each occluded
component, we select a visible driver within its active rigid
group, prioritizing relations with stronger geometric and
temporal evidence.

4.3. Multi-Object Reconstruction and Physical Re-
lations
Reconstruction under Complex Motion.
Tab. 2 evalu-
ates our method under diverse real-world object motions.
Unlike the consistent improvements observed in synthetic
occlusion scenarios, the gains vary across multi-object se-
quences. The larger improvements in MultiObj2 and Spin2
contrast with the unchanged result in MultiObj1 and the
degradation in BallPush.
This suggests that physical-
relation constraints are beneficial when persistent rigid cou-
plings can be identified and visual observations become in-
complete or unreliable, rather than acting as a general reg-
ularizer for arbitrary object motion. Additional quantitative
and qualitative results on HO3D [6] are provided in the sup-
plementary material.
Persistent and Transient Interactions.
Fig. 4 illustrates
the distinction between persistent rigid couplings and tem-
porary interactions in a real-world sequence. Objects may
exhibit contact or similar motion over a short interval with-
out remaining rigidly attached. Enforcing such relations
throughout the sequence would incorrectly constrain their
subsequent independent motions. Our method therefore ap-
plies pose refinement and motion propagation only to rela-
tions verified as persistent, while excluding transient inter-
actions from the reconstruction constraints.
4.4. Analysis of Physical Relation Constraints
Roles of Pose Refinement and Propagation.
Tab. 3 ex-
amines the individual contributions of pose refinement and
graph-guided motion propagation. Replacing 4DPM’s orig-
inal motion extension with graph-guided propagation yields
a larger improvement than applying pose refinement alone.
This suggests that persistent physical relations provide more
reliable motion transfer through occlusion than the original
motion grouping. Pose refinement further improves recon-
struction by correcting inconsistent observed poses. Com-
bining both modules achieves the highest F-score and recall,
maintaining coherent object configurations across changing
visibility.
Temporal Smoothness versus Relational Constraints.
Tab. 4 investigates whether temporal smoothing or 4DPM
grouping can substitute for persistent physical relations.
Independent temporal smoothing reduces relative pose er-
rors but degrades reconstruction F-score, indicating that
smoother individual trajectories do not necessarily yield
more accurate scene reconstructions. The complete eval-
uation across all smoothing configurations is reported in the
supplementary material.
We further replace our inferred relations with the original
4DPM motion-extension groups while retaining the same
refinement and propagation modules. Despite improving
rotational accuracy, this alternative increases translation er-
ror and reduces reconstruction F-score. This suggests that
enforcing rigid constraints alone is insufficient when the un-
derlying component associations are unreliable. Together,
these results highlight the importance of identifying reliable
physical relations, rather than merely smoothing or cou-
pling component motions.

construction when enforced without sufficient verification.
Temporal verification and graph-level consistency checks
progressively reduce false-positive relations from four to
one, while correctly identifying all 17 evaluated ground-
truth rigid relations. These improvements increase relation
precision from 80.95% to 94.44%, accompanied by sub-
stantial reconstruction gains. The results demonstrate that
the reliability of inferred relations is critical when using
them as persistent motion constraints.
5. Limitations
Our method inherits the piecewise-rigid assumption of
4DPM and cannot model complex non-rigid deformations.
Relation inference depends on initial geometry and mo-
tion estimates, potentially coupling unattached components
with similar motion. Although articulated components can
be reconstructed independently, our constraints only han-
dle persistent fixed couplings. Finally, motion propagation
requires a visible rigid partner, and sequence-level verifica-
tion requires the full video, limiting incremental reconstruc-
tion.
6. Conclusion
We presented PRC4D, a framework that leverages persistent
rigid relations to maintain spatial consistency across chang-
ing visibility. By verifying physical relations through geo-
metric and temporal evidence, our method refines observed
component poses and propagates motion through occlu-
sion without altering the underlying geometry. Experiments
on synthetic occlusion and real-world multi-object bench-

marks demonstrate improved reconstruction accuracy. Our
ablations further highlight that reliable relation verification
is essential to prevent incorrect motion constraints from de-
grading persistent reconstruction.
