Abstract — 3D object reconstruction is essential for robotic perception and manipulation, yet conventional approaches pre-dominantly rely on visual observations that can be limited by occlusion or unavailable in visually constrained environ-ments. In this work, we propose a vision-free, training-free, and category-agnostic framework for reconstructing 3D object meshes from sparse tactile contacts without relying on pre-trained shape models. A 6-DoF robotic arm equipped with a 20-DoF multi-finger robotic hand collects tactile observations using 3 × 3 pressure sensor arrays mounted on the finger pads. The acquired tactile measurements are transformed into 3D contact constraints through robot kinematics and integrated into a multi-scale Gaussian Process Implicit Surface (GPIS). To address sparse and incomplete surface observations, the proposed method incorporates contact-confidence weighting, weak pseudo-surface constraints, and an observation-driven geometric prior to preserve local surface geometry and provide structural guidance in unobserved regions. Real-world experi-ments on six objects achieve a Chamfer Distance (CD) of 1.610, an Earth Mover’s Distance (EMD) of 7.738, and an F-Score of 95.00, with reconstruction completed in an average of 49.99 s per object. These results demonstrate that sparse tactile sensing can provide sufficient geometric information for continuous 3D object reconstruction without visual or semantic information. 

I. INTRODUCTION 3D object reconstruction is an important component of robotic perception and manipulation, enabling robots to understand object geometry for downstream tasks such as grasping, interaction, and shape-aware manipulation [1], [2]. Many existing 3D reconstruction approaches rely primarily on visual observations, including RGB and depth images, to recover object geometry [1]. Although vision provides rich global information, its effectiveness can be limited in real-world manipulation scenarios. In particular, parts of an object may be occluded by the robotic hand, surrounding objects, or the object itself, making it difficult to obtain complete geometric information from visual observations alone [3], [4]. This limitation becomes more pronounced in cluttered environments, where multiple objects may further restrict camera visibility [3]. Tactile sensing provides an alternative means of perceiving object geometry through direct physical interaction. Unlike vision, tactile sensing does not rely on line-of-sight visibility and can acquire local contact information from regions that are difficult to observe from external viewpoints [1], [2], [5]. These characteristics make tactile sensing particularly

In this work, we investigate whether a robot can recon-struct the 3D geometry of an object using tactile data alone, without relying on external cameras or explicit object class labels. We propose a vision-free 3D mesh reconstruction framework using a 6-DoF robotic arm equipped with a 20-DoF right robotic hand. During interaction with an object, tactile data are collected from sensors mounted on the finger pads of the robotic hand and used to infer the object geom-etry. We employ Gaussian Process Implicit Surfaces (GPIS) [6], [7] to reconstruct a continuous object surface from the acquired tactile contacts and extract its 3D mesh. Since GPIS does not require data-driven model training, the proposed framework can reconstruct previously unseen objects directly from tactile measurements without a separate training stage or explicit object category information. 3D object reconstruction has primarily been developed based on visual observations such as RGB or depth images. However, vision alone has limitations in capturing occluded regions and local surface geometry that is difficult to observe. Tactile sensing has therefore been employed as a comple-mentary modality that acquires local surface information through direct physical contact. Accordingly, vision–touch reconstruction methods have been developed to combine global visual information with local tactile measurements, demonstrating improved reconstruction performance over ei-ther modality alone [2], [8]. Nevertheless, these methods still depend on visual observations, limiting their applicability when visual information is degraded or unavailable. During robotic manipulation, regions of interest can also be occluded by the hand or the object itself. These limitations have motivated tactile-only approaches that acquire object shape information solely through physical contact without relying on vision [9], [10]. 

A. Hardware 

The type of tactile sensor determines the form and amount of information obtained from each contact. Recent systems widely employ vision-based tactile sensors such as GelSight, DIGIT, and TacTip, which capture dense local contact in-formation by imaging deformations of the sensing surface [2], [5], [8]. Compact sensors have also been developed for integration with multi-fingered robotic hands; for exam-ple, DIGIT was specifically designed with a compact form factor that enables tactile perception and manipulation with multiple fingers [2]. However, these sensors produce high-dimensional tactile images, requiring additional processing or learned inference to extract contact locations or local geometric information for 3D reconstruction [2], [5], [8]. For tactile-only perception, obtaining a sufficient number of contacts during exploration is important for observing a broad region of an object. Earlier Gaussian Process (GP)-based tactile exploration methods primarily acquired con-tact observations sequentially [10], [11], whereas multi-finger robotic hands can obtain multiple contact observations through several fingers during a single interaction. TactoFind [12] demonstrates this capability using a three-finger hand, recognizing objects without vision by representing tactile ob-servations using binary contact signals and fingertip positions rather than the full tactile images. This suggests that contact locations accumulated through multi-finger exploration can themselves serve as useful geometric observations without requiring dense local geometry to be inferred from tactile images. In this work, we use 3 ×3 pressure arrays mounted on the finger pads of a multi-finger robotic hand. Unlike vision-based tactile sensors that require geometric information to be extracted from tactile images, the pressure arrays directly provide numerical contact signals at sensing elements with known locations. We therefore transform the locations of active sensing elements into 3D contact points through the forward kinematics (FK) of the arm–hand chain and use them as geometric observations for 3D shape reconstruction. This allows contact information acquired from multiple fingers to be directly used for reconstruction without additional tactile-image processing or learned geometry inference. 

B. Reconstruction 

Recent tactile-based 3D reconstruction methods have em-ployed high-resolution information from vision-based tac-tile sensors together with learned reconstruction models. TouchSDF extracts CNN-based features from tactile im-ages and combines them with DeepSDF to reconstruct continuous 3D shapes [5], while Touch2Shape employs a touch-conditioned diffusion model to generate object shapes from sparse tactile observations [13]. TouchAnything further leverages a pretrained visual diffusion model together with sparse tactile contacts and class-level descriptions for shape estimation [14]. Although these approaches can infer object shape from limited tactile observations using learned models, they rely on pretrained models or training data. In contrast, tactile-only reconstruction methods have in-vestigated estimating object surfaces directly from physical contacts without relying on visual observations or learned shape models. Early approaches employed Gaussian pro-cesses and GPIS to represent sparse contact observations as continuous implicit surfaces and combined them with active tactile exploration to progressively reconstruct object surfaces [10], [11], [15], [16]. However, because physical contact is inherently limited to accessible regions, insuffi-ciently observed regions remain uncertain, making accurate reconstruction of the complete object shape from sparse contacts challenging. To address this problem, our approach directly incorpo-rates sparse 3D contact observations acquired through multi-finger pressure sensing into a training-free GPIS framework. We further complement shape estimation in unobserved regions with a geometric prior, enabling continuous 3D mesh reconstruction without model training or explicit object class information. III. METHOD 

A. Overview 

We reconstruct 3D object geometry from tactile obser-vations and kinematic information acquired through robotic contact exploration. An robotic arm equipped with an robotic and sequentially performs lower and upper boundary detec-tion, top-surface scanning, and side-surface scanning. The observed contact heights and the distribution of top-surface contacts determine the vertical range and horizontal reference position for subsequent side-surface scanning. The collected contacts and FK-derived normals are augmented through Pseudo-Contact Generation (PCG) and converted into signed surface constraints. A Cross-Section Prior (CSP) defines the GP mean, while multi-scale Gaussian regression estimates a residual field that adjusts this mean to the tactile constraints. The final mesh is extracted from the zero-level set using Marching Cubes [17]. 

B. Robotic Arm & Hand Motion 

An OMY robotic arm equipped with an HX5-D20-MRT hand collects contact observations from the top and side surfaces of an object. The arm controls the hand position and approach direction, while the hand adopts configurations that promote sensing-surface contact. Only the four non-thumb fingers are used for contact exploration, as the shorter thumb has limited reach under the selected scanning configurations. The Tool Center Point (TCP) is the end-effector motion-control reference, distinct from tactile contact points. The object is assumed to remain stationary and accessible along the scanning trajectories. A rule-based policy sequentially performs lower and upper boundary detection, top-surface grid scanning, and layered side-surface scanning. For lower boundary detection, the four fingers flex their distal joints, and the TCP rises in 10 mm increments at a fixed horizontal position. For upper boundary detection, it descends from a non-contact position at the same interval. Tactile signals are measured at each position, and the median 

z-coordinate of active taxels at the first valid contact defines the corresponding boundary height. The height difference determines the number of side-scanning layers. Exploration terminates if no contact is detected within the prescribed interval. After top contact is detected, the hand adopts the top-scanning configuration, and the TCP rises 25 mm and moves 

50 mm along the robot-base +x-axis to establish the grid origin. It then traverses a 7 × 4 grid with 20 mm spacing along −x and 10 mm along +y. At each location, the TCP descends 30 mm from the grid-origin height, records tactile signals for 0.50 s , and returns to the same height. This fixed-displacement approach promotes sensing-surface contact to acquire top-surface contact points. After completing the top scan, the horizontal reference po-sition for side-surface scanning is computed as the arithmetic mean of the valid contact coordinates. E. Cross-Section Prior (CSP) 

Sparse tactile contacts leave distant regions weakly con-strained. We therefore construct a CSP directly from the current tactile contacts. We assess whether the observed layers exhibit similar radial profiles and cross-sectional sizes. When this consistency supports a shared cross-section, the valid layer contours are enclosed by a common polygonal envelope, which defines a geometric GP mean over the ob-served vertical extent with margins. The polygon is estimated from the input contacts rather than selected from a predefined collection of shapes. Reliable measured side contacts and generated side pseudo-contacts provide the layerwise xy contours used to construct the CSP. Non-side measurements and top pseudo-contacts contribute to its vertical extent. CONCLUSIONS This paper presented a training-free and vision-free frame-work for 3D object reconstruction using tactile sensing alone. Contacts acquired from 3×3 pressure arrays on a multi-finger robotic hand mounted on a robotic arm are integrated using GPIS to reconstruct continuous object geometry without requiring visual observations, object class information, or learned generative shape priors, while the exploration policy adapts the number of scan layers to the measured object height. Experiments on six real objects demonstrated the feasibility of the proposed approach in a real-world robotic setting, while multi-finger sensing enables multiple contact measurements to be acquired within each exploratory action. Because no generative model is involved, reconstruction is completed in 49.99 s from the accumulated contacts. Future work includes replacing the fixed exploration sequence with an adaptive touch policy driven by surface uncertainty, and improving reconstruction quality from sparser contact sets so that fewer exploratory actions are required. By enabling both geometric perception and physical interaction on the same robotic platform, this work provides a practical foundation for reconstructing and manipulating unknown objects when visual sensing is unavailable.
