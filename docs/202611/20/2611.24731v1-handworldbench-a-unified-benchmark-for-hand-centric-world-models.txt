## Abstract 

Recent advances in world models are opening new possi-bilities for fine-grained embodied tasks, with hand–object interaction offering a particularly demanding setting. Mod-eling these interactions involves capturing how hand actions change objects and how the resulting states persist over time. Yet systematic evaluation tailored to these hand-centric ca-

## 1. Introduction 

Interactive world models are evolving from open-world ex-ploration toward fine-grained embodied interaction. Ad-vances in generating action-controllable environments have established a foundation for predicting visual experience under user intervention [2, 17]. In embodied tasks, this prediction must capture how an agent’s actions change the objects it engages with. Hand–object interaction brings this requirement into focus: hands support varied contacts and transformations, connecting egocentric experience to dexter-ous manipulation. This connection has motivated growing interest in hand-centric world models. A growing body of work extends world modeling from locomotion and robotic-arm manipulation to hand-centric interaction. Recent methods use meshes, joints, and poses to control hand motion [5, 6, 25, 30]. Contact and scene modeling support object interaction and continued manip-ulation [7, 11, 12], with applications to data synthesis and digital teleoperation [6, 31]. Yet these advances are largely assessed through model-specific tasks and validation targets, making progress across methods difficult to compare. This growing field still lacks a unified benchmark for systematic evaluation across tasks and embodiments. Existing video and world-model benchmarks evaluate vi-sual quality, interaction and navigation control, and embod-ied utility [4, 8, 13, 29]. Manipulation-focused benchmarks further assess physical executability, human-to-robot trans-fer, and agreement with real executions [3, 9, 20] (Table 1). These benchmarks provide evidence about interaction quality and task outcomes; comparing hand-centric models further requires resolving how the prescribed motion produces those outcomes. Small finger contacts can determine an opera-tion’s outcome, while occlusion requires object states and previously observed details to remain consistent when they reappear. Coordinated finger motions also make control fi-delity consequential: similar overall hand movements can differ in joint configuration, contact location, and object response. Evaluation must distinguish errors in following the supplied control from errors in the resulting interaction, using correspondences between native control inputs and generated motion. These judgments must extend across hu-man hands and simulated and real dexterous hands with different morphologies and kinematics, as well as operations with different completion and persistence requirements. Ex-isting protocols have yet to organize these requirements into a common evaluation of hand-centric world models. We introduce HandWorldBench , a unified benchmark for hand-centric world models across human hands, simu-lated dexterous hands, and real dexterous hands. Our data curation pipeline yields 20,500 high-quality egocentric clips from over 600 hours of footage. From this corpus, we manu-ally select 500 clips spanning seven task families to form the evaluation set. To compare methods across embodiments, we establish shared structural semantics and spatiotemporal correspondences, then adapt the resulting controls to each model’s native interface. Task annotations connect action requirements to evidence of object-state changes, subsequent persistence, and the preservation of previously observed de-tails. Our six-dimensional protocol combines visual and geometric measurements with these task-specific judgments to evaluate generated interactions under shared scoring crite-ria. Our evaluation of eleven models shows that stronger hand-pose control can coexist with weak object-state evolution and persistence. Several specialized hand-control models also underperform general-purpose text/image-to-video genera-tors on dexterous-hand tasks. These findings identify a gap between motion control and interaction prediction: realizing a prescribed movement does not ensure that its consequences are modeled coherently, and specialization in hand control does not ensure robust performance across embodiments. Our contributions are: • A unified benchmark. HandWorldBench establishes com-mon interaction tasks and six evaluation dimensions for comparing world models across human hands and simu-lated and real dexterous hands. • A data curation pipeline and corpus. We construct high-quality interaction data through quality screening and signal-alignment checks, producing 20,500 clips from which we manually select 500 for evaluation. • A systematic evaluation. We systematically evaluate 11 representative models under a unified protocol, charac-terizing their performance across hand-centric tasks and embodiments.

Video-quality bench-marks organize perceptual, temporal, and semantic diagnos-tics, extending to anatomical, physical, and commonsense assessments of plausible world behavior [8, 32]. ## 3. HandWorldBench 

We construct HandWorldBench by curating egocentric in-teraction data across embodiments (Section 3.1), organiz-ing model inputs through unified control interfaces, and establishing task annotations (Section 3.2). We evaluate the generated interactions with seventeen metrics across six di-mensions (Section 3.3), then aggregate these measurements into category scores and HWBS CORE for model comparison (Section 3.4). 

3.1. Dataset Construction 

Figure 2(a) outlines data curation from our in-house human-hand recordings, HandsOn [16] (simulated dexterous hands), and our own real dexterous-hand recordings. Starting from over 600 hours of footage across the three embodiments, we validate video readability, timestamps, and alignment with available hand and camera signals. Screening checks camera stability, hand visibility, and image quality. Visual checks assess blur, exposure, and hand size. We extract continuous clips of 80 n + 1 frames ( n ≥ 1), screen for overlapping sources and visual duplicates, and check exported frame counts and signal correspondence. Source intervals and available validity masks accompany each clip throughout processing. From 20,500 curated clips, we manually select 500 for evaluation using the criteria in Appendix A.3; 20,000 remain for non-test use. Evaluation cases cover seven task families and 22 task categories (composition, durations, and coverage in Appendices D.1–D.3), including material shap-ing, tools and mechanisms, and object handling (Figure 2, right). 

3.2. Control Interfaces and Annotations 

We unify control through native-signal parsing, structural normalization, spatiotemporal alignment, and model-specific encoding (Figure 2(b)). Human-hand inputs provide native 3D keypoints, left/right validity, camera calibration, and visual-inertial odometry poses. For dexterous hands, forward kinematics uses joint states, palm poses, and robot assets to obtain landmarks and surfaces, which are transformed into camera coordinates. Task-specific evaluation uses reference-defined cases and their annotated temporal windows. Individual-metric checks are described in Appendix J.3.

4.2. Overall Performance 

Appearance and interaction outcomes. JointCon-trolVideo leads with a HWBS CORE of 55.51, followed by HunyuanVideo 1.5 at 52.22 and Wan2.2 at 51.65 (Table 2). Appendix I.2 specifies score and paired-difference confidence-interval estimation. HunyuanVideo 1.5 leads visual quality and appearance consistency, while Joint-ControlVideo gains its advantage in instruction following, physical consistency, and temporal coherence (Figure 3). Photometric Consistency favors HunyuanVideo 1.5 (0.740 versus 0.612), while State Regulation favors JointCon-trolVideo (0.425 versus 0.240). These measures test retaining visible scene properties and producing changed states that remain available to subsequent actions. Selecting for appearance preservation therefore favors a different model from selecting for persistent manipulation outcomes. 

Motion control and state prediction. The clearest separa-tion occurs between variables constrained by motion inputs and the object changes that must be inferred from them. Hand2World leads Pose Control at 0.529 but scores 0.021 on State Progression and 0.000 on State Regulation. Joint-ControlVideo has lower Pose Control of 0.416, yet reaches 0.385 and 0.425 on the two state metrics. The separation extends to object motion: EgoSim leads Trajectory Accuracy at 0.276, while its Evolution Correctness is 0.057. Geomet-ric conditions specify how the hand should move; they leave the model to infer how contact changes the object and how that change persists. The ranking reversal separates accurate tracking of explicit motion inputs from prediction of their object-state consequences. 

4.3. Cross-Embodiment and Task Analysis 

Cross-embodiment performance. JointControlVideo’s pose-tracking advantage over HunyuanVideo 1.5 persists across dexterous-hand settings, while their ordering in state 7prediction reverses. On simulated dexterous hands, Joint-ControlVideo scores 0.335 in Pose Control, compared with 0.164 for HunyuanVideo 1.5. HunyuanVideo 1.5 neverthe-less leads Evolution Correctness and State Progression at 0.323 and 0.399, versus 0.304 and 0.364. On real dexterous hands, JointControlVideo leads both state metrics at 0.443 and 0.475, against 0.228 and 0.270. Appendix I.1 describes controlled checks of visual context, evaluation horizon, and reference access for these comparisons. The advantage in tracking a geometric input thus persists more consistently than the advantage in predicting its effects on objects. 

Performance across task families. Task-level results fur-ther locate the gap between reproducing an operation and re-alizing its defining object relation. In Assemble & Connect, PAM leads Action Adherence at 0.552, whereas Wan2.2 leads Evolution Correctness at 0.652. For assembly, match-ing a manipulation pattern can leave the required connec-tion between parts unrealized; the two rankings capture this distinction between recognizable behavior and its physical result. Move & Arrange also exposes dependence on em-bodiment within one task family. On real dexterous hands, JointControlVideo leads action adherence and evolution cor-rectness at 0.563 and 0.682. On simulated hands, it ties Hun-yuanVideo 1.5 in action adherence, while HunyuanVideo 1.5 leads evolution correctness. The relative advantage of spe-cialized control therefore depends on the embodiment–task combination and the object relation that defines successful execution. 

4.4. Interaction Diagnostics 

Motion and outcome rankings diverge even on matched tasks: Spearman correlations across the eleven models are ρ = −0.34 for Pose Control–State Progression and 

ρ = −0.23 for Trajectory Accuracy–Evolution Correctness (Figure 4(a,b)). Task-bootstrap and leave-one-model-out checks are specified in Appendix I.1. State Progression and State Regulation share a task-achievement requirement, reflected in their strong correlation of ρ = 0 .89 (Figure 4(c)). State Regulation gates retention credit on state establishment, so both scores depend on reach-ing the required state. Yet EgoSim and CogVideoX1.5 have similar progression scores of 0.175 and 0.164, while their regulation scores are 0.036 and 0.127. The shared prerequi-site brings the overall rankings closer; subsequent retention still separates models with comparable progress. Entity similarity and historical consistency operate at dif-ferent levels of detail ( ρ = 0 .38 ; Figure 4(d)). On this matched subset, JointControlVideo and HunyuanVideo 1.5 have similar entity scores but different memory retention (Appendix I.1). Similar overall features can coexist with changes to local attributes established earlier in the interac-tion. Memory evaluation therefore asks whether the current object remains faithful to its observed history. 

4.5. Alignment with Human Judgments 

We collect 20,244 preference judgments from 153 partici-pants, covering 2,911 video pairs generated by eleven models across three egocentric embodiment settings. Each judgment records a video-pair preference for overall performance or one evaluation dimension. Figure 5 shows strong alignment between automatic scores and human preferences at the model level: Spear-man's ρ is 0.964 for HWBS CORE and ranges from 0.934 to 0.991 across the six dimensions. At the video-pair level, preference agreement is 81.7% for overall performance and ranges from 76.9% to 84.1% across the six dimensions. Phys-ical Consistency achieves the highest pairwise agreement of 84.1%. Response handling and task-bootstrap confidence intervals are detailed in Appendices J.2–J.3. 

4.6. Qualitative Analysis 

During soleplate wiping, CogVideoX1.5 retains the perfora-tion pattern, whereas DWM replaces the holes with irregular markings (Figure 6(a)). Wiping changes contact and visibil-ity while the underlying pattern should persist. The contrast exposes a failure to keep the current appearance consistent with local properties established by the object’s history, even as the manipulation continues. Ball placement separates embodiment preservation from the required object relation (Figure 6(b)). JointControlVideo places the ball in the bowl while retaining the dexterous hands. PAM and DWM introduce human hands, and the ball remains outside the bowl in the last displayed frame. Continued hand motion accompanies both a change of act-ing embodiment and an unmet placement goal, showing how these two requirements can fail within one generated interaction. Carton lifting tests continuity beyond the manipulated object (Figure 6(c)). JointControlVideo lifts the carton but distorts the background beneath it; Hand2World replaces the dexterous hands with human hands in later frames. As the action reveals more of the scene, the generated back-ground must remain compatible with the surrounding struc-ture, and the manipulator must retain its supplied embodi-ment throughout the operation. Further examples appear in Appendices K.1–K.2. 

## 5. Conclusion 

We presented HandWorldBench, a unified benchmark that brings human hands, simulated dexterous hands, and real dexterous hands into a common framework of interaction tasks, control interfaces, and evaluation criteria. A curation pipeline yields 20,500 high-quality egocentric clips, from which 500 interactions covering seven task families are manually selected for evaluation. Across 8eleven models, accurate tracking of control signals does not consistently translate into the object changes and persistent states that those signals should induce. The benefits of specialized control vary with embodiment and task, while general-purpose video generators achieve stronger interaction outcomes in several comparisons. Strong agreement with human judgments supports the evaluation at both the model-ranking and video-pair levels. Hand-WorldBench provides a common basis for assessing whether progress in motion control extends to interaction predic-tion that remains coherent across embodiments and tasks.
