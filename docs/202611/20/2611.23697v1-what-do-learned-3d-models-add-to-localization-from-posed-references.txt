# Abstract 

Localizing a query from a few posed reference images avoids building a 3D point map. We ask what the cam-era predictions of learned 3D models add to geometric esti-mation from the same references, and where failures arise. Across eight learned models and variants and five test sets, we compare reference budgets, swap pose components, and intervene on the references a model sees. At fine pose-error thresholds, geometric estimation with one additional refer-ence is significantly more accurate than every model on two references on Cambridge and NAVER B1 and never signif-icantly worse, alone or as a fallback behind a shared ac-ceptance gate; with the same three references, the more ac-curate approach depends on the scene. The gain from the learned translation magnitude depends on reference selec-tion: it improves seven of the eight models on 12-Scenes with the spread-out pairs we select, but none of the five we test with the two top-ranked retrieved references. Where retrieval also returns references from other locations, at NAVER Gangnam Station, models fail at different stages. A robust alignment brings Pi3X and MapAnything within 3.3 pp of adding references as needed. In controlled tests, aligning on the nearby references largely restores VGGT, Pi3X and MapAnything but not Depth Anything 3, and pose-conditioned Pi3X recovers only if the far poses are masked and left out of its input-pose normalization. 

# 1. Introduction 

Visual localization estimates a query camera pose. Many systems match image features to a prebuilt 3D point map [27, 28]. A structureless alternative estimates the pose from retrieved reference images with known poses and in-trinsics [25, 40], avoiding the need to maintain a global point map. The remaining choices are how to estimate the query pose and when to retrieve additional references. Geometric estimation uses query–reference correspon-dences, local triangulation, and PnP. Learned 3D models such as VGGT [34], Depth Anything 3 [21], π3 [37], and MapAnything [15] predict camera poses and 3D points from sparse images, and recent localizers combine such predictions with known reference poses [8, 10, 14, 39]. Such models might reduce reliance on geometric estima-tion or additional references. Yet reported comparisons vary in matcher, reference budget, and pose acceptance policy, while whole-pose accuracy obscures which learned compo-nents help. We therefore ask what learned prediction adds beyond geometry and whether another reference is more useful. The essential matrix determines rotation and transla-tion direction, but not magnitude; a second posed reference constrains magnitude, allowing it to be substituted indepen-dently. Additional references can be retrieved when the ini-tial pose is rejected.

# 2. Related Work 

Localization without a 3D map. Structureless methods localize a query from posed references alone: they inter-sect translation directions estimated by two-view geome-try across multiple references [40], or build local models on the fly from the retrieved references [25, 29]. Panek et al . [25] survey such methods and show that they can ap-proach methods with a 3D map. Earlier work studies how the number of retrieved reference images affects local tri-angulation [13, 26]. In separate work on combining abso-lute and relative poses, Panek et al . [24] compare structure-based and structureless methods as the database is subsam-pled. Given the retrieved references with their known poses, MapAnything is less accurate, close only at the loosest tol-erance; PnP on matches lifted with its depth or point maps stays far below geometric estimation at fine tolerances, and a hybrid that can also pick a structureless pose stays below that pose alone. That comparison covers one model on one test set with 5–20 retrieved references. Like earlier con-trolled comparisons in localization [6, 29, 30], we control the references, test eight models and variants on five test sets, analyze individual pose components, and compare a learned prediction with an additional reference. 

Metric scale from learned depth. Two-view geometry gives the rotation and the translation direction but not its length. The feature-matching baselines of the Map-free benchmark take the length from predicted depth [2], MicKey predicts matches in metric camera coordinates [3], and MADPose fits the relative pose together with the scale and shift of predicted depth maps [38]. Our learned-scale hybrid pose estimate (Sec. 3) takes only the length from a learned 3D model, to isolate the effect of the learned trans-2lation magnitude. 

Learned 3D models for localization. DUSt3R [35] and MASt3R [20] predict 3D points for pairs of reference im-ages, aligned afterwards; VGGT [34], Depth Anything 3 [21], π3 [37] and MapAnything [15] predict cameras and 3D points for a few reference images in one pass, and Depth Anything 3, MapAnything and π3’s successor Pi3X [36] can also take known poses as input. Localizers built on such models regress the pose relative to retrieved reference im-ages [10], are trained to localize from posed reference im-ages or their features [4, 7, 8], or lift learned relative poses with the known reference poses [14, 39]. They are usually compared with other learned localizers or with pipelines that use a 3D map, at a fixed number of reference images. We compare them with geometric estimation on the same reference images, the geometric solvers sharing one set of matches, and vary the number of reference images. 

Selecting views for reconstruction. RobustVGGT [12] shows that views of other scenes degrade VGGT’s recon-struction and rejects them using its internal features. With posed references, we ask where the error enters: in con-trolled tests, mostly the alignment for VGGT, Pi3X and Ma-pAnything, the prediction for Depth Anything 3, and the pose input for pose-conditioned Pi3X (Sec. 7). 

Reliability of learned pose estimates. Inlier counts are the usual sign that a geometric pose is reliable, and their image coverage adds to it [11]. For learned 3D models, GeoCond [1] predicts relative-pose reliability from condi-tioning cues and model-stability signals, using input-order variation as one training target. Concurrent work exam-ines VGGT features to identify failed relative poses [32]. These works assess reliability without posed reference im-ages. With posed references, we examine whether learned translation magnitude is reliable, how much selecting a cor-rect estimate would improve localization, and whether ad-ditional references benefit the learned model or geometric estimation more. 

# 3. Comparison Protocol 

Common experimental setup. The database holds refer-ence images with known poses and intrinsics; no 3D points are stored. MegaLoc [5] retrieves 30 reference images per query, and a D-optimal design on their camera positions, which favors spread-out cameras, selects K of them ( K

counts the posed reference images, not the query; K = 2 

unless stated). The rule was fixed beforehand and makes two-reference geometry harder than the two top-ranked re-trieved references do (Sec. 5). Geometric estimation and every learned model receive the same selected reference images. All geometric pose estimates use the same LoMa matches [23] (Sec. 4 also uses an older matcher), and one shared acceptance gate, based on pose uncertainty, decides which queries need another pose estimate. Additional ref-erences beyond the initial selection are introduced only in the budget analyses of Secs. 4 and 5 and in Secs. 6 and 7. Throughout, geometric estimation denotes pose es-timation from feature correspondences, including those ob-tained with a learned matcher. Learned prediction denotes the camera estimates produced by a learned 3D model. 

Pose components. Let A be the reference image with the most essential-matrix inliers and B the other reference im-age. The essential matrix between the query and A gives the query rotation R and a unit translation direction d from 

A toward the query, but not its translation magnitude λ:

C = CA + λd. (1) The geometrically estimated translation magnitude is the one-point scale supported by the raw matches to B. Alearned model predicts camera centers c in its own frame. The known baseline between the two reference cameras supplies the metric scale, and the model contributes the ra-tio of the query–reference distance to that baseline; their product is the learned translation magnitude 

λlearned = s∥cq − cA∥, s = ∥CA − CB ∥∥cA − cB ∥ . (2) The learned-scale hybrid pose estimate keeps the geo-metric (R, d) and takes only λ from the learned model. The reverse swap takes the rotation and the translation direc-tion together from the learned model and fits the geometri-cally estimated translation magnitude again; it does not sep-arate the rotation from the direction. With additional refer-ence images, the learned scale is the median over reference-image pairs. 

Solvers and the shared acceptance gate. Geometric es-timation follows local triangulation [25]; here, the matches to each reference image are filtered by its essential ma-trix, query keypoints shared by two reference images give tracks, and the triangulated points give 2D–3D matches for RANSAC PnP and refinement. The position uncertainty σ

is the largest standard deviation of a Gauss–Newton covari-ance scaled by the residuals, with the triangulated points treated as exact. The shared acceptance gate accepts a pose when κσ < τ , with κ = 5 and the position tolerance τ

(a larger κ is stricter); it rejects cases with fewer than six points or no PnP pose. σ measures the fit, not the chance of success, so the gate can accept incorrect pose estimates. Un-less the stricter initial gate (κ = 15 ) is used (Secs. 6 and 7), 3accepted queries keep the PnP pose, so gated comparisons change only the pose estimate for the rejected queries and measure what it adds there, not its accuracy alone; Secs. 4 to 6 also report ungated results. For rejected queries, the classical pose estimate is PoseLib’s 5+1-point generalized relative pose [18] with LO-RANSAC and bundle adjust-ment (4-pixel threshold, chosen on development queries); Tabs. 1 and 2 use it there. 

Learned models. We analyze VGGT in greatest detail. Seven more models and variants receive the same refer-ence images in the same order: Depth Anything 3 [21], Pi3X [36, 37] with and without reference-pose condition-ing, MapAnything [15] with and without reference-pose conditioning, MASt3R [20] and Reloc3r [10]. Each model runs with its published weights at its own input resolution, without task-specific training; for pose-conditioned Pi3X, we zero the masked query’s pose features, which would oth-erwise be NaN. Pi3X normalizes the input poses by their mean distance from the first view, masked views included; we place the query’s placeholder at the references’ mean distance, so that this scale equals the one from the refer-ences alone. Models that accept intrinsics use the same intrinsics as geometric estimation; upright NAVER queries are padded rather than cropped. Unless the model places the query itself, its predicted cameras are aligned to the known reference poses in closed form: rotation from the predicted against the known reference orientations, scale from the ref-erence baselines, then translation. VGGT’s settings were chosen on development queries, the others’ are defaults. 

Data and evaluation. We sort 12-Scenes [33] queries by scene and split alternating frames into development (2,822) and held-out (2,819) sets. The held-out set evaluates new frames from the same rooms. The other test sets cover different scenes: 7-Scenes [31] (every 20th test frame, 850 queries), Cambridge Landmarks [16, 17] (1,918), and NAVER Gangnam Station [19], floors B2 (916) and B1 (2,620), where the queries are the validation split and the database is the mapping split. We use structure-from-motion (SfM) reference poses [6] for 7-Scenes and dataset poses for 12-Scenes. A query is correct when its position and rotation errors are within 5 cm and 5◦ on 12-Scenes and 7-Scenes, and 25 cm and 2◦ on Cambridge and NAVER; 

accuracy is localization recall, differences are in percent-age points (pp), and a query without a pose estimate counts as incorrect. Paired 95% intervals come from a bootstrap over scenes, or over 20-second blocks of capture time on NAVER, which has one scene per floor. Cambridge, 7-Scenes and 12-Scenes have only 5, 7 and 12 scenes to re-sample, so their intervals may be too narrow.

# 4. Sensitivity to the Comparison Protocol 

Before analyzing individual pose components, we examine how the comparison protocol changes the relative perfor-mance of VGGT and geometric estimation. Table 1 uses VGGT’s query pose aligned to the same known reference poses and varies one experimental choice at a time. 

The matcher. With SuperPoint and LightGlue [9, 22] and two references, VGGT, evaluated alone, leads geometric es-timation on four of five test sets, by up to 33.3 pp. With LoMa on the same references and evaluated alone, VGGT retains an advantage only on 12-Scenes; on the other sets, it shows no statistically significant gain or performs worse. 

The number of reference images. Increasing the bud-get to four references with LoMa favors geometric estima-4tion: evaluated alone, VGGT is less accurate on all five test sets, and behind the shared acceptance gate it is not signif-icantly better on any. The four-reference selection contains the original pair for 98–100% of queries, except NAVER B2 (91%), where few of the retrieved references depict the most likely location and the selection can draw from all 30. 

Evaluation policy. When evaluated alone, VGGT is 9.0 pp ahead of PnP on 12-Scenes but 27.3 pp behind on Cam-bridge. A shared acceptance gate that retains accepted PnP estimates reduces the Cambridge difference to −1.1 pp, while leaving the 12-Scenes difference at +8.5 pp. Re-placing PnP for rejected queries with PoseLib’s generalized relative pose further reduces the 12-Scenes difference to +2.4 pp; on NAVER B1, the difference changes from +3.7 to −0.2 pp. Thus the gate and fallback determine which queries contribute to the measured gain. Overall, an older matcher and fewer references fa-vor VGGT’s predictions; pose-conditioned Pi3X, evaluated alone, still outperforms PnP with LoMa and two references on 12-Scenes and both NAVER floors (Tab. 1). Evalua-tion alone and the shared acceptance gate measure differ-ent quantities: behind the gate both keep the same accepted PnP pose, so the difference counts only what each estimate adds for rejected queries, and with a classical estimate there all fifteen differences in Tab. 1 are closer to zero than alone. The remaining comparisons therefore fix LoMa, specify the reference budget, and apply one shared acceptance gate.

The learned parts come from the model’s cameras aligned to the two reference images, with the pose estimate placed at reference image A. For the other three models, the learned magnitude also improves 12-Scenes for MASt3R and pose-conditioned MapAnything (+2.0 and +3.1 pp), not for Reloc3r.

Comparison with other classical pose solvers. On held-out 12-Scenes, VGGT’s learned-scale hybrid estimate (92.5% behind the gate) exceeds an oracle choosing the better of PoseLib and our refined decomposition with the true pose (90.1%). Selecting between the magnitudes. On held-out 12-Scenes, even an oracle choice between the two magnitudes adds only 0.7 pp over always using the learned-scale hybrid estimate. We next ask whether an additional reference is better used to compute a new pose.

It narrows at looser thresholds, is no longer clear on 12-Scenes at 10 cm, 5◦, and disappears or reverses on NAVER at 1 m, 5◦ and 2 m, 10 ◦. Against VGGT, it remains when references are taken in retrieval order rather than selected geometrically. Matching the added reference takes 26 ms and solving the pose 32 ms on held-out 12-Scenes, against 120 ms for one VGGT pass. 

Adding references after gate rejection. The procedure we evaluate (add refs) adds references as needed before us-ing a learned fallback. It keeps the two-reference pose only 6when the stricter initial gate ( κ = 15 , set on development queries) accepts it. Otherwise, it adds the next retrieved references one at a time, keeping them, until the shared ac-ceptance gate accepts a pose or five references are in use (Tab. 3c). If none is accepted, it keeps the two-reference pose when the shared acceptance gate accepted it, and oth-erwise returns VGGT’s learned-scale hybrid estimate. The comparator uses that hybrid estimate as soon as the shared acceptance gate rejects. Re-checking the two-reference pose with the stricter gate and adding references improves recall over this compara-tor by 4.9 to 13.9 pp on held-out 12-Scenes, Cambridge, and both NAVER floors, and slightly on 7-Scenes (Tab. 3c; differences taken before rounding). It uses 0.24–1.26 ad-ditional references per query on average, depending on the test set. The stricter initial gate matters: the shared accep-tance gate accepts incorrect two-reference poses for 28.2% of all Cambridge queries and 21.7% of NAVER B2 queries. 

Incorrect estimates and fallback behavior. Accepted poses can be incorrect: the gate tests pose fit, and this pro-cedure still returns an incorrect pose for 39.1% of Cam-bridge queries and 40.6% of NAVER B2 queries. Trian-gulating from all five references for every query is 0.8–7.1 pp more accurate (Tab. 4) than adding references as needed, but uses three additional references for every query instead of at most 1.26 on average.

References from other locations. Widely spread refer-ences usually include some from other locations, which the models place inconsistently: after the alignment over all five references, Depth Anything 3 and Pi3X without pose con-ditioning leave errors of 66–71% of reference spread on B2 and 59–60% on B1. A robust alignment changes only the references used to estimate the alignment, not the predicted cameras, and needs no true pose: it aligns to each pair of references, keeps the pair most other references agree with (within a quarter of the median reference distance and 10 ◦,the middle of three settings), and refits on those. Pi3X and MapAnything without pose input are then within 3.3 pp of add refs on both floors, and VGGT and Depth Anything 3 are lower by 4.8–8.6 pp (Tab. 4), and Pi3X keeps part of its recall in the most dispersed third (Fig. 2). Pose-conditioned Pi3X changes little (14.5 and 13.8 pp lower); MASt3R re-mains 18–28 pp lower. 

Spatially compact reference subsets. Among the ten three-reference subsets of the five, the most compact one (smallest mean distance to its centroid, from the known ref-erence positions) narrows pose-conditioned Pi3X’s gap to add refs from about 15 to 2.5 pp on both floors (Tab. 4). Where all five show one place, the compact three trail add refs by 4.1 and 11.3 pp on 7-Scenes and Cambridge. The most compact three exceed the most dispersed by 26.6 and 20.7 pp on B2 and B1, 14.4 and 12.5 pp more than for geometric estimation (intervals above zero). A controlled test keeps the retained references fixed and changes only what enters inference (Tab. 5): adding two far references and aligning on the near three after inference restores Pi3X, VGGT and MapAnything to within 4.2 pp of the three alone but leaves Depth Anything 3 28–35 pp below, so the far im-ages also degrade its prediction, and pose-conditioned Pi3X 78–81 pp below; given the far poses, pose-conditioned Ma-pAnything also drops by 66–74 pp. Masking the far poses restores pose-conditioned Pi3X only if the near three alone also normalize its input poses. At Cambridge, given the far poses, pose-conditioned Pi3X drops by 16 pp. 

Implications. At NAVER, most of the deficit of most models thus comes from references at other places, and in our controlled tests the failure stages need different han-dling. For VGGT, Pi3X and MapAnything, use alignment robust to inconsistent references; Depth Anything 3 also needs them left out before inference. For pose-conditioned Pi3X as evaluated, a reference whose pose is masked must also be left out of the normalization of its input poses. The robust alignment and the compact three choose references without the true pose; the controlled tests use it only to com-pose the sets, not as a selection rule. 

# 8. Limitations 

Our conclusions are limited to the tested solvers, matcher, alignment, reference selection rule, and the camera predic-tions of eight learned models and variants, evaluated with published weights and the inference adaptations of Sec. 3, without task-specific training. Pose-conditioned Pi3X’s training data are not published, whereas the reported train-ing data for π3 exclude our test sets. The geometry-based pipeline uses a learned matcher, so removing the learned 3D model does not remove all learning. The held-out 12-Scenes queries come from the same rooms as the develop-ment queries, and the NAVER result comes from two floors of one station, with a controlled check at Cambridge. Our oracle ceilings cover only selection among the estimates we computed. Analyses that use the true pose explain failures and are not rules, and differences whose intervals include zero do not prove two policies equal. 

# 9. Conclusion 

At fine thresholds, geometric estimation with one more ref-erence is significantly more accurate than all eight learned models on two references on Cambridge and NAVER B1 and never significantly worse; with the same three refer-ences, the more accurate approach depends on the scene. The gain from the learned translation magnitude depends on reference selection. References from other locations cause failures at separable stages: aligning on the nearby refer-ences largely restores VGGT, Pi3X and MapAnything but not Depth Anything 3, and pose-conditioned Pi3X needs their poses masked and left out of its input-pose normal-ization.
