## Abstract 

In contact-rich dexterous manipulation, occlusion by the fingers and interacting objects obscures local geom-etry, preventing visual observations from fully captur-ing contact locations. Visually similar scenes may also differ subtly in the relative positions of interacting ob-jects, leading to different contact states. We propose 

3DTouch , a tactile-geometric conditioning approach for vision-language-action (VLA) models. 3DTouch constructs a shared 3D visuo-tactile representation by expressing RGB-D scene points and dense five-finger tactile measure-ments in the robot-base frame, preserving each taxel’s spa-tial location and force vector. Hand–object and object– object relations derived from hand landmarks and pose-aligned reconstructed surfaces explicitly describe the rel-ative arrangement of interacting entities. Together, these representations condition the VLA action expert on spatially localized contact forces and relative surface geometry, pro-viding explicit cues for contact adjustment and object align-ment. We evaluate 3DTouch on five real-world contact-rich dexterous manipulation tasks using a UR7e robot arm equipped with an XHand dexterous hand. 3DTouch im-proves the average success rate by 42 percentage points over the corresponding VLA baseline and by 20 percent-age points over the strongest evaluated alternative. Abla-tion studies reveal the complementary benefits of scene ge-ometry and spatial tactile feedback, with further gains from shared spatial grounding and explicit geometric relations. 

## 1. Introduction 

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have demonstrated promising capabilities in general robotic manipulation through large-scale pretraining on robot manipulation data [1, 2, 16]. However, contact-rich dexterous manipulation re-mains challenging, as small deviations in object alignment or contact conditions can lead to task failure. Similar visual observations may correspond to different physical interac-tion states, requiring the model to refine actions according to additional contact information. Moreover, visual obser-vations alone usually lack critical local manipulation geom-etry, especially under occlusion which commonly occurs in dexterous manipulation. These limitations show the neces-sity for fusing complementary geometric and tactile infor-mation into VLA models to support precise action control. Recent studies have explored incorporating 3D spatial information and tactile feedback into robot policies. 3D-aware VLA methods mainly adopt two schemes to inte-grate spatial information, using 3D positional encoding to fuse depth with visual features [24], or conditioning the action expert with separately encoded geometric features extracted from 3D reconstruction [36] or observed point clouds [4]. Additionally, tactile-aware VLA methods in-corporate tactile feedback into action generation by encod-ing tactile data as additional input tokens alongside visual-language tokens [12, 32]. 3D visuo-tactile methods further represent visual observations and gripper tactile measure-ments as 3D points for visuo-tactile fusion, preserving their spatial relationships [11]. Despite these advances, two key limitations remain for contact-rich dexterous manipulation. First, although 3D visuo-tactile methods have explored unified visual-tactile 3D representations for policy learning, how to effectively encode spatially grounded multi-finger tactile feedback for pretrained VLA models remains underexplored. In partic-ular, without explicitly associating multi-finger taxel force measurements with their corresponding 3D locations in scenes, the model may struggle to relate local contact con-ditions of the hand to surrounding objects. Second, down-sampled scene point clouds are often too coarse and heavily occluded during five-finger grasping to capture small inter-action clearances. To address these challenges, we propose 3DTouch , a 3D conditioning approach to enhance pretrained VLAs with spatially encoded tactile representations and explicit inter-action relations for contact-rich dexterous manipulation. As shown in Fig. 1, 3DTouch consists of two complementary modules. 3D Tactile Grounding (3DTG) represents RGB-D scene point clouds and dense five-finger tactile measure-ments in a shared robot-base coordinate system. We evaluate 3DTouch on five real-world contact-rich dexterous manipulation tasks using a UR7e robot arm equipped with an XHand dexterous hand. 3DTouch im-proves the average success rate by 42 percentage points over the corresponding VLA baseline and 20 percentage points over the strongest tactile-aware VLA. Ablation stud-ies demonstrate the complementary benefits of scene geom-etry and spatial tactile feedback, with shared spatial ground-ing improving the average success rate by 11 percentage points over hand-local tactile encoding. Explicit interaction geometry further improves the average success rate by 12 percentage points, showing improvements in target local-ization and grasp stability during dexterous manipulation. Our main contributions are summarized as follows: • We propose 3D Tactile Grounding (3DTG), which en-codes dense five-finger tactile measurements and RGB-D scene geometry in a shared 3D coordinate system, pro-viding spatially localized tactile feedback. • We introduce Geometric Relation Modeling (GRM), which constructs structured hand–object and object– object representations from hand landmarks and 3D ob-ject geometry, explicitly encoding task-relevant relative displacements and distances for dexterous action control. • Extensive experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of 3DTouch on real-world dexterous manipulation tasks, with ablations validating the benefits of multimodal spa-tial grounding and explicit geometric relations, particu-larly with stronger target localization and grasp stability. 22. Related Work 

Vision-Language-Action Models. VLA models adapt pre-trained vision-language representations for robotic control. RT-2 discretizes actions into tokens [38], and OpenVLA learns from Open X-Embodiment demonstrations [16]. Moving beyond discrete action tokens, diffusion Trans-formers originally explored in image synthesis [21, 26] have been adapted to robotic control [18]. π0 introduces a flow-matching action expert for continuous action gen-eration [2], while π0.5 uses heterogeneous co-training for open-world generalization [1]. To accelerate action gener-ation, recent drifting-based policies enable native one-step inference [7, 8]. However, conventional VLA models pri-marily use vision and language, sometimes supplemented by proprioception, and lack direct measurements of local contact forces; contact states can remain ambiguous under visual occlusion. We extend π0 with tactile and geometric conditioning while retaining its vision-language input path-way and flow-matching objective. 

3D-Aware Manipulation Policies. RVT processes ren-dered scene views [9], while 3D Diffuser Actor and DP3 condition action diffusion on 3D representations [15, 31]. Transferring pretrained 2D knowledge to point clouds, as explored in open-vocabulary 3D scene understand-ing [22, 33], has also been applied to robotic manipu-lation, with Lift3D employing positional mapping [14]. Within VLAs, SpatialVLA introduces spatial position en-coding and adaptive action discretization [24], PointACT employs multi-scale point–action attention [4], while FAL-CON and GEAR-VLA leverage spatial foundation models to enhance action generation [35, 36]. We complement these approaches with tactile feedback and explicit surface relations computed using object shape priors aligned by es-timated poses. 

Tactile-Aware Policies. Early work uses vision and touch for grasp adjustment [3]. Robot Synesthesia represents bi-nary contact signals as 3D points [30], while TARS learns visuo-tactile affordances through a unified point-cloud rep-resentation [27]. 3D-ViTac associates scalar tactile read-ings with their 3D locations and jointly encodes visual and tactile points [11]. ForceVLA incorporates six-axis force feedback through a force-aware mixture-of-experts [29], while Tactile-VLA combines tactile feedback with hybrid position–force control and tactile-informed reasoning [12]. FAVLA and ReTouch further exploit high-frequency con-tact feedback for reactive action generation [17, 34]. Om-niVTLA aligns tactile representations with visual and lin-guistic semantics through dual-path tactile encoding [6]. MLA and DreamTacVLA establish spatial correspondences by projecting tactile sensor locations into image space for contrastive feature alignment, combined with future sensory prediction [19, 28]. In contrast, our 3D Tactile Ground-ing (3DTG) explicitly represents both the 3D location and force vector of each taxel across multiple fingers in a shared metric frame with scene geometry. Unlike 3D-ViTac’s joint point-cloud encoding, 3DTG separately encodes scene ge-ometry and finger-wise force distributions to accommodate their distinct spatial structures. 

Relational Geometry for Manipulation. ReKep formu-lates manipulation tasks as relational keypoint constraints for trajectory optimization [13], while OmniManip lever-ages reconstructed object meshes and estimated poses to define spatial constraints between object-centric interac-tion primitives [20]. TriRelVLA models entity-level in-teractions through a task-conditioned graph that combines learned object and hand representations with relative posi-tions, poses, and distances [37]. Rather than prescribing de-sired geometric constraints or modeling relations at the en-tity level, our Geometric Relation Modeling (GRM) explic-itly characterizes current interaction geometry at the sur-face level, computing relative displacements and distances between kinematic hand landmarks and nearby object sur-faces, and between representative regions of interacting ob-jects. These fine-grained geometric relations complement spatially grounded multi-finger tactile feedback for contact-rich dexterous manipulation. 

## 3. Method 

In this section, we present the proposed 3DTouch frame-work. Sec. 3.1 provides an overview of the approach. Sec. 3.2 introduces 3D Tactile Grounding (3DTG), which represents scene geometry and tactile feedback in a shared 3D coordinate frame. Sec. 3.3 describes Geometric Rela-tion Modeling (GRM), which constructs hand–object and object–object geometric relations. Finally, Sec. 3.4 details how these representations condition the pretrained VLA ac-tion expert and how the model is trained. 

3.1. Overview 

We propose a method for contact-rich dexterous manipula-tion that incorporates two complementary types of 3D in-formation into a pretrained vision-language-action (VLA) model. These include (i) scene point clouds and spatially grounded tactile observations, and (ii) explicit interaction geometry among task-relevant entities. As illustrated in Fig. 2, our method consists of two modules: 3D Tactile Grounding and Geometric Relation Modeling . The 3D Tactile Grounding module transforms RGB-D observations into scene point clouds in the robot base frame and ex-tracts local and global scene features. In the same coordi-nate frame, it encodes the spatial positions and force mea-surements of tactile taxels on each finger into tactile to-kens. The Geometric Relation Modeling module directly models relative geometric relations between entities, rather than relying solely on their individual geometric features. 3.2. 3D Tactile Grounding 

Scene Point-Cloud Encoding. We construct scene point clouds from RGB-D observations by back-projecting depth measurements and transforming the resulting points into the robot base frame. After workspace cropping, voxel downsampling, and fixed-size sampling, we obtain Pt =

{(pi, ci, v i)}Ni=1 with N = 4096 , where pi, ci, and vi de-note the 3D position, RGB color, and color-validity indica-tor, respectively. To capture scene geometry at multiple spatial scales, we adopt a PointNet++ [23]-style hierarchical set-abstraction encoder. Two successive FPS-KNN stages progressively sample 256 and 32 centers, with 24 and 16 neighbors per center, respectively. The first stage aggregates relative coor-dinates, RGB features, and color-validity indicators through shared MLPs and mean-max pooling. The second stage fur-ther aggregates first-stage features and relative center coor-dinates, with an MLP encoding of each center’s absolute position in the robot base frame added to the resulting local token, alongside a modality embedding. In parallel, a global branch encodes all input points using centroid-relative coor-dinates and RGB features, followed by global average pool-ing. An MLP encoding of the scene centroid’s absolute po-sition and a modality embedding are then added to obtain the global token. 3.3. Geometric Relation Modeling 

Although scene point clouds capture global geometry and spatial tactile observations provide local contact feedback, their encoded features do not explicitly characterize geo-metric relations between interacting entities. Such relations are particularly important during precise approach, align-ment, and contact adjustment. We therefore construct ex-plicit hand-object (HO) and object-object (OO) relations to complement the sensory representations. 

Geometry Construction. 

We obtain 17 finger keypoints from the current joint states through forward kinematics. Reference object meshes are reconstructed offline from unobstructed obser-vations using SAM 3D [5], from which object surface points are sampled for subsequent geometric relation modeling. During offline training-data preparation, SAM2 [25] gener-ates object segmentation masks, while RGBTrack [10] pro-vides frame-wise 6D pose pseudo-labels to supervise the pose estimator. 

Pose Estimator. To avoid running the SAM2–RGBTrack pipeline online, we train a lightweight pose estimator, as illustrated in Fig. 3. It predicts each object’s 6D pose in the robot-base frame from the current RGB-D obser-vation and sampled reference surface points. Separate lightweight CNNs encode RGB and depth observations, while the reference points are embedded and pooled into an object query that attends to the visual features through cross-attention. We supervise the estimator with offline pose pseudo-labels using translation, rotation, and surface-point alignment losses. The alignment term compares ref-erence points transformed by the predicted and pseudo-labeled poses. The predicted poses map the reference points into the robot-base frame, where they are used alongside hand keypoints to construct hand–object and object–object relations.

## 4. Experiments 

4.1. Experimental Setup 

Robot Platform. We evaluate 3DTouch on a real-world manipulation platform consisting of a UR7e robot arm and an XHand dexterous hand. The XHand is equipped with dense five-finger tactile sensing, with 120 three-dimensional force taxels per finger. We use a front camera view for visual observations and calibrated RGB-D sensing to obtain scene geometry. 

Data and Tasks. We collect approximately 100 real-robot demonstrations for each of five contact-rich manipulation tasks: (1) Funnel Insertion , inserting a funnel into the open-ing of a flask; (2) Plug Assembly , aligning and inserting a plug into its corresponding socket; (3) Button Press ,pressing a button and completing a subsequent transfer op-eration; (4) Sponge Wiping , grasping a sponge and wip-ing handwritten marks from a whiteboard; and (5) Pipette Press , removing a pipette from its holder and pressing its plunger. The demonstrations include visual observations, task instructions, robot states, actions, and five-finger tac-tile measurements. 

Evaluation Protocol and Metrics. We evaluate all meth-ods on real-robot rollouts using binary task success, where a trial is considered successful only if the complete task objective is achieved. We report the success rate (%) for each task and the unweighted mean across all five tasks. All methods are evaluated over 20 real-robot trials per task, following the same task-specific success criteria and evalu-ation protocol. 

Baselines. We compare 3DTouch with seven baselines: π0,

π0.5, π0 + tactile, π0.5 + tactile, Tactile-VLA, Simple DP3 + tactile, and PointACT. The π0 and π0.5 baselines are pre-trained vision-language-action policies fine-tuned on task-specific demonstrations. For the π0 + tactile and π0.5 +tactile baselines, we incorporate raw tactile measurements into the robot state inputs without explicit spatial encoding. Tactile-VLA serves as a tactile-aware VLA baseline, Sim-ple DP3 + tactile as our tactile-augmented variant of the point-cloud diffusion policy, and PointACT as a 3D-aware VLA with multi-scale point–action interaction. We train all baselines on the same task-specific demonstrations, adapt-ing the input modalities to each method’s requirements. 

Implementation Details. We sample 4,096 points from RGB-D scene point clouds and retain tactile measurements from all 600 taxels. The sampled scene points and tac-tile measurements are encoded into 33 scene tokens and 5 tactile tokens, respectively. The GRM module generates 5 hand–object and 5 object–object relation tokens. We im-plement 3DTouch using a pretrained π0 backbone with 18-dimensional joint actions (6 arm and 12 hand joints), exe-cuting 16 actions per inference.

## 5. Conclusion 

We introduced 3DTouch, a tactile-geometric conditioning approach that grounds tactile feedback in scene geome-try and explicitly models interaction relations for contact-rich dexterous manipulation. Across five real-world tasks, 3DTouch achieves an average success rate of 65.0%, out-performing the strongest evaluated baseline by 20.0 per-centage points. Ablations reveal complementary benefits of scene geometry and spatial tactile feedback, with further gains from shared spatial grounding and explicit interac-tion geometry. 3DTouch also exhibits smaller performance degradation under mild target pose variations than a tactile-augmented VLA baseline. These findings highlight the im-portance of interpreting local contact forces in their spatial context and explicitly modeling the geometry of interacting entities. Future work will focus on reducing reliance on ref-erence object geometry and pose estimation and extending 3DTouch to diverse robot embodiments.
