Abstract —Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) are expected to achieve semantic reasoning, intelligent decision-making, and long-horizon navigation under strict onboard constraints. While recent foundation-model-driven approaches have shown promis-ing progress toward this goal, existing methods remain limited by challenges in 3D data availability, trajectory feasibility, long-horizon reasoning, and computational efficiency in complex environments. To bridge these gaps, we propose VLN-AR, a hier-archical vision-language-navigation (VLN) framework for aerial robots. VLN-AR decouples multimodal reasoning and low-level control, enabling structured prediction of 3D pose commands and navigation intentions that are translated into dynamically feasible actions. To mitigate data scarcity and domain gaps, we introduce a fully automated 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)-based pipeline to generate scalable photorealistic UAV navigation

I. I NTRODUCTION 

# AUTONOMOUS unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) have been widely deployed in diverse applications including infrastructure inspection, search and rescue, environmental monitoring, and logistics delivery. In such scenarios, UAVs are often required to operate in unstructured and partially observ-able environments. Beyond reliable low-level flight control, these settings demand high-level intelligence, including the ability to comprehend natural-language instructions, reason about spatial semantics, and generate safe and dynamically feasible actions under strict onboard computational constraints. arXiv:2611.23930v1 [cs.RO] 18 Nov 2026 2 IEEE TRANSACTIONS ON ROBOTICS. Enabling such capabilities is critical to advancing UAV sys-tems from task-specific navigation toward general embod-ied autonomy. While Vision-Language-Navigation (VLN) [1] and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) [2], [3] paradigms have shown considerable potential for autonomous robots, such methods remain underexplored for agile, resource-constrained UAVs. Most existing UAV navigation methods follow modular perception–planning–control pipelines [4]–[6], relying on pre-defined algorithms, hand-crafted policies, or explicit mapping strategies. While effective in well-defined tasks, they lack general task understanding and reasoning capabilities, particu-larly the ability to interpret open-vocabulary language instruc-tions. Recent advances in multimodal large language models (MLLMs) [7]–[10] have enabled VLN and VLA systems for ground robots, allowing natural language to be grounded in visual observations and translated into executable commands within a unified framework. However, adapting these methods to agile aerial robots is non-trivial due to fundamental dif-ferences in system dynamics, action spaces, data distributions, safety constraints, and deployment requirements. In particular, several critical challenges are as follows:  

> •

Scarcity of 3D navigation data and domain shift. Most navigation foundation models are pretrained on large-scale ground-view 2D navigation datasets, which differ drastically from UAV-centric views that exhibit large variations in pose and scale. Existing UAV navigation datasets are often limited to outdoor environments with coarse visual features and insufficient fine-grained struc-tural details, resulting in sim-to-real domain gaps.  

> •

Mismatch between 3D action representation and safety-aware control. UAV navigation requires safe and reliable control. End-to-end continuous commands de-rived from foundation models often fail to guarantee safety or satisfy dynamical constraints, while semanti-cally discrete action representations offer limited maneu-verability. Balancing 3D maneuverability, control stabil-ity, and collision avoidance remains a core challenge.  

> •

Insufficient reasoning for long-horizon embodied nav-igation. Existing VLN/VLA methods typically rely on single-frame or short-term reasoning, making it difficult to integrate historical observations in complex indoor environments. Long-horizon aerial navigation requires spatial grounding, conditional inference, and sequential task decomposition, which cannot be sufficiently sup-ported by single-step prediction schemes.  

> •

Prohibitive onboard computational constraints. State-of-the-art foundation models are computationally in-tensive, making real-time deployment on small UAVs with strict size, weight, and power (SWaP) constraints challenging [11]. Practical onboard deployment therefore requires preserving strong inference capability while sig-nificantly reducing computational and memory overhead. To address these challenges, we propose VLN-AR , a hi-erarchical Vision Language Navigation framework for Aerial 

Robots in complex environments. The proposed system in-tegrates data generation, multimodal learning, and onboard deployment into a unified closed-loop framework (Fig. 1). At its core, VLN-AR decomposes the navigation problem into two tightly coupled components: a navigation-oriented vision language model (VLM) that performs multimodal reasoning and predicts structured navigation intentions, and a lightweight action expert that translates these intentions into dynamically feasible control commands. Instead of directly generating low-level actions, the model generates diverse navigation repre-sentations tailored to different task requirements. Specifically, it outputs a structured representation comprising 3D pose commands, 2D bounding-box guidance, and chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning, establishing an effective interface between high-level cognition and low-level control. The proposed model achieves a maximum success rate of 98.1% on single navigation tasks and an overall aerial navigation success rate of 92.7%, while attaining success rates of 78.15% and 76.88% on the AerialVLN and OpenFly datasets, respectively. To mitigate flight data scarcity and sim-to-real domain gaps in UAV navigation, we develop a high-fidelity data genera-tion pipeline based on 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) [12], enabling photorealistic and temporally consistent UAV-centric observations. Combined with an automated data collection and annotation pipeline, it produces a large-scale 3D aerial navigation dataset with approximately 4 million samples, covering navigation data, general visual question answering (VQA), grounding data, reasoning data, spatial VQA, and more. Building upon this data foundation, we introduce a progres-sive three-stage training strategy that sequentially enhances multimodal grounding, navigation-specific motion prediction, and reasoning-aware navigation, as shown in Fig. 1(II). The first two stages perform supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on large-scale mixed datasets to improve scene understanding and spatial reasoning for navigation, while the third stage applies reinforcement learning in closed-loop simulation to further improve reasoning-aware navigation [13]. To support long-horizon and ambiguity-aware navigation, we incorporate a lightweight cognitive-driven agent that ag-gregates historical observations, task states, and action states into a unified temporal memory, enabling structured reason-ing, task decomposition, intelligent exploration, and failure recovery in partially observable environments. For efficient onboard deployment, we further combine instruction-guided visual token sparsification with operator-level acceleration and low-precision computation, achieving a 7.1×–9.4× speedup on resource-limited UAV platforms. This work substantially extends the previous work [14], which introduces preliminary components of the 3DGS-based data pipeline, hierarchical navigation architecture, training framework, and onboard deployment stack. The present study further introduces a cognitive navigation agent, instruction-guided token sparsification, and richer structured model out-puts for planning and replanning. Additionally, the training and robustness evaluation have been significantly expanded, and a wider range of scenarios have been tested, thereby constructing a more complete onboard aerial VLN framework. The main contributions are summarized as follows: 1) We propose VLN-AR, a hierarchical onboard VLN WU et al. : A HIERARCHICAL ONBOARD VISION-LANGUAGE-NAVIGATION FRAMEWORK FOR AERIAL ROBOTS IN COMPLEX ENVIRONMENTS 3

II. R ELATED WORKS 

A. Traditional Modular UAV Navigation 

Early autonomous navigation systems for UAVs predom-inantly adopt modular, cascaded architectures following the classic perception–decision–planning–control pipeline [15]– [18]. State estimation and environmental perception are typ-ically implemented via LiDAR-based or visual perception modules [19], [20], while semantic scene understanding is achieved through object detectors such as YOLO [21], Faster R-CNN [22], and 3D scene graphs [23]. High-level mission logic is subsequently resolved by rule-based or decision-tree modules, followed by motion planning algorithms such as A* [24], RRT [25], Fast-Planner [26], and Ego-Planner [27] to generate feasible reference trajectories. Such conventional approaches offer clear interpretability, ro-bust stability, and low computational overhead. However, they are often customized for specific application scenarios, such as target tracking [28], gap traversal [29], and autonomous land-ing [30], and lack the ability to understand open-vocabulary language instructions or exploit rich semantic information from visual observations. Furthermore, the sequential multi-module structure is prone to error propagation and accu-mulation, leading to significant performance degradation in cluttered, dynamic, and unstructured environments.

C. Onboard Deployment for Robotics 

Driven by advances in edge computing, onboard deploy-ment of foundation models on resource-constrained robotic platforms, particularly UAVs, has attracted increasing at-tention. Common approaches include quantization, pruning, knowledge distillation, and operator fusion to reduce latency and meet deployment requirements [44]. However, preserving multimodal capabilities under strict computational and energy constraints remains challenging for UAVs. Existing robotic foundation models such as OpenVLA [2] and the π0 family [3] typically rely on high-performance GPUs and are difficult to deploy onboard UAVs. 2) Instruction-guided Token Sparsification in Vision Trans-former: To improve efficiency under multi-image inputs and enable real-time deployment on resource-constrained UAV platforms, we introduce an instruction-guided token sparsi-fication mechanism within the Vision Transformer (ViT) of 

πVLM , as illustrated in Fig. 4. Empirically, we observe that a significant portion of inference latency is concentrated in the ViT stage on lightweight embedded computing platforms (e.g., NVIDIA Jetson Orin NX), primarily due to the quadratic com-plexity of self-attention over dense token sets. Moreover, real-world navigation images often contain large task-irrelevant regions (e.g., sky, repetitive textures), leading to unnecessary computation and degraded attention efficiency. Therefore, our goal is to jointly reduce redundant visual computation while preserving navigation-critical spatial semantics. In this work, the interval between historical frames is 0.5 s, the number of historical frames is K = 8 , and the total duration covered by the historical frames is 4 s. Our instruction-guided progressive token sparsification strategy is inspired by recent layer-wise visual token compres-sion studies [48]–[50]. From a system perspective, the input consists of multi-frame historical observations and the current RGB frame. Historical observations are first temporally aggre-gated and downsampled (e.g., from 640 × 480 p to 320 × 240 p) to reduce redundancy, while the current observation is kept at full resolution. Both are processed through a hierarchical ViT backbone with periodic feature compression layers, yielding a structured set of vision tokens. Importantly, the sparsification is guided by task semantics rather than purely visual heuristics, ensuring that navigation-critical regions are preserved. Specif-ically, the task instruction is represented using the pretrained Qwen3-VL language embeddings, while the intermediate vi-sual features are projected into the same embedding space using the pretrained Qwen3-VL vision projection modules. 1) Stage I. Scene Reconstruction and Annotation: Un-like traditional mesh-based rendering approaches, we employ 3DGS to capture real-world illumination variations and contin-uous geometric details. We first collect video streams covering diverse indoor (e.g., offices and corridors) and outdoor (e.g., parks and streets) environments, which are then converted into high-quality 3DGS scene representations and imported into the Unity Engine. In addition to 3DGS scene reconstruction, we leverage scene understanding tools (e.g., InstaScene [51]) to extract various scene attributes, including structural layout information, object labels, corresponding 3D bounding boxes, scene maps, and more. These semantic annotations serve as core inputs for subsequent automated task generation and data acquisition. 

2) Stage II. Automated Navigation Generation: Building upon the rich semantic annotations described above, we de-velop a closed-loop automated data collection pipeline. The detailed procedure is described below. (a) Task definition and waypoint randomization: Instead of directly generating task instructions, we adopt a reverse-engineering strategy to generate navigation tasks. Given the scene map, collision-free and safe regions are first identified, from which start and goal positions are randomly sampled. The corresponding orientations are then generated accordingly to ensure kinematic feasibility. After defining valid navigation tasks, language instructions (e.g., “Go to the coffee table, go to the sink, and go to the room”) are automatically synthesized using a set of rule-based templates or VLMs. By constraining the sampling process within expert-defined feasible regions, we ensure that the generated tasks are both sufficiently challenging and reliably solvable. (b) Dynamics-constrained motion planning: In simulation, a UAV equipped with an RGB, depth, and lidar sensor employs a numerical optimization-based trajectory planner [52] that explicitly accounts for obstacle avoidance, height constraint, dynamical feasibility, trajectory smoothness, and time optimality to generate flight trajectories. The planner fully exploits the UAV’s maneuverability, enabling not only horizontal obstacle avoidance but also vertical motion along the Z-axis to bypass obstacles, thereby achieving collision-free navigation in complex 3D environments. 

3) Stage III. Multimodal Navigation Data Collection: 

Once a feasible trajectory has been generated, the system executes the flight in simulation and records synchronized multimodal observations. During this process, the UAV tracks the planned trajectory using an adaptive flight controller [53], while continuously collecting onboard visual inputs and cor-responding state measurements (e.g., position, velocity, and attitude commands). (a) Synchronized data collection: Within the Unity simu-lation, the UAV receives odometry information published by the SE(3) simulator to update its pose in real time. During autonomous flight along the planned trajectory, the system records multimodal data at 10 Hz, including ego-centric RGB images, depth images, lidar point clouds, and the UAV’s 6-DoF odometry in the world coordinate frame. In addition, data augmentation techniques such as trajectory randomization and observation randomization are applied to enhance data WU et al. : A HIERARCHICAL ONBOARD VISION-LANGUAGE-NAVIGATION FRAMEWORK FOR AERIAL ROBOTS IN COMPLEX ENVIRONMENTS 7

diversity and prevent overfitting during training. (b) Instruction diversity enhancement: After collecting RGB images and flight trajectories, we further refine task instruc-tions using multi-frame visual context and an MLLM such as Qwen3-VL-32B-Instruct [54] to enhance the diversity of language annotations. In this step, a video understanding model is employed to re-describe the collected visual data in a more diverse and semantically enriched form. The proposed framework progressively enhances the model’s spatial understanding, task planning, and high-level reasoning capabilities in complex aerial environments. 

1) Stage I. Grounding-reasoning-enhanced SFT: In the first stage, we perform full-parameter SFT on a mixture of large-scale datasets, including VQA, object grounding, spatial reasoning, and reasoning tasks. C. System-Level Onboard Deployment Optimization 

To enable real-world deployment on general UAV plat-forms, we design a lightweight inference framework tailored for severely resource-constrained airborne computing systems. Due to strict limitations in payload, power consumption, and flight endurance, high-performance general-purpose comput-ing platforms are difficult to deploy directly. In this section, we adopt the NVIDIA Jetson Orin NX platform 1 as the onboard computing platform and perform system-level co-optimization under its constraints in computation throughput and memory bandwidth. After structural integration and payload pruning, the total weight of the perception–computation module is reduced to approximately 80g, making it suitable for micro-UAV deployment. However, executing a full foundation model on such a platform still faces a fundamental trade-off between computational capacity and real-time performance. To address this issue, we design an onboard inference acceleration strat-egy from two complementary perspectives: reducing computa-tional redundancy and improving system execution efficiency, consisting of the following three mechanisms. First, at the algorithmic level, we reduce computational redundancy in the ViT by introducing visual token sparsifica-tion, as described in Sec. III-A2. Importantly, the visual token sparsification is introduced during deployment optimization, not during training. This reduces the input token length at the source, thereby alleviating downstream inference cost and mit-igating the computational complexity inherent in Transformer-based architectures. Second, at the system execution level, we optimize the ViT inference pipeline to address limited operator efficiency under the ARM architecture. Specifically, we perform structural optimization including attention compu-tation reordering, feature dimension restructuring, and opera-tor scheduling refinement to improve parallelism and cache

In addition, we accelerate key matrix operations via SIMD instructions and optimized memory access patterns, improving computational efficiency under constrained power budgets. On top of this, we introduce a set of system-level inference graph optimizations, including Flash-Attention for efficient attention computation, fusion of Feed-Forward Net-works (FFN) with normalization layers to reduce intermediate memory traffic, and KV-cache preloading to improve mem-ory bandwidth utilization. Furthermore, CUDA Graphs are employed to statically schedule computation graphs, reducing kernel launch overhead and improving pipeline stability. Finally, we incorporate a heterogeneous low-precision quan-tization strategy along the model execution path. Both model weights and dynamic activations (including KV-cache) are uniformly quantized, significantly reducing memory footprint and improving inference throughput, while preserving spatial grounding and localization accuracy as much as possible. motion planning that accounts for UAV dynamic constraints, resulting in unstable navigation behavior, lower SR, and longer TT. In contrast, our action expert explicitly models UAV dynamics and collision constraints, and incorporates trajectory time optimization, thereby significantly reducing collision risk and improving navigation efficiency. 

3) Comparison with End-to-end VLA Methods: The pro-posed method consistently outperforms end-to-end VLA ap-proaches across all datasets. Similar gaps are observed on other datasets. This advantage arises from limitations of end-to-end VLA methods, which directly predict low-level controls and must jointly learn language understanding, perception, and control. Under limited data and large model sizes, this leads to poor generalization and unstable trajectories, especially in complex scenarios. In contrast, our method predicts 3D pose commands, simplifying learning and decoupling high-level reasoning from motion execution. This results in more stable trajectories, improved accuracy, and better handling of complex instructions and dynamic environments. 

4) Comparison with Zero-shot MLLM-based Navigation: 

For a relatively fair comparison, we adopt a consistent work-flow: the visual observations and language instruction are provided as inputs, and the models are required to predict the target 3D poses and yaw angles in the UAV body frame, which are parsed using the same format and passed to the same action expert for trajectory generation. In complex or long-horizon tasks, this often results in unstable trajectories and inefficient paths. These results demonstrate that existing MLLMs, benefiting from large-scale pretraining, have already acquired preliminary navigation capabilities. However, there remains a significant gap in general-purpose navigation per-formance, with substantial room for improvement in success rate, localization accuracy, and robustness. Furthermore, the previous work VLA-AN [14] shows a success rate more than 10 percentage points lower on the AerialVLN and OpenFly datasets, possibly because these benchmarks contain more long-horizon tasks, for which our Agent mechanism, finer-grained design, and more diverse training data provide greater benefits. On our self-built dataset, where long-horizon tasks account for a smaller proportion, the performance gap is correspondingly reduced.

4) Limitations in Real-world Experiments: Despite the overall robustness of the system, the real-world experiments reveal several limitations. First, in scenes containing many visually similar objects, ambiguous language instructions may cause the semantic model to localize an object that resembles the target but is not the actual goal, resulting in navigation deviations. Second, most failure cases stem from inaccurate spatial estimation, which may occur at the interface between semantic understanding and 3D geometric mapping. For dis-tant structures or small targets, the predicted goal point may suffer from the 2D-to-3D projection process, which can lead to coordinate deviations and cause the UAV to either undershoot or overshoot the target location, thereby reducing the success rate.

3) Analysis of ViT Token Sparsification: To investigate the impact of the similarity-based filtering mechanism, we compare our approach against a baseline that performs to-ken reduction without our similarity calculation module. As illustrated in Fig. 13, the experimental results demonstrate that without instruction guidance, both the Target Token Recall (TTR) and Navigation Success Rate (NSR) exhibit distinct degradation as the token retention ratio decreases. This suggests that task-critical spatial semantics—essential for identifying navigation goals—are inadvertently discarded when the model lacks cross-modal task awareness. In contrast, our instruction-guided mechanism maintains a high-fidelity performance plateau ( T T R ≈ 92 .5% , N SR ≈ 91 .7% ) even at an aggressive sparsification level of approximately 25% token retention. Furthermore, the curves reveal a clear theoretical inflection point near a 25% retention ratio. Above this bound-ary, the cross-modal similarity effectively anchors navigation-critical entropy by filtering task-irrelevant regions (e.g., sky and repetitive textures as seen in outdoor navigation tasks). Below this threshold, the information bottleneck constraints lead to irreversible semantic loss. These findings confirm that our strategy acts as a semantic denoiser rather than a destructive compressor, achieving a superior trade-off between computational efficiency on the constrained computation ve-hicle and aerial navigation accuracy. Specifically, in extreme scenarios where targets are visually subtle, our method ensures robust semantic preservation where unguided pruning fails catastrophically.

VI. C ONCLUSION 

This paper presents VLN-AR, a hierarchical embodied aerial navigation framework that effectively decouples high-level semantic reasoning from low-level flight control for aerial robots. By leveraging a 3DGS-based automated data generation pipeline, we bridge the sim-to-real gap and address the data scarcity inherent in aerial navigation. Combined with the three-stage training paradigm, the instruction-guided token sparsification enables VLN-AR to execute sophisticated long-horizon reasoning and precise navigation on resource-constrained onboard platforms. Experimental results demon-strate superior cognitive robustness and generalization in com-plex, unseen environments, providing a scalable pathway for autonomous aerial robots. In future work, we aim to evolve VLN-AR into a Universal Aerial Foundation Model. Key research directions include: 1) incorporating temporal causal reasoning to navigate dynamic environments with human-robot interaction; 2) fusing multi-modal perception (e.g., point cloud and semantic map) to extend to diverse input modalities for aerial navigation under degraded sensing conditions; and 3) optimizing the framework for next-generation hardware to achieve end-to-end inference rates exceeding 10 Hz, facilitating more agile and responsive flight missions.
