## Abstract 

Efficient and accurate 3D surface reconstruction from multi-view images remains a fundamental challenge in Gaussian splatting. Many existing methods rely on sparse geometric initialization and iterative densification, resulting in sub-stantial optimization overhead and difficulties in balancing rendering quality and geometric accuracy, particularly in reflective and weakly textured regions. To address these limitations, we propose Pol2DGS, a geometry-first frame-work that uses multiview polarization cues to recover dense surface priors. We integrate surface normals into depth maps using sparse geometric anchors. These depth maps are then fused across views. Curvature-adaptive sampling and oriented Gaussian initialization then support efficient optimization. Experiments on the PISR dataset demonstrate an approximately 5× speedup over PhyGaP, which is a state-of-the-art Gaussian-based reconstruction method. Pol2DGS reduces the average optimization time from approximately 21 minutes (PhyGaP) to 4 minutes while maintaining compet-itive novel-view synthesis quality and mesh reconstruction accuracy. These results demonstrate the effectiveness of Pol2DGS in accelerating Gaussian optimization from polari-metric multi-view inputs. arXiv:2611.24404v1 [cs.CV] 18 Nov 2026 1. Introduction 

Recovering accurate 3D surfaces from multiview images [12, 34,40,41] and reproducing object appearance from unseen viewpoints are fundamental problems in 3D vision. 2D Gaus-sian splatting (2DGS) [19] represents local surfaces using oriented disks distributed in 3D space and efficiently synthe-sizes novel views through rasterization. However, rendering speed after model construction and the time required to build the model are distinct measures of efficiency [10,25]. Start-ing from a sparse point cloud still requires repeated adjust-ment of disk positions, orientations, and scales, along with densification to establish surface coverage [19,23]. In re-flective and weakly textured regions, color variations across views provide limited reliable geometric guidance for these adjustments [5,7]. Reducing scene optimization time while preserving novel view synthesis quality and mesh recon-struction accuracy therefore remains a challenge in Gaussian surface reconstruction. Polarization observations provide information about sur-face orientation, supporting geometric estimation beyond color observations [7,9]. In neural implicit reconstruction, PANDORA [9] uses polarimetric inverse rendering to jointly recover object shape and separate diffuse and specular re-flection components. NeRSP [16] combines Stokes vec-tor rendering errors with multiview azimuth consistency to constrain the shape of reflective surfaces from sparse views. PISR [5] incorporates per-pixel perspective view-ing rays into polarization constraints to directly constrain implicit surface normals. In explicit Gaussian reconstruc-tion, Sparse2DGS [38] generates a dense initial point cloud from depth predicted by multiview stereo matching, then selectively updates primitives based on rendered geometry. PhyGaP [39] uses polarimetric deferred rendering to esti-mate geometry and material properties, supporting reflection decomposition and relighting. PolarGS [15] employs polar-ization information for depth completion and back-projects the completed depth to generate new Gaussians. Inspired by these methods, we focus on geometric initial-ization. We investigate how polarization observations can determine the initial positions, orientations, and spatial ex-tents of 2D Gaussians before training to reduce subsequent optimization time. Our research faces two core challenges: The first challenge is recovering surface positions. Since polarization-derived normals specify tangent-plane orienta-tions but not positions along viewing rays. Under perspec-tive projection, normals constrain log-depth gradients, so integration recovers only relative depth [31]; we use sparse geometric anchors to fix its scale. Cross-view scale incon-sistencies can place back-projected points on different depth layers despite accurate normals [3,31], introducing position errors into Gaussian initialization and subsequent training. Depth anchoring and multiview surface alignment are there-fore both necessary. A further challenge is the mismatch between depth sampling density and required primitive den-sity [10,26]. Assigning one Gaussian to each depth sample creates redundant coverage in low-curvature regions, while uniformly coarser sampling risks losing detail in curved re-gions. A single scale is also insufficient when curvature differs between the two principal directions. Initializing representative primitives from dense observations using cur-vature adaptive spacing and scales emerges as a viable option. During training, merging redundant primitives and subdivid-ing inadequately represented regions constitutes a principled strategy [1,18,32], so that the primitive count reflects local surface requirements. In light of these considerations, we propose Pol2DGS, a method that uses polarization-derived surface priors to ac-celerate 2D Gaussian optimization. Given calibrated multi-view color images, polarization observations, and foreground masks, we first estimate polarization-derived normals and their confidence, and then we convert these normals into perspective log-depth gradients and recover depth for each view through least-squares integration with sparse geomet-ric anchors. After multiview consistency checks, we fuse the depth maps using a truncated signed distance function (TSDF) [8] and extract an initial surface. We then fit a quadratic surface in local tangent-plane coordinates. The two principal curvatures determine the sampling spacing along their respective principal directions. Sampled posi-tions define Gaussian centers, while surface normals and principal directions determine disk orientations. Local sam-pling spacing initializes the two tangential scales. Training thus begins with a set of oriented disks that already provides surface coverage. The primitive distribution is then refined according to observations and local geometry. Our main contributions are as follows: • We integrate polarimetric normals with sparse geometric anchors, then use anisotropic, curvature-adaptive surface sampling to initialize Gaussian positions, orientations, and scales without depth sensors. • We replace the native 2DGS [19] primitive update strategy with insertion guided by surface geometry, deterministic splitting along major axes, merging subject to coverage constraints, and pruning based on opacity. • Our Pol2DGS reduces average measured runtime from roughly 21 to 4 minutes (5× speedup) while maintaining competitive novel-view synthesis quality and mesh recon-struction accuracy.

2.2. Polarization-Guided 3D Reconstruction 

Polarization provides surface orientation cues in weakly tex-tured regions, but orientation alone does not determine depth. The method in Ref. [7] employs polarization-derived az-imuths to trace iso-depth contours and propagate reliable stereo depths. PANDORA [9] incorporates polarization into neural inverse rendering to recover shape and illumination while separating diffuse and specular radiance. NeRSP [16] combines supervision on rendered Stokes vectors with multi-view azimuth consistency for sparse-view reconstruction. PISR [5] directly constrains implicit surface normals using the polarization angle and the viewing ray at each pixel. Gaussian methods exploit polarization at different stages. PolGS [17] uses measured Stokes vectors to supervise dif-fuse and specular reflection, while PhyGaP [39] uses po-larimetric deferred rendering and self-occlusion-aware envi-ronment maps for reflection decomposition and relighting. PolarGS [15] incorporates polarization into PatchMatch [2] depth completion and back-projects the completed depths to add Gaussians. Our Pol2DGS uses polarization to establish surface coverage before Gaussian optimization. Sparse geo-metric anchors fix the depth scale during normal integration. On the fused surface, local normals and principal directions determine disk orientation, while principal curvatures guide sampling intervals and disk scales.

3.1. Polarization-Guided Surface Recovery 

As shown in Fig. 3, polarization observations provide lo-cal directional cues about surfaces but exhibit two types of directional ambiguity. First, linear polarization defines an unoriented axis, making the observed angle of linear polariza-tion (AoLP) ϕ indistinguishable from ϕ + π. This gives rise to a π ambiguity. Second, under standard reflection models, diffuse and specular reflection produce orthogonal polariza-tion directions. An additional π/ 2 ambiguity therefore arises when the reflection mechanism is unknown [28,44]. More-over, recovered normals cannot directly determine surface positions [31]. 3.3. Geometry-Guided Primitive Adaptation 

We first freeze positions, rotations, and scales, optimizing only spherical harmonic coefficients and opacity. We then enable geometry updates with a lower learning rate. After each update, center displacement, relative rotation, and scale changes are bounded by their respective allowed ranges to control deviations from the reference surface. We also re-move the original Clone operation [19,23] and manage the primitive set through Insert, Split, Merge, and Prune. 

Insert targets regions with persistently low coverage. We recover patches through robust integration using polarization-derived normals and reliable boundary depths. After confirm-ing support from at least two other views and the absence of conflicts, we sample these patches to generate new Gaus-sians. We first check their projected extents, then insert them temporarily and render the scene. The new primitives are retained if coverage increases in missing regions and back-ground coverage, image error, and depth changes remain within their allowed limits. Otherwise, they are removed. We establish surface anchors from their patches, temporar-ily freeze their geometry, and apply a protection period for primitive updates. 

Split refines the local representation provided by existing primitives. Candidates must have large average screen-space gradients over recent training iterations. They must also exhibit at least one of the following conditions: excessive projected coverage, substantial scale growth relative to ini-tialization, large neighborhood normal variation, or a large deviation from the reference tangent plane. Observation-count and opacity criteria must also be satisfied. We con-struct two child Gaussians along the major axis in the tangent plane: 

μ± = μ ± 0.45 smax tmax ,smax ,± = 0 .65 smax , o± = 1 − √1 − o. (9) where μ and o are the parent’s center and opacity, respec-tively. The quantity smax is the larger of the two tangential scales, and tmax is the corresponding unit tangent direction. The subscripts + and − identify the two children. The chil-dren inherit the parent’s minor-axis scale and orientation and replace the parent. The opacity adjustment reduces changes in local coverage caused by splitting.

## 4. Experiments 

4.1. Implementation and Dataset 

We train for 15000 iterations, freezing geometry for the first 1500. We set λ1 = 0 .5, λ2 = 0 .3, λ3 = 0 .1, and λ4 = 1 ;

Lsmooth combines normal and depth terms with weights 0.2 and 0.8, respectively. We select seven scenes from two datasets: vase, owl, and gnome from PANDORA [9], and BlackLoong, RedLoong, LayingRabbit, and StandingRab-bit from PISR [5]. Both datasets contain real-world cap-tures, and PISR additionally provides ground-truth meshes obtained using structured-light scanning. 

4.2. Comparison 

4.2.1. Mesh Reconstruction 

As shown in Table 1, Pol2DGS achieves the shortest re-ported runtime (4.3min), yielding a 5× speedup over Phy-GaP [39] while improving both CD and F-score on three of the four scenes. Compared with polarization-guided im-plicit methods [5,9,16], our method substantially reduces training time while maintaining competitive reconstruction quality. It is important to appreciate that, Pol2DGS fur-ther outperforms PhyGaP on Black Loong and Red Loong, which contain complex surface structures and fine-scale ge-ometric details. These gains are consistent with the role of polarization-derived surface priors in guiding Gaussian placement and orientation before optimization. Curvature-adaptive sampling allocates samples according to local geo-metric variation, providing finer coverage in geometrically complex regions [20,21,30]. Further strong evidence that our design may help preserve fine surface structures within a limited optimization budget is shown in Fig. 4. As shown, Pol2DGS avoids overfitting surface patterns as geometry and produces cleaner, more coherent surfaces than the other competing methods. The overall shape and key geometric structures are likewise preserved.

## 5. Conclusion 

Pol2DGS accelerates 2D Gaussian surface reconstruction through geometric priors from polarization and primitive updates guided by geometry. Polarimetric normals, sparse geometric anchors, and anisotropic sampling adapted to lo-cal curvature initialize Gaussian positions, orientations, and scales without depth sensors. Experimentally, this initializa-tion improves geometric accuracy and reduces optimization time. Insertion guided by surface geometry, deterministic splitting along the major axis, merging under coverage con-straints, and pruning based on opacity further refine the representation. Ablations demonstrate that these operations jointly improve surface accuracy while largely preserving image quality. Across the evaluated scenes and under our timing protocol, optimization averages 4 minutes and 22 sec-onds. This yields a speedup of approximately 5 to 10 times over the mainstream methods while maintaining competitive mesh accuracy and novel view synthesis quality.
