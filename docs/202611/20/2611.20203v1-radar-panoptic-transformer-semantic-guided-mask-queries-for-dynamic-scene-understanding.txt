# Radar Panoptic Transformer: Semantic-Guided Mask Queries for Dynamic Scene Understanding 

Mayank Mayank 1,2, Bharanidhar Duraisamy 1,3, Rohit Mohan 2, and Abhinav Valada 2

Abstract — Automotive radar provides long-range sensing and direct Doppler velocity measurements, but its sparse and non-uniformly sampled point clouds pose significant challenges for panoptic segmentation. Dynamic objects often yield only a few detections amid a dominant static background, providing limiting the geometric cues for separating object instances. We propose Radar Panoptic Transformer (RPT) , an end-to-end point-based network that incorporates radar-adaptive feature extraction with semantic-guided mask prediction. Its backbone aggregates pose-aligned temporal context through grouped vector attention and learns per-point neighborhood radii conditioned on range, azimuth, and radar cross section (RCS). Point-wise semantic predictions initialize queries at likely foreground detections and guide masked cross-attention through query-point class agreement, producing instance masks without geometric clustering. An auxiliary RCS-consistency loss matches instance-level statistics between predicted and annotated instances. RPT achieves state-of-the-art performance on RadarScenes with 71 .6% mIoU and 82 .2% PQ. We further evaluate RPT on MAN TruckScenes, where it outperforms the evaluated baselines in a long-range, full-surround radar setting with sensing ranges up to 230 m . RPT achieves real-time end-to-end inference at 31 Hz on RadarScenes, exceeding the sensor frame rate. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Reliable autonomous driving requires a holistic understand-ing of dynamic scenes, including the semantic structure of the environment and the identification of individual traffic participants. Panoptic segmentation provides this representa-tion by assigning each sensor measurement a semantic class and, for thing classes, an instance identifier [1]. Although ex-tensively studied for cameras [2] and LiDAR point clouds [3], [4], panoptic segmentation from automotive radar remains less explored. Radar provides distinct sensing capabilities, including direct radial Doppler velocity measurements, long-range operation, and robustness to adverse illumination and weather [5], [6]. In particular, Doppler information provides an additional motion cue for distinguishing individual traffic participants, motivating radar-based panoptic segmentation for dynamic scene understanding. Radar perception has predominantly focused on object detection and tracking [5]–[7], which yield object-level outputs rather than semantic labels and instance identities for individual radar detections. Distant objects may also be represented by only a few detections, while static returns dominate the scene [10]. Fixed-radius neighborhoods are poorly suited to spatially varying sampling density, while the severe foreground-background im-balance hinders reliable identification of sparsely represented dynamic instances. Radar panoptic segmentation remains a recent research direction [11]. Existing approaches use refinement pipelines that rely on class-agnostic upstream instance estimates [11], [12], use fixed-radius neighborhoods, and have largely been evaluated at shorter operating ranges. We introduce Radar Panoptic Transformer (RPT) , an end-to-end point-based architecture for panoptic segmentation of sparse automotive radar point clouds as shown in Fig. 1. RPT combines KPConv [13] for local geometric encoding with grouped vector attention [14] to integrate pose-aligned arXiv:2611.20203v1 [cs.CV] 15 Nov 2026 historical radar context into current-scan features. To account for non-uniform radar sampling, we introduce learned per-point neighborhood radii conditioned on range, azimuth, and RCS. To couple semantic and instance reasoning, we introduce a semantic-guided mask decoder that conditions object-query initialization and masked cross-attention on point-wise semantic predictions, enabling class-aware instance prediction without geometric clustering. We further introduce an RCS-consistency loss that regularizes predicted instances through their instance-level RCS mean and variance. We perform extensive evaluations on RadarScenes [10] and the long-range MAN TruckScenes benchmark [15], where our proposed RPT consistently outperforms existing methods. II. R ELATED WORK 

Radar Representation Learning : Automotive radar perception is commonly formulated using either structured bird’s-eye-view (BEV) representations or native point-domain processing. BEV methods regularize sparse measurements into grids or pillars and have shown strong performance for 3D object detection. K-Radar [7] demonstrated 4D radar detection under diverse weather conditions, while MMF-BEV [16] introduced RCS-based radar encoding with safety aware BEV fusion. DRIFT [17] further combines point and pillar representations to capture complementary local and global context. However, rasterization weakens the direct correspondence to individual radar returns required for point-wise semantic and instance prediction. Point-based approaches preserve irregular measurement ge-ometry together with radar-specific attributes such as Doppler velocity and RCS. RadarScenes [10] enabled systematic point-wise semantic evaluation, while GRT [8] introduced radar-specific attention for sparse semantic segmentation and RVT [9] exploited Doppler cues for moving-object segmentation. These methods establish the effectiveness of native radar representations, but predict semantic or motion labels rather than class-aware object instances. Our proposed RPT extends point-based processing to panoptic segmentation, using pose-aligned temporal context to enrich sparse current-scan features. 

3D Panoptic and Mask-Based Segmentation : LiDAR panoptic segmentation provides the closest methodological foundation. Panoptic-PolarNet [18] and DS-Net [19] combine semantic prediction with geometric instance cues and subsequent group-ing, while EfficientLPS [3] employs range-aware features and learned panoptic fusion. These approaches benefit from dense LiDAR geometry, where object centers and local spatial structure provide reliable instance cues, an assump-tion that is considerably weaker for sparse radar returns. Query-based methods reduce the dependency on geometric clustering. Mask3D [20] represents instances through learned mask queries, while MaskPLS [4] extends this formulation to LiDAR panoptic segmentation for autonomous driving. Although such set-based prediction is permutation invariant, generic queries are designed for substantially denser point clouds. In radar, severe foreground sparsity and dominant static background make unconstrained object discovery more challenging, motivating semantic guidance during query initialization and refinement. 

Radar Instance and Panoptic Segmentation : Radar instance and panoptic segmentation remain comparatively recent research directions. RIT [12] exploits temporal measurements for class-agnostic moving-instance segmentation, demonstrat-ing the importance of motion and temporal context. Sid-dhartha et al. [21] jointly predict semantic labels and instance identifiers, but resolve instance ambiguity through ordered labels rather than permutation-invariant set prediction. Sem-RaFiner [11] is the closest radar-specific panoptic approach. It builds on RIT instance predictions and introduces radius-based attention to address spatially varying radar density before semantic-instance refinement. However, instance discovery depends on an upstream class-agnostic instance estimate, and the neighborhood support is not explicitly conditioned on radar reflectivity and viewing geometry. RPT instead jointly couples radar-specific representation learning with semantic-guided set prediction. III. R ADAR PANOPTIC TRANSFORMER

A. Datasets and Evaluation 

RadarScenes offers point-wise semantic labels and track identities, enabling direct panoptic supervision. TruckScenes provides six surround 4D radars with nearly 360 ◦ cover-age and ranges up to 230 m . Since only 3D boxes are annotated, radar detections are temporally aligned to the annotation timestamp and labeled by point-in-box association. In comparison, nuScenes [27] provides comparatively sparse, filtered radar detections, while VoD [28] is limited to a single forward-facing 4D radar. Our selected datasets are strongly background dominated, with static returns accounting for 96 .7% of RadarScenes and 95 .8% of TruckScenes. RadarScenes dynamic classes comprise approximately 0.17 –

1.42% of total radar detections and include vehicles and VRUs. 

17 .31% of the instances are singletons. TruckScenes contains 

1.37% car, 2.76% truck, 0.02% pedestrian, and 0.01% two-wheeler returns. Since distant objects are often represented by only a few detections, and pedestrian/two-wheeler samples are extremely limited in TruckScenes, we restrict evaluation to cars and trucks. We report mIoU for semantic segmentation and PQ for panoptic segmentation [33].

D. Ablation Studies 

We analyze the contributions of the radar-adaptive encoder, semantic-guided instance decoder, and temporal context on RadarScenes. 

1) Radar-Adaptive Encoder: Tab. V evaluates the feature extraction and neighborhood construction used by the encoder. Without temporal feature fusion, RPT obtains 67 .7% mIoU. Introducing pose-aligned temporal encoding with fixed-radius neighborhoods increases performance to 70 .9% mIoU. Replac-ing the fixed radius neighborhood with the proposed learned neighborhood further improves mIoU to 71 .6% . These results show that temporal feature enrichment provides the dominant gain, while adapting the receptive field to radar-dependent sampling provides an additional improvement. 

2) Instance Refinement: Tab. VI isolates the contribution of the instance prediction stage. Replacing the proposed semantic-guided query decoder with MeanShift clustering [34] yields 73 .2% PQ, whereas semantic-guided mask prediction increases PQ to 81 .6% . The 8.4-point gain shows the benefit of integrating semantic evidence directly into instance for-mation rather than applying geometric clustering to semantic predictions. Adding the RCS-consistency objective further improves PQ by 0.6 points to 82 .2% , indicating comple-mentary benefit from radar-native instance-level reflectivity supervision. 

3) Temporal Context: Tab. VII studies the temporal horizon while retaining the complete RPT architecture. Increasing the number of pose-aligned historical scans from T = 1 to 

T = 2 improves mIoU from 69 .4% to 71 .6% and PQ from 

80 .0% to 82 .2% . Extending the context to T = 3 provides no further improvement, suggesting that additional historical measurements contribute limited information. 

E. Runtime 

Tab. VIII compares model complexity and inference latency. RPT contains 5.4 M parameters and processes each scan in 32 .1 ms on an Nvidia RTX A6000, corresponding to approximately 31 Hz and 24% lower latency than SemRaFiner. Despite temporal feature extraction and mask-based panoptic prediction, RPT remains competitive with lighter semantic-only baselines. Its inference rate also exceeds the 17 Hz and 20 Hz radar frame rates of the evaluated datasets, supporting real-time operation while maintaining benchmark performance. 

F. Qualitative Evaluation 

Fig. 3 and Fig. 4 highlight the radar-specific advantages of RPT over the generic Mask3D formulation. Mask3D relies on voxelized sparse features and generic learnable queries, without explicitly modeling radar temporal continuity, range-dependent sampling, or reflectivity. In dense traffic cases as in RadarScenes, these mechanisms help separate adjacent vehicles with similar Doppler signatures and recover sparsely observed dynamic targets that Mask3D misses or assigns to static background. Semantic-guided query initialization and semantic-compatible masked attention further focus instance discovery on sparse foreground returns. On TruckScenes, the same radar-adaptive temporal encoding enables coherent truck masks in the front and rear longitudinal distance of approximately 180 m while Mask3D exhibits incomplete masks and false foreground assignments. V. C ONCLUSION 

We presented Radar Panoptic Transformer (RPT) , alightweight and computationally efficient framework for joint semantic and instance understanding directly from automotive radar point clouds. RPT combines pose-aligned temporal context, radar-adaptive neighborhoods conditioned on range, azimuth, and RCS, enhanced with semantic-guided mask queries for instance formation without explicit geometric clustering. The model achieves state-of-the-art radar panoptic segmentation on RadarScenes and, to the best of our knowledge, provides the first reported radar panoptic evaluation on the long-range, full-surround MAN TruckScenes setting, where it consistently outperforms the evaluated baselines.
