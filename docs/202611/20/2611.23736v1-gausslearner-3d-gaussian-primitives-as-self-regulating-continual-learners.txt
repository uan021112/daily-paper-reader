Abstract
Continual learning of 3D Gaussian fields aims to maintain
faithful reconstructions of the evolving world. However, di-
rectly fine-tuning an existing Gaussian field on new observa-
tions can cause catastrophic forgetting of previously recon-
structed content. To address this challenge, existing methods
employ selective local optimization, replay, or parameter
regularization, yet still struggle to balance adaptation to
scene changes with preservation of still-valid content. Our
key insight is to view 3D Gaussian primitives themselves as
self-regulating continual learners. Based on this insight, we
introduce GAUSSLEARNER, which equips each Gaussian
with a closed continual-learning loop connecting observa-
tion, self-regulation, and adaptation at the primitive level. At
each novel state, GAUSSLEARNER first attributes pixel-wise
inconsistencies in current observations to their contributing
Gaussians. Each Gaussian then self-regulates through a
learning weight that balances current observations against
cross-state reliability. This weight governs both Gaussian
parameter adaptation and optimizer memory refresh. As
current inconsistencies are resolved, the adaptation outcome
renews each Gaussian’s reliability for future states. The
resulting field is a globally coupled population of locally
self-regulating learners. Across scenes from CL-Splats, WAT,
and our CL-3D dataset, GAUSSLEARNER improves average
PSNR by 3.41 dB over the strongest baseline. On CL-3D,
it achieves the best reconstruction quality on both changed
and unchanged views.
1. Introduction
Continual learning for 3D scenes aims to update a scene rep-
resentation as new observations keep arriving [21, 34]. These
observations can reveal newly explored regions [48], rear-
ranged objects [42], or changes in scene appearance [7]. The
central challenge is to balance adaptation to scene changes
with preservation of still-valid scene content [33]. This ca-
pability is essential for long-term robotic navigation [8],
AR/VR [36, 37], and embodied intelligence [52].
Existing methods address this challenge through selec-
tive local optimization, replay, or parameter regularization.
CL-Splats and O-SCD first detect changed regions in 2D
observations, lift them into 3D, and restrict optimization to

Our central insight is to view 3D Gaussian primitives
as self-regulating continual learners. In prior paradigms, a
continual-learning policy acts on the Gaussian field, with in-
dividual Gaussians serving as the update targets. As shown in
Fig. 2, we instead aim to equip each Gaussian with a closed
continual-learning loop, in which each primitive receives
attributed observations, self-regulates through its learning
weight, and adapts through coordinated parameter updates
and optimizer-memory refresh.
Based on this insight, we propose GAUSSLEARNER, a
continual-learning framework that enables every Gaussian to
self-regulate its adaptation across scene states. At each scene
state, GAUSSLEARNER first attributes pixel-wise inconsis-
tencies in current observations to the contributing Gaussians.
Each Gaussian learner then weighs its attributed observa-
tions against cross-state reliability to form a per-Gaussian
learning weight. This weight governs both how far the Gaus-
sian follows its current Adam proposal and how strongly
its optimizer memory is refreshed. As current inconsisten-
cies are resolved, the adaptation outcome renews the Gaus-
sian’s cross-state reliability for future scene states. The
resulting field is a globally coupled population of locally
self-regulating continual learners.
We evaluate GAUSSLEARNER on CL-Splats, WAT, and
our self-captured CL-3D dataset. Across all scenes, GAUS-
SLEARNER improves PSNR by 3.41 dB on average over the
strongest baseline. It also achieves the best reconstruction
quality on both changed and unchanged views on the CL-3D
dataset, demonstrating its ability to adapt to revised scene
content while preserving content that remains valid.
Overall, our key contributions are:
• We propose GAUSSLEARNER, a continual-learning frame-
work that views 3D Gaussian primitives as continual learn-
ers, thereby enabling effective scene adaptation while mit-
igating catastrophic forgetting.
• We develop a self-regulating Gaussian optimization pro-
cess in which a per-Gaussian learning weight jointly gov-
erns parameter adaptation and optimizer-memory refresh.
• We conduct extensive experiments across datasets, demon-
strating that GAUSSLEARNER effectively adapts to
changed content while preserving still-valid content.
2. Related Work
2.1. Continual Learning of 3D Gaussian Scenes
Continual learning of 3D Gaussian scenes updates a recon-
structed field across discrete scene states while preserving
still-valid content [3]. This differs from online Gaussian
SLAM, which integrates streaming observations into a cur-
rent map [13, 16, 25, 30, 46, 50, 55]. Multi-state methods
mainly use selective local optimization [22, 54], replay, or
parameter regularization. CL-Splats [1] and O-SCD [11]
detect changes in 2D before updating selected Gaussians.
GaussianUpdate [51] uses generative replay, which reduces
forgetting but adds overhead and may retain obsolete con-
tent. CGS [48] protects historically important Gaussians
through regularization, which can resist necessary changes.
Other work explores probabilistic updates and scene mem-
ories [18, 26, 38, 44]. Appendix F provides a unified view
of field-level continual Gaussian Splatting methods, high-
lighting our shift to per-Gaussian self-regulation. GAUS-
SLEARNER coordinates parameter adaptation and optimizer-
memory refresh through a learning weight determined by
current observations and cross-state reliability.
2.2. Continual Learning for Implicit Mapping
Implicit maps encode geometry and appearance in shared
parameters, so fitting new observations can disturb unob-
served regions [5, 27, 35, 39, 49, 56]. Existing methods
use replay [7, 29, 47], parameter regularization or isola-
tion [2, 20, 24, 41], and distillation [12]. These strategies
reduce forgetting but may retain outdated scene content. Dis-
tributed mapping reconciles models without sharing raw
data [4, 9], while TACO [53] uses temporal consensus to pre-
serve reliable history and revise conflicting regions. GAUS-
SLEARNER pursues the same adaptation and preservation
balance in an explicit field, but makes each Gaussian a state-
ful learner that regulates its own adaptation.

3. Method
3.1. 3D Gaussian Primitives as Continual Learners
Following [1, 48, 51], we consider continual learning of
3D Gaussian fields over a sequence of discrete scene states
t = 0, . . . , T. At each subsequent state t ≥1, the system
receives registered RGB-D observations Ot. The objective
is to update the previously reconstructed field Gt−1 into Gt,
incorporating changed content while preserving previously
reconstructed content that remains valid.

sian primitives themselves continual learners. We represent

3.4. Learning-Weight-Driven Gaussian Adaptation
The resulting learning weight Wi translates each Gaussian’s
self-regulation into two complementary adaptation mech-
anisms that control its immediate parameter response and
refresh optimizer memory for subsequent rounds.
Weight-Scaled Parameter Adaptation.
At the parameter
level, self-regulation concerns how much of an optimizer
update is actually realized. Scaling a gradient does not gener-
ally scale an Adam update by the same factor, because Adam
normalizes it using first- and second-order moments [19].
Thus, we separate proposal construction from proposal ac-
ceptance: Adam first constructs a complete parameter pro-
posal under the reconstruction objective L, and Wi deter-
mines how much of that proposal is realized:
eθi = Adam(θi, ∇θiL),
θi ←θi + Wi(eθi −θi).
(7)
A small Wi realizes only a limited portion of the proposal,
whereas a value near one accepts it almost completely. The
same Wi modulates position, scale, rotation, opacity, and ap-
pearance, with normalized interpolation applied to rotations.
Each Gaussian is therefore updated coherently as a complete
3D primitive.

Self-
regulation also extends beyond the current parameter adap-
tation to the optimizer history carried into subsequent
rounds. Larger learning weights call for stronger adaptation.
We therefore attenuate accumulated Adam moments more
strongly for these Gaussians, giving subsequent gradients
greater influence. Smaller weights retain more optimizer his-
tory as the need for adaptation decreases. Accordingly, Wi

4. Experiments
4.1. Experimental Setup
Datasets and Metrics.
We evaluate our method on three
real-world datasets for continual scene reconstruction. The
CL-Splats dataset [1] focuses on local updates from sparse
observations, with an initial state and one update state per
scene. The World Across Time (WAT) dataset [7] provides
dense observations across multiple states. We conduct exper-
iments on four scenes from each of these two datasets.
Existing datasets, however, offer limited coverage of
mixed geometric and appearance changes under incom-
plete revisits. Their evaluation also primarily focuses on
updated regions and provides a less direct assessment of
how well methods preserve historical content beyond cur-
rent observation coverage. To better assess adaptation and
preservation under mixed scene changes, we introduce the
Continual-Learning-3D (CL-3D) dataset, comprising six
real-world scenes. They cover scene expansion, object addi-
tions, removals and rearrangements, and illumination varia-
tions, combining geometric and appearance changes. Further
dataset details are provided in Appendix C.
We report PSNR and SSIM [40] to evaluate novel-view
synthesis (NVS) quality. For the CL-3D dataset, we explic-
itly divide the test views into changed and unchanged subsets
to separately evaluate adaptation to revised scene content
and preservation of still-valid content.

Baselines.
We compare GAUSSLEARNER against repre-
sentative 3D continual learning baselines.
• Selective local optimization. Both detect changes in 2D
to guide local updates. CL-Splats [1] backprojects change
masks, while O-SCD [11] uses multi-view fusion before
selective reconstruction and global refinement.
• Replay.
CLNeRF [7] preserves previous knowledge
through replayed historical supervision.
• Parameter regularization.
3DGS+EWC [20] and
3DGS+MAS [2] are constructed by augmenting the
training objective with another regularization term.
3DGS+EWC adds a quadratic penalty weighted by di-
agonal Fisher information, while 3DGS+MAS weights the
penalty using parameter importance estimated from the
sensitivity of rendered outputs.
We also report 3DGS [17], which directly fine-tunes the
existing scene using only current observations. All Gaussian-
based methods share the same initial reconstruction and
evaluation protocols.
Implementation Details.
The initial reconstruction is op-
timized for 30,000 iterations. Each subsequent state uses 10
rounds of 300 iterations on CL-Splats or 1,000 on CL-3D
and WAT. Support, pressure, and learning weights are recom-
puted from all current-state training views before the first
round and after each round, remaining fixed during the fol-
lowing round. We retain opacity pruning and densification
for adaptive control of the Gaussian population [17], where
pruning removes the reliability and splitting copies the par-
ent’s reliability to each child. More details are provided in
Appendix B. All experiments use a single NVIDIA GeForce
RTX 4090 GPU.

4.4. Ablations
We examine observation aggregation, learning-weight con-
struction, parameter adaptation, optimizer-memory refresh,
and reliability renewal within GAUSSLEARNER’s self-
regulating optimization process (Tables 4 and 5).
Direction-Aware Pooling.
View-Wise Sum replaces our
directional pooling operator with a sum of per-view support
and pressure followed by the same normalization, which
lowers performance on both changed and unchanged views.
Directionally balanced aggregation better captures geometric
diversity across viewing directions, thus providing a stronger
observational basis for Gaussian self-regulation.
Reliability-Aware Self-Regulation.
Setting W = P re-
moves the reliability-dependent adaptation share β, making
attributed pressure alone determine the learning weight for
both parameter adaptation and optimizer-memory refresh.
Its lower performance on both view splits shows that cross-
state reliability helps regulate how strongly an inconsistency
in current observations should drive learning.
Parameter Adaptation.
Pre-Adam Gradient Scaling ap-
plies W to gradients before Adam, where moment normaliza-
tion can alter its intended adaptation scale [19]. Its lower per-
formance supports acting on the completed Adam proposal,
where W directly controls how much parameter change each
Gaussian realizes.

5. Limitations
Despite its effectiveness in balancing adaptation and preser-
vation, GAUSSLEARNER has several limitations. It remains
sensitive to challenging illumination conditions, including
specular reflections and refraction, as illustrated in Ap-
pendix E.4. Incorporating an inverse rendering framework
may improve robustness. Moreover, GAUSSLEARNER main-
tains a reconstruction of the latest scene state. The rep-
resentation does not support arbitrary replay of previous
states. Object-centric state management could extend GAUS-
SLEARNER toward historical-state retrieval.
6. Conclusion
We present GAUSSLEARNER, a continual learning frame-
work that views 3D Gaussian primitives as self-regulating
continual learners rather than merely as update targets. Each
Gaussian balances current observations against cross-state
reliability through a learning weight that governs both param-
eter adaptation and optimizer-memory refresh. Adaptation
outcomes then renew its reliability to guide future learning.
We also introduce CL-3D to evaluate continual reconstruc-
tion under mixed scene changes and incomplete observations,
with separate assessment of adaptation and preservation.
Across 14 scenes, GAUSSLEARNER improves average PSNR
by 3.41 dB over the strongest baseline, O-SCD. Together
with its balance between adaptation and preservation, these
results demonstrate the benefits of this continual-learning
paradigm. Ablations further validate the effectiveness of our
designs within the closed continual-learning loop.
