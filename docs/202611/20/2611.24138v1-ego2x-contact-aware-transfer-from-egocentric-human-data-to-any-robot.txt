Abstract 

Embodied models depend on real interaction data, yet col-lecting it on physical robots is slow and labor-intensive. Recent ego-to-robot pipelines retarget first-person view hu-man videos to robot data via transferring hand and arm motion. They judge fidelity mainly by visual appearance. However, trajectories often fail in terms of physics: fingers hover beside objects, and grippers close on empty space. We argue that how the object moves and the contacts that drive the motion count. Thus, we present Ego2X, an object-centric framework that converts monocular egocentric video into physically executable robot data across dexterous hands and parallel grippers. Ego2X centers on CORE (Contact-centric Object-interaction REpresentation), which records each con-tact on the object by its surface anchor, as well as relative force, grasp mode, and interaction state. Throughout the pipeline, CORE corrects hand–object drift via object-relative constraints, steers inverse kinematics toward contacts the robot must realize, and preserves held objects during arm erasure and robot compositing. 1. Introduction 

Vision-language-action policies and robot world models de-pend on physical interaction data. Teleoperated demonstra-tions are slow to collect and tied to specific hardware [21, 48], whereas egocentric human videos capture diverse manipu-lation skills at scale [1, 15, 18]. Converting human video into robot observations and actions provides a direct route to scalable embodied learning. Recent ego-to-robot pipelines perform this conversion by retargeting 3D human hand kinematics to robot joints [19, 20, 22, 40]. Phantom [22] composites rendered arms over inpainted hands, EgoMimic [20] aligns human and robot action spaces, and HuRo [19] and Ego2Robot [40] map reconstructed 3D hand trajectories via inverse kinematics. 1arXiv:2611.24138v1 [cs.RO] 18 Nov 2026 Their transferred unit is hand motion. The monocular hand estimates drift by centimeters along the camera ray, whereas stable manipulation demands millimeter precision on the object surface. Consequently, retargeted fingers hover beside objects, and parallel grippers close on empty space. Recovering and transferring object-frame contact from monocular RGB video poses three coupled challenges. First, monocular hand and object estimators exhibit depth and scale drift along the viewing ray. Therefore, using the hand to fix the object scale and that object to correct the hand depth couples their errors in a circular loop. Second, contacts occur on occluded surfaces and span timing, surface geometry, and force distribution that raw images do not directly expose. Third, the recovered contacts must drive robots with distinct kinematics and contact surfaces while replacing the human arm in video without erasing the held object. We present Ego2X , an object-centric framework that converts monocular egocentric video into physically exe-cutable robot data across dexterous hands and parallel grip-pers (Fig. 1). Ego2X centers on CORE (Contact-centric Object-interaction REpresentation), which records each con-tact in the object frame by its surface anchor, outward normal, push direction, relative force, grasp mode, and interaction state (Fig. 2). Recovered from tactile timing and camera-ray surface intersection, CORE unifies every stage of the conversion pipeline. To resolve ray-depth drift without circu-lar scale coupling, Ego2X diagnoses object pose reliability and decouples depth estimation. Furthermore, it fuses hand scale with multi-view ray triangulation on the object path while shifting the hand along the camera ray against hand-ruler-free multi-view object geometry. To bridge morpholo-gies, a URDF compiler maps robot collision geometry into mode-compatible contact pads, and contact-driven inverse kinematics pulls dexterous pads onto CORE anchors or syn-thesizes antipodal object-frame grasps for parallel grippers. Finally, CORE interaction states guide object-protected arm erasure and contact-edge de-skinning during video composit-ing, while closed-loop simulation replay verifies physical task completion. Across HOT3D [1], EgoDex [18], and RoboTwin 2.0 [9], Ego2X substantially improves geometric accuracy, physi-cal contact realization, and downstream policy performance. On HOT3D, contact-driven depth correction cuts in-contact hand ray-depth error by 33 .7% under tracked object poses and lowers monocular object translation error by 30 .4–

47 .3 mm under estimated poses. On EgoDex, contact-driven retargeting realizes 62 .9% of annotated contacts for dex-terous hands compared to 27 .9% for the official HuRo pipeline [19], and achieves 63 .2% physical contact fit for parallel grippers compared to 9.7% for Ego2Robot [40]. In RoboTwin 2.0, post-training X-VLA [49] on Ego2X episodes alone, without simulator demonstrations, raises task success rate from 40 .5% zero-shot to 67 .0% across 200 

paired scenes. Our contributions are threefold: • Contact-centric Object-interaction REpresentation (CORE) : We identify that object interaction dynamics constitute the primary transferable value in egocentric videos. To capture this, we formulate an object-centric conversion paradigm that explicitly models contact points, forces, grasp modes, and interaction states within the ob-ject coordinate frame (Sec. 3.2). • CORE-driven Ego2X Pipeline : Leveraging our contact-aware representation, we develop the Ego2X framework to bridge the human-to-robot embodiment gap (Sec. 3). Specifically, it integrates contact-driven hand–object depth correction (Sec. 3.3.1), cross-morphology contact retarget-ing (Secs. 3.3.2 and 3.4), and contact-guided layered video rendering (Sec. 3.3.3). • Egocentric Video Post-training for VLAs : As far as we know, we are the first to exploit post-training of embodied models using egocentric videos, specifically, with object interaction and contact priors from egocentric videos to synthesize robot trajectories (Sec. 3.4). Without any ex-pert demonstrations in simulation, our converted data ef-fectively post-trains downstream Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and substantially improves their task suc-cess rates. 

2. Related Work 

Learning manipulation from human videos. Hu-man videos support robot manipulation learning primarily through policy alignment or embodiment conversion. Pol-icy alignment methods extract latent plans [38], align ac-tion spaces [20], imitate object-centric motions on fixed hu-manoids [23], or pretrain VLA models [44]. Conversely, em-bodiment conversion pipelines composite rendered arms over inpainted hands [22], retarget 3D hand trajectories to robot joints [19, 40], or edit videos generatively [16], with recent benchmarks evaluating policy transfer [43]. Unlike pipelines that transfer free-space hand motion or single-robot wrist poses, Ego2X compiles object-frame contacts via CORE across diverse morphologies. 

Hand–object reconstruction and contact. Monocular hand estimators [6, 27, 45], 3D object models and track-ers [10, 42], and hand–object benchmarks [1, 5, 18] provide 3D geometric priors from RGB inputs. Prior works model hand–object contact via 2D interaction regions [32, 46], vi-sual tactile cues [14, 47], mocap contact maps [33], grasp taxonomies [12], and contact-guided mesh fitting [13, 30]. Building on these priors, Ego2X fuses tactile timing, camera-ray intersection, and two-part load priors in the object frame to correct ray drift without circular scale coupling.

3. Ego2X 

Given a monocular egocentric video {It}Tt=1 and a target robot URDF, Ego2X reconstructs metric hands and signed object surfaces (Sec. 3.1) and extracts object-frame CORE records (Sec. 3.2). From these records, it outputs a veri-fied LeRobot [2] episode comprising robot actions, training masks, and an object-preserving video (Fig. 2 and Secs. 3.3 and 3.4). 

3.1. Hand and Object Geometry 

Camera and interaction masks. We reconstruct hands and objects in a gravity-aligned metric world frame. Ego2X uses calibrated intrinsics K and camera poses TW C when avail-able [1, 18]. For uncalibrated video, DROID-calib [17] es-timates K, DROID-SLAM [34] tracks TW C , MoGe-2 [39] recovers metric scale, and GeoCalib [36] aligns gravity. Within tactile contact windows, EgoHOS [46] hand and active-object masks seed SAM 3 [4] video propagation with backward re-anchoring across occlusions. 

Metric hand and object surfaces. WiLoR [27] detects 2D hand boxes that ACE-Ego-Hand [25] completes across oc-clusions, from which EgoHand [6] predicts metric 3D hand skeletons. We fit a per-frame MANO [31] mesh with clip-consistent shape and partition its surface into 24 anatomical regions (Tab. 1). For objects, SAM 3D [10] reconstructs a canonical mesh with an exact non-convex signed distance field (SDF) ϕo. FoundationPose [42] then tracks metric 6-DoF poses TtW O , regularized via SO(3) projection and gravity-aligned symmetry resolution.

3.3. CORE-Driven Interaction Recovery 

3.3.1. Credibility-Aware Decoupled Depth Correction 

Jointly scaling the object from the hand while pulling the hand toward the object induces circular ray-depth drift. We therefore score pose credibility and decouple the object and hand refinement paths. 

Pose credibility. A monotonic GBDT trained via leave-one-clip-out (LOCO) cross-validation maps 29 GT-free contour, multi-view, depth, and temporal features to identify usable object poses ( terr < 30 mm , θsym < 15 ◦). Under monocular scale ambiguity, isotonic-calibrated single-frame confidence peaks at 0.557 , causing hard single-frame gating to starve coverage. Instead, we decouple the two paths without gating the hand per frame.

3.4. URDF Compilation and Simulation Verifica-tion 

To generalize without per-robot tuning, our URDF compiler samples link collision meshes into phalanx/palm or opposed-jaw pad pools with normals, pinch midpoints, aperture maps, and kinematic chains to parameterize Eq. (5). For closed-loop verification in RoboTwin 2.0 [9], the compiler maps object trajectories into the workspace via a height-preserving planar similarity transform, scores TOG across six closure-axis rolls, and applies centroid clamping (clamping antipo-dal grasp centers toward the object support-hull centroid) to generate live object-locked phase waypoints and retain physically passing rollouts for VLA post-training. 

4. Experiments 

We evaluate Ego2X across contact recovery (Sec. 4.2), de-coupled depth correction (Sec. 4.3), cross-morphology retar-geting and URDF compilation (Sec. 4.4), de-skinned com-positing (Sec. 4.5), and closed-loop simulation verification and VLA post-training (Sec. 4.6). 

4.1. Experimental Setup 

Benchmarks and protocol. We evaluate 3D contact and depth accuracy on HOT3D [1] across disjoint develop-ment, confirmation, held-out, and fresh splits, video con-version across 10 manipulation categories on EgoDex [18], and closed-loop execution and VLA post-training in RoboTwin 2.0 [9]. Comparisons pair clips on HOT3D, takes on EgoDex, and scene seeds in RoboTwin 2.0, reporting 10 4-resample cluster-bootstrap 95% CIs, Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, and Holm-adjusted exact McNemar tests. 

Baselines. On EgoDex, Ego2X and official HuRo stage-8 [19] use monocular RGB EgoHand [6] reconstructions, whereas official Ego2Robot [40] uses privileged device-tracked 3D hands. We also evaluate a controlled wrist-following ablation (sharing our front-end, wrist targets, and solver without contact terms in Eq. (5)) and antipodal grip-per baselines on the identical SDF ϕo. Mesh surface clear-ance and closed-loop physics evaluate manipulation inde-pendently of CORE under shared scene mapping, planning, controllers, and verification checks.

5. Conclusion 

We presented Ego2X, an object-centric framework that con-verts monocular egocentric video into physically executable robot demonstrations. Specifically, CORE records object-frame surface anchors, two-part force priors, grasp modes, and interaction states. This unified representation enables de-coupled camera-ray depth correction, cross-morphology re-targeting, de-skinned compositing, URDF compilation, and closed-loop simulation verification across dexterous hands and parallel grippers. To the best of our knowledge, this 8paper is the first to explore post-training embodied models using egocentric videos. We hope this work will open a new avenue for scalable post-training of embodied models by unlocking egocentric human videos. With CORE, Ego2X improves the success rate of the X-VLA policy by 26.5% without relying on expert simulation demonstrations. 

Future Work. We plan to extend Ego2X in three direc-tions. First, contact point localization remains limited by object pose estimation errors under heavy occlusion. Second, CORE models contact loads as visual-kinematic priors rather than sensor-calibrated Newton forces, which we aim to ad-dress via tactile integration. Finally, due to current hardware and schedule constraints, physical robot deployment is still underway and will be completed in future work.
