Abstract
Existing 3D generation methods synthesize visually com-
pelling scenes from fixed inputs, yet struggle to continually
expand and revise a 3D world as new observations arrive.
Iterative approaches often fuse real observations and gener-
ated content into a single representation, treating generated
content as established structure rather than revisable pre-
dictions. Consequently, early generation errors can persist
and accumulate despite new evidence. We propose World-\delta ,
an incremental 3D scene generation framework combining
uncertainty-aware persistent world modeling with delta-
guided diffusion for continual scene expansion and correc-
tion. Our framework comprises three components: 1) Vi-
sual Evidence Encoding integrates geometric and semantic
cues from incoming images into unified world tokens. 2)
Observation-Calibrated World Modeling maintains a per-
sistent latent world state that distinguishes observation-
supported content from provisional completion hypotheses.
New evidence drives selective world deltas to revise con-
flicting hypotheses while preserving well-supported con-
tent. 3) Incremental World Diffusion Generation uses delta-
derived masks to restrict diffusion to regions requiring up-
dates, freezing unchanged regions. Only affected 3D chunks
are decoded and integrated into the existing scene.
To-
gether, these components enable continual world expansion
and revision while reducing redundant generation and pre-
serving scene consistency. Experiments demonstrate the ef-
fectiveness of World-\delta in incremental 3D scene generation,
observation-driven correction, scene consistency, and up-
date efficiency over successive scene updates.

Typically, iterative methods relate new observations to
the current scene through geometric alignment and fusion,

In this work, we introduce World-\delta , an incremental 3D
scene generation framework for continuous observations.
Visual Evidence Encoding fuses geometric and seman-
tic cues into world tokens. Observation-Calibrated World
Modeling maintains an uncertainty-aware persistent state,
inferring selective deltas to integrate new evidence and re-
vise conflicting hypotheses while preserving supported con-
tent. Incremental World Diffusion Generation uses these
deltas to mask diffusion conditioned on the updated state,
freezing unaffected latents. Only changed 3D chunks are
decoded and integrated into the existing scene, enabling ef-
ficient expansion and correction. Our main contributions to
incremental 3D generation are summarized as follows:
• We introduce World-\delta , a framework that continually ex-
pands and corrects a 3D world from incoming RGB ob-
servations, integrating evidence while preserving reliable
history and keeping provisional completions revisable.
• Observation-Calibrated World Modeling maintains an
uncertainty-aware persistent state, comparing new evi-
dence with history to infer selective updates that revise
conflicting hypotheses while retaining supported content.
• Incremental World Diffusion Generation uses state deltas
to guide selective diffusion and decode only affected
chunks, preserving unchanged content and reducing re-
dundant computation during continual world updates.
• We aggregate and adapt large-scale Gaussian datasets for
incremental 3D Gaussian generation. Experiments show
improved rendering quality while balancing observation-
driven correction, historical retention, and update cost.
2. Related Work
3D Generation. Recent advances in 3D and 4D generation
combine structured scene representations with diffusion pri-
ors [1, 5, 16, 17, 26–29, 35, 40]. Lyra-1 [1] distills video
diffusion priors into explicit Gaussian scenes, whereas HY-
World-2.0 [27] combines panorama generation, view ex-
pansion, and Gaussian scene composition to construct com-
plete worlds. Despite producing high-quality results, these
methods offer limited support for observations arriving after
initial generation. Our work aims to integrate such evidence
into existing scenes to enable coherent world expansion.
Persistent World Modeling. Existing methods encode spa-
tial and spatiotemporal context in learned scene representa-
tions [12, 30, 32, 41] or retain observation history through
retrieval and explicit spatial memories [20, 24, 33, 39]. 3D-
Belief [36] maintains explicit beliefs about unobserved re-
gions and updates them with new observations. These ap-
proaches improve spatial consistency, but retaining history
alone does not specify how to reconcile new evidence with
prior hypotheses or localize updates. We calibrate persistent
states using provenance, observation confidence, and gener-
ative uncertainty to distinguish supported content from pro-
visional completions and infer selective updates.
Incremental Diffusion Generation. Incremental 3D dif-
fusion must update scenes while preserving unaffected
content.
Existing pipelines generate complete samples
under fixed conditioning [17] or complete partial scenes
through view-space inpainting and fusion or pixel-space
diffusion [2, 7, 11, 13, 15, 38]. Hole filling can miss er-

4. Experiments
4.1. Implementation Details
Network configuration.
Evidence is encoded as 256-
dimensional world tokens. Each chunk has 83 64-channel
latent cells and up to 4,096 Gaussians. A six-block Trans-
former predicts velocities for 50 Euler steps. The Gaussian
decoder uses two valid 3 × 3 × 3 convolutions, giving a
two-cell dependency radius on the latent grid. Each selected
output chunk is decoded with a two-cell halo that supplies
the required neighboring features. Only the chunk’s owned
Gaussian outputs are retained. Halo cells provide context
without extending the writable region. This bounded de-
pendency determines the affected-chunk selection in Eq. (7)
and supports reuse of unaffected decoded patches.
Training data.
We aggregate training splits from
SceneSplat-49K [21]: 3RScanGS, ARKitScenesGS, Mat-
terport3DGS, HyperSimGS, ReplicaGS, ScanNetPPGS-
V1/V2, ScanNetGS, DL3DV-GS, HoliCityGS, AriaSyn-
theticEnvGS, and crowdsourced scenes. We normalize co-
ordinates and convert scenes into canonical chunk targets,
pairing RGB prefixes with Gaussian and latent supervi-
sion for initialization, expansion, and revisiting. Reference
Gaussians and available geometric labels provide offline su-
pervision. Inference uses only RGB prefixes.
Training configuration. Training covers codec, state, flow,
and sequential calibration stages.
Non-codec stages use
AdamW with learning rate 10−4, weight decay 0.01, and
16-chunk batches. Adaptation uses 10−5. Evidence back-
bones remain frozen. Codec pretraining ramps βKL to 10−4
during the codec’s Gaussian reconstruction training stage.

We evaluate single-image generation and continual RGB
updates with identical observations and query views. All

and evaluation environments. All training is conducted on
eight NVIDIA A100 GPUs, and all inference runs on a sin-

3D Generation Comparison. We compare with Lyra-1 [1],
HY-World-2.0 [27], 3D-Belief [36], InfiniSplat [31], and
SplatAnything [37] on RealEstate10K [42] and Tanks and
Temples [14] to evaluate rendering quality and generation
cost from a single RGB image.
As shown in Table 1,
World-δ achieves favorable overall performance across the
two datasets, with clear advantages on most of the re-
ported measures. Fig. 5 presents qualitative comparisons on
DL3DV [18], RealEstate10K, and ScanNet v2 [8]. While
the baselines often leave substantial holes in unseen re-
gions, World-δ uses learned priors to generate more com-
plete scenes, illustrating scene generation and completion
across varied indoor layouts from the available RGB inputs.
3D Incremental Generation Comparison. We compare
reconstruction baselines S3PO-GS [6] and OF3GS [4], and

As shown in Table 2, World-δ shows consistent advantages
over the reconstruction and generation baselines across the
reported comparisons on both datasets. Fig. 6 illustrates
scene expansion and revision as new observations arrive.
Together, these results support the use of selective world
updates to integrate new evidence with favorable rendering
quality and update cost over successive update rounds.

Dependency-Aware Incremental Decoding. In Fig. 7, we
compare decoder neighborhoods on ScanNet v2 with fixed
latents and a fixed boundary region to assess agreement and
efficiency. Boundary MAE measures depth error against
reference geometry. Maximum RGB discrepancy measures
deviation from full decoding. The default matches full de-
coding’s reported MAE with negligible RGB discrepancy
and lower runtime and memory use. Enlarging the halo
and owner set increases cost without lowering MAE. Zero
halo increases depth error and RGB discrepancy. These re-
sults support selecting owners by network dependencies and
reusing unaffected chunks from the cache. Read-only halo
cells supply context without enlarging the writable mask.
Long-Range Scene Expansion. Fig. 8 presents exploration
with World-δ in two large indoor scenes through succes-
sive observations. When a previously unseen room is ob-
served, our method automatically revises and expands the
scene at room scale, generating plausible spatial transitions
while preserving established content. As exploration pro-
ceeds, newly revealed regions are progressively integrated
into a coherent global environment. These results demon-
strate that World-δ supports both observation-driven correc-
tion and expansion within local scenes and coherent scene
construction over larger spatial extents.
Limitations. World-δ assumes a largely static scene, so ob-
ject motion can be confused with errors in earlier comple-
tions. Fusing observations from different motion states may
8


leave stale geometry and inconsistent appearance. Future
work could explore motion-aware masks to separate dy-
namic regions from background updates and object tracking
to maintain evolving object states.
5. Conclusion
We present World-δ, an observation-driven framework for
continually expanding and correcting generated 3D worlds.
Distinguishing observed evidence from provisional comple-
tions keeps uncertain content revisable while preserving re-
liable history. We encode geometric and semantic evidence
in a persistent state tracking provenance, confidence, and
generative uncertainty. Calibrated state deltas then guide
masked latent diffusion and dependency-aware decoding,
restricting generation and explicit assembly to affected re-
gions. Experiments on single-image generation and con-
tinual updates show improved rendering quality and a fa-
vorable balance of observation-driven correction, historical
retention, and computational cost.
