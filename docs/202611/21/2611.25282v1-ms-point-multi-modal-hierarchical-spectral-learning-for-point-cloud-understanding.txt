ABSTRACT
Point–image alignment learns useful object representations, but different local fea-
ture patterns can produce the same pooled descriptor and receive the same align-
ment signal. We introduce MS-POINT, a cross-modal pretraining framework that
directly supervises intermediate point features and their neighborhood responses.
At selected encoder stages, we compute neighborhood averages and residuals be-
fore pooling and align their branch descriptors with image features. Branch-level
objectives align each representation with its visual target, while cross-level con-
sistency and adaptive fusion coordinate information across branches.
A com-
mon interface of intermediate features and spatial neighborhoods supports both
graph encoders and point Transformers. Evaluations across object classiﬁcation,
few-shot adaptation, dense prediction, and low-label scene learning demonstrate
the transfer value of this design. Same-backbone comparisons and capacity- and
computation-matched controls connect these improvements to the structure of su-
pervision. Together, our ﬁndings support directly supervising intermediate fea-
tures and neighborhood variation to learn transferable, geometry-aware represen-
tations across modalities.

INTRODUCTION
A point cloud and an image observe the same object through different sensing processes. Matching
their representations provides supervision for a 3D encoder that must transfer to tasks with limited
labels. CrossPoint learns this agreement through object-level contrast; MM-Point enriches it with
multiple views and projection spaces (Afham et al., 2022; Yu & Song, 2024). How directly does
object-level alignment constrain the features of neighboring points?
With object-level alignment, the visual target is assigned to the ﬁnal descriptor. Gradients reach the
intermediate layers through this descriptor, so their features receive visual supervision only after
pooling. Different arrangements of neighboring features can yield the same pooled descriptor. Any
projection of that descriptor then gives the same output for both arrangements. This motivates
computing neighborhood responses before pooling and giving each branch its own visual target.
The distinction matters most when the downstream task changes. Recognizing a chair and separating
its legs from its seat require different features. Part segmentation must retain distinctions between
nearby regions even when they yield the same object-level descriptor. We therefore seek intermedi-
ate features that remain useful when the downstream task requires more than object recognition.
A point encoder already computes features at several depths. We use these features as additional
inputs to cross-modal learning. At selected depths, we also compute the neighborhood average and
each point’s deviation from it. Pooling and aligning these responses in separate branches lets image
supervision act on local feature patterns as well as the ﬁnal object descriptor.
We introduce MS-POINT to supervise selected encoder stages and the neighborhood variation
within their features. Selected encoder states receive direct visual targets, while complementary
neighborhood-consensus and deviation responses describe the neighborhood average and the resid-

• Complementary graph responses, semantic-depth targets, and joint branch/fusion objectives that

• Evidence from classiﬁcation, few-shot adaptation, segmentation, and low-label scenes, sup-
ported by same-backbone and capacity- and computation-matched interventions.

RELATED WORK
Hierarchical representations and prediction targets.
PointNet++ aggregates nested neighbor-
hoods, while DGCNN constructs dynamic feature-space relations (Qi et al., 2017b; Wang et al.,
2019). Thus, locality and hierarchy are already present in many point encoders. Point-BERT and
Point-MAE introduce masked point-token learning, and Point-M2AE extends it across scales (Yu
et al., 2022; Pang et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2022). These methods make clear that the prediction
target, not only the backbone, shapes the learned representation.
Beyond a single reconstruction objective.
PointGPT studies autoregressive point-patch genera-
tion (Chen et al., 2023); PointDif uses diffusion-based denoising (Zheng et al., 2024). PCP-MAE
examines the shortcut introduced by supplying masked patch centers to a decoder and instead pre-
dicts centers (Zhang et al., 2024). This connects to our motivation: the pretraining objective de-
termines which features receive direct supervision. TPM expands masked learning through mul-
tiple masks (Liu et al., 2025), while Point-PQAE uses cross reconstruction between decoupled
views (Zhang et al., 2025b).
Point–image supervision.
CrossPoint and MM-Point provide the closest object-level contrastive
references (Afham et al., 2022; Yu & Song, 2024). I2P-MAE and Inter-MAE exploit images within
masked learning, and ReCon combines generative and contrastive signals (Zhang et al., 2023; Liu
et al., 2024; Qi et al., 2023). These methods establish visual guidance and multi-level learning

CLASSIFICATION AND RECENT BASELINES
Tables 1–2 compare synthetic ModelNet objects (Wu et al., 2015) and real ScanObjectNN scans (Uy
et al., 2019). DGCNN-based MS-Point reaches 93.6/96.5 on ModelNet40/10 and 89.6 on ScanOb-
jectNN, exceeding published MM-Point by 1.2/1.1/1.8 points. Controlled gains over MM-Point are

elNet40/10 and 90.4 on ScanObjectNN, improving over the evaluated masked-learning systems,
including Inter-MAE and Point-MAE with TPM. Accuracy improves on both synthetic objects and
real scans.

BACKBONE GENERALITY AND STRUCTURAL CONTROLS
With the point Transformer and ResNet-50 ﬁxed, hierarchy, responses, and fusion progressively
improve three downstream tasks (Table 8). The full objective gains 1.1 ModelNet40 points, 2.1

perform duplicated features by 0.7/1.4 classiﬁcation points and 0.6 part mIoU. Random neighbor-

CONCLUSION
MS-Point adds cross-modal supervision at selected encoder stages. Separate branches pool and
project the neighborhood averages and residuals, then align the resulting descriptors with image
features. Controlled comparisons and transfer evaluations support this design across graph and
token encoders. The resulting principle extends naturally toward local objectives in which geometry
and visibility identify the information shared across modalities.
The results support supervising intermediate features and neighborhood responses alongside the ﬁnal
object representation. Explicit geometric correspondence and visibility provide a basis for extending
these targets to dense prediction.

SUPERVISION LOCATION AND OBSERVABLE STATISTICS
Let Hl(X; θ) be the feature map at stage l, Φ an object-pooling operator, and ql a projection
head. Final-only alignment evaluates qgΦ(Hg), whereas a hierarchy objective evaluates a family
{qlΦ(Hl)}. The ﬁnal loss already updates earlier stages through the chain rule. Direct targets add
stage-speciﬁc readouts and gradient paths. Concatenating stage features before pooling gives the
global loss a single descriptor, whereas branch-level targets supervise each selected stage directly.
Matching the number of active projection heads at the ﬁnal map and at intermediate stages pro-
vides a control for supervision location. Active heads contribute to optimization, whereas unused
parameters leave the training path unchanged.

The ﬁve objectives act simultaneously on shared encoder parameters, so their effects depend on
their interactions. Same-branch contrast encourages agreement across point augmentations. Cross-
level contrast coordinates features across depths with a default weight of λcross = 0.4. Branch-
level image alignment supplies direct visual targets, while fused alignment constrains the aggregate.
When branch alignment is removed, the fused objective continues to provide image supervision.
Soft directional decoupling acts in the projected space, where each response can retain object identity
while expressing a different direction. Appendix J derives its tangential gradient. We evaluate this
mechanism through transfer accuracy and response similarity together: we seek complementary
responses that improve transfer while preserving shared object semantics.
