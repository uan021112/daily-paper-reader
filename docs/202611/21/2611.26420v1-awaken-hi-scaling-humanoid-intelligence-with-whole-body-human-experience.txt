Abstract
A central goal of humanoid robotics is for robots to perform the everyday work people do in homes
and workplaces. Existing egocentric video datasets cover many of these activities but provide little
synchronized whole-body motion data for learning loco-manipulation at scale. We build Awaken-Human,
which pairs approximately 50,000 hours of egocentric stereo video with synchronized whole-body human
motion and covers more than 100 skills. Building on this, we also introduce Awaken-HI (Humanoid
Intelligence), a vision-language-action model for humanoid loco-manipulation that jointly predicts
original human motion and retargeted robot actions through a shared action expert. We show that
loco-manipulation skills transfer more effectively from people to humanoids when human trajectories
directly supervise the shared action expert. Increasing human pretraining data reduces held-out robot
action prediction error according to an empirical scaling law and raises the mean success rate on
downstream real-robot tasks. After full-corpus pretraining and T800 adaptation, Awaken-HI achieves a
mean success rate of 93.75% across eight individual tasks and outperforms GR00T N1.7 and 𝜋0.5 in both
individual and long-horizon evaluations. The same policy generalizes to objects unseen during robot
post-training and to new pairings of familiar objects and destinations.

Awaken-HI: Scaling Humanoid Intelligence with Whole-Body Human Experience
1. Introduction
Humanoid robots could help people with a wide range of tasks in homes and workplaces. Many require whole-
body loco-manipulation, in which the robot coordinates its steps and posture with its hand movements [17, 24].
Vision-language-action (VLA) models [5, 16, 31] and world action models [22, 47, 48] have shown that robots
can learn diverse manipulation skills from demonstrations. For humanoids, however, the cost of whole-body
teleoperation limits the range of tasks and environments represented in robot demonstrations [13, 49].
Human experience of everyday activity offers a more scalable alternative to robot teleoperation. Recent
work draws on the variety of activities and environments in these videos [43, 46] to pretrain manipulation
policies [23, 41, 52]. Despite this diversity, visual observations alone provide no explicit targets for whole-body
motion. Prior work therefore retargets recorded human motion to obtain robot action targets [7, 21]. However,
differences in body proportions and joint structure can cause the resulting postures and hand trajectories to
deviate from those in the human demonstration [2]. These deviations motivate retaining the original human
motion as a training signal alongside the retargeted robot actions. Using that signal at scale requires egocentric
video paired with synchronized whole-body motion across diverse tasks and environments. Yet many large
egocentric datasets provide video alone or primarily track the head and hands [11, 15], while datasets such as
Nymeria [28] offer only a few hundred hours of paired recordings.
We address this data gap with Awaken-Human, a dataset for humanoid pretraining that pairs approximately
50,000 hours of egocentric stereo video with synchronized whole-body human motion. Collected under a
common protocol using lightweight wearable sensors, the demonstrations cover more than 100 skills across
homes, workplaces, retail spaces, and storage areas. We reconstruct 3D hand motion from the video and
annotate the demonstrations with subtask descriptions and frame-level depth maps. For each demonstration,
we retain the original human trajectory alongside the corresponding retargeted robot actions.
Building on Awaken-Human, we introduce Awaken-HI, a VLA model for humanoid loco-manipulation. By
predicting human motion alongside retargeted robot actions, its shared action expert receives direct supervision
from the original human trajectories. During pretraining, the expert also predicts camera-frame wrist poses,
while separate heads estimate current and future depth and describe the current subtask. We then post-train
the policy on approximately 150 hours of T800 teleoperation data using the same robot action interface.
We observe that Awaken-HI’s robot action prediction error on held-out data follows an empirical scaling law,
decreasing as the pretraining set grows from 5,000 to 50,000 hours of human demonstrations. With the same
T800 demonstrations for post-training, more human pretraining data also raises the policy’s mean success rate
on three household tasks. Controlled ablations show that adding human motion supervision reduces action
prediction error and improves real-robot task success compared with pretraining on retargeted actions alone.
After full-corpus pretraining, we post-train Awaken-HI on the same T800 demonstrations used for GR00T
N1.7 [31] and 𝜋0.5 [16]. Awaken-HI outperforms both baselines on all eight individual loco-manipulation
tasks, averaging 93.75% success. It retains this advantage on long-horizon industrial and household tasks. The
policy also follows instructions that pair familiar objects and destinations in new ways and applies a learned
manipulation skill to objects unseen during robot post-training.
Our main contributions are as follows:
• We introduce Awaken-HI, a VLA model for humanoid loco-manipulation that jointly predicts original
human motion and robot actions through a shared action expert.
• We build Awaken-Human, a dataset pairing 50,000 hours of egocentric stereo video with synchronized
whole-body human motion across diverse tasks and environments.
• We find that as human pretraining data grows, action prediction error decreases according to an empirical
scaling law and real-robot success rates rise. Awaken-HI also generalizes to new pairings of familiar
objects and destinations and to objects unseen during robot post-training.

2. Related Work
2.1. Foundation Models for Robot Control
Generalist robot policies map observations and instructions to actions across a wide range of manipulation
tasks [5, 6, 16, 20, 31, 39, 44]. For a humanoid, loco-manipulation additionally requires moving and staying
balanced while adjusting posture to reach objects at different heights and distances. One line of work separates
the control interfaces for the upper and lower body. WholeBodyVLA [17] and Ψ0 [41] predict upper-body
actions and coarse base commands, which a locomotion controller converts into lower-body motion. More recent
approaches predict whole-body motion, enabling the policy to learn coordination between locomotion and
manipulation [21, 24, 51]. Our Awaken-HI follows this approach and learns motion priors through large-scale
human pretraining.
2.2. Egocentric Data and Motion Alignment
Egocentric datasets have grown rapidly in scale, but largely at the cost of recorded whole-body motion or
a consistent protocol. Nymeria [28] uses an inertial motion-capture suit, and HumanVerse in 𝜆0 [21] uses
a headset with body-worn trackers, yet each covers only a few hundred hours. Larger datasets such as
Ego4D [11], EgoVerse [33], and EgoDex [15] provide video with at most head, hand, or upper-body poses,
while Ego-Exo4D [12] derives body pose from exocentric cameras. Pretraining corpora such as EgoScale [52]
and Ego4WAM [38] exceed 10,000 hours by aggregating many sources, which differ in sensors and annotation
pipelines. We build Awaken-Human to fill this gap with 50,000 hours of egocentric video and whole-body
motion recorded by wearable trackers, all captured under the same protocol. Table 1 compares Awaken-Human
with other egocentric datasets in terms of scale, capture setup, and annotation coverage.
To construct robot-action supervision, human motion is mapped to robot embodiments through direct joint
mappings [10] or geometric retargeting, as in GMR [2]. Differences in body proportions and joint structure
can alter the resulting postures and end-effector trajectories [2, 14]. Controller dynamics introduce further
discrepancies between references and execution [7, 18]. Controller-aware refinement [7, 50] and causal state
reconstruction, as in EgoAlign [18], improve the consistency of human-derived supervision with robot behavior.
Our annotation pipeline replaces noisy hand annotations with 3D hand poses reconstructed from egocentric
video and uses them to refine end-effector alignment, reducing end-effector errors relative to GMR.

2.3. Learning from Egocentric Demonstrations
Human demonstrations are typically used in embodied learning to supervise perception, world modeling, and
action learning. R3M [30] pretrains a visual encoder on egocentric human video so that manipulation policies
can be learned from fewer robot demonstrations. World-action models instead use future frames as prediction
targets, learning how scenes change as people interact with objects even without action labels [38, 51]. When
hand or body trajectories are available, human demonstrations can also supervise action learning [34, 41, 45].
EgoMimic [19] aligns human hand trajectories with robot end-effector trajectories in a common coordinate
frame to train a shared manipulation policy. EgoScale [52] and 𝜆0 [21] retarget human motion into robot
hand or whole-body actions and train on these labels. However, constrained by morphological differences,
retargeting inevitably introduces global errors and distorted postures [2, 7]. We therefore train Awaken-HI on
the original human motion together with the retargeted robot actions, learning motion priors directly from the
demonstrations.
3. Awaken-Human
3.1. Large-Scale Egocentric Corpus
We build Awaken-Human to study how the scale of human demonstrations affects humanoid pretraining. The
corpus pairs 50,000 hours of egocentric video with synchronized whole-body human motion recorded by
wearable sensors, making it the largest such dataset to our knowledge. It comprises approximately 17.02
million episodes, 5.41 billion pose frames, and 11.18 million valid language instructions.
Our setup consists of a PICO headset and five body-worn trackers, synchronously recording stereo RGB video
and skeletal sequences from PICO’s body-tracking system [7, 18]. Collectors follow task goals and adapt
their walking paths, stance, and grasping strategies to the scene, preserving natural repositioning and motion
transitions within continuous interactions. The corresponding task instructions are retained alongside raw
video, motion trajectories, and camera and device parameters for subsequent annotation and alignment.
The demonstrations cover more than 100 skills across homes, workplaces, retail spaces, and storage areas.

3.2. Egocentric Data Annotation
We develop an annotation pipeline for whole-body egocentric demonstrations that first undistorts raw stereo
fisheye images using the camera calibration parameters. It then performs temporal segmentation and semantic
annotation, visual hand-motion reconstruction, and stereo depth estimation to produce time-aligned language,
human motion, and depth supervision for pretraining.
Semantic annotation. We fine-tuned a vision-language model (VLM) for egocentric demonstration annotation.
The model produces initial annotations comprising temporal segment boundaries, segment-level language
descriptions, and atomic action labels selected from a predefined library. Human reviewers verify and correct
these initial annotations to produce segment-level semantic labels for training and data selection.
Visual hand re-annotation. The PICO hand annotations contain tracking gaps, positional errors, and temporal
jitter that affect supervision of fine-grained hand motion. To address these issues, we use our in-house 3D
hand reconstruction model to generate MANO-based 21-keypoint hand skeleton annotations from egocentric
video [36]. The model generates hand-quality labels alongside the skeleton annotations, using hand visibility
ratios to identify low-quality segments with incomplete or occluded hand observations during manipulation.
We use these quality labels to exclude low-quality segments and replace the original PICO hand annotations
with the retained reconstructions. These annotations provide human hand motion supervision and reference
targets for subsequent robot end-effector alignment.

3.3. Human-to-Humanoid Alignment and Dataset Construction
We align annotated human demonstrations with the T800 action space through whole-body retargeting and
end-effector refinement. The resulting trajectories undergo quality filtering, corpus curation, and dataset
partitioning, while the corresponding human motion annotations are retained for joint pretraining.
Robot action target construction. We develop a T800-specific retargeting pipeline based on GMR [2]. We
first scale the recorded human skeletons to T800’s height and use visually re-annotated hand motion to align
the end-effector references. We then retarget the aligned human motion to robot root and joint trajectories
and optimize wrist orientations to refine end-effector alignment. The retargeted trajectories are combined
with bilateral hand commands mapped from reconstructed hand configurations to form robot action targets.
Compared with the original GMR pipeline, our pipeline reduces mean end-effector position error from 16.423
cm to 2.422 cm and mean orientation error from 39.590° to 15.596°, averaged over both end effectors and
all valid frames. We further inspect retargeted trajectories for root discontinuities, joint-limit violations, and
excessive wrist alignment errors, and exclude low-quality segments.
Corpus curation and dataset splits. We curate the full corpus to reduce redundancy and balance scene
proportions. We extract V-JEPA 2 [3] video features and cluster them in a shared representation space.
Together with segment-level semantic labels, these clusters guide the removal of redundant demonstrations
and adjustment of scene proportions. We then use the same visual and semantic information to select a fixed
held-out validation set and construct nested training subsets of increasing scale from the remaining data.

observations from selected clusters.

all parameters of these modules on 150 hours of industrial and household teleoperation data using only the
robot action flow-matching loss ℒact.

Deployment. At deployment, we integrate the robot action branch’s velocity field from noise and inverse-

5. Experiments
We first study how scaling whole-body egocentric pretraining data affects action prediction and downstream
task performance. We then compare Awaken-HI pretrained on the full corpus with VLA baselines on whole-body
loco-manipulation tasks and evaluate its generalization. Finally, we ablate human motion, wrist pose, and
depth supervision to assess their contributions.
Tasks. We evaluate eight whole-body loco-manipulation tasks illustrated in Figure 5. Medicine Box Transfer
requires the robot to walk to a shelf, pick up a medicine box, and place it in the bin. Black Material Picking
requires the robot to walk to a cart and pick up the black material. Black Material Shelf Stocking requires the
robot to turn while holding the black material, place it on the middle shelf, withdraw its hand, and turn back.
White Material Picking requires the robot to walk to a cart and pick up the white material. White Material Shelf
Stocking requires the robot to turn while holding the white material, place it on the upper shelf, withdraw its
hand, and turn back.
Bimanual Object Storage requires the robot to walk to a table and pick up a cup and a toy car with one hand
each. The robot then turns to the storage table and places the cup in the pink bin and the toy car in the green
bin. Garment Hanging requires the robot to walk to a table, pick up a garment, and turn toward a rack to hang
it. Laundry Basket Relocation requires the robot to walk to a wardrobe, open its door, and crouch to lift a
laundry basket. The robot then steps backward and carries the basket with both hands before placing it on the
floor to the right of the wardrobe.
For long-horizon evaluation, we chain Black Material Picking, Black Material Shelf Stocking, White Material
Picking, and White Material Shelf Stocking into an industrial stocking task. Bimanual Object Storage, Garment
Hanging, and Laundry Basket Relocation form the household tidying task, in that order.
Experimental setup. We conduct experiments on EngineAI T800 using approximately 150 hours of teleoper-
ation demonstrations for robot post-training. Each trajectory synchronizes camera observations, measured
robot states, and control targets, with language instructions. Our baselines are 𝜋0.5 [16] and GR00T N1.7 [31].
Each method uses one multitask policy for all eight tasks, post-trained on this dataset for 30,000 steps with a
global batch size of 256. For the main comparison, we initialize Awaken-HI from the checkpoint pretrained on
Awaken-Human. The baselines otherwise follow their officially recommended configurations, and all models
run inference on a single NVIDIA RTX 5090 GPU.
Evaluation protocol. We evaluate each method in ten trials per individual task and per long-horizon task.
For each individual or long-horizon task, methods share ten predefined initial configurations specifying robot
poses, object states, and target regions, with variations in starting position and object placement. Standalone
evaluations of Black Material Shelf Stocking and White Material Shelf Stocking begin with the corresponding
material already held. An individual trial succeeds when the robot autonomously completes the task’s core
manipulation goals. In each long-horizon trial, the robot and scene are not reset between component tasks, and
success requires consecutive completion of all component tasks. The main comparison covers all eight individual
tasks and both long-horizon tasks; real-robot evaluations for scaling and auxiliary-supervision ablations are
restricted to the three household tasks.

6. Conclusion
We presented Awaken-HI, which jointly predicts original human motion and retargeted robot actions through a
shared action expert. We constructed Awaken-Human, pairing 50,000 hours of egocentric stereo video with
synchronized whole-body motion. Scaling pretraining data reduces held-out action prediction error in line with
an empirical power law and improves downstream task success. After post-training, a single policy achieves a
mean success rate of 93.75% across eight individual loco-manipulation tasks. Original human motion thus
remains an informative pretraining target even after retargeting.
