Abstract. Urban photogrammetric point clouds are commonly pro-cessed as local 3D patches, limiting the context available for distin-guishing geometrically similar categories. Orthographic projection of the same data yields a co-registered digital orthophoto map (DOM)/RGB-D representation that supports efficient large-context modeling but com-presses vertical geometry. Existing projection–point methods mainly fuse these two representations at the feature level while retaining a classifier shared across all samples. We present the Aerial Content-Aware Sample-Adaptive Classifier (ACASAC), which complements feature fusion with decision-level adaptation. A point-aligned aerial region bridges the full aerial crop and local 3D patch. ACASAC models scene-to-local varia-tion within the projection and the representation discrepancy between aligned 2D and 3D supports, then transfers both relations to refine the local point class representation. The refined representation, scene-level aerial context, and fixed fused-head prior jointly form a classifier for the current sample. ACASAC improves the fixed dual-representation head by 2.1 and 2.7 mIoU points on SensatUrban and UrbanBIS, respectively, and by 2.1–2.3 points across four tested encoder combinations. 

Keywords: urban point cloud semantic segmentation · sample-adaptive classifier · digital orthophoto map · class prototype · multimodal fusion 

## 1 Introduction 

Semantic segmentation of urban photogrammetric point clouds is fundamental to 3D scene understanding in applications such as autonomous systems, digi-tal twins, and smart-city management. Urban scenes cover substantially larger spatial extents, exhibit strongly non-uniform point densities, and contain seman-tic categories that are difficult to distinguish from local geometry alone. Traffic roads, footpaths, and parking areas, for example, may all appear as locally flat

Reliable urban point-cloud segmentation therefore requires both fine-grained 3D structure and contextual information beyond a sampled point neighborhood. A photogrammetric point cloud can be represented either as irregular 3D points or as a regular DOM/RGB-D grid generated by orthographic projection. The point representation preserves local geometry but is typically processed within spatially limited patches, whereas the projected representation enables efficient large-context modeling at the cost of compressed vertical structure. Both representations therefore provide complementary spatial organizations of a common source rather than independent observations. Projection-based methods use this complementarity to extend contextual rea-soning beyond local 3D neighborhoods. BEVSeg [33] performs semantic infer-ence in a bird’s-eye-view space, while Orth. [17] predicts from orthographic RGB and elevation images before transferring the resulting labels back to 3D points. Dual-branch approaches retain the point representation: APNet [22] introduces geometry-aware 2D–3D fusion, and DAPSS [18] incorporates pretrained DOM guidance and adaptive multimodal fusion. These methods improve representa-tion learning, but their final classifier is generally shared across samples.

These methods mainly focus on representation-level fusion, while their fi-nal classifier is generally shared across all samples. Feature fusion provides each point with additional context, but it does not explicitly adapt the class repre-sentations used for prediction. This limitation is relevant to urban scenes, where category presence, frequency, co-occurrence, and spatial layout vary consider-ably across regions. A globally fixed classifier must accommodate these hetero-geneous sample distributions using a single set of decision directions, which may be suboptimal for locally ambiguous categories. Although sample-adaptive and prototype-based classifiers have been explored in image and point-cloud seg-mentation [13,19,21,28,31,32], adapting a local point classifier using a broader point-cloud-derived projection has received less attention. Directly relating the full projected crop to the local point patch is non-trivial because they differ simultaneously in spatial support and representation form. The former covers a broader region through a regular 2D grid, whereas the latter describes a smaller region through irregular 3D samples. We therefore introduce the point-aligned local projected region as an intermediate bridge. The method constructs class prototypes from the full projected crop, the projected region aligned with the sampled points, and the local point features. The relation be-tween the two projected supports characterizes the change from local to broader context, while the relation between the aligned projected and point supports describes how the same local region is represented in 2D and 3D. These two relations are transferred to refine the local point class representation, which is combined with scene-level aerial context and the fixed fused-head classifier prior. Fig. 1 provides a prototype-based visualization of this motivation. The left panel shows multimodal fusion forms meaningful class clusters at the dataset level, while several categories remain close in the two-dimensional embedding. The right panel illustrates how the adaptive prototypes for one sample are dis-placed from the fixed classifier prototypes. While t-SNE does not directly reflect separability in the original high-dimensional space, these observations are con-sistent with the sample-dependent feature variation considered here. The main contributions are summarized as follows: 

– We formulate urban photogrammetric point-cloud segmentation as adual-representation decision-adaptation problem. In addition to fusing point-cloud-derived projection features with local 3D features, the proposed method adapts the classifier to the semantic composition of the current projection-crop–point-patch sample. 

– We introduce a point-aligned local projected representation as a bridge be-tween broader projected context and local 3D observations. It organizes their discrepancy into a cross-scale relation within the projected representation and a locally aligned projection–point relation, which are transferred to cor-rect the local point class representation while retaining the fixed classifier prior. 

– Extensive experiments on SensatUrban and UrbanBIS demonstrate that ACASAC consistently outperforms matched fixed classifiers, CAC, and SSA-Seg under the same multimodal fusion setting. Ablation studies further support the contributions of scene-level aerial context, cross-modal relation transfer, and prototype-guided training. 2 Related Work 

2.1 Point-based Methods 

Point-based methods directly learn geometric representations from irregu-lar point clouds. PointNet [14] introduces permutation-invariant point-wise learning, while PointNet++ [15] extends it with hierarchical local aggrega-tion. KPConv [20] employs learnable kernel-point convolutions, and Point Transformer [29] models local interactions with self-attention. For large-scale scenes, RandLA-Net [8] greatly improves efficiency through random sampling and lightweight local aggregation, inspiring subsequent urban point-cloud methods [3,5,6,24,25]. Nevertheless, point-based networks mainly aggregate information within local 3D neighborhoods, making large-context reasoning relatively expensive. 

2.2 Hybrid Segmentation Methods 

Hybrid methods exploit complementary image context and 3D geome-try [2,12,27]. On the one hand, KPRNet [10] maps features from 2D projections back to 3D space, while 3D-MiniNet [1] utilizes structured projections for fast processing. Furthermore, Robert et al. [16] learn to aggregate multi-view image features based on viewing conditions and benchmark different fusion stages. When applied to aerial urban scenes, BEVSeg [33] pioneered the projection of point clouds into a digital orthophoto map (DOM). Subsequently, Orth. [17] fused DOM and elevation maps using KNN for 2D-to-3D back-projection. Along similar lines, APNet [22] achieves geometry-aware multimodal fusion, whereas DAPSS [18] incorporates pretrained DOM priors with adaptive fusion. Nonetheless, the aforementioned approaches predominantly optimize feature extraction and fusion, leaving the final classification still governed by globally shared parameters. 

2.3 Dynamic Classifiers 

Recent studies [11,19,28,30] revisit the classification stage by constructing class representations from contextual information. ISNet [9] introduces class-level contextual representations to enhance pixel features. CAC [21] generates sample-adaptive semantic prototypes from coarse predictions and contextual features, with a ground-truth-guided classifier providing distillation supervision during training. SSA-Seg [13] further models both semantic and spatial prototype adaptation: coarse masks guide class-wise semantic and spatial centers, while a training-only teacher constrains adaptation through response-, semantic-, and spatial-domain distillation. ECAC [19] additionally employs a memory bank to construct representative global class prototypes. For 3D point clouds, NAPL [31] predicts an adaptive number of prototypes for intra-class geometric diversity. In contrast, ACASAC conditions a local point classifier on a broader projection and transfers cross-support and locally aligned projection–point relations. 3 Method

The core idea of SAC is to transfer two complementary relations: the scene-to-local discrepancy within the aerial modality and the local discrepancy between the aerial and point-cloud modalities. These relations are anchored at the local point-cloud prototypes to predict scene-conditioned point-cloud prototypes. The scene-level and local aerial prototypes Asa and Ala share the image-branch classifier and projector, whereas the local point-cloud prototype Alp uses a separate point-branch classifier and projector. Applying Eq. (1) to the full aerial sample, its point-aligned local support, and the local point features yields Asa , Ala , and

Oracle-guided prototype supervision. The adaptive prototypes are esti-mated from soft coarse predictions and may therefore inherit errors from the early predictions. To provide an ideal training target, we construct a training-only oracle prototype Ao from the fused point features f f and their point-wise ground-truth labels Y f . Specifically, the one-hot ground-truth assignments re-place the coarse probabilities p in Eq. (1) to compute the oracle class centers CoP ,which are combined with the fused-head prior and mapped by an oracle-specific projector as Ao = θo(CoP ⊕Cf ). Because θo is independent of the student projec-tors, the oracle prediction is explicitly optimized by cross-entropy. The resulting Ao serves as a ground-truth-conditioned upper-bound target for the dynamic classifier, representing the ideal scene-adaptive prototype under the available labels. Following CAC [21], Ao then supervises the scene-level aerial prototype Asa ,the local point-cloud prototype Alp , and the final dynamic classifier As through the class-wise KL-divergence paths marked by red lightning in Fig. 2. ## 4 Experiments 

4.1 Experimental Setup Evaluation Metrics. We adopt overall accuracy (OA) and mean intersection over union (mIoU) for evaluation. 

Datasets. SensatUrban [7] is a UAV-based urban photogrammetric point-cloud dataset covering 7.64 km 2 in three UK cities and 13 semantic classes. We use the official split of 33 training, 4 validation, and 6 test blocks. UrbanBIS [23] contains nearly 2.5 billion annotated points from six metropolitan scenes, cov-ering approximately 10.78 km 2 and seven classes. We use its official split of 106 training, 39 validation, and 48 test scenes. 

Implementation Details. Following DAPSS [18], we adopt the same pro-tocol to construct aligned image–point cloud pairs. SensatUrban is voxelized at 0.2 m, while UrbanBIS uses its official 0.3 m resolution, No additional data augmentation is applied.. During training, each sample contains 10 , 000 locally sampled points and a corresponding 512 ×512 image crop. All models are trained on a single NVIDIA RTX 4090 for 100 epochs with a batch size of 16 using Adam with an initial learning rate of 0.01 , and ExponentialLR with a decay factor of

4.2 Quantitative And Qualitative Results 

Figures 4 and 5 illustrate how the sample-adaptive head changes local predic-tions. On SensatUrban, the fixed-head baseline labels the surface beneath a car as parking, whereas ACASAC recovers the correct class by using surrounding context. On UrbanBIS, ACASAC produces cleaner boundaries in noisy residen-tial and parking scenes, especially in mixed-class regions. These examples are consistent with scene-conditioned classification reducing the influence of locally atypical features; they provide qualitative evidence rather than isolating this mechanism. 4.3 Ablation Study 

Table 3 evaluates the prototype information used by ACASAC. Starting from the fixed classifier prior at 65.71% mIoU, introducing the local aerial and lo-cal point-cloud prototypes individually increases performance to 66.88% and 66.95%, respectively. Their direct combination reaches 67.27%, indicating mod-est complementarity between the two locally aligned representations. Among the individual prototypes, the scene-level aerial prototype achieves the highest result of 67.31%, which is consistent with the broader projected support providing a more effective contextual cue. Directly combining the local point-cloud and scene-level aerial prototypes without the local aerial bridge yields 67.43% mIoU. Adding the local aerial pro-totype increases this result to 67.76%, supporting the usefulness of the point-aligned aerial representation in connecting the local point and broader aerial supports. This comparison nevertheless evaluates direct prototype fusion and does not independently isolate the causal contribution of the bridge. Within the relation-transfer variants, using only the scene-to-local or the locally aligned cross-modal relation obtains 67.03% and 67.15% mIoU, respec-tively. Jointly modeling both relations raises performance to 67.64%, exceeding the two single-relation variants by 0.61 and 0.49 points. The complete ACASAC configuration further incorporates the scene-level aerial prototype and achieves the highest tested result of 68.18%. It outperforms the relation-transferred pro-totype alone by 0.54 points and the direct concatenation of all raw prototypes by 0.42 points. These single-run results are consistent with the two relations providing complementary information and with structured relation transfer of-fering a useful inductive bias over direct prototype concatenation; they do not establish statistically significant or independently causal effects. ## 5 Conclusion 

In this paper, we present the Aerial Content-Aware Sample-Adaptive Classifier (ACASAC) for multimodal urban point-cloud segmentation. ACASAC combines the fixed fused-head prior with scene-level aerial and relation-transferred point-cloud prototypes to adapt the classifier to each paired sample. Experiments on SensatUrban and UrbanBIS, together with prototype and encoder ablations, demonstrate consistent improvements over matched fixed and adaptive heads within the evaluated settings.
