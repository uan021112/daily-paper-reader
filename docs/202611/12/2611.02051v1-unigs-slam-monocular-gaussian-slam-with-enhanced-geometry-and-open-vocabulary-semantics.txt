ABSTRACT
Monocular semantic SLAM aims to reconstruct scene geometry and build se-
mantic maps from RGB observations. Existing Gaussian-based methods often
attach semantic labels or features to evolving Gaussian primitives, making se-
mantic fusion difficult to maintain and increasing reconstruction overhead. Hy-
brid Gaussian–voxel representations provide more stable semantic support, but
often rely on RGB-D input or known poses and do not revise historical seman-
tic evidence after geometry updates. We present UniGS-SLAM, an RGB-only
framework that decouples open-vocabulary semantics from Gaussian reconstruc-
tion while preserving bidirectional interaction. Foundation priors enhance monoc-
ular depth and pose estimation, while semantic observations are fused into an inde-
pendent sparse voxel map with observation provenance, enabling historical contri-
butions to be revised as reconstruction evolves. The fused semantics further guide
Gaussian filtering and local surface refinement. Experiments on Replica, Scan-
Net, and KITTI-360 show leading tracking, rendering, and geometric reconstruc-
tion performance among representative RGB-only methods, together with strong
open-set semantic mapping on Replica and ScanNet. We also achieve a 2.37× re-
construction speedup over the fastest compared semantic SLAM baseline. Project
page: https://3dagentworld.github.io/UniGS-SLAM/.

INTRODUCTION
Recent advances in 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) (Zhao et al., 2026a) have enabled efficient and
high-fidelity SLAM systems with explicit scene representations and real-time rendering capabili-
ties (Matsuki et al., 2024; Sandstr¨om et al., 2025). Beyond geometric reconstruction, embodied AI
applications require semantic maps that associate open-vocabulary concepts with objects and their
spatial locations, enabling tasks such as language-guided navigation, object search, and interac-
tion (Yang et al., 2025a; Deng et al., 2025). Building such semantic-aware maps from a single RGB
camera is particularly challenging, as semantic understanding relies on accurate geometry while the
underlying scene representation continuously evolves during SLAM optimization (Yoo et al., 2025).
Early semantic Gaussian SLAM methods attach semantic labels or feature representations directly
to Gaussian primitives, enabling unified reconstruction and semantic understanding (Li et al., 2024b;
Zhu et al., 2025). More recent approaches incorporate foundation-model semantics to support open-
vocabulary scene understanding through explicit Gaussian labels or semantic feature representa-
tions (Yang et al., 2025a; Yan et al., 2026). However, tightly coupling semantics with Gaussian
primitives introduces two fundamental limitations. First, Gaussian primitives are continuously opti-
mized, split, and pruned during reconstruction, making their spatial support unstable and complicat-
ing consistent semantic fusion for the same physical surfaces. Second, semantic attributes become
part of the Gaussian optimization and rendering pipeline, introducing additional computational over-
head and limiting the efficiency of online semantic reconstruction.
A natural solution is to decouple semantic representation from Gaussian primitives using more sta-
ble spatial structures. Hybrid Gaussian–voxel approaches maintain semantics in structured voxel
spaces, providing stable units for multi-view semantic fusion (Yang et al., 2025b; Deng et al., 2025).
However, existing systems typically rely on RGB-D observations and accurate camera poses, which
limits their applicability to monocular settings. Moreover, incremental semantic fusion does not
explicitly revise historical semantic evidence when backend SLAM optimization updates previous
poses or geometry. As a result, semantic observations fused under outdated geometry may remain
inconsistent even after the underlying reconstruction is corrected.
To address these challenges, we propose UniGS-SLAM, which decouples semantic representation
from Gaussian optimization while preserving bidirectional interaction between semantics and geom-
etry. Gaussian primitives remain dedicated to geometric reconstruction, whereas open-vocabulary
semantics are fused in an independent sparse voxel map with observation-level provenance, allow-
ing semantic evidence to be revised after geometric updates. Meanwhile, the fused semantic infor-
mation is further used to refine Gaussian geometry through semantic-guided filtering and surface
refinement.
Specifically, we first enhance monocular Gaussian SLAM with foundation-model geometry priors
through geometric context selection, anchor alignment, confidence-aware depth fusion, and long-
horizon pose guidance, providing reliable geometry for semantic lifting. We then maintain open-
vocabulary semantics in a spatially decoupled sparse voxel representation, where observation-level
contributions can be updated when SLAM corrects historical poses or depths. Finally, the semantic
map feeds back into reconstruction by guiding floater removal and local surface refinement, allowing
semantic understanding to improve Gaussian geometry.
Extensive experiments demonstrate that our RGB-only framework achieves strong performance in
geometric reconstruction, rendering quality, and open-vocabulary semantic mapping. We further
show that semantic decoupling enables efficient online reconstruction, while semantic-guided re-
finement improves the quality of the reconstructed Gaussian scene.
Our main contributions are summarized as follows:
Geometry-Enhanced Monocular Semantic Gaussian SLAM. We enhance monocular Gaussian
SLAM with foundation-model geometry priors and confidence-aware fusion, providing reliable ge-
ometric support for reconstruction and semantic lifting.
Spatially Decoupled Open-Vocabulary Semantic Mapping. We maintain open-vocabulary se-
mantics in an independent sparse voxel representation with observation-level provenance, enabling
semantic evidence revision after geometric updates.

RELATED WORK
Gaussian Splatting SLAM. 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023; Zhao et al., 2026b) en-
ables efficient rendering and high-fidelity mapping in dense SLAM (Matsuki et al., 2024; Keetha
et al., 2024; Yugay et al., 2023; Huang et al., 2024; Cheng et al., 2025a). Existing systems either
track cameras through Gaussian rendering or combine separate geometric tracking with Gaussian
mapping. DROID-Splat (Homeyer et al., 2025) extends DROID-SLAM (Teed & Deng, 2021) with
Gaussian reconstruction, while HI-SLAM2 (Zhang et al., 2025) advances HI-SLAM (Zhang et al.,
2024) with Gaussian mapping and adaptive depth-prior alignment. Splat-SLAM (Sandstr¨om et al.,
2025) further maintains map consistency under global pose and depth updates. Rendering-based
tracking can struggle under weak geometric constraints, while in RGB-only settings, monocular ge-
ometric ambiguity can lead to inaccurate pose and depth estimates that subsequently propagate into
Gaussian mapping.
Recent advances use learned geometric priors to strengthen reconstruction (Cheng et al., 2025b;
Yu et al., 2025). MASt3R-SLAM (Murai et al., 2025) demonstrates foundation-based monocular
tracking, and ARTDECO (Li et al., 2025b) integrates foundation predictions into structured Gaus-
sian reconstruction. S3PO-GS (Cheng et al., 2025c) addresses outdoor scale inconsistency through
pointmap alignment, while KiloGS-SLAM (Yu et al., 2026) targets long-term tracking scalability.
Despite this progress, integrating foundation priors requires reconciling their scale across prediction
windows and rejecting unreliable geometry.
Our method integrates foundation priors into monocular Gaussian SLAM through cross-window
alignment, confidence-aware depth fusion, and long-horizon pose guidance.
Semantic Gaussian Mapping and SLAM. Semantic SLAM has explored neural implicit repre-
sentations in SNI-SLAM (Zhu et al., 2024) and multi-view feature consensus for open-vocabulary
understanding in RADIO-ViPE (Nasser et al., 2026). For Gaussian-based semantic reconstruction,
OpenGS-Fusion (Yang et al., 2025b) and OmniMap (Deng et al., 2025) combine Gaussian appear-
ance modeling with volumetric semantic fusion, but require RGB-D input and supplied poses.
Semantic Gaussian SLAM brings together online localization, high-fidelity rendering, and scene
understanding. Early methods attach semantic labels or features to Gaussians (Li et al., 2024b; Zhu
et al., 2025; Li et al., 2024a), but are restricted to predefined categories. OpenGS-SLAM (Yang
et al., 2025a), Open-Set Semantic Gaussian SLAM (Yan et al., 2026), and FeatureSLAM (Thirgood
et al., 2026) incorporate foundation-model semantics to support open-set scene understanding, with
Open-Set Semantic Gaussian SLAM further reducing storage through a shared expandable feature
pool. However, these RGB-D systems tie semantics to evolving Gaussian primitives through labels,
features, or indexing vectors; feature-based approaches also incur additional semantic optimization
and rendering costs. This coupling complicates semantic maintenance, and cross-view agreement
alone cannot correct historical evidence fused under outdated poses or depths.
Our RGB-only framework fuses open-vocabulary semantics in an independent voxel map and revises
historical contributions after pose or depth updates.

METHODOLOGY
As illustrated in Fig. 2, our framework takes a calibrated monocular RGB sequence as input and
maintains a Gaussian map for geometry and appearance together with an independent sparse voxel
map for open-vocabulary semantics. We first use foundation priors to improve monocular track-
ing and Gaussian reconstruction (Sec. 3.1). Based on this geometry, we fuse and revise semantic
observations in the voxel map (Sec. 3.2). The resulting semantic map then provides feedback to
refine Gaussian geometry (Sec. 3.3). Additional method and implementation details are provided in
Appendix A.3.

Long-horizon pose guidance.
The historical context also provides trajectory proposals beyond
the local tracking window. We align these proposals to the SLAM coordinate system using a Sim(3)
transformation and screen them for geometric consistency. Accepted proposals are blended with
the current poses to initialize global visual bundle adjustment, with a coefficient γ controlling the
guidance strength. Bundle adjustment then refines poses and depths under the visual correspondence
constraints, allowing observation-driven optimization to correct residual prior errors. The updated
geometry is propagated to Gaussian reconstruction.
3.2
SPATIALLY DECOUPLED OPEN-VOCABULARY SEMANTIC MAPPING
We maintain persistent open-vocabulary semantics in an independent sparse voxel map, retaining
observation-level contributions to support revision as the reconstructed geometry evolves.
Observation-based voxel fusion.
For each semantic keyframe, a region-language frontend ex-
tracts region masks, normalized language embeddings, and confidence scores (Deng et al., 2025).
Using the current Gaussian-rendered depth and corresponding camera pose, we lift each region ob-
servation o into the voxel map. Its contribution to voxel v is denoted by ∆zov = woveo, where
eo is the language embedding and wov is its fusion weight, which is zero outside the observation’s
voxel support. Each voxel aggregates these contributions into a normalized semantic feature, en-
abling open-vocabulary retrieval through similarity comparison with text-query embeddings. We
also associate observations across views using spatial overlap and language similarity to maintain
entity identities. Alongside the fused statistics, we retain the original region observations and their
individual voxel contributions to support subsequent geometry-aware semantic revisions.
Revision under geometry updates.
Changes to camera poses or Gaussian geometry can inval-
idate the spatial support of previously fused observations. For each affected observation, we first
retract its stored contribution, then reproject the cached region observation using the updated depth
and pose and insert its new contribution:

3.3
SEMANTIC-GUIDED GAUSSIAN GEOMETRY REFINEMENT
Voxel semantics guide the selection of floater candidates and compatible surface neighborhoods for
Gaussian refinement. Geometric and photometric evidence determines the corrections.
Verified floater filtering.
Weak or missing semantic support identifies potential floaters in spa-
tially sparse Gaussian neighborhoods, but is insufficient to justify removal. We verify these can-
didates using counter-evidence from multiple distinct views: either repeated free-space violations
relative to reliable predicted mapping depths DM
t
from Sec. 3.1, or photometric evidence that reduc-
ing their opacity improves image reconstruction. Occluded Gaussians are not treated as free-space
violations, and unobserved or semantically uncovered regions remain unknown. For verified can-
didates, we attenuate opacity and subsequently prune only those with sufficiently low opacity and
renewed multi-view counter-evidence. This filtering is implemented as a map-management proce-
dure without introducing an additional semantic loss term.

4.1
EXPERIMENT SETUP
Datasets. Experiments are conducted on eight scenes from Replica (Straub et al., 2019), six scenes
from ScanNet (Dai et al., 2017), and four static segments from KITTI-360 (Liao et al., 2022).
Dataset and scene-selection details are provided in Appendix A.4.1.
Baselines. We compare with representative visual SLAM methods (Sandstr¨om et al., 2025; Li et al.,
2025b; Cheng et al., 2025c; Homeyer et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2025) and semantic mapping sys-
tems (Zhu et al., 2024; Li et al., 2024b; Zhu et al., 2025; Li et al., 2025a; Yang et al., 2025a; Li et al.,
2024a; Yan et al., 2026; Jiang et al., 2026; Nasser et al., 2026).
Implementation Details. All experiments are run on a server equipped with a single NVIDIA RTX
A6000 GPU. Our system uses Depth Anything 3 (Lin et al., 2025) as geometric foundation model.

CONCLUSION
This work shows that geometry and semantics in monocular semantic SLAM can be decoupled in
representation while remaining connected in optimization. UniGS-SLAM maintains geometry in
9


Preprint
a Gaussian map and open-vocabulary semantics in an independent voxel map, enabling semantic
evidence to follow geometric updates without entering Gaussian optimization and rendering. The
semantic map further feeds back to improve Gaussian reconstruction through targeted filtering and
surface refinement. Our results demonstrate that this design supports accurate reconstruction, strong
semantic mapping, and efficient RGB-only SLAM. More broadly, separating geometric and seman-
tic state while preserving controlled interaction offers a useful design principle for semantic SLAM
with evolving scene representations.

A.4.2
EVALUATION DETAILS
Closed-set semantic evaluation. Following Open-set SLAM (Yan et al., 2026), we evaluate on
four Replica scenes (room0, room1, room2, and office0) with ground-truth semantic supervision.
Ground-truth semantic labels replace predictions from the semantic frontend, while the downstream
Gaussian and voxel mapping procedures remain unchanged. We report 2D segmentation mIoU
in percent. For its closed-set evaluation, Open-set SLAM trains a classification head on rendered
semantic features using cross-entropy supervision from ground-truth labels. We use the results
reported by Open-set SLAM (Yan et al., 2026) for Open-set SLAM itself and for the compared
baselines SGS-SLAM (Li et al., 2024b) and GS3LAM (Li et al., 2024a). We reproduce SemGauss-
SLAM (Zhu et al., 2025) and FreeOcc (Jiang et al., 2026) using their publicly available code with
ground-truth semantic supervision. For OpenGS-SLAM (Yang et al., 2025a), we use the Ours
(Prior) results reported in its Table I, where all methods use ground-truth semantic labels as se-
mantic priors for mapping.
Open-set semantic evaluation. To match our input setting, we restrict the quantitative comparison
to systems operating with RGB-only input and no externally supplied camera poses. We consider
OpenGS-SLAM (Yang et al., 2025a), Open-set SLAM (Yan et al., 2026), RADIO-ViPE (Nasser
et al., 2026), and FreeOcc (Jiang et al., 2026) as related open-vocabulary semantic mapping sys-
tems. At the time of our experiments, complete SLAM implementations and evaluation code for
OpenGS-SLAM and Open-set SLAM were not publicly available, preventing reproducible compar-
isons under a common protocol. We therefore compare against RADIO-ViPE and FreeOcc using
their publicly available implementations in RGB-only configurations, with camera poses estimated
by each system. We adopt the 3D semantic segmentation protocol used by RADIO-ViPE, which
follows RayFronts (Alama et al., 2025), and apply it to Replica and ScanNet. Following the without-
background setting in RADIO-ViPE, we exclude background categories, including structural classes
such as walls and floors, from evaluation. We report mean class accuracy (mAcc), overall accuracy
(fAcc), mean IoU (mIoU), and frequency-weighted IoU (fIoU), all on a 0–1 scale.
Time Efficiency evaluation. We evaluate the end-to-end runtime of our method and four pub-
licly available baselines: SNI-SLAM (Zhu et al., 2024), SGS-SLAM (Li et al., 2024b), SemGauss-
SLAM (Zhu et al., 2025), and GS3LAM (Li et al., 2024a). All five methods are measured on a
single NVIDIA RTX A6000 GPU under the same experimental setup and with a common end-to-
end timing scope. Timing includes data processing, semantic segmentation, tracking and mapping,

A.4.4
MORE ABLATION STUDIES
Overall Component Ablation.
We evaluate the contributions of geometry enhancement and
semantic-guided geometry refinement on all eight Replica scenes. Our SLAM backbone follows the
architecture of DROID-SLAM (Teed & Deng, 2021) and related Gaussian SLAM systems (Home-
yer et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2025). Table 11 reports scene-averaged results as we progressively
add the two components to this backbone. The first row disables both components, and the last row
includes both.
Adding geometry enhancement increases PSNR from 36.79 to 38.37 dB (+1.58 dB) and SSIM from
0.958 to 0.974, while reducing LPIPS from 0.036 to 0.030. It also reduces L1 depth error from
1.564 to 1.260 cm, a 19.4% reduction. Adding semantic-guided refinement further improves PSNR
to 38.65 dB (+0.28 dB) and SSIM to 0.975, and lowers LPIPS to 0.026. L1 depth error decreases
further to 1.170 cm, a 7.1% reduction relative to geometry enhancement alone. These results show
that geometry enhancement improves both appearance and depth reconstruction, while semantic-
guided refinement provides additional gains across all four metrics.
Effect of Semantic Guidance.
To isolate the contribution of semantic guidance, we compare
our full refinement with a geometry-only variant that retains the same geometric refinement pro-
cedure but removes semantic cues for candidate and neighborhood selection. As shown in Table 12,
geometry-only refinement yields a modest improvement over no refinement, whereas semantic guid-
ance further reduces Acc. from 12.07 to 9.79 cm and improves F-score from 49.14% to 50.97%. This
indicates that the gain is not merely due to additional geometric refinement; semantic cues improve
the selection of reliable floater candidates and compatible surface neighborhoods.
