## Abstract 

Understanding and reasoning over 3D scenes from point clouds is essential for a broad range of AI applications. Recent 3D large language models have shown promising results, yet they still face two fundamental limitations: (i) insufficient capability for grounded multi-step reasoning in complex 3D scenes, and (ii) a text-centric inference paradigm that lacks effective interaction between textual reasoning and visual evidence. As a result, these models often fail to verify intermediate inferences, disambiguate similar object in-stances, and avoid hallucinations. To address these limitations, we propose MMCoT 3, the first 3D–Text Interleaved Chain-of-Thought (CoT) framework for 3D scene understanding. The core idea is to explicitly interleave textual reasoning with task-relevant 3D evi-dence, enabling the model to consult scene observations throughout the reasoning process. In addition, since a textual mention in 3D scenes may correspond to multiple candidate objects, naive object– mention alignment can inject incorrect evidence and mislead sub-sequent reasoning. To mitigate this multi-instance ambiguity, we design an ambiguity-aware evidence aggregation mechanism that uses query-guided cross-attention to fuse candidate object tokens and inject both evidence and ambiguity cues into the reasoning pro-cess. To better equip the model with multi-step reasoning capability, we further develop PointCoT, a large-scale 3D-specific CoT instruc-tion tuning dataset with approximately 176.2K instances spanning captioning, question answering, dialogue, and planning tasks. Ex-tensive experiments on six benchmarks show that our method consistently improves performance and achieves state-of-the-art re-sults, validating the effectiveness of our proposed ambiguity-aware 3D–text interleaved reasoning for 3D scene understanding.

Recent advances [20, 45, 47] have enabled large language models (LLMs) to integrate and reason over rich point-cloud geometry, extending them from text-only modeling to multimodal 3D scene understanding. Representative tasks in 3D scene understanding include 3D captioning [8], 3D question answering [3, 30], 3D dialogue [22], and scene-aware task planning [22]. These tasks provide an important testbed for evaluating whether models can jointly perceive, ground, and reason over complex 3D scenes under natural-language queries. Existing methods typically employ 3D LLMs [19, 42] to encode point clouds and text separately, align them in a shared embed-ding space, and directly generate outputs with the LLM. Despite the successes, current methods are still facing two critical issues: 

Firstly, insufficient 3D reasoning capability. The sparse, irreg-ular, and geometry-centric nature of 3D point clouds makes 3D arXiv:2611.01676v1 [cs.CV] 1 Nov 2026 Shan et al. 

understanding tasks inherently reasoning-intensive. To answer a query, models must jointly reason about object attributes, spatial relations, scene configurations, and task-specific context. However, existing methods still lack the robust reasoning capacity needed for such complex 3D scenes, often leading to incorrect target identifi-cation when multiple instances share similar visual characteristics. 

Secondly, lack of interactions between textual reasoning and visual evidence. Most existing methods only use 3D representa-tions as a static background context, without actively consulting and verifying them during reasoning (Fig. 1(a)). Moreover, without effective interaction between textual reasoning and visual evidence, the model cannot properly ground, verify, and revise intermediate conclusions during multi-step inference. Consequently, prior meth-ods are prone to produce hallucinations [4] in multi-step reasoning. For example, the model may hallucinate non-existent objects or produce spatial reasoning inconsistent with the actual scene layout. Recently, researchers attempted to reduce the incorrect target identification by improving the selection and reasoning of scene evidence. For example, TSP3D [16] introduces a pruning strategy to progressively discard query-irrelevant regions from the 3D scene representation, thereby suppressing redundant scene content and concentrating computation on potentially relevant targets. Another research direction is to incorporate Chain-of-Thought (CoT) rea-soning to enhance the model’s capacity for complex 3D inference, enabling it to better organize and integrate multiple cues, such as object attributes, spatial relations, and global scene context, during multi-step reasoning. Nevertheless, existing approaches fall short of fully utilizing visual evidence throughout the reasoning process. As a result, the model cannot effectively revisit 3D evidence to verify intermediate conclusions, refine hypotheses, or resolve ambiguities. On the other hand, to alleviate the issue of lacking interactions between text and visual information, recent 2D multi-modal rea-soning studies [14, 52] have begun to explore multi-modal CoT paradigms that explicitly interleave textual reasoning with visual evidence during inference. Such a paradigm has shown state-of-the-art performance in purely 2D text-centric inference scenarios. Un-fortunately, directly transferring this paradigm to 3D scene under-standing remains highly nontrivial: (1) there is a lack of large-scale, high-quality 3D-specific CoT dataset, which hinders the further im-provements of 3D understanding tasks. (2) unlike 2D images/videos, 3D point clouds exhibit a sparse, irregular, and large-scale manner, where only a small fraction of the observed content is relevant to the query. Thus, it is considerably more difficult to identify in-formative 3D evidence compared with 2D multi-modal reasoning tasks. A natural idea of alleviating the above limitations is to explicitly interleave intermediate textual reasoning with task-relevant 3D ev-idence, enabling the model to consult scene observations precisely during multi-step reasoning. A naive solution is to align object-level 3D tokens with object mentions in the textual CoT sequence and inject the matched evidence into the corresponding reasoning steps. However, such a direct interleaving is inherently brittle in the realistic 3D scenes, where multiple instances frequently share the same category label. Thus, a textual entity may match an incor-rect token from its associated object tokens, causing subsequent reasoning steps to depend on a mismatched 3D evidence. Even worse, the ambiguity can further propagate through the CoT pro-cess, impairing both the faithfulness of intermediate reasoning and the quality of the final prediction. Therefore, effective 3D–Text interleaved reasoning requires not only explicit evidence injection, but also an ambiguity-aware mechanism for identifying relevant candidates, aggregating uncertain evidence, and robustly resolving multi-instance ambiguity. Following the above analysis, we first develop PointCoT , a large-scale 3D-specific CoT dataset with 176.2K instances, by enriching the LEO instruction-tuning dataset [22] with high-quality rationales across six representative task settings, including 3D captioning [8], 3D question answering on ScanNet [3], 3D question answering on 3RScan [22], 3D dialogue [22], 3D embodied reasoning [30], and scene-aware task planning [22]. To enhance the capability of responding to various queries, we further propose a difficulty-adaptive CoT generation strategy: for complex queries, we explicitly decompose them into progressive multi-step sub-problems to fa-cilitate structured reasoning. For simple queries, we complement them with concise grounding cues that guide the model toward task-relevant visual evidence. By providing explicit CoT supervi-sion for diverse 3D tasks, this dataset enables the model to develop stronger multi-step reasoning capability in complex 3D scenes. To enhance the limited 3D reasoning capability of existing meth-ods and the text-centric inference paradigm, we propose a novel 3D–Text Interleaved Multi-Modal CoT framework MMCoT 3 for 3D scene understanding. By coupling textual CoT reasoning with 3D evidence, our framework not only strengthens the 3D LLM’s capacity for grounded multi-step reasoning, but also moves beyond text-centric inference by enabling the model to actively consult and verify scene observations during intermediate reasoning (Fig. 1(b)). To address the brittleness of direct 3D–Text interleaving in re-alistic 3D scenes, where multiple instances often share the same semantic category, we further develop an ambiguity-aware evi-dence aggregation mechanism tailored for 3D scene understanding. Instead of prematurely committing to a single object when multiple candidates match a query mention, we aggregate the matched object tokens into a fused evidence representation through query-guided cross-attention. Meanwhile, we explicitly estimate the ambiguity of the candidate set and encode it as an additional signal, allowing the model to assess the reliability of the injected evidence during reasoning. We then interleave both the task-relevant 3D evidence and its associated ambiguity cue with the textual CoT sequence, enabling the LLM to consult scene observations precisely when intermediate reasoning unfolds, while remaining aware of potential instance-level uncertainty. In this way, our framework not only strengthens compositional reasoning over complex 3D scenes, but also establishes an explicit ambiguity-aware evidence-checking process throughout multi-step inference, improving robustness to instance-level ambiguity and mitigating hallucinations. ## 2 Related work 2.1 3D Multi-Modal Large Language Models 

Recent advances in artificial intelligence have driven rapid progress across visual understanding tasks [25, 26, 35, 36]. Advances in multi-modal LLMs have inspired a growing body of work on point-cloud-based 3D large language models [17, 20, 22, 24, 27, 38, 44, 48, 49, 53]. Existing methods mainly focus on bridging 3D representations with language models through feature alignment, unified tokenization, or instruction tuning, as exemplified by 3D-LLM [20], SegPoint [19], Point-LLM [47], 3D-LLaVA [13], and 3D-SceneQ [35], which incor-porates query-guided adaptive pruning and multi-modal feature en-hancement. Other works improve object-centric and embodied un-derstanding by incorporating richer supervision, such as multi-view semantics, inter-object relations, embodied observations, or large-scale instruction data [22, 24, 49, 53]. Meanwhile, OneLLM [18], Point-Bind LLM [17], and GPT4Point [32] extend this line toward broader multi-modal alignment and generation. Unlike existing 3D LLMs, which primarily focus on representation alignment, in-struction tuning, and task adaptation, our work explicitly targets grounded multi-step reasoning in 3D scenes through 3D-specific Chain-of-Thought supervision and 3D–Text interleaved reasoning with task-relevant evidence. 

## 2.2 Chain-of-Thought Reasoning for Multi-Modal Large Language Models 

Chain-of-Thought (CoT) elicits explicit intermediate reasoning be-fore the final answer and has been shown to improve LLMs’ accu-racy and robustness [28, 40, 43, 51]. Following this trend, CoT has been extended to 2D vision-language models [23, 29, 54], where Multi-modal CoT leverages multi-modal rationales to guide pre-diction and reduce hallucinations [54], and subsequent Visual CoT works further promote grounded CoT-style visual QA through tailored methods and benchmarks [9, 37]. Beyond 2D, CoT-style reasoning has been extended to 3D tasks involving geometry and spatial relations. CoT-Pose [5] maps abstract prompts to detailed pose cues for 3D pose generation, while CoT3DRef [1] predicts an anchor chain before the final target for data-efficient 3D grounding. AffordBot [41] further uses multi-modal CoT to select informative views and reason step-by-step about 3D affordances and interac-tions, and MM-Spatial [12] leverages metric depth via CoT use at inference. Despite recent progress, existing CoT-enabled 3D LLMs largely remain within text-only reasoning chains, treating 3D obser-vations mainly as static prefix context, while failing to fully exploit visual evidence for intermediate reasoning. In contrast, we argue that 3D–text interleaved multi-modal CoT is crucial for faithful spatial reasoning, as it can explicitly couple intermediate deduc-tions with geometric cues and multi-view context. Additionally, to support systematic evaluation, we build a 3D CoT benchmark for assessing the effectiveness of 3D–text interleaved reasoning.

## 3.1 Difficulty-Adaptive Generation 

As illustrated in Fig. 2(b), to support CoT data construction across diverse 3D understanding tasks, we develop a difficulty-adaptive data generation strategy, rather than relying on a single unified prompting scheme. Specifically, we propose six prompt templates according to the characteristics of different datasets and task types, and use Deepseek-R1 [15] to generate CoT supervision for each sample. The key principle is to align the prompt design with the rea-soning demands of the target task: for complex tasks, the prompts explicitly encourage progressive multi-step decomposition, whereas for simple tasks, they instead emphasize concise grounding cues. For complex tasks, such as dialogue and scene-aware planning, the prompts encourage the model to decompose the problem into logical sub-steps, identify relevant context, and progressively or-ganize the response. This leads to more structured supervision for grounded multi-step reasoning. In contrast, for simpler tasks such as question answering and captioning, we design prompts that emphasize compact grounding cues, including target objects, reference objects, spatial relations, and discriminative attributes. Such prompts encourage the model to produce compact rationales that remain closely tied to the 3D scene. Table 1 further shows that PointCoT covers both easy and complex settings, reflecting the diverse reasoning demands of different 3D understanding tasks.

## 4 Method 

To address the limitations of previous 3D understanding methods, as shown in Fig. 3, we introduce a novel 3D understanding par-adigm called MMCoT 3. Specifically, MMCoT 3 primarily consists of four steps: candidate selection (Sec. 4.2), query-based token fu-sion (Sec. 4.3), ambiguity modeling (Sec. 4.4), and ambiguity-aware evidence injection (Sec. 4.5). ## 4.1 Overview 

Given a point-cloud 3D scene and a question 𝑞 , MMCoT 3 aims to generate a grounded response consisting of a CoT rationale and the final answer. MMCoT 3 is trained in two stages: (1) 3D vision-language alignment , which maps object-centric 3D repre-sentations into the LLM feature space, and (2) instruction tuning ,which equips the model with 3D-text interleaved reasoning ability through ambiguity-aware CoT supervision. In the 3D vision-language alignment stage, we encode the input scene with an object-centric 3D encoder, PointNet++ [31]. For each object 𝑜 𝑖 , the encoder extracts a geometric embedding, which is further processed by a Spatial Transformer [6] to model inter-object interactions and produce object-centric 3D tokens. A linear projec-tion then maps these tokens into the LLM feature space, yielding the final 3D object representation 𝑓 3𝐷 ∈ R𝑁 ×𝑑 , where 𝑁 is the num-ber of objects and 𝑑 is the LLM hidden dimension. Using the system prompt, question 𝑞 , and 𝑓 3𝐷 as input, the model learns a shared multi-modal feature space for grounded generation. Meanwhile, textual tokens are projected into the same feature space through a trained text projection layer, so that 3D object representations and textual representations are aligned for downstream reasoning. In the instruction tuning stage, rather than treating 3D features as static prefix context, MMCoT 3 injects task-relevant 3D evidence into intermediate reasoning. Specifically, given a textual CoT, we identify object-related mentions, retrieve candidate 3D object to-kens from the scene (Sec. 4.2), fuse ambiguous candidates via query-based token fusion (Sec. 4.3), estimate the ambiguity of the fused evidence (Sec. 4.4), and inject the resulting evidence and ambigu-ity tokens into the corresponding reasoning positions (Sec. 4.5). This yields an ambiguity-aware 3D-text interleaved multi-modal CoT, enabling the model to condition intermediate reasoning on object-level 3D evidence. We insert the fused evidence token 𝑓 𝑚 and ambiguity token 𝑎 𝑚 into the aligned textual feature sequence 𝐻 . For a mention 𝑚 spanning

## 5.2 Comparison with State-of-the-Art Methods 

We evaluate MMCoT 3 on six 3D scene understanding benchmarks built on ScanNet and 3RScan. As shown in Tables 2 and 3, MMCoT 3

achieves consistently strong performance across captioning, ques-tion answering, situated reasoning, dialogue, and planning, demon-strating robust generalization across both tasks and datasets. As shown in Table 2, on the ScanNet-based [11] benchmarks, MMCoT 3 sets a new state of the art on all five Scan2Cap [8] metrics, achieving 79.9 CIDEr, 44.3 BLEU-4, 31.2 METEOR, 65.3 ROUGE, and 63.8 Sim. Compared with the strong 3D LLM baseline LEO, this corresponds to gains of 7.5, 6.1, 3.3, 7.2, and 8.5 points, respectively. On ScanQA, MMCoT 3 achieves the best CIDEr and METEOR scores, while remaining competitive on BLEU-4 and ROUGE. This pattern suggests that MMCoT 3 mainly improves semantic grounding and reasoning quality, whereas lexical overlap metrics are less directly aligned with the gains brought by evidence-grounded multi-step reasoning. On SQA3D, MMCoT 3 reaches 65.4 EM@1, exceeding the best prior result by 10.9 points, further verifying its advantage on more challenging situated reasoning. The gains further generalize to the 3RScan-based [39] bench-marks. As shown in Table 3, MMCoT 3 consistently surpasses both the instruction-tuned baseline and LEO across planning, dialogue, and question answering, achieving 83.9 Sim on 3RScanPlanning, 84.1 Sim on 3RScanDialog, and 54.3 EM@1 on 3RScanQA. Com-pared with LEO, the improvements are 2.8, 10.8, and 2.4 points, respectively. The especially pronounced gain on 3RScanDialog highlights the advantage of our framework for more compositional and context-dependent reasoning, where accurate grounding of intermediate reasoning steps is particularly important. Overall, these results suggest that the gains of MMCoT 3 arise from a stronger evidence-grounded reasoning paradigm rather than task-specific engineering. For complex tasks such as planning and dialogue, 3D–Text interleaving enables the model to decompose long-horizon decisions into grounded intermediate steps and con-tinuously verify them against the scene, thereby reducing error accumulation. For relatively simpler tasks such as captioning and question answering, it still provides consistent benefits by strength-ening object-level grounding and improving the recognition of attributes and spatial relations, leading to more accurate and se-mantically faithful predictions.

## 5.4 Ablation Study 

We conduct ablation studies to assess the contribution of each core component in MMCoT 3, including textual CoT supervision, 3D–Text interleaving, evidence fusion, and ambiguity modeling. As shown in Table 4, introducing textual CoT supervision already yields clear gains over the instruction-tuned baseline on all three benchmarks, improving Scan2Cap from 69.2 to 73.4 CIDEr, SQA3D from 52.7 to 59.6 EM@1, and 3RScanPlanning from 53.9 to 69.7 Sentence Similarity. These improvements confirm the importance of explicit reasoning supervision for 3D scene understanding. Adding naive 3D–Text interleaving leads to mixed results: it further improves Scan2Cap from 73.4 to 75.7 CIDEr, but degrades SQA3D from 59.6 to 55.3 EM@1 and 3RScanPlanning from 69.7 to 66.1 Sim. This suggests that direct mention-to-object alignment is brittle in realistic 3D scenes, especially when multiple instances share similar semantics. Introducing evidence fusion substantially recovers the performance, particularly on 3RScanPlanning, where MMCoT 3: Empowering 3D Scene Understanding with 3D-Text Interleaved Multi-Modal Chain-of-Thought Reasoning 

the score rises from 66.1 to 78.6, demonstrating the benefit of ag-gregating multiple candidate object tokens rather than prematurely committing to a single one. Finally, adding ambiguity modeling further improves all three benchmarks and yields the full MMCoT 3,achieving the best results of 79.4 CIDEr, 65.4 EM@1, and 83.9 Sim. These results show that both evidence fusion and ambiguity mod-eling are crucial for robust 3D–Text interleaved reasoning. 

## 6 Conclusion 

In this paper, we presented MMCoT 3, the first 3D–Text interleaved Chain-of-Thought framework for point-cloud-based 3D scene un-derstanding. By explicitly interleaving intermediate textual reason-ing with task-relevant 3D evidence, MMCoT 3 moves beyond the text-centric inference paradigm of prior 3D LLMs and enables active evidence consultation throughout multi-step reasoning. To further address the brittleness of direct object–mention alignment in real-istic 3D scenes, we introduced an ambiguity-aware evidence aggre-gation mechanism that fuses candidate object features and injects both evidence and ambiguity cues into the reasoning process. We also developed PointCoT, a large-scale 3D-specific CoT dataset that provides explicit supervision for grounded multi-step reasoning across diverse 3D understanding settings. Extensive experiments on six benchmarks built on ScanNet and 3RScan demonstrate the ef-fectiveness and generality of MMCoT 3 across captioning, question answering, embodied reasoning, dialogue, and planning. We hope this work will inspire future research on more reliable, evidence-grounded, and interpretable 3D multimodal reasoning.
