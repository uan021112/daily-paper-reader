ABSTRACT
Humans can easily recognize the same objects across different viewpoints of a
3D scene, whereas teaching models such multi-view-consistent perception typi-
cally requires videos or calibrated multi-view captures, which are considerably
more expensive and less diverse than large-scale monocular data. In this pa-
per, we introduce Feature Puzzles, a novel framework for learning multi-view-
consistent segmentation using only single-image semantic segmentation super-
vision. Specifically, we propose a simple yet effective pipeline that constructs
multi-view-consistent segmentation pseudo-labels through image cropping and
geometric transformations.
We further introduce a lightweight, plug-and-play
network that leverages the multi-view-consistent image features learned by ex-
isting pretrained 3D foundation models and the generated pseudo-labels to per-
form multi-view-consistent segmentation. Importantly, our flexible framework
supports various types of prompts provided from one or multiple views and can
be readily extended to open-vocabulary 3D segmentation. Under this single-image
supervision, our feed-forward model achieves the best mIoU on NVOS, remains
competitive on SPIn-NeRF and ScanNet++ against baselines using per-scene op-
timization or in-domain multi-view training, and demonstrates zero-shot transfer
to open-vocabulary 3D segmentation. Visual comparisons are available on our
project page: https://featurepuzzles.github.io/.

INTRODUCTION
Spatial intelligence requires not only recovering geometric structure but also recognizing and rea-
soning about scene content consistently across viewpoints (Yang et al., 2025). Multi-view-consistent
perception, particularly multi-view-consistent segmentation, is essential for building coherent rep-
resentations of the physical world and provides a foundation for downstream tasks such as robotic

interaction (Qu et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2026b), embodied navigation (Zhang et al., 2024; Ma et al.,
2026), and open-vocabulary 3D understanding (Peng et al., 2023).
Recent advances in feed-forward 3D reconstruction (Wang et al., 2025a; 2026b; Lin et al., 2025;
Wang & Agapito, 2025; Wang et al., 2025b; Lan et al., 2026) have substantially improved the ef-
ficiency of recovering 3D geometry while enabling the processing of long sequences. Building on
these advances, IGGT (Li et al., 2026) and IGGT4D (Zou et al., 2026) extend feed-forward recon-
struction models with instance-level understanding of static and dynamic scenes, while Embodied-
Splat (Lee et al., 2026) enables online 3D reconstruction and semantic understanding. Despite their
effectiveness, these approaches typically rely on multi-view-specific resources, such as posed RGB-
D scans, curated cross-view instance datasets, or multi-view distillation and self-supervision signals,
which are considerably more difficult and costly to acquire than single-image semantic supervision.
This reveals a key supervision gap: pretrained multi-view backbones already provide strong geo-
metric priors, but consistent cross-view semantics still depend on scarce multi-view annotations.
In contrast, single-image segmentation data is abundant but lacks cross-view correspondence. We
therefore ask whether single-image semantic supervision alone can supply this missing semantic
layer without real multi-view annotations. We emphasize the scope of this question. We do not
claim that 3D or multi-view consistency itself can be learned from monocular images alone; the ge-
ometric prior comes from the pretrained backbone, and our contribution is a supervision framework
that adapts this representation to multi-view-consistent semantics.
To address this gap, we propose Feature Puzzles, a training framework that turns large-scale single-
image segmentation data into synthetic multi-view supervision. It decomposes each image into
overlapping crops and treats them as synthetic views, avoiding the need for multiple observations of
the same scene. For these shared-parent crop views, correspondence is exact in the parent-image co-
ordinate system, enabling randomly sampled 2D masks and dense visual features to be transformed
consistently across views without additional multi-view semantic annotation.
Building on this supervision, we introduce a lightweight adaptation scheme for feed-forward 3D re-
construction models. We fuse intermediate multi-scale features and adapt only late backbone blocks
with low-rank adapters, which can be disabled at inference to recover the original depth and camera
outputs. This equips existing backbones with multi-view-consistent perception without redesigning
the reconstruction pipeline. We instantiate Feature Puzzles for two complementary tasks: prompt-
able multi-view segmentation, which accepts prompts from one or more views and predicts masks
jointly across all views in a single forward pass, and open-vocabulary 3D segmentation, which dis-
tills DINOv3 features to learn dense, multi-view-consistent representations for 3D recognition. As
shown in Figure 1, Feature Puzzles extends one reconstruction backbone to promptable segmenta-
tion, spatial tracking, and open-vocabulary 3D understanding with minimal architectural changes.
We organize our evaluation around a single hypothesis: shared-parent single-image supervision
with exact synthetic-view correspondence can teach multi-view-consistent semantics to a pretrained
3D geometry representation. Each experiment tests a consequence of this hypothesis. Promptable
segmentation tests whether object identity transfers across genuine camera views. Instance spa-
tial tracking tests whether this correspondence survives spatial and temporal displacement. Driving
scenes test whether it transfers beyond indoor distributions. Open-vocabulary 3D segmentation tests
whether the same principle extends from masks to dense semantic feature fields. For task-specific
training, promptable segmentation uses only single-image SA-1B mask supervision, while open-
vocabulary 3D segmentation uses single-image DINOv3 feature distillation. Neither task uses task-
specific multi-view or video annotations. Under this supervision, our model achieves the best mIoU
on NVOS in a single forward pass, remains competitive on SPIn-NeRF and ScanNet++, and demon-
strates transfer to out-of-distribution driving scenes. Ablations further support the contributions of
multi-layer features, backbone adaptation, and cross-view processing.

RELATED WORK
Feed-forward 3D reconstruction.
Reconstruction has moved from per-scene optimiza-
tion (Sch¨onberger & Frahm, 2016; Sch¨onberger et al., 2016) to feed-forward prediction:
VGGT (Wang et al., 2025a), π3 (Wang et al., 2026b), MapAnything (Keetha et al., 2026), and
Depth Anything 3 (Lin et al., 2025) regress cameras, depth, and point maps in a single pass, while
Spann3R (Wang & Agapito, 2025), CUT3R (Wang et al., 2025b), LingBot-Map (Chen et al., 2026),

and STream3R (Lan et al., 2026) extend this to streaming or causal reconstruction. Closest to our
data construction, Puzzles (Ma et al., 2025a) synthesizes multi-view training data from a single
image or clip, but only for geometric pointmap regression, without transferring semantic masks or
features or defining a cross-view perception objective. These reconstruction models primarily fo-
cus on geometric structure rather than task-specific semantics. We build on a pretrained multi-view
backbone and ask how single-image supervision can add a consistent semantic layer.
Feed-forward semantic 3D scene understanding. Per-scene methods lift 2D vision–language fea-
tures into a NeRF or 3DGS field (Kerr et al., 2023; Zhou et al., 2024; Qin et al., 2024; Shi et al.,
2024; Wu et al., 2024; Cheng et al., 2025; Kim et al., 2025; Li et al., 2025b; Ma et al., 2024) and
must re-optimize for every scene. Feed-forward alternatives predict semantics together with geom-
etry in one pass. LSM (Fan et al., 2024), Uni3R (Sun et al., 2026), SAB3R (Chen et al., 2025),
and Ov3R (Gong et al., 2026) distill CLIP- or DINO-style features into a reconstruction network,
while EmbodiedSplat (Lee et al., 2026) builds a semantic 3DGS online from RGB(-D) streams.
For instance understanding, PanSt3R (Zust et al., 2025) adds a panoptic head to frozen MUSt3R
and DINOv2 features, EPS3D (Zhu et al., 2026) distills LSeg and SAM into a VGGT-based model,
IGGT (Li et al., 2026), IGGT4D (Zou et al., 2026) and S4VY (Zhang et al., 2026a) learn cross-
view instance embeddings, and SceneDINO (Jevti´c et al., 2025) predicts a 3D DINO feature field
from a single image. Most rely on multi-view scene data—including posed scans, curated cross-
view instance datasets, or video—during semantic training. In contrast, Feature Puzzles uses no
task-specific real multi-view or video supervision, while inheriting its geometric prior from a pre-
trained multi-view backbone. Its promptable branch uses single-image SA-1B masks, and its open-
vocabulary branch a frozen DINOv3 teacher; cross-view supervision is created by shared-parent
pull-back. Optional in-domain fine-tuning is reported separately from zero-shot evaluation.
Promptable and cross-view segmentation. SAM (Kirillov et al., 2023a) and SAM2 (Ravi et al.,
2025) provide promptable segmentation for images and video but do not explicitly model 3D cross-
view consistency, so masks can drift across views of a static scene. NVOS (Ren et al., 2022), SPIn-
NeRF (Mirzaei et al., 2023), SA3D (Cen et al., 2023), SAGA (Cen et al., 2025a;b), DCHM (Ma
et al., 2025b) and OmniSeg3D (Ying et al., 2024) restore consistency by distilling SAM masks into
a per-scene field, at a cost of minutes to hours per scene. MASA (Li et al., 2024) learns 2D instance
association from augmented static images, while MV-SAM (Jeong et al., 2026) trains on SA-1B
but relies on a frozen π3 pointmap predictor for cross-view consistency at inference. The closest
work is VGGT-Segmentor (Gao et al., 2026), which also builds on VGGT and offers a single-image
self-supervised variant trained on SA-1B. It formulates cross-view segmentation as pairwise source–
target matching guided by explicit VGGT point tracks. Because targets are processed independently,
larger unordered view sets require additional pair selection, association, or tracking. Feature Puzzles
instead trains and reasons jointly over shared-parent N-view sequences, predicting all views in one
forward pass without pairwise matching, point tracking, or predefined view ordering.

METHOD
3.1
OVERVIEW
We propose Feature Puzzles, which turns a single image into overlapping synthetic views with ex-
actly aligned supervision (Section 3.2). A pretrained VGGT-Omega backbone encodes these views
(Section 3.3) and feeds two lightweight heads for promptable multi-view segmentation (Section 3.4)
and open-vocabulary 3D features (Section 3.5).
3.2
SINGLE-IMAGE MULTI-CROP PUZZLES
Figure 2 shows how Feature Puzzles converts one annotated image into a pseudo-view sample. We
call the original image the parent image, containing RGB, instance masks, dense teacher features,
and semantic labels. It is cropped into overlapping sub-images, each treated as a synthetic view.
Since all sub-images share the same parent, overlapping regions inherit exactly aligned supervision
by construction, enabling multi-view-consistent training without real multi-view or video data.

3.3
PRETRAINED MULTI-VIEW BACKBONE
As shown in Figure 3, we build on a pretrained VGGT-Omega (Wang et al., 2026a) multi-view
transformer. We keep its early blocks frozen and adapt late blocks with LoRA (Hu et al., 2022),
preserving the original depth and camera heads at inference. The segmentation heads consume a
shared representation Fn obtained by fusing cached multi-layer tokens. Adapters are initialized to
preserve the pretrained backbone at the start of training, enabling two-pass inference with adapters
off for reconstruction and on for segmentation. We further evaluate the same adaptation recipe
on LingBot-Map (Chen et al., 2026), a streaming reconstruction backbone with a different multi-
view attention design, providing evidence that the approach is not specific to VGGT-Omega. Layer
choice, fusion, and initialization are detailed in Appendix B.1.

EXPERIMENTS
Feature Puzzles extends feed-forward reconstruction with scene understanding.
We evaluate
promptable segmentation on NVOS and SPIn-NeRF (Section 4.1), instance tracking on ScanNet++
(Section 4.2), OOD tracking on nuScenes-mini (Section 4.2), and open-vocabulary 3D segmentation
following EmbodiedSplat (Section 4.3).
4.1
PROMPTABLE MULTI-VIEW SEGMENTATION
We evaluate promptable multi-view segmentation on NVOS (Ren et al., 2022) and SPIn-
NeRF (Mirzaei et al., 2023) following the SAGA protocol, using a single reference-view mask
prompt. Task-specific training uses only single-image SA-1B-style masks, with no video or real
multi-view supervision. As shown in Table 1, our feed-forward model achieves the best mIoU on
NVOS (93.2%) and outperforms SAM2-Video on SPIn-NeRF (88.5% vs. 86.6%), while remaining
below MV-SAM and the strongest per-scene methods there. Across both datasets, inference requires
no scene-specific optimization and generalizes directly to unseen scenes.
4.2
INSTANCE SPATIAL TRACKING
ScanNet++ evaluation. We follow the IGGT (Li et al., 2026) protocol on ScanNet++ (Yeshwanth
et al., 2023), propagating a reference mask to all frames in one forward pass over 19 instances from
7 scenes. IGGT is trained on ScanNet++ RGB-D and is therefore in-distribution, whereas our task-
specific segmentation training uses only SA-1B and is OOD to ScanNet++. As shown in Table 2,
our method achieves 63.60% mIoU and 87.50% T-SR, approaching IGGT (73.02% / 98.90%) while
outperforming SAM2* and SpaTracker+SAM.
OOD evaluation on nuScenes-mini. We adapt the same protocol to nuScenes-mini (Caesar et al.,
2020) (9 scenes, 6 surround cameras), using projected 3D boxes as pseudo-masks. Here, all methods
are OOD: IGGT is trained on ScanNet++ RGB-D, while our task-specific segmentation training still
uses only SA-1B. Despite wide baselines and minimal frustum overlaps across the six cameras,
our method in one forward pass achieves 4.78% T-mIoUall, 20.97% T-mIoUvis, and the highest T-
SRvis of 88.89%. IGGT obtains higher T-SRall through HDBSCAN matching but lower mIoU. See
Appendix E for detailed visual comparisons on ScanNet++ and nuScenes-mini.
4.3
OPEN-VOCABULARY 3D SEGMENTATION
We follow the EmbodiedSplat protocol (Lee et al., 2026) on ScanNet, ScanNet200, and ScanNet++,
using 11 ScanNet and 4 ScanNet++ scenes with stride 10. Features are voxel-averaged at 0.02 m,
lifted with ground-truth geometry, and classified using Talk2DINO text embeddings. Thus, the
benchmark measures semantic feature quality under fixed geometry. We compare with per-scene
3DGS methods (Qin et al., 2024; Shi et al., 2024; Wu et al., 2024; Cheng et al., 2025; Kim et al.,
2025; Li et al., 2025b; Katragadda et al., 2025) and feed-forward EmbodiedSplat. See Appendix E
for coherent semantic reconstruction across diverse large-scale environments.

Speed and memory.
We study how a single forward pass of the promptable model scales with the
number of input views (Figure 4a–b). We report a 6-view operating point from the view-count sweep
and a long-view stress test on SceneNN-MV scene 005 (40–248 frames), whose longest contiguous
sequence contains 248 frames. At 6 views the model achieves 97.8% precision and 84.2% recall.

the substantial precision degradation at large view counts indicates that perceptual scalability, rather

reflect differences in cross-view context, including LingBot-Map’s causal attention.
6
CONCLUSION
We presented Feature Puzzles, a simple recipe that turns a single-image dataset into synthetic multi-
view supervision by cropping a parent image into overlapping sub-images and pulling labels back, so
supervision targets are exactly consistent across overlapping affine crop views. A pretrained VGGT-
Omega backbone with layer-selective LoRA feeds two heads—promptable multi-view masks and
open-vocabulary 3D features—whose task-specific adaptation requires no real multi-view or video
supervision.
Despite this task-specific supervision requirement, a single forward pass achieves
the best mIoU on NVOS, transfers to instance spatial tracking on ScanNet++ and to the out-of-
distribution nuScenes-mini setting, and enables zero-shot open-vocabulary 3D segmentation from
SA-1B supervision. With additional in-domain semantic fine-tuning, the same representation fur-
ther exceeds EmbodiedSplat in mIoU across all evaluated subsets. These results are consistently
achieved through efficient feed-forward inference without scene-specific per-scene optimization.

Validity criteria.
Three filters keep the transferred labels informative.
1. Annotation quality, applied in parent space before transfer: area ≥1024 px2, SAM pre-

parent-space area threshold also helps ensure that instances remain resolvable after the

2. Cross-view support: an instance is retained only if it is non-empty in at least κ = 3 of the
N views, since instances visible in fewer views contribute mostly empty channels and little
cross-view signal; at most M = 16 instances are kept per sample.
3. Foreground coverage of the boxes themselves: a candidate box is accepted only if the mean
of the union-of-annotations mask inside it is at least τfg = 0.5, which prevents crops that
fall on unannotated background.
Truncated and absent instances.
An instance that is truncated or absent in view n is not re-fitted:
its visible part is retained, and Gn,m = 0 when the box misses it entirely.
A.3
DETAILED COMPARISON WITH IMAGE-TO-CLIPS
Image-to-Clips (Ma et al., 2025a) targets 3D reconstruction, so each synthesized patch must carry
geometric labels. It assigns a per-patch camera by solving a PnP problem with RANSAC over
the back-projected depth, applies a centroid-based random rotation, re-renders the point cloud, and
then validates the result with front-surface-coverage and view-frustum tests, discarding views whose
occlusion holes are too large. The resulting labels are estimates, with residual error contributed by
depth, by pose fitting, and by rendering.
Our Feature Puzzles views include both 3D augmentations and in-plane crop-and-resize transfor-
mations. Since the supervision is appearance-based (masks, dense features, and semantic classes)
rather than geometric, both constructions preserve consistent parent-derived semantics, while the
affine crop views additionally provide exact view-to-parent mappings. Table 5 summarizes the two
constructions term by term.

A.4
DETAILED COMPARISON WITH VGGT-SEGMENTOR
VGGT-Segmentor (Gao et al., 2026) targets ego–exo cross-view segmentation. Given a synchro-
nized image pair and a source-view mask, it fuses the mask into VGGT features, projects points
sampled from the source mask into the target view with the VGGT track head, and refines the result-
ing target mask. Its main model is trained on paired Ego-Exo4D masks, and a correspondence-free
variant is pre-trained with single-image self-supervision on a subset of SA-1B. Both variants remain
pairwise at training and inference.
Feature Puzzles differs in the unit of reasoning. Pairwise methods match every target independently
to a reference, so a target seen from a very different viewpoint cannot benefit from intermediate
views unless pairwise associations or tracking chains are built explicitly. For large unordered view
sets, this can require repeated pairwise processing and explicit association across views. Feature
Puzzles trains on N-view puzzle sequences and predicts masks for all views jointly: cross-view
attention allows information to be exchanged across all views, including through intermediate ob-
servations, prompts from any subset of views are fused into shared object tokens, and consistency
is supervised at the sequence level through targets in every view. No point correspondence, pair
selection, tracking, or view ordering is needed. Table 6 summarizes these differences.

Adapter rank increases with depth: starting from a base rank of 16, it is multiplied by two for

B.2
PROMPTABLE SEGMENTATION HEAD
Prompt encoding and token layout.
The prompt encoder produces a dense coverage map C ∈
RB×N×M×Hp×Wp, a prompt-presence indicator, and sparse prompt tokens. Conditioned tokens are
projected to a working dimension of 768. Cross-view self-attention operates over the N view tokens
at each spatial patch, followed by four SAM-style two-way attention blocks Ravi et al. (2025). Four
object tokens per view and instance are initialized by aggregating prompt-covered image tokens
across all prompted views; sparse prompt tokens are appended to the query sequence, with padded
prompts removed by a key-padding mask. Image and object tokens attend to one another in both
directions. When depth and camera poses are available, a 3D positional encoding is added to the
image tokens. The weighted pixel decoder projects and fuses decoder features and upsamples them
to D ∈RB×N×C×H×W , and the mask object token predicts the dynamic kernel k ∈RB×N×M×C.
Loss terms and design.
We set (λbce, λdice, λiou, λcons, λpt) = (5, 5, 1, 0.01, 1). The design
follows one principle: because pull-back places a target Gn,m in every view rather than only the
prompted one, mask supervision can be evaluated across all N views, directly encouraging cross-
view agreement even where no prompt is provided. Additional terms regularize confidence, object
tokens, and prompted point locations.
• Lbce, Ldice: pixel-wise binary cross-entropy with logits and Dice loss between σ(Yn,m)
and Gn,m, summed over all views. Since a target exists in views that received no prompt,
a prediction correct only in the prompted view is penalized, so the mask term alone already
drives cross-view agreement. Dice complements the pixel-wise term on small instances,
where background dominates.
• Liou: regression of the auxiliary head onto the measured overlap between σ(Yn,m) and
Gn,m, giving a per-view confidence used to rank masks at inference.
• Lcons: cross-view variance of the mask object token around its centroid,

B.4
IMPLEMENTATION DETAILS
Common training setup.
Both tasks use cached VGGT-Omega blocks {4, 11, 17, 23}, 2048-
dimensional backbone tokens, a working dimension of 768, bfloat16 mixed precision, AdamW,
and cosine learning-rate decay with linear warm-up. Training is distributed over eight A800 GPUs.
Promptable segmentation. We train with N = 6 views, four object tokens, four two-way attention
layers, and eight attention heads. The probability of placing prompts in multiple views is 0.5, and
the supported modalities are masks, boxes, and points with at most six points per prompt. Low-rank
adaptation starts at block 18 with base rank r = 16 and scale α = 16.
Open-vocabulary segmentation. For open-vocabulary pre-training, we use six 256 × 256 crops,
predict 128 × 128 features, sample 512 pixels for the Gram objective, and use at most 12 view pairs
for the warp objective. ScanNet fine-tuning uses a learning rate of 5 × 10−5. At inference, frames
are sampled with stride 10 and fused using 0.02 m voxels.
Inference details. The architecture supports prompts from one or multiple views, which are encoded
jointly, and masks for all views are returned in one forward pass, with no scene-specific parameters
optimized. The same backbone still serves depth and camera reconstruction when the segmentation
adapters are disabled.

FEATURE PUZZLES LABEL
Exact Cross-View Label Correspondence.
We define exact cross-view label correspondence
as pixel-to-label alignment within the synthetic-view construction, rather than exact correspondence
to the true 3D scene. For affine crops, images and labels share the same known transformation. For
3D-augmented views, rendered RGB and labels use the same estimated depth and virtual-camera
projection; depth errors may affect geometric realism but not their mutual pixel alignment under
that projection. Since all views inherit supervision from the same parent label field, shared visible
regions remain label-consistent, while disoccluded regions are masked out from the loss.
