# UNI LOOP : Unified Loop Closure Detection from Retrieval to Geometric Verification and Relative Pose Estimation 

Jingwen Yu 1,2, Pengyu Wang 1, Jin Wu 3, Weinan Chen 4, Ping Tan 1, and Hong Zhang 2

Fig. 1: UNI LOOP unifies retrieval, geometric verification, and relative pose estimation within a single MASt3R backbone. Unlike cascades of separately trained visual models, UNI LOOP reuses the image encoder for global retrieval, the cross-view decoder for pairwise verification, and the geometric heads for relative pose constraints, sharing an image encoding across all stages. This framework proposes a new paradigm for loop closure detection, improving robustness and accuracy while reducing redundant computation. 

Abstract — Loop closure detection conventionally cascades separate models for global retrieval, geometric verification, and relative pose estimation, leading to redundant computation and disconnected visual representations. We propose U NI LOOP , a unified framework that reuses the hierarchical representations of the pretrained geometric foundation model throughout this pipeline. A shared image encoder produces features for compact global descriptors; the same features are passed to the cross-view decoder for pairwise verification; and the original geometric predictions provide relative camera constraints for pose-graph optimization. Experiments on place recognition, loop verification, and relative pose estimation show that U NI LOOP improves loop-closure robustness and accuracy while reducing redundant backbone computation relative to modular pipelines. U NI LOOP 

achieves the best Recall@10 on MSLS (96.6) and Pitts30k (99.3) among the compared methods, while its verification branch reaches 95.63 AP and 96.29 ROC-AUC, surpassing the existing geometric verification baselines.

A visual place recognition model retrieves candidate matches, a geometric verification (e.g., local feature image matching model) module checks their consistency, and a relative pose module recovers the camera pose between the query and its top candidate, which is finally fed as a constraint into pose graph optimization. Although effective, this design duplicates image encoding, prevents intermediate representation sharing, and yields independently optimized modules whose objectives are only loosely aligned with the final loop closure decision. Recent geometric foundation models (GFMs) offer a different opportunity. MASt3R [1] contains a natural hier-archy of 3D-aware visual representations, where its image encoder produces single-view features, its cross-view decoder constructs pair-conditioned representations, and its geometric heads predict dense 3D structure and matching cues. This hierarchy aligns with the stages of loop closure detection: global representations serve retrieval, while patch-level repre-sentations support verification and relative pose estimation. Motivated by this observation, we introduce UNI LOOP ,a unified loop-closure framework that reuses a single GFM backbone, MASt3R, for retrieval, verification, and relative pose estimation. We attach a SALAD aggregator [2] to the MASt3R encoder and train the resulting global descriptors with contrastive learning. For verification, we build on the pairwise reasoning paradigm of Doppelgangers++ [3] to classify retrieved pairs from intermediate MASt3R decoder states. Finally, we retain MASt3R’s dense outputs and convert them into relative camera constraints for the downstream pose-graph optimization. arXiv:2611.06378v1 [cs.RO] 4 Nov 2026 A key design principle of UNI LOOP is that the three tasks do not merely coexist as independent heads: the same image encoding is reused across retrieval and pairwise decoding, avoiding redundant backbone inference. Training is staged rather than fully joint—we first train the SALAD retrieval branch, then freeze it and supervise the verification head on hard negatives mined from the trained retrieval branch. Our main contributions are summarized as follows:  

> •

We propose UNI LOOP , a unified loop-closure detection framework that maps the hierarchical representations of a 3D geometric foundation model onto the retrieval, verification, and relative pose estimation stages, sharing a single backbone across all three.  

> •

We instantiate this design through (i) a MASt3R-encoder retrieval branch that aggregates encoder tokens into compact global place descriptors without a separate image backbone, (ii) a lightweight verification transformer that classifies retrieved pairs from pair-conditioned MASt3R decoder states, and (iii) the retained MASt3R geometric predictions, converted into relative camera constraints for pose-graph optimization.  

> •

We establish a staged training and evaluation protocol that measures both module-level performance and end-to-end retrieval-conditioned loop-closure quality. The rest of the paper is organized as follows. Section II reviews related work. Section III presents the UNI LOOP 

framework and training strategy, and Section IV reports the experimental evaluation. Finally, Section V concludes the paper and outlines future work. II. R ELATED WORK 

A. Visual Place Recognition 

Modern visual place recognition (VPR) aggregates patch-level features from pretrained vision transformers into com-pact global descriptors for retrieval under viewpoint and ap-pearance changes [4], [5]. SALAD [2] uses optimal-transport aggregation with a dustbin to suppress non-informative regions, and MegaLoc [6] scales this paradigm through large-scale training, but their representations are independent of geometric downstream tasks. UNI LOOP instead applies SALAD-style aggregation to the MASt3R [1] encoder, so the same geometry-aware tokens support both retrieval and subsequent geometric reasoning. 

B. Loop Closure Detection and Geometric Verification 

Loop closure detection typically retrieves candidates before testing their geometric consistency [7]. Because global descriptors are prone to perceptual aliasing [8], verification commonly relies on local correspondences and epipolar geometry or learned pairwise classifiers. Doppelgangers++ [3] demonstrates that geometric features from GFMs can dis-ambiguate image pairs. UNI LOOP builds on this idea by classifying intermediate, pair-conditioned decoder states from the shared backbone, thereby rejecting geometrically inconsistent retrieved candidates. 

C. Geometric Foundation Models 

Geometric foundation models (GFMs) predict dense, view-consistent 3D representations from images. DUSt3R [9] pre-dicts pointmaps for unconstrained pairs, and MASt3R [1] adds dense local descriptors and reciprocal matching. VGGT [10] extends this to multi-view reconstruction, and VGGT-Omega [11] scales it with substantially larger training data. Their encoder–decoder–head hierarchy naturally supports the retrieval, verification, and pose-estimation tasks addressed by 

UNI LOOP .

D. Geometric Foundation Models for Loop Closure 

GFMs support navigation [12], SLAM [13], and crowd-sourced mapping [14], but in loop closure they serve mainly as frozen matchers or pose regressors: FLOCC-SLAM [15] pairs a VPR model with a frozen MASt3R pose regressor; MASt3R-SLAM [16] reuses features for retrieval and pointmap matching without training; and MR.ScaleMaster [17] though leverages various GFMs as front-end, it still relies on backend heuristics for loop rejection and scale consistency. UNI LOOP 

instead trains task-specific retrieval and verification heads on the MASt3R hierarchy, while retaining its geometric heads for relative pose estimation. III. UNI LOOP

D. Training Strategy

During retrieval adaptation, we distill knowledge from a frozen MegaLoc [6] teacher into our MASt3R–SALAD retrieval branch. We evaluate the global retrieval and loop-verification branches separately. Retrieval is assessed with Recall@ K

(K ∈ { 1, 5, 10 }), i.e., the fraction of queries whose ground-truth match is retrieved among the top-K database images. For loop verification, we report average precision (AP), ROC-AUC, and maximum recall at 100% precision (MR). This branch-wise evaluation isolates the contribution of candidate retrieval and subsequent geometric disambiguation. 

A. Datasets a) Training.: Stage-I retrieval adaptation is trained on GSV-Cities [18], which provides multiple street-level views per place and thus supports per-place metric-learning batches with natural positive pairs. Stage-II pair mining and Stage-III verification training use Mapillary Street-Level Sequences (MSLS) [19], a large-scale multi-city dataset with substantial appearance, viewpoint, and seasonal change. 

b) Evaluation.: We evaluate retrieval on five standard VPR benchmarks: MSLS [19], Pitts250k and Pitts30k [20], [21], Tokyo 24/7 [22], and Baidu [23]. Together, these bench-marks cover city-scale retrieval under substantial viewpoint and appearance variation. For verification, we evaluate on the MSLS validation set, which contains 150K positive pairs and 180K negative pairs for loop verification.

V. C ONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK 

We introduced UNI LOOP , a unified framework for full loop closure detection built on the hierarchical representations of GFM, i.e., MASt3R. The shared image encoder supports global retrieval through a trainable SALAD aggregation head, pair-conditioned decoder features support loop verification, and dense geometric outputs provide the basis for relative pose constraints. By reusing a common backbone, UNI LOOP 

connects candidate generation and geometric validation while avoiding redundant image encoding. Several directions remain for future work. First, UNI LOOP 

can be extended to multi-view GFMs, which jointly reason over more than two images. Such backbones could provide a richer multi-view representation and improve efficiency by reducing the repeated pairwise decoding required by the currently two-view MASt3R backbone. Second, our current MegaLoc distillation supervises only the final global descrip-tor. Incorporating consistency constraints on intermediate latent representations may transfer richer retrieval knowledge and further improve the student retrieval branch. Finally, we will deploy UNI LOOP in real robot localization systems and evaluate its impact on long-term pose-graph optimization. This evaluation will establish the practical effectiveness of the unified retrieval, verification, and the relative-pose pipeline under real-world operating conditions. REFERENCES [1] V. Leroy, Y.
