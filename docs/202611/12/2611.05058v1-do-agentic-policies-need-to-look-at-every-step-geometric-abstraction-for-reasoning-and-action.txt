Abstract: Recent multimodal language models can reason directly about robot scenes, but it is unclear whether they need to repeatedly reinterpret raw visual observations at every step of an interaction. We propose shifting routine spatial reasoning from images to text and symbols through a 3D geometric abstraction that is refreshed locally from observations and read directly by the language models. Specifically, our interface represents scene geometry via typed, evidence-aware symbolic references to object parts, grounds them from calibrated RGB-D, and returns compact textual checkpoints with metric residuals after each action. Symbolic task structure can thus be reused across decisions while metric values are rebound to the current scene, with direct visual reasoning invoked only on demand. Across offline policy synthesis and continuous online execution, reasoning over grounded text and symbols improves multi-step manipulation completion while substantially reducing repeated multimodal queries, positioning explicit geometric state as an effective substrate for agentic robot policies.

## 1 Introduction 

Recent frontier multimodal models have demonstrated increasingly strong 3D spatial reasoning and, in some cases, can directly drive robot manipulation without task-specific policy training [1, 2, 3, 4]. However, deploying such models in closed-loop manipulation often requires repeatedly reinterpreting visual observations as the scene evolves, even though many manipulation decisions are governed by a compact set of task-relevant geometric quantities [5, 6]. Repeatedly recovering these quantities from raw images introduces both reasoning uncertainty and additional computational cost: metric and structural relations remain challenging for vision-language models, whereas explicitly representing spatial information in text can improve reasoning reliability [7, 8, 9, 10]. Moreover, visual tokens often carry redundancy, incurring unnecessary overhead during reasoning [11]. This raises a central question: does a robot agent need to reinterpret the visual scene at every decision step? 

Existing interfaces trade off richness and compactness. Raw images retain detailed scene information but require repeated visual interpretation, while conventional perception APIs often expose only coarse object-level quantities such as centers or bounding boxes. ReKep [12] shows that explicit 3D keypoints and relational constraints can provide an effective interface for manipulation. Meanwhile, language-model-based robotics is moving toward persistent and structured policies: Code as Policies [13] synthesizes executable programs, ASPIRE [14] extends this paradigm through execution–diagnosis– repair and skill reuse, and Graph-as-Policy [15] represents policies as modular graphs. Policies reason in text and symbols over stable geometric roles whose metric values are rebound to the current scene during execution. To address this question, we propose shifting routine spatial reasoning from images to text and symbols through an explicit 3D geometric abstraction (Fig. 1). Rather than reducing objects to single 3D centroids or vague natural-language descriptions, our abstraction models scene geometry as a structured set of typed 3D object parts—including centers, vertices, edges, and oriented surfaces— annotated with timestamps and evidence status ( observed , inferred from shape priors, or unavailable 

under occlusion). Local perception grounds this 3D state from calibrated RGB-D and exposes it to the language model in two forms. First, symbolic references (e.g., red.bottom_face , blue.top_face )and geometric operators (such as surface alignment and support checks) let policies compose actions independently of specific object coordinates. Second, compact textual checkpoints report updated 3D coordinates, metric goal residuals, and evidence status after each action. Separating reusable symbolic structure from scene-dependent metric grounding lets the language model reason directly in text and symbols, invoking visual reasoning on demand when richer scene context is needed. We study this textual and symbolic 3D interface in two regimes. In policy synthesis , the language model revises an executable policy between rollouts using execution feedback, while geometric grounding and control run locally during rollouts. In online execution , the agent repeatedly reasons and selects skills within a single continuing episode using the updated textual checkpoint after each action, without resetting the scene after failures. Both settings use matched sensors, actuators, and initial skills. Across both regimes, our experiments show that reasoning over grounded text and symbols accelerates policy acquisition, substantially improves multi-step online construction completion, and reduces repeated multimodal queries and interaction latency. We use these two settings to examine when explicit textual and symbolic 3D state is sufficient for manipulation and when direct visual reasoning remains necessary. Our contributions are threefold: (i) we formulate a 3D geometric abstraction that exposes task-relevant scene geometry to language models as structured text and symbols—combining typed, evidence-aware part references with reusable symbolic structure and local metric grounding; (ii) we instantiate this abstraction as a reasoning-to-action interface with composable geometric operators, local RGB-D grounding, and compact textual checkpoints; and (iii) we empirically evaluate the interface across policy synthesis and continuous online execution, characterizing its impact on manipulation completion, model interaction cost, and the remaining role of direct visual input.

## 3 Geometric Abstraction as a Reasoning-to-Action Interface 

Our interface represents scene geometry through typed symbolic references that are grounded in current RGB-D observations at execution time. Updated geometry and goal residuals are returned as compact textual checkpoints for subsequent reasoning (Fig. 2).

3.4 Policy synthesis and online execution 

We evaluate the same interface in two operational regimes. In policy synthesis (single-policy acquisi-tion), the language model revises an executable program between batches of rollouts; within each rollout, geometry is grounded locally and the program executes without further language-model calls. In online execution , the language model instead participates directly in the continuing interaction loop:

After each action, new observations re-ground the relevant references, ensuring that recovery decisions start from the observed post-action scene rather than from the intended action outcome. Images remain available on demand when the geometric state is insufficient. 

## 4 Experiments 

We evaluate the geometric abstraction in simulation with an SO-100 robot arm equipped with calibrated side and wrist RGB-D cameras. Across all experiments, the agent receives the same task specification, sensor access, actuator interface, and initial skills; no simulator object poses or private evaluator state are exposed. We compare a Pixel Reasoning agent that reasons directly from visual observations with a Geometric Abstraction agent that additionally receives our grounded geometric references and textual checkpoints. We study the interface in two regimes: policy synthesis across repeated rollouts and continuous online execution within a single, non-resetting episode. 

4.1 Single-policy acquisition 

In the policy synthesis regime, we test whether geometric abstraction helps an LLM acquire effective single-program policies within a small revision budget. The simulated SO-100 performs three manipulation tasks with randomized object poses and colors (Figure 3): Bridge+cylinder (placing two base blocks, spanning them with a beam, and placing an upright cylinder on top), Three-cube stacking (assembling a three-cube tower in a prescribed color order), and Ring-on-peg (grasping a hexagonal ring and threading it onto a vertical peg).

## 5 Limitations and Discussion 

Our evaluation focuses on simulated table-top manipulation with regular object geometries. Real-world deployment remains an essential next step to assess robustness under realistic sensor noise, heavy occlusion, and camera calibration drift. We deliberately used lightweight CPU-based geometric estimators to keep local grounding fast and self-contained, leaving learned 3D perception modules and articulated or deformable objects for future work. In continuous online execution, geometric abstraction reduced decision rounds and on-demand image attachments across tasks, indicating that its primary benefit lies in providing direct, actionable spatial state throughout the reasoning–action loop rather than merely compressing prompt length. More concise checkpoint schemas and learned symbolic abstractions could further reduce interaction overhead. For novel object categories, VLM-assisted shape initialization could broaden coverage, provided its one-time grounding cost is accounted for alongside policy execution and amortized across subsequent interactions. Finally, decoupling local visual grounding from high-level policy reasoning opens the possibility of driving manipulation with fast text-only language models. By exposing verified, sensor-grounded 3D state in text, the interface allows a language model to reason over physical geometry without processing dense visual tokens at every step. Evaluating diverse models on standardized geometric checkpoints could clarify how effectively language models perform spatial reasoning when metric relations are made explicit, and help delineate which manipulation decisions are well served by structured geometric state versus direct visual inspection. 

## 6 Conclusion 

We investigated whether agentic robot policies need to reinterpret visual observations at every decision step. Across both offline policy synthesis and continuous online execution, our results demonstrate that maintaining an explicit geometric abstraction improves manipulation completion while substantially reducing repeated multimodal queries. By pairing reusable symbolic references with metric values refreshed from local RGB-D observations, the interface allows visual sensing to remain continuously active in the control loop without requiring the language model to process images at every step—positioning structured geometric state as a practical substrate for agentic reasoning and action, with direct visual reasoning invoked on demand. 8References

## C Geometric abstraction implementation 

The geometric abstraction converts calibrated RGB-D observations into metric entities, symbolic references, and compact textual checkpoints. It runs locally on the CPU using fixed geometric estimators throughout both policy synthesis and online execution. 

From pixels to metric geometry. Color segmentation masks associate visible pixels with scene objects. For a valid pixel ˜u = ( u, v, 1) ⊤, its 3D world position is recovered via back-projection: 

pW = RW C 

 D(u, v )K−1 ˜u + tW C , (6) where D(u, v ) is optical-axis depth, K is the camera intrinsic matrix, and (RW C , tW C ) transforms camera coordinates to the world frame. Background depth points estimate the tabletop plane, while known robot link geometry masks out the manipulator. Cuboid and cylinder estimators fit object pose and visible support surfaces using dimensions initialized from RGB-D under declared shape and table-support priors. Simulator ground-truth object poses and dimensions are never accessed. 

References and current evidence. Symbolic references such as red.top_face resolve to typed geometric primitives: points, axes, or oriented faces with centers and outward normals. Each record stores world-frame coordinates, physical units, observation timestamps, and evidence status (observed , inferred , or unavailable ). Initialized shape dimensions persist across an episode, whereas a movable object’s pose must be refreshed from current visual evidence after movement. For partially occluded rings, initialized boundary templates are registered to currently visible contour edges; underconstrained or ambiguous fits return unavailable . Unobserved object faces retain model-derived labels marked as inferred, while the stationary peg may retain an earlier verified measurement with its original timestamp. Geometric support predicates report metric residuals and missing evidence rather than certifying physical contact or dynamic stability. 

From geometry to agent input. The Geometric Abstraction agent receives task-relevant object ge-ometry, symbolic references, evidence metadata, and requested goal residuals formatted as structured text (with dense boundary point arrays omitted from online Ring-on-peg checkpoints for compact-ness). Multiple geometric queries can reuse a single RGB-D capture, and local sensing can refresh the geometric state after an action without attaching new images to the LLM prompt. Initial and on-demand camera images remain accessible whenever visual inspection is requested. In policy 12 synthesis, generated programs invoke geometric operators locally during rollouts; in online execution, the agent reads updated textual checkpoints to select its next skill or recovery action. 13
