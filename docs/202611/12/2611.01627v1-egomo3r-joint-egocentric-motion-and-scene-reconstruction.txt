# Abstract 

Understanding holistic 3D human motion and the surrounding environment from egocentric video is fundamental for applications in AR/VR and robotics. Although these tasks are inherently coupled, existing methods treat them separately. In particular, egocentric motion reconstruction methods typically assume access to metric, gravity-aligned camera trajectories from specialized inertial sensors. Recovering such trajectories from monocular egocentric video remains a significant challenge due to rapid head movements and motion blur. We present EgoMo3R, a joint optimization framework that simultaneously reconstructs 3D human motion and scene geometry from casual egocentric video. It leverages the robustness of low-level visuo-geometric primitives, including monocular depth, optical flow, and point tracks, integrated with a diffusion-based human motion prior via first-order optimization. We propose an alternating optimization scheme that establishes a synergistic feedback loop: scene reconstruction provides physical grounding and trajectory conditioning for the motion prior, while the motion prior regularizes the camera trajectory and anchors the scene in a metric, gravity-aligned world frame. We evaluate EgoMo3R on the highly dynamic sequences of the EgoExo4D and Nymeria datasets and show that it outperforms specialized baselines in both 3D scene reconstruction and egocentric motion estimation. 

# 1 Introduction 

A holistic 3D understanding of human motion and the surrounding environment from egocentric video is crucial for human-centered applications such as AR/VR, assistive technologies, and healthcare, while also offering a scalable pathway for robot learning. In the egocentric setting, 3D human motion and scene reconstruction are inherently coupled: recovering the user’s full-body motion requires pre-cise trajectory estimation of the egocentric camera that naturally follows the head motion. Traditional 

visual SLAM methods [4, 11, 47, 67, 68] typically treat camera trajectory estimation as a second-order optimization problem relying on multi-view correspondences to optimize for camera poses and sparse geometric representations. However, these methods often struggle on dynamic egocentric 

locomotion videos with narrow fields of view and motion blur. This ‘inside-out’ perspective results in sparse, low-quality correspondences leading to an underconstrained optimization, particularly during the rapid, rotation-heavy head movements common in daily activities [5]. While recent data-driven feedforward reconstruction methods [71, 74] offer a promising alternative, they often struggle to generalize due to the egocentric domain gap. Consequently, existing egocentric motion reconstruction methods largely bypass these geometric challenges by assuming access to a perfect metric and gravity-aligned camera trajectories from a dedicated inertial SLAM pipeline [80, 49, 24]. While joint modeling of humans and 3D scenes has shown promise in third-person settings [46, 8, 58], these

In this paper, we take the first step towards the joint modeling of egocentric motion and scene geometry with EgoMo3R (see Fig. 1). We demonstrate that EgoMo3R enables the synergistic reconstruction of both 3D human motion and the surrounding environment from a casually captured egocentric video. At its core, our approach formulates this task as a first-order joint optimization problem that recovers camera poses, scene geometry, and human motion in a single reference frame by leveraging data-driven low-level geometry and motion priors. Our first insight is that while feedforward 3D reconstruction models often struggle with the egocentric domain gap, low-level visuo-geometric primitives, such as monocular depth, optical flow, and point tracks, exhibit significantly higher robustness. Although these cues can be individually noisy and mutually inconsistent, they provide complementary geometric constraints that, when aggregated within a global optimization framework, yield a coherent reconstruction. Specifically, our formulation utilizes monocular depth as a geometric base and point tracking for long-term temporal correspon-dence. To ensure convergence, we leverage optical flow to initialize the camera trajectory, effectively guiding our first-order optimization away from the poor local minima typical of rapid, rotation-heavy egocentric locomotion sequences. Our second key insight is that egocentric human motion and scene reconstruction can be mutually reinforcing. Accurate 3D scene reconstruction with a precise camera trajectory directly informs head motion and offers explicit physical grounding for the body within the 3D environment. Conversely, 3D human motion serves as a unique prior for camera trajectory estimation, beyond simple constraints such as constant velocity or smoothness, common in traditional SLAM. Human motion priors also anchor the optimization in a canonical upright (metric, gravity-aligned, grounded) world frame, thereby improving the reconstruction on challenging egocentric videos. However, unlike exocentric scenarios, jointly optimizing human motion with scene geometry is non-trivial in the egocentric domain, where the body remains largely out of view. To address this, we employ a motion diffusion generative model as a robust motion prior. In particular, we use a motion diffusion model conditioned on SE (3) -invariant egocentric trajectory and formulate an alternating optimization framework that iteratively refines the 3D scene and human motion. This setup creates a synergistic feedback loop: improved camera pose provides more accurate conditioning for the motion prior, which in turn improves physically grounded reconstruction. We evaluate EgoMo3R on EgoExo4D [18] and Nymeria [42] datasets and show that it outperforms strong baselines in both motion and scene reconstruction. Finally, we provide an extensive ablation of our 2optimization framework, including the technical design and integration of neural motion priors into our reconstruction pipeline. In summary, we make the following contributions: (1) We present a joint first-order optimization framework that simultaneously recovers metric camera poses, scene geometry, and human motion from a single egocentric video. (2) We show that integrating complementary low-level visuo-geometric primitives and motion prior mitigates the challenging egocentric domain gap. (3) Our alternating optimization incorporates a diffusion-based motion prior to provide physical grounding and gravity alignment for both the human motion and the reconstructed scene. (4) EgoMo3R outperforms strong specialized baselines in both 3D scene reconstruction and egocentric motion estimation. 

# 2 Related Works 

Egocentric Motion Reconstruction: The front-facing nature of egocentric RGB cameras [15] presents significant challenges for motion reconstruction due to limited body visibility. To compensate for this, many works use simulation-based physical motion priors [41, 84, 83], data-driven motion diffusion priors [49, 32, 80, 24, 19, 6], or autoregressive motion priors [72, 20] learned from large-scale motion datasets. However, these priors critically depend on an accurate, metric, and gravity-aligned egocentric camera trajectory. Several recent works [49, 80, 9, 19] sidestep this challenge by assuming access to such trajectories from highly optimized inertial SLAM systems [15] with multi-modal sensors, which are not always available in casual capture scenarios. Although classical monocular SLAM or VIO [67, 68] methods can provide an SE (3) -invariant trajectory, these estimates are typically inaccurate and prone to significant jitter in egocentric setup. To remedy this, some methods [32, 41, 84, 83] attempt to learn camera pose estimation directly from optical flow features. We demonstrate that such flow-based learned pose estimation models struggle with egocentric locomotion in cluttered scenes, which subsequently degrades downstream motion reconstruction. In contrast, EgoMo3R presents a robust optimization framework that simultaneously recovers camera poses, 3D scene geometry, and human motion from casual egocentric videos in a synergistic manner. 

Camera Pose Estimation & 3D Reconstruction: Classic SfM [61, 62, 48, 1] and SLAM [4, 11, 47] frameworks typically formulate camera pose and 3D scene estimation as a second-order bundle adjustment problem, relying on multi-view correspondences from locally invariant keypoint descriptors [39, 59]. To improve robustness, various approaches integrate data-driven components ranging from learned sparse features [60, 12] to dense correspondence learning [68, 67, 65]. Recent methods [87, 70, 64, 35, 23, 73] further leverage robust priors, such as long-term point tracking [25, 14, 26], monocular depth [75, 79], video depth [7], and semantic segmentation [29, 56], to handle dynamic scenes and sparse views. While several works have adapted similar strategies to the egocentric setting [85, 31, 82], they struggle on egocentric locomotion videos with rapid, rotation-heavy camera movement. Although recent end-to-end feedforward models [77, 30, 86, 71, 74] are promising due to their simplicity, their performance often degrades on challenging egocentric inputs. Closing this gap through finetuning is difficult since existing egocentric datasets lack large-scale, dense, high-quality metric 3D groundtruth. EgoMo3R addresses these challenges by combining robust visuo-geometric primitives with a diffusion human motion prior, ensuring accurate, physically grounded, and gravity-aligned reconstruction. 

Joint Human and Scene Reconstruction: Several works have leveraged the presence of humans within a scene to overcome the limitations of classical reconstruction approaches. For instance, Ma et al. [43] and Xu et al. [78] use 3D human pose estimation and person re-identification, respectively, to define virtual correspondences for geometry estimation in extreme views. Recent methods [51, 46, 58, 38] perform joint 3D reconstruction of humans and scenes by optimizing global objectives on top of independent predictions. More recently, Human3R [8] builds upon CUT3R [74] using visual prompting to efficiently train for simultaneous 3D human mesh and scene reconstruction in a feedforward manner. These approaches demonstrate that joint reconstruction is not only achievable but that the two tasks can be mutually beneficial [88, 8]. However, such methods rely on explicit human-related visual cues within the image. In contrast, the user’s body remains largely invisible in egocentric viewpoints. In this setting, human-specific cues are not encoded in the raw pixels, but rather in the camera motion that generated them. EgoMo3R leverages these implicit motion cues for the synergistic reconstruction of humans and scenes in the egocentric domain. 33 Method 

3.1 Problem Formulation 

Given an egocentric video sequence I1: N ∈ RN ×H×W ×3 captured by a head-mounted camera, our goal is to jointly estimate camera trajectory, 3D scene geometry, and the underlying 3D human motion in a physically grounded canonical world frame with known gravity direction and ground plane. We denote the camera poses as extrinsics C ∈ RN ×4×4 representing camera-to-world SE (3) 

transformations. The 3D scene geometry is represented by a sequence of dense pointmaps [77] 

P ∈ RN ×H×W ×3, where each element P n,i,j ∈ R3 corresponds to the 3D coordinates of pixel (i, j )

at time n in the local camera coordinate frame. The global 3D scene representation P W in the world frame is obtained by transforming local pointmaps P using the estimated camera poses C. The 3D human motion M 1: N is defined by SMPL-X parameters [50] Mn = ( Rrn, trn, θ n) comprising root rotation Rrn ∈ R3, translation trn ∈ R3 (at pelvis), and body pose angles θn ∈ R21 ×3.

3.2 Visual Geometry Priors 

While end-to-end egocentric reconstruction often struggles with egocentric domain shift, low-level visuo-geometric primitives offer a more robust basis for optimization. We formulate a first-order optimization framework that treats these primitives as soft constraints. Rather than treating 3D points as free variables, we adopt per-frame relative depth Dr ∈ RN ×H×W , predicted by an off-the-shelf monocular depth estimator [76], as our base geometry. While modern monocular estimators [75, 76, 79] provide robust relative depth, their metric scale consistency often degrades in egocentric settings. In egocentric settings, monocular depth and optical flow often degrade due to motion blur and narrow fields of view. Consequently, small errors compound over time, leading to significant trajectory drift and distorted 3D reconstructions. Traditional SLAM pipelines [11, 67, 23, 35] typically employ a two-tiered system to correct such drift via loop closure. We demonstrate in our experiments that relying on visuo-geometric priors already yields strong performance in camera pose estimation and 3D scene reconstruction. However, these purely visual constraints remain insufficient for recovering the underlying human motion, gravity direction, or floor grounding. 

3.3 Motion Prior 

The egocentric setup is unique in that the camera trajectory C directly follows the human head motion. Prior works [32, 80, 49] have leveraged this to perform egocentric human motion reconstruction, often without access to paired datasets, achieving impressive results even when the body largely remains out-of-view. These methods typically formulate motion reconstruction as sampling from 

p(M |C), where C is assumed to be a metric, gravity-aligned, and floor-grounded camera trajectory. This conditional distribution p(M |C) is usually modeled via a conditional motion diffusion model 

fϕ(M t, C) with learnable parameters ϕ, where M t denotes the noisy motion at diffusion timestep t.However, incorporating a strong diffusion-based motion prior into our joint optimization framework is non-trivial for several reasons. First, unlike prior works, we do not have a priori access to a gravity-and floor-aligned camera trajectory C. Furthermore, training unconditional diffusion models remains inherently difficult for complex distributions such as images [33, 22] and human motion [55, 81]. We observe that directly minimizing Eq. 1 via gradient descent often yields suboptimal results and poor alignment between the camera trajectory C and human motion M . This instability stems primarily from two factors. First, tracking failures can introduce significant outlier correspondences that bias the optimization. While we employ Median Absolute Deviation (MAD) filtering – analogous to RANSAC [16] in traditional SLAM – a poor initialization can lead to the erroneous rejection of valid correspondences, causing the optimization to converge to a local minimum. To mitigate this, we initialize the camera trajectory by optimizing C solely with the Lflow objective. As discussed in Sec. 3.2, Lflow is a 2D reformulation of the 3D constraint Cn+1 Pn+1 ,i +δu,j +δv = CnPn,i,j , which has a closed-form solution using Procrustes analysis [17]. This warm-start enables more accurate outlier detection and stabilizes subsequent optimization steps. Second, the simultaneous optimization of C and M introduces noisy gradients from Lmotion , as our diffusion prior fϕ(M t, A(C)) is itself a function of the evolving camera trajectory C [34]. To resolve this, we propose an alternating optimization scheme that iterates between two distinct modes: 

1. Camera and Scene Refinement: We optimize the camera poses C and depth scales S using Lflow ,

Ltrack , Ldepth , and Lscene , while keeping the human motion M fixed. This stage refines the 3D scene reconstruction while leveraging the gravity and ground-plane priors from fixed motion M .

2. Motion and Prior Alignment: We optimize only the motion parameters M using Lmotion and 

Legocam , while keeping (C, S) fixed. This stage enforces the generative motion prior and eliminates physical implausibilities in the camera trajectory, such as jitters or sudden jumps. It also defines the canonical frame to improve 3D reconstruction. This mode can be interpreted as sampling from the posterior p(M |A (C)) with guidance [13] provided by the camera-head alignment loss Legocam .We demonstrate empirically that this alternating strategy significantly improves joint reconstruction results compared to direct optimization. # 4 Experiments 

4.1 Dataset 

We evaluate our approach on EgoExo4D [18] and Nymeria [42] datasets. Both datasets provide multimodal egocentric data captured via Project Aria smartglasses [15]. The Aria MPS kit provides high-quality groundtruth camera poses obtained through an extensive offline VIO and SLAM system that fuses data from multiple sensors [15]. All frames are undistorted to a linear pinhole camera following the official Project Aria guidelines [15], at 512 ×512 resolution with a focal length of 150 px and without cropping. All methods, including EgoMo3R, receive these identical rectified frames. To evaluate 3D reconstruction, we filter the MPS SLAM point clouds using 2D observation metadata to retain only high-confidence points. For 3D human motion, Nymeria provides groundtruth motion annotations, while for EgoExo4D, we use the 3D human motion fits provided by UniEgoMotion [49] as groundtruth. For our testing, we sample video clips uniformly at random from both datasets. To further assess performance in challenging locomotion scenarios, we additionally include segments where groundtruth camera poses exhibit high movement. We report metrics on this combined test set in the main paper, while separate metrics on the uniform and locomotion-heavy subsets are provided in the Appendix.

4.3 Human Motion Reconstruction Results Baselines. We evaluate human motion reconstruction of EgoMo3R against baseline methods that don’t rely on a metric gravity-aligned camera trajectory. For EgoEgo [32], its head pose estimator is trained on optical flow features from the EgoExo4D dataset (see Sec. 4.2), while the gravity direction predictor is trained on AMASS [44]. For UniEgoMotion [49] and EgoAllo [80], we obtain a metric camera trajectory from CUT3R or ViPE and estimate the floor plane using their floor-segmented [56] pointmaps. Since floor estimation depends on reconstruction quality, CUT3R offers a balance of pose and geometry accuracy, whereas ViPE provides more accurate poses but weaker geometry. As an oracle upper bound, we also condition both methods on groundtruth trajectories. Given UniEgoMotion’s support for flexible inputs, we also train its variant on EgoExo4D that directly reconstructs human motion from input video without requiring trajectory input. Both EgoEgo and UniEgoMotion baselines are trained on the EgoExo4D training set and evaluated on both EgoExo4D and Nymeria sequences to analyze their generalization capacity. 

Metrics. We evaluate methods on their ability to track head motion using head rotation and head translation error [49, 32]. MPJPE calculates mean per-joint position error (in meters) after globally aligning predicted body joints with groundtruth body joints. MPJPE-PA is calculated after separately aligning each frame’s motion and only measures the local human pose accuracy regardless of its global alignment. FC (Foot Contact) [49] quantifies the plausibility of foot-ground contact.

# 5 Conclusion & Future Work 

We present EgoMo3R, an optimization framework that jointly reconstructs egocentric human motion, camera trajectory, and metric, gravity-aligned scene geometry from casually captured monocular egocentric video. By combining low-level visuo-geometric cues with a diffusion-based motion prior conditioned on an SE (3) -invariant representation of the camera trajectory, our approach exploits the inherent coupling between camera trajectory, scene geometry, and body pose in the egocentric setting. EgoMo3R outperforms specialized baselines on the EgoExo4D and Nymeria datasets, and our ablations show that a learned motion prior outperforms a simple smoothness penalty. This suggests that learned human motion distributions are a promising direction for egocentric camera and scene reconstruction. Looking ahead, we plan to explore motion priors that, unlike diffusion models, allow efficient likelihood estimation during optimization, such as VAE-based formulations [57]. Future work could also investigate feedforward models that jointly reconstruct scene and motion in a single pass, for which EgoMo3R reconstructions could serve as pseudo-labels. We believe that EgoMo3R provides a strong foundation for future research on joint modeling of egocentric motion and 3D scenes.

15 A Extended Evaluation and Results 

Given that large-scale EgoExo4D and Nymeria datasets consist of egocentric sequences ranging from several minutes to over an hour, we extract 8-second segments for evaluation (following UniEgoMotion [49]). To ensure a comprehensive analysis, our evaluation set contains two distinct subsets: (1) a representative subset sampled uniformly at random from the full dataset, and (2) a locomotion-heavy subset containing high camera translation and rotation. While the main paper reports results on the combined set containing 718 samples from EgoExo4D and 828 samples from Nymeria, we provide disaggregated results for each subset here. Additionally, we also evaluate a subset characterized by rotation-heavy camera movement which is challenging for visual SLAM methods [5]. The disaggregated results are provided in Table 6 and 7. We further evaluate per-frame depth accuracy of monocular and feedforward geometry models (Table 8), sensitivity to the choice of off-the-shelf priors (Table 9), and performance on 3 × longer sequences (Table 10). # B Implementation Details 

We use RAFT [66] to compute both forward and backward optical flow, incorporating both into the optimization objective. Long-range correspondences are obtained using CoTracker3 [26] by seeding a 35 × 35 point grid at every 8-frame intervals and tracking them bi-directionally. Initial relative depth Dr and the metric scale prior Sm are provided by MoGe-2 models [76]. For the optimization objective, we set the loss weights to 1.0, 1.0, 5 × 10 −3, 5 × 10 −3, and 5 for Lflow , Ltrack , Lscene , Ldepth ,and Legocam , respectively. For the motion prior Lmotion , we sample the noise level t in decreasing order following [40] and scale the loss weight according to the signal-to-noise ratio of the noised sample [54]. To handle outliers in Lflow and Ltrack , we filter correspondences based on the Median Absolute Deviation (MAD) of the reprojection error. During each round of alternating optimization, we optimize (C, S) for 1000 steps, followed by 100 steps of optimization for M . Note that when 

(C, S) is fixed, optimizing the motion for T steps is equivalent to DDIM sampling of M for T

diffusion steps under the guidance of Legocam . We use the Adam optimizer with an initial learning rate of 0.01 , decaying to 0.001 after 850 steps. EgoMo3R takes approximately 8 min per 8-second clip on one NVIDIA L40S GPU: about 32 s to compute the visuo-geometric priors, 3 min and 4 min for scene optimization in the first and second rounds, and 5 s per round for motion optimization. This is slower than the feedforward baselines (a few seconds) and the optimization-based ViPE (about 1.5 min). However, after one round of optimization, at less than half the total cost, EgoMo3R already outperforms all baselines on metric 3D reconstruction on the EgoExo4D locomotion subset (Tabs. 5 and 6). Our motion prior p(M |A (C)) is implemented as a conditional denoising diffusion model 

fϕ(M t, A(C)) . Following [49], we adopt an egocentric body motion representation that implicitly defines a canonical reference frame with a ground plane and gravity direction. The denoiser is implemented as a 1D UNet [28], though we observed that transformer-based architecture [69] yields comparable performance. To evaluate the zero-shot motion generalization of EgoMo3R, we do not utilize motion annotations from EgoExo4D or Nymeria during training; and instead train exclusively on motion sequences from the AMASS dataset [44]. 

# C More Discussion on Motion Prior 

Our motion prior design (see Section 3.3) is motivated by the broader literature on inverse problems, guided sampling, and conditional generative models. Recall that our objective is to reconstruct body motion M , camera trajectory C, and depth scales S that are consistent with the observations provided by the egocentric video I. Since the camera trajectory C is tightly coupled with the head trajectory 

H(M ) derived from the body motion (enforced via Legocam ), we regularize the optimization of M

(and consequently C) using a prior on M . This formulation can be viewed as an inverse problem that aims to recover an unknown signal x from noisy and incomplete observations y = O(x) + ϵ,where a data term Ldata (O(x), y ) is minimized alongside a prior term Lprior (x). In our context, we optimize M using data terms ( Lflow , Ltrack , Legocam , etc.) regularized by the motion prior p(M |· ).Notably, the observations provide direct constraints only on a small subset of the motion M (i.e., the head trajectory), leaving the rest of the body pose highly dependent on the prior. 16 Figure 5: Point-tracking Failure : Inaccurate long-range correspondences from the point-tracking model cause EgoMo3R to predict stationary camera trajectory (green) and stationary motion (blue), whereas the groundtruth camera and motion trajectories (grey) move in a straight line. A straightforward approach to solving such inverse problems is to penalize the negative log-likelihood of x during optimization ( Lprior (x) = − log p(x)). Early works utilized simple pose priors modeled via Gaussian Mixture Models (GMMs) for single-frame tasks, such as fitting a 3D body model to 2D images [3, 50]. While GMMs allow for exact likelihood calculation and efficient sampling, their modeling capacity is limited for high-dimensional, complex distributions like human motion. Recently, some works have used VAE-based [50, 57] or diffusion-based [40] priors for their superior expressive power. However, since these models do not provide an explicit likelihood, incorporating them into a first-order optimization is non-trivial. Consider a diffusion model that predicts E[x0|xt],where the subscript denotes the noise level. One way to solve an inverse problem with such a model is to apply guidance [13] during each denoising step: 

E[x0|xt, y ] = E[x0|xt] + λ∇xt log p(y|xt)

where the second term provides guidance toward the observation y. [13] trained a separate classifier 

p(y|xt) for all noise levels to provide exact guidance. However, in many inverse problems, defining the guidance likelihood p(y|xt) on noisy data xt (rather than clean data x0) is analytically intractable, as it requires marginalizing over x0: p(y|xt) = R p(y|x0)p(x0|xt)dx 0. While various approximations have been proposed [36, 10, 52, 89, 45] that work well for simple pose or motion fitting with full-body 2D observations [57, 3, 50, 40], these approximations often fail when observations y are weak and highly localized, such as controlling only a specific body part trajectory [63, 28, 27], leading to suboptimal or physically implausible results. Instead, recent works [63, 28] directly model the conditional distribution p(x|y). This not only circumvents the intractability of the guidance term but also simplifies the learning task, as conditional generation is generally easier to learn than unconditional generation [33]. For egocentric recon-struction, prior methods [80, 49, 32, 19] typically assume access to a gravity-aligned, metric camera trajectory C and model p(M |C) using conditional diffusion. This approach is highly effective be-cause the head trajectory strongly constrains the distribution of the remaining body joints, especially torso and lower body. In our setting, however, we are also optimizing for C. One possible solution is to use an unconditional motion prior p(M ) and perform guided optimization using correspondence losses. But, as previously noted, this often leads to suboptimal results due to approximations. Instead, we use a conditional diffusion prior p(M |A (C)) , where the conditioning is based on the SE (3) -invariant trajectory A(C).This allows us to incorporate the motion prior early in the optimization process. Following [54], we do not backpropagate gradients through the prior model itself to maintain optimization stability. While this design provides effective regularization for C and improves 3D reconstruction (see Table 4, row 5), direct optimization often causes the motion to collapse to a solution that satisfies the trajectory 

C but disregards global physical plausibility, leading to worse motion metrics. We hypothesize this 17 is due to simultaneous optimization, where gradients on M are often noisy relative to the data-term gradients on C during early optimization steps. While it is possible to mitigate this by carefully scheduling the loss weights as C approaches the optimal solution, such tuning is often brittle. Instead, we propose a simple alternating scheme that iterates between optimizing (C, S) and M . As shown in Table 4 (row 6) in the paper and Table 5, this strategy significantly improves both 3D reconstruction and motion reconstruction metrics. 

# D Limitations & Future Work 

While EgoMo3R achieves superior 3D scene reconstruction and accurate human motion estimation, it is not without limitations. We discuss several of them here and outline potential future directions. The core design of EgoMo3R relies on the robustness and generalization of low-level visuo-geometric models on challenging egocentric data. We observe that while optical flow generally provides stable short-range correspondences, long-range point tracking remains a primary source of failure. One mode of point-tracking failure arises in regions with monotonic textures or around handheld objects. These erroneous tracks can derail camera trajectory optimization, leading to poor local minima, as illustrated in Fig. 5. A secondary source of failure involves frames with extreme motion blur, where both the relative depth and the metric scale prior Sm can become unreliable. This can result in sudden jitters or jumps in the estimated camera poses C. Although these local inaccuracies typically do not compromise the global trajectory, they can degrade the qualitative consistency and temporal smoothness of the resulting 3D reconstruction and motion M . Another minor failure mode is the lack of floor detection in all frames. While this does not catastrophically degrade performance, it weakens the alignment between motion and 3D reconstruction, leaving the system to rely primarily on the egocentric camera trajectory. Note that EgoMo3R focuses on reconstructing scene and body pose from egocentric locomotion video input. This is motivated by the fact that torso and lower-body motion are highly correlated with head motion. However, wrist motion and hand manipulation are largely unrelated to the rest of the body motion. Some prior works in egocentric motion reconstruction, such as EgoAllo [80] and HaMoS [9], use off-the-shelf 3D hand predictors and guide motion generation with accurate hand trajectories to reconstruct whole-body motion. However, our focus is primarily on locomotion without egocentric trajectory input, where body dynamics are more strongly coupled. Future work could explore leveraging 3D scene context to reconstruct globally accurate hand motion alongside whole-body motion, which remains highly challenging without canonical trajectory. Note that, like any SLAM method, dynamic objects violate our correspondence assumptions. We use Median Absolute Deviation (MAD) filtering to mitigate the effect of bad correspondences, which often arise from dynamic objects. However, when a large number of pixels in the input video belong to dynamic objects, MAD filtering may also remove valid correspondences. Many prior methods mask out dynamic objects using off-the-shelf estimators [23] or learned confidence masks [86, 35, 82]. While such dynamic-object masking did not provide significant benefits in our case, it can be easily incorporated into our pipeline by prompting the segmentation model used in our work with additional queries. Additionally, EgoMo3R inference is relatively slow due to its optimization-based framework compared to feedforward geometry models. However, it provides more accurate reconstruction and can serve as a source of pseudo-labels to improve feedforward methods. Further improvements may also be achieved through explicit loop closure, akin to traditional SLAM pipelines. Although the canonical camera trajectory C used in EgoAllo and UniEgoMotion contains more information than the SE (3) -invariant trajectory A(C), our motion reconstruction still outperforms these baselines. We hypothesize that because canonical camera trajectory is highly informative of lower-body motion, any error in canonicalizing the predicted camera trajectory can significantly affect motion generation in the absence of a feedback loop. In contrast, EgoMo3R conditions motion reconstruction on looser conditioning, thereby providing greater flexibility. However, removing canonicalization also discards crucial information about body motion, forcing the model to rely more on guidance from Lscene and Legocam (both in the canonical frame), which is often brittle (see Sec. C).
