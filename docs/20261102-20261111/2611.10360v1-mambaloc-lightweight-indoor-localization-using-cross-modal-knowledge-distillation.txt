Abstract —Accurate indoor localization on resource-constrained edge devices remains challenging due to the tension between modeling fidelity and computational efficiency. Wi-Fi Channel State Information (CSI) enables low-cost deployment using existing Wi-Fi infrastructure but is heavily distorted by multipath propagation. Transformer-based solutions mitigate these effects but their quadratic complexity limits real-time edge deployment. Ultra-Wideband (UWB) provides geometrically precise ranging yet requires dedicated infrastructure. We present MambaLoc, a cross-modal distillation framework that improves the accuracy–efficiency balance in CSI-based localization. MambaLoc uses a linear-time Selective State Space Model (Mamba) as a lightweight CSI student, guided during training by a UWB-Transformer teacher. A Feature Alignment Module bridges the representational gap between heterogeneous RF modalities, allowing the CSI model to inherit UWB-derived geometric structure without UWB hardware at inference. A probabilistic Gaussian Mixture regression head models multi-modal spatial ambiguity from multipath effects, avoiding the physically implausible averaging of deterministic regression. Evaluations on a real-world dataset show MambaLoc achieves a median localization error of 0.230 m, reducing error by 22.2% over a standalone Mamba baseline, while requiring 65.1% fewer FLOPs and 58% lower inference latency than Transformer-based approaches. 

Index Terms —Indoor Localization, CSI, UWB, State Space Models, Knowledge Distillation, Edge Intelligence 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Accurate indoor localization is critical for autonomous systems in GPS-denied environments, including warehouse robotics, drone navigation, and wearable IoT devices. While GNSS provides robust outdoor positioning, signals degrade indoors due to attenuation and multipath propagation [1]. As computation shifts to the edge, localization must deliver both high accuracy and efficient inference under strict hardware constraints. RF-based approaches leverage existing wireless infrastruc-ture. Wi-Fi CSI provides fine-grained amplitude and phase measurements across multiple subcarriers, implicitly encoding environmental geometry. However, indoor RF propagation is dominated by multipath effects: reflected and diffracted components arrive with different delays and phase shifts, distorting direct range estimates and introducing unreliability [2]. Localization from CSI therefore requires models that integrate information across packet sequences, separating per-sistent geometric patterns from short-term noise.

Deep learning has substantially improved CSI-based local-ization. Transformer architectures model long-range temporal structure via self-attention (e.g., TIPS [3]), but self-attention scales quadratically ( O(N 2)), making continuous inference impractical under Size, Weight, and Power (SWaP) constraints. To address this trade-off, we introduce MambaLoc , built on Selective State Space Models (SSMs). By leveraging the Mamba architecture [4], our approach achieves linear-time ( O(N )) inference with constant memory state. We fur-ther adopt a Cross-Modal Knowledge Distillation strategy inspired by the Learning Using Privileged Information (LUPI) paradigm [5]: UWB data serves as privileged training con-text, and a UWB-Transformer teacher guides a lightweight CSI-Mamba student via a Feature Alignment Module. The framework can be interpreted as learning a latent spatial representation of RF propagation, where the teacher provides geometric supervision that guides the CSI model toward physically consistent spatial embeddings. The remainder of this paper is organized as follows: Section II reviews related work; Section III covers theoretical foundations; Sections IV and V describe the MambaLoc architecture and distillation curriculum; Section VI presents empirical results. Our key contributions are: (1) Cross-modal RF knowl-edge distillation , a framework transferring geometric structure from UWB CIR to WiFi CSI across both sensing modali-ties (UWB →CSI) and architectures (Transformer →Mamba), enabling robust localization without UWB at inference; (2) efficient CSI sequence modeling via Selective State Space Models as an alternative to Transformers (0.39 vs. 1.12 

GFLOPs); (3) a cross-modal feature alignment module that projects teacher and student representations into a shared latent space, stabilizing distillation and reducing modality mismatch; and (4) a probabilistic spatial regression head using a GMM to model multipath-induced uncertainty. II. R ELATED WORK 

A. Sensing Modalities for Indoor Localization 

Indoor localization modalities carry distinct trade-offs against edge deployment requirements: SWaP, robustness, and infrastructure cost [1]. Vision SLAM [6] and LiDAR [7], [8] approach centimeter-level precision but demand high-throughput processing, degrade in low-light or texture-poor environments, introduce privacy concerns, and add significant payload [9]. UWB offers an excellent accuracy–efficiency arXiv:2611.10360v1 [cs.AI] 7 Nov 2026 balance but requires installing and maintaining a purpose-built anchor network whose geometry critically determines coverage quality; NLOS can still cause outliers even in UWB, making large-scale deployment non-trivial [1]. Geometry-based RF methods (ToA, TDoA, AoA) need no dedicated hardware but are acutely sensitive to NLOS conditions [10]; high-quality AoA additionally demands antenna arrays and tight clock synchronization. Wi-Fi FTM/RTT and BLE lower the infrastructure bar further [11], but FTM depends on compatible access points while BLE suffers RSSI instability and requires dense deployment [1] [12]. Sensor fusion mit-igates individual weaknesses but multiplies cost, power, and calibration complexity, which is contrary to lightweight edge deployment. Commodity Wi-Fi CSI occupies a uniquely attractive po-sition: available on already-deployed infrastructure with no additional hardware, it provides per-subcarrier complex chan-nel estimates encoding rich multipath structure far beyond coarse RSSI. The central challenge is extracting reliable spatial features from noisy, high-dimensional CSI, which brings us to deep learning approaches. 

B. Deep Learning for CSI Localization 

Classical RF localization relies on geometric properties (ToA, TDoA, AoA) [10], [13], often assuming reliable LOS propagation; multipath and NLOS conditions introduce severe biases [14]. In contrast, data-driven approaches map CSI fingerprints directly to locations. Early work used MLPs [15] and stacked denoising autoencoders with probabilistic frame-works [16]; hybrid CNN-LSTM architectures [17] added tem-poral modeling. Attention-augmented CNNs [18] capture both local and global context. Transformer-based models (TIPS [3], Swin-Loc [19]) achieve state-of-the-art accuracy; Transformer-CNN hybrids [20] combine global temporal modeling with local spatial extraction for UWB NLOS classification. How-ever, quadratic complexity is prohibitive for edge deployment. Quantization and distillation [21] compress Transformers for microcontrollers, yet even compressed models struggle under tight memory budgets. This motivates Mamba [4], which offers linear complexity while remaining suitable for edge hardware. Cross-modal supervision aligns with the LUPI paradigm [5]: high-precision sensors guide models that ultimately rely on cheaper sensors. DistilLoc [22] transfers knowledge from an RTT-based teacher to an RSSI-based student using F-Net, which replaces self-attention with FFT-based mixing (O(N log N )). MambaLoc goes further with a strictly linear 

O(N ) Mamba student and cross-modal, cross-architecture supervision, using UWB as a geometric teacher to enforce alignment between the student’s CSI latent space and UWB-derived spatial structure that would otherwise be unrecoverable from noisy Wi-Fi data alone. III. B ACKGROUND & T HEORETICAL FOUNDATIONS 

A. Sequence Modeling with Structured State-Space Models 

Indoor localization using CSI is inherently a sequential inference problem requiring long temporal dependency model-

ing in high-resolution wireless signals. Structured State-Space Models (SSMs) have re-emerged as a compelling alternative to attention-based architectures. S4 [23] and Mamba [4] combine continuous-time dynamical systems with the efficiency of re-current models. Mamba’s selective scan mechanism conditions state transition parameters on the current input xt, enabling adaptive propagation or suppression of information, which is beneficial for discriminating LOS signals from multipath noise. During training, Mamba leverages parallel scan opera-tions; at inference it runs as a recurrent model with constant memory state, processing long sequences in linear time on resource-constrained edge hardware.

C. Aleatoric Uncertainty in Wireless Localization 

Indoor multipath creates scenarios where different locations produce identical signal fingerprints, an inherent ambiguity termed aleatoric uncertainty [24]. Standard regression mod-els confronted with two equally plausible locations (e.g., the true location and a strong reflection) tend to predict their average, often resulting in impossible locations. MambaLoc addresses this with a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) head that predicts a full probability distribution, explicitly capturing multi-modal ambiguity. Rather than maximizing likelihood (which can be unstable), we minimize the distance between ground truth and the distribution’s expected value, capturing the uncertainty structure while providing stable gradients [25]. IV. M ETHODOLOGY 

A. System Overview 

MambaLoc transfers spatial and geometric knowledge from a high-fidelity UWB-based teacher to a computationally efficient CSI-based student following the LUPI paradigm [5]. As shown in Figure 1, the framework operates in two phases:  

> •

Offline Cross-Modal Distillation: A UWB-Transformer teacher trained on CIR measurements provides proba-bilistic supervision and intermediate features. The CSI-Mamba student aligns its latent representations with the teacher through a multi-level distillation pipeline.  

> •

Online Inference: After training, the teacher and all UWB components are discarded. The CSI-Mamba stu-dent performs real-time localization independently, en-abling low-latency inference on edge devices. A key innovation lies in the Feature Alignment Module, which aligns intermediate representations across heteroge-neous modalities and architectures rather than distilling only final outputs. 

B. Data Representation and Preprocessing 1) UWB Teacher Input: The teacher processes UWB Chan-nel Impulse Response (CIR) sequences xu ∈ RT ×Du , where T

denotes temporal snapshots and Du the feature dimensionality. The input comprises complex CIR magnitude and phase, capturing multipath structure where early peaks correspond to LOS and dominant reflections [26]. We augment with normalized first-path power level and total received power level as NLOS discriminators, since signal attenuation patterns differ significantly between LOS and NLOS states. All features are standardized (zero mean, unit variance). 

2) CSI Student Input: The student consumes Wi-Fi CSI matrices xc ∈ RT ×Dc , supporting passive, device-free lo-calization [27]. CSI is extracted from IEEE 802.11n frames using a 3×3 MIMO configuration across Nsc =30 subcarriers, yielding 270 complex measurements per packet. Concatenat-ing amplitude and phase gives Dc = 2 × 270 = 540 . Raw phase is distorted by sampling frequency offset and packet detection delay; a linear phase sanitization procedure is applied prior to learning [15]. 

C. Network Architectures 1) UWB-Transformer Teacher: An encoder-only Trans-former [3] maps normalized CIR inputs to a latent space of dimension dt=256 via a linear embedding, followed by 

L=6 encoder layers with H=8 attention heads. Multi-head self-attention captures global temporal context across variable-length CIR sequences, learning complex multipath and tem-poral correlations critical for accurate ranging. Its quadratic pairwise interaction ( O(N 2)) is acceptable in an offline train-ing role. The output feeds a probabilistic GMM regression head. 

2) CSI-Mamba Student: The student uses the Mamba archi-tecture [4] for linear-time complexity. As shown in Figure 2, each Mamba block processes input X through two parallel branches. The main branch expands features, applies a short 

Conv1D to capture local temporal dependencies, and then runs the Selective SSM: 

ht = Aht−1 + Bxt, yt = Cht + Dxt. (1)

After training, only the CSI-Mamba student is retained, achieving Transformer-level accuracy at linear-time inference cost [31]. V. T RAINING & D ISTILLATION STRATEGY 

Training MambaLoc must bridge two simultaneous gaps: (i) a modality gap between UWB CIR and Wi-Fi CSI, and (ii) an architectural gap between the Transformer teacher and the Mamba student. Naive end-to-end distillation leads to unstable optimization. We adopt a three-stage Cross-Modal Distillation curriculum following LUPI principles, where UWB is avail-able only during training and acts as a geometric oracle. 

A. Stage I: Teacher Pre-Training 

The UWB-Transformer teacher is trained independently on UWB CIR measurements and ground-truth coordinates using MSE loss, and although optimized for regression, it implicitly learns a probability distribution over the spatial grid via its GMM weights, producing richer supervisory signals than deterministic regression. Teacher parameters are then frozen. 

B. Stage II: Cross-Modal Aligner Warming 

Before student distillation, the Feature Alignment Module is pre-trained for a few epochs, with both teacher and student backbones frozen, to map CSI features toward UWB features. This initialization ensures the alignment loss provides mean-ingful gradients at the start of Stage III, while full training of the module continues in that phase. 

C. Stage III: Student Distillation with Adaptive Curriculum 

The CSI-Mamba student trains jointly under three supervi-sion signals. 

1) Task Supervision: Direct MSE supervision on the stu-dent’s own GMM expected output ensures the student remains grounded in real CSI observations rather than over-depending on the teacher, preserving robustness in CSI-only deployment. 

2) Feature Alignment: The weighted Euclidean, cosine, and cross-attention loss from Section IV acts as a geometric regularizer, encouraging CSI features, which are often domi-nated by noise, to align with the physically grounded UWB representation. 

3) Probabilistic Knowledge Distillation: KL divergence with temperature annealing:

4) Adaptive Weighting: α(t) and β(t) are adjusted based on loss trends: if a component deteriorates, its weight increases. The feature alignment weight γ(t) follows a curriculum de-cay, starting high to guide early representation learning and decreasing as the student develops CSI-based independence. These strategies were found to stabilize training significantly compared to fixed-weight alternatives through extensive ex-periments. VI. E XPERIMENTAL EVALUATION 

A. Dataset & Evaluation Metrics 

We evaluate on OPERAnet [32], which to our knowledge is the only publicly available dataset providing synchronized UWB and CSI measurements under a shared ground-truth positioning system, making it the canonical benchmark for this problem. Data was collected in two furnished indoor environments ( 5.09 m ×10 .02 m and 7.20 m ×7.24 m ) over ap-proximately 8 hours, covering six daily activities: walking, sitting, standing, lying down, standing up, and body rotation.  

> •

CSI: Intel 5300 NICs ( 5 GHz), 1600 Hz packet rate; one transmitter and two receivers in LOS and bi-static configurations; 3×3 MIMO, 30 subcarriers per receiver.  

> •

UWB: Two synchronized passive systems (Decawave EVK1000 and MDEK1001) providing CIR data at 

≈1.0016 ns temporal resolution.  

> •

Ground Truth: High-resolution 2D positions from a dedicated UWB anchor-tag system; all modalities syn-chronized via NTP ( < 20 ms). The primary metric is Median Absolute Error, measured as Euclidean distance between predicted and ground-truth locations. Median Absolute Error is robust to outliers and reflects typical localization performance.

We presented MambaLoc, a CSI-based localization frame-work combining linear-time SSMs with cross-modal distilla-tion using UWB as privileged training information. Results demonstrate that the modality gap between UWB and CSI is reduced by aligning intermediate feature representations rather than relying on output-level distillation alone. The three-stage curriculum and adaptive weighting strategy are critical for stable convergence when distilling across sensing modalities and architectures, achieving a median localization error of 0.230 m with 65.1% fewer FLOPs and 58% lower latency than Transformer-based approaches. Future work will evaluate the framework in cross-building scenarios [33] and investigate domain adaptation for cross-environment generalization. The distillation framework may also be extended to other RF sensing tasks such as activity recognition and environmental mapping, and to additional sensing modalities, directions that may clarify the role of cross-modal supervision and efficient sequence modeling in edge-native RF perception systems.
