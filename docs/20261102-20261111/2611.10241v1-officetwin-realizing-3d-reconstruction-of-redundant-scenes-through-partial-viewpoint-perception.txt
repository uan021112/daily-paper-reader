Abstract 

Feed-forward 3D reconstruction turns an uncalibrated monoc-ular stream into a dense point cloud in a single inference pass, orders of magnitude faster than classical structure-from-motion (e.g., COLMAP), and reliable on the weakly textured, repetitive surfaces where keypoint matching fails outright. However, raw point clouds lack the object identity, physical properties, and watertight volume required for robot learn-ing. This gap widens under robot-native capture. A camera mounted on a robot constrained to a few degrees of free-dom sees each object only partially, and the repetitive, weakly textured structure of an office makes distinct workstations nearly indistinguishable, leaving redundant and partial obser-vations that downstream reconstruction cannot resolve. Exist-ing image-to-3D generation methods struggle with these con-ditions, hallucinating geometry under incomplete views and confusing repeated instances. We present OfficeTwin, a robot-native framework that converts these incomplete observations into simulation-ready digital twins. OfficeTwin first imposes metric and structural constraints, then exploits the office’s structural redundancy by registering repeated cubicles into a shared canonical frame, where complementary observations recover geometry occluded in any single view without hal-lucination. On a real office scan, OfficeTwin recovers every cubicle as an individually watertight, metric solid, outper-forming feed-forward reconstruction and generative baselines that fail to produce simulation-ready assets. 

# 1 Introduction 

Feed-forward 3D reconstruction now recovers dense 3D ge-ometry from a monocular RGB stream in a single forward pass, without the slow per-scene optimization of classi-cal multi-view stereo or the per-scene fitting of radiance fields (Deng et al. 2025; Wang et al. 2024). For Embodied AI, Sim-to-Real transfer requires simulated environments to faithfully reproduce absolute metric scale and watertight ge-ometry that govern obstacle avoidance and contact-rich ma-nipulation. To scale to arbitrary environments, twins should be generated directly from the robot’s onboard RGB cam-era along traversable trajectories, avoiding costly operator scanning. Yet these reconstructions remain unusable as sim-ulation assets: they lack structure and watertight geometry. Converting raw point clouds into structured, watertight simu-lation assets is difficult, particularly for robot-captured office imagery, for two reasons. First, body-mounted cameras con-

Second, an office is filled with near-identical, weakly tex-tured cubicles ( redundant structure ). As shown in Fig. 3, existing methods that try to turn these partial, redundant observations into a clean, per-object 3D model fail under these conditions. Single-image generators such as SAM3D (Chen et al. 2025), Hunyuan3D-2 (Zhao et al. 2025), and TRELLIS (Xiang et al. 2025) hallucinate or merge neighbouring instances under partial and redundant views. Feed-forward reconstruction provides dense geometry but remains scale-free and topologically incomplete (Xiong et al. 2026; Im 2026), while learned completion lacks ex-plicit structural constraints (Wang et al. 2026; Liu et al. 2026). These failures share a root cause: the initial recon-structions are scale-free, structure-blind, and incomplete. Re-pairing them requires jointly: (i) recovering absolute metric scale; (ii) achieving instance-correct segmentation despite destabilizing redundancies; and (iii) completing and closing geometry left open by partial views, without hallucination or pose-drift ghosting. Our central observation is that the of-fice’s redundancy, the very property that defeats prior meth-ods, makes the third and hardest sub-problem solvable. Every cubicle recurs many times. A region occluded in one instance is almost always observed in another, so repeated instances can complete one another rather than being invented. As shown in Fig. 1, we propose OfficeTwin, a robot-native pipeline that turns these obstacles into structure and ad-dresses the three sub-problems in turn. To recover scale, 

semantic metric anchoring converts a real-world dimension read by a VLM into a single global metric factor. To make segmentation instance-correct, structure-aware grounding 

reprojects the reconstructed 3D wall topology onto each view as a hard constraint that restricts the foundation model to one cubicle at a time. To complete missing geometry, 

redundancy-driven completion registers repeated cubicle in-stances into a shared canonical frame, allowing complemen-tary views to fill occlusions. Finally, it removes residual ghosting via multi-view consensus and fits watertight Man-hattan solids. Unlike pipelines that isolate semantic segmen-tation from geometric meshing (Häne et al. 2016; Feng and Atanasov 2024), our components share the recovered scene structure throughout the entire process. # 2 Related Work 

Feed-Forward Foundation Models and Scalability. Recent feed-forward transformers estimate visual geometry without slow per-scene optimization (Campos et al. 2021; Keetha et al. 2024), regressing structure directly from RGB inputs to bypass traditional feature-matching failures in texture-less scenes. While architectures like VGGT (Wang et al. 2025a) and π3 (Wang et al. 2025b) resolve camera poses and local geometry, their scaling remains constrained by quadratic memory growth. Long-sequence extensions like VGGT-Long (Deng et al. 2025) and Pi-Long (Deng 2025) leverage chunk-based reconstruction and Sim(3) alignment to mitigate this limitation, yet these pure geometry-driven approaches cannot resolve missing surfaces or ghosting arti-facts caused by partial observations. OfficeTwin adopts the scalable chunking strategy of Pi-Long (Deng 2025) but in-troduces voxel consensus and structural reasoning to derive simulation-ready reconstructions. 

Visual Foundation Models and 3D Segmentation. 

Segment-anything architectures have advanced zero-shot in-stance segmentation. SAM3 (Carion et al. 2025) and its multi-modal 3D counterpart SAM3D (Chen et al. 2025) ex-tend this to open-vocabulary understanding, while several works lift 2D models into 3D via feature projection (Kerr et al. 2023). Applied directly to indoor imagery, however, these models lack structural constraints and struggle to sep-arate overlapping, repetitive instances (e.g., adjacent desks), resulting in mask spillover. OfficeTwin instead grounds seg-mentation in scene geometry, projecting reconstructed wall topology into each view as an exclusion region for SAM3. 

Occlusion Completion. Unobserved scan regions are tra-ditionally addressed via three paradigms. Generative and dif-fusion models synthesize plausible geometry (Liu et al. 2023; Figure 2: Full-scene reconstruction (ceiling removed). From left: TSDF fusion yields an inseparable hole-riddled mesh and structureless Poisson surface; Pi-Long/VGGT-Long yields a scale-ambiguous cloud; OfficeTwin recovers 23 individual, water-tight metric solids instead of one fused mass. Wu et al. 2024) but frequently introduce physical layout vi-olations detrimental to Sim-to-Real applications. Learned completion infers missing surfaces using self-supervised ex-ternal datasets or local structural patch priors (Dai, Diller, and Nießner 2020; Rao, Nie, and Dai 2022), which remain unanchored to the specific scene context and prone to hallu-cinating inaccurate details. Structural fitting approaches opti-mize polygonal surfaces or structured layout topologies (Nan and Wonka 2017; Chen, Qian, and Furukawa 2022) but as-sume a globally complete input scan. Conversely, OfficeTwin requires neither external priors nor a complete scan: it com-pletes each workstation from the complementary observa-tions of its repeated instances, keeping only surfaces that sev-eral instances corroborate. Completion thus stays anchored to observed geometry, yielding a watertight solid per instance rather than a plausible surface. 

Digital Twins for Sim-to-Real Robotics. High-fidelity Digital Twins are a key enabler for scaling robotic learn-ing (Szot et al. 2021), and Sim-to-Real paradigms such as mapless navigation require training environments that match real-world physics (Yang et al. 2025). By construction, feed-forward models such as π3 (Wang et al. 2025b) and pipelines such as Pi-Long (Deng 2025) output geometry only up to an arbitrary Sim(3) transformation; this scale ambiguity biases the spatial representations a Reinforcement Learning (RL) agent learns, for instance distorting grasp distances and ob-stacle clearances. OfficeTwin resolves this via a VLM-driven scaling stage that reads real-world dimensions from visual landmarks and, via resolution-aligned frustum culling, an-chors the output to absolute metric scale. 

# 3 Methodology 

The OfficeTwin pipeline takes as input a scale-ambiguous point cloud Praw reconstructed from a monocular RGB se-quence by a feed-forward reconstruction model, and converts it into a metrically scaled, watertight Manhattan mesh M

through the five phases of Fig. 1. The pipeline successively recovers metric scale, identifies cubicle topology, segments individual workstations, registers repeated cubicles into a shared canonical frame, removes residual ghosting by multi-view consensus, and finally fits watertight Manhattan solids. Throughout, repeated cubicle layouts serve as internal super-vision, allowing incomplete geometry to be recovered from complementary observations rather than learned priors.

# 4 Experiments 

We evaluate OfficeTwin on the global subset of Office Hours (Cantu et al. 2026), a real-world office cubicle bench-mark for embodied scene understanding, uniformly sampling 

1,133 RGB frames from an open-plan office containing 23 

cubicles ( 22 U-shaped and 1 L-shaped). The sequences are captured by a mobile robot along fixed corridor paths, result-ing in systematic partial-view observations of near-identical cubicles, matching the conditions targeted by our method. The dataset also provides annotated 2D layouts for topology extraction and cubicle decomposition. Since no CAD model is available, Chamfer distance and instance mAP cannot be computed. Instead, we evaluate geometric usability (wa-tertightness, metric scale), redundancy consistency (cross-instance corroboration), and reconstruction success (cubi-cle recovery and shape-type accuracy). We compare against feed-forward reconstruction (VGGT (Wang et al. 2025a), DUSt3R (Wang et al. 2024)), TSDF fusion, and single-image generative models (Hunyuan3D-2 (Zhao et al. 2025), TREL-LIS (Xiang et al. 2025), SAM3D (Chen et al. 2025)). Video results are available in the supplementary material. 

4.1 Reconstruction Capability and Watertightness 

Qualitative and quantitative evaluations of the reconstructed geometry are provided in Fig. 2 and Table 1. While feed-forward methods (VGGT, DUSt3R) retain only scale-ambiguous, topology-free point clouds, TSDF fusion yields a metric but severely defective surface. Specifically, TSDF fu-sion fails on two fronts. Semantically, it lacks object identity: 

92 .3% of its 2.97 M triangles merge into a single connected component, welding all 23 cubicles, partitions, desks, and the floor into an inseparable mass that prevents asset re-posing or physical property assignment. Geometrically, it fails to form a closed volume, leaving 260 ,351 open bound-ary edges and fragmenting residual triangles into 15 ,197 

detached debris units ( 14 ,975 having fewer than 100 faces), rendering the mesh unusable as a collision proxy. In contrast, OfficeTwin leverages the recovered wall topology to partition the scan into independent workstation instances by classify-ing structural walls and bounding the desk planes (Fig. 4). It stands as the only approach that simultaneously delivers ab-solute metric scale, watertight geometry ( 0 open edges), and object-level decomposition required for high-fidelity physics simulation (Fig. 2).

# 5 Conclusion 

We presented OfficeTwin, a framework that leverages sequence-level structural redundancy to reconstruct water-tight, metrically scaled digital twins of repeated office in-stances. By registering repeated cubicles into a shared canon-ical frame and enforcing multi-view consensus on a layout-consistent grid, our method resolves occlusions and instance ambiguities that defeat feed-forward and generative base-lines. It recovers all 23 cubicles as watertight solids in 350,s, with future work extending this redundancy-driven paradigm to environments with weaker, non-architectural repetition.
