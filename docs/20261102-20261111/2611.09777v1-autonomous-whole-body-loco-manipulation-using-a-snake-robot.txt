# Autonomous Whole-body Loco-manipulation Using a Snake Robot 

Adarsh Salagame 1†, Henry Noyes 1†, Haroon Hublikar 1, Alireza Ramezani 1∗

Abstract — Snake robots can leverage their hyper-redundant morphology for both locomotion and manipulation through dis-tributed body contact, but whole-body loco-manipulation is par-ticularly challenging since the entire body serves simultaneously as propulsive mechanism and manipulation tool. Prior work on wheeled, legged, and snake robot platforms has decoupled these functions through dedicated appendages, body segmentation, or grippers. We present closed-loop loco-manipulation with our snake robot COBRA without this distinction, such that any part of the body may make contact. We develop a contact-implicit dynamic model unifying robot–ground, robot–object, and object–ground interactions within a Moreau–Jean time-stepping framework, and formulate a trajectory optimization over the full-order dynamics, guided by a geometric planner and nominal proportional controller, to autonomously push an object to a goal location during sidewinding. We validate the planner and controller on hardware across varied configura-tions and present simulation results for a simplified trajectory optimization that resolves robot–object and object–ground con-tacts. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Snake robots have emerged as compelling platforms for operation in diverse and unstructured environments. Their hyper-redundant morphology enables locomotion over sandy slopes [1], flat or stepped rigid terrain [2], [3], and through complex obstacles such as ladders [4], ropes [5], and pipes [6]. This environmental versatility constitutes the principal advantage of the snake robot form factor, and the ability to interact with objects in the workspace extends their utility beyond pure locomotion into the domain of manipulation. Loco-manipulation, the simultaneous execution of loco-motion and object manipulation, has been explored across a range of robotic morphologies. Legged and wheeled plat-forms equipped with dedicated manipulators have demon-strated tasks such as door opening and object transport [7], [8], [9], [10], where the legs or wheels provide a stable, repositionable base while an arm executes the manipulation objective. Snake robots, however, lack such a clear func-tional decomposition between locomotion and manipulation subsystems. Instead, they achieve manipulation through alter-native strategies: dedicated end-effector grippers positioned via whole-body posture control, whole-body wrapping, or distributed contact along the body [11], [12], [13]. This dual use of the body for both propulsion and environmental interaction introduces a tightly coupled planning problem

Applying this perspective to snake robots reveals a richer space of manipulation modalities than is typically exploited. Beyond gripper-based 6-DoF manipulation through bilateral constraints, snake robots can perform non-prehensile single-contact pushing under unilateral constraints as well as whole-body multi-contact interactions for object relocation and reorientation. Despite this breadth, prior loco-manipulation work on snake robots has focused predominantly on gripper-based approaches in which one end of the robot carries a gripper while the remaining body provides locomotion and stability. HARDWARE PLATFORM 

COBRA [13], [18], [19] (Fig. 1) is a snake-like robot comprising 11 joints arranged in a series kinematic chain with alternating pitch and yaw axes. Each joint is inde-pendently actuated by a servo on a shared RS-485 bus, centrally controlled by an Nvidia Jetson Orin NX housed in the head module. A stereo camera and IMU in the head provide localization and environment mapping, while absolute encoders at each joint supply kinematic feedback for the remainder of the body. We consider the task of whole-body loco-manipulation via sidewinding locomotion: given an initial robot configuration and a known object location, the robot must navigate to the object and push it to a designated goal location using closed-loop control (Fig. 2). The robot interacts with the object through distributed body contact during sidewinding, without the use of a dedicated end effector or gripper. Robot and object poses are obtained from a motion capture system; object pose estimation and visual-inertial robot localization from the head-mounted stereo camera and IMU is left for future work. III. M ODELING 

We develop a contact-implicit dynamic model for snake robot loco-manipulation. The robot–ground and robot–box contact interactions are treated within a unified Moreau–Jean time-stepping framework. A reduced-order representation ab-stracts the oscillatory full-body motion into a stable planning space. These components compose into a single trajectory optimization for loco-manipulation gait generation.

We implement the planner and nominal controller on hard-ware and test autonomous loco-manipulation performance with various configurations of robot and object poses with respect to the goal. We then test a longer trajectory where the robot must push the box through a set of waypoints before reaching the goal. Finally, we evaluate the box-ground and robot-box contact resolution in simulation using a simplified version of the trajectory optimization presented in (19) which omits the robot-ground interaction dynamics and directly solves for F , c x. In experiment, robot and object poses are obtained from motion capture. In future work, these will be replaced by visual-inertial localization and object detection using the onboard stereo camera and IMU. We first discuss the loco-manipulation experiments using the planner and nominal controller. The planner proportion-ality constant is set to η = 0 .4, the pure-pursuit lookahead distance to dla = 0 .3 m, and the controller proportional gain to k∆ = 0 .2. V. CONCLUDING REMARKS 

The contact-implicit modeling framework developed in this work unifies robot–ground, robot–object, and ob-ject–ground interactions within a single Moreau–Jean time-stepping formulation, enabling whole-body loco-manipulation without body segmentation or dedicated grip-pers. A reduced-order representation of the sidewinding gait eliminates the oscillatory dynamics from the planning space, yielding a stable manifold over which both geometric planning and feedback control operate directly. Hardware experiments demonstrate that this reduced-order planning approach, paired with a nominal proportional con-troller, is sufficient for reliable object transport: across 20 trials spanning a wide distribution of start and goal config-urations, including multi-waypoint trajectories, the system achieved a 100% success rate in delivering a box to within 0.2 m of the goal. The contact-implicit trajectory opti-mization, evaluated independently on the object interaction subsystem, recovers physically consistent pushing forces that satisfy all friction bounds and executes faster than real time, confirming its viability as an online planning component. These results establish the computational and experimental foundations for full-order contact-implicit loco-manipulation, in which gait generation and object interaction are resolved jointly rather than through the decoupled subsystems vali-dated here. Immediate extensions include closing the loop around the full trajectory optimization to enable gait adap-tation on uneven terrain, and replacing motion capture with onboard visual-inertial localization and object detection for field deployment.
