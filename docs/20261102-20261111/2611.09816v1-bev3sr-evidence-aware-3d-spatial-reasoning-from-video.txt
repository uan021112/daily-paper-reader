# BEV3SR: Evidence-Aware 3D Spatial Reasoning from Video 

## Fan Zhu, Rui Yu University of Louisville Louisville, KY, USA  

> {fan.zhu, rui.yu }@louisville.edu

## Abstract 

3D spatial reasoning from video remains challenging for VLMs because videos provide sequential, view-local obser-vations rather than persistent metric scene records. Many questions must be answered in a query-specified perspective rather than the observed camera viewpoint, and prompt-ing with a fixed set of RGB views may miss sparse or oc-cluded visual cues. To address these gaps, we introduce 

BEV3SR , a training-free and backbone-agnostic frame-work for RGB-D videos with camera parameters. BEV3SR turns video observations into queryable metric bird’s-eye-view (BEV) scene memory and formalizes each query into an evidence-aware spatial task. The Spatial Task For-malizer specifies the query’s reference frame, spatial con-straints, objective, and evidence requirement. The evidence requirement determines the appropriate reasoning pathway. A query treated as requiring geometric evidence is han-dled by the Geometric Reasoner , which deterministically computes the answer using the metric BEV records in the query-specified reference frame. Otherwise, a query treated as requiring visual evidence is passed to the Visual Ev-idence Aligner , which uses BEV-indexed object records and their associated supporting views to retrieve object-consistent and scene-context RGB frames for VLM ground-ing. Within a standard 16-frame budget, BEV3SR con-sistently improves different VLM backbones across multi-ple 3D scene QA benchmarks, delivering large gains on spatially intensive questions and significant improvements on open-ended scene QA. These results demonstrate that BEV3SR offers an effective, efficient, and interpretable framework for explicit 3D spatial reasoning from video. 

## 1. Introduction 

With the rapid progress of multimodal large language mod-els (MLLMs) [1, 4, 11, 24, 26, 27, 33], visual-spatial un-derstanding is expanding beyond local spatial judgments in isolated images toward scene-level reasoning [3, 28, 37] about the 3D physical environments. The video approxi-mates how humans and embodied agents observe physical environments through a moving viewpoint, provides rich multi-view evidence about object appearances, scene lay-outs, and spatial relations, and has therefore become a nat-ural setting for advancing spatial reasoning in 3D scenes [23, 25, 29, 37, 40]. However, recent studies suggest that current MLLMs still struggle to leverage video observations for reliable 3D spatial reasoning. This limitation reflects several challenges repeatedly identified in prior work. First, video-based reasoning requires models to consoli-date fragmented, camera-centric observations into a persis-tent metric scene representation. However, existing Vision Language Models (VLMs) often rely on local or frame-level visual evidence, making it difficult to maintain a unified scene-level context across multiple views [9, 35, 37]. Sec-ond, many 3D scene questions must be answered in a query-specified allocentric reference frame, rather than from the egocentric viewpoint of camera. Existing MLLMs often conflate these two coordinate systems: they may recognize the relevant objects but still answer according to the camera view, mistaking image-left or image-right for the agent’s left or right [32, 44, 45]. Third, finite-budget video prompt-ing does not guarantee question-relevant evidence retrieval. Because MLLMs can only ingest a limited number of sam-pled frames, relevant objects or spatial relations may be missed when they are sparse, occluded, briefly visible, or visually verifiable only from particular viewpoints. Thus, even a strong VLM may answer incorrectly because the necessary visual evidence is not present in its input context [43, 46]. We illustrate these three limitations in Fig. 1 us-ing a representative 16-frame uniformly sampled prompting VLM baseline [4]. To address these limitations, recent studies have ex-plored two broad directions for enhancing the 3D aware-ness of MLLMs. The first direction improves implicit 3D representations inside MLLMs by injecting explicit 3D sig-nals [7, 20, 30, 47, 49], by aligning MLLMs with 3D re-construction or geometric priors from feed-forward geom-etry foundation models [13, 34, 41, 42], or by introduc-ing spatially supervised training objectives [19, 31, 35]. arXiv:2611.09816v1 [cs.CV] 6 Nov 2026 Figure 1. Three limitations of the VLM baseline and how the proposed BEV3SR addresses them. The baseline uses Qwen3-VL-8B [4] prompted with 16 uniformly sampled RGB frames. 

The second direction makes spatial reasoning more ex-plicit at inference time through active or synthetic viewpoint exploration [38, 46], chain-of-thought [39], or geometric tools [8]. These methods either enrich the model’s implicit 3D scene representation, acquire additional query-relevant views at test time, or make reference-frame reasoning and metric computation more explicit. However, they typically address these limitations separately rather than providing a scene memory that jointly supports query-specified geomet-ric reasoning and finite-budget visual evidence alignment. Motivated by these observations, we revisit BEV not as a conventional top-down image or a visual prompt, but as a BEV-indexed metric scene memory for 3D scene question answering. Its key role is organization rather than visual-ization: it consolidates multi-frame observations into per-sistent object-level records, where each object is linked to a stable metric location, spatial extent, and supporting RGB views in a shared scene-level coordinate system. This al-lows BEV to serve as a queryable spatial interface, support-ing both view evidence retrieval for visual grounding and deterministic metric computation for geometric reasoning. Based on this design, we propose BEV3SR , atraining-free and backbone-agnostic framework for BEV -indexed metric scene memory-based 3D Spatial Reasoning. BEV3SR consists of three coupled components. First, it turns RGB-D video with camera parameters into queryable metric BEV scene memory with object-level records and supporting views. Second, the Spatial Task Formalizer 

converts each natural-language query into an evidence-aware spatial task, specifying the query reference frame, spatial constraints, objective, and evidence requirement. Third, the evidence requirement determines the appropri-ate reasoning pathway. This design is conceptually aligned with the dual-stream view of visual cognition [16], which separates object-oriented perception from spatial reasoning for action. A query treated as requiring geometric evidence is handled by the Geometric Reasoner , which determinis-tically computes the answer using metric BEV records in the query-specified reference frame. Otherwise, a query treated as requiring visual evidence is passed to the Vi-sual Evidence Aligner , which uses BEV-indexed object records and associated supporting views to retrieve object-consistent and scene-context RGB frames for VLM ground-ing. In this way, BEV3SR unifies persistent scene memory, finite-budget visual evidence alignment, and geometric rea-soning within a single 3D scene QA framework. Our contributions are threefold. First, we formulate video-based 3D spatial reasoning as evidence-aware task execution over BEV-indexed metric scene memory, posi-tioning BEV as an object-level spatial index rather than a visual prompt. Second, we introduce explicit spatial task formalization for natural-language 3D scene QA, enabling query-defined reference-frame reasoning and evidence-based pathway selection. Third, we instantiate this for-mulation with two complementary modules for determin-istic geometric reasoning and finite-budget visual evidence alignment, and demonstrate consistent gains over base-line method across multiple VLM backbones. Under the same 16-frame budget, BEV3SR improves SQA3D from 51.6/55.0 to 62.6/64.8 EM/EM-R and ScanQA from 27.8 to 30.6 EM and 82.6 to 95.6 CIDEr. On the static spatial subset of VSI-Bench, BEV3SR achieves 63.8 Avg., outper-forming the baseline by 10.0 points and remaining compet-itive with recent 3D-aware models, all without task-specific training. 

## 2. Related Work 

3D-aware MLLMs for spatial reasoning. Recent work improves the 3D awareness of 2D-pretrained MLLMs by providing explicit spatial signals or spatial supervision. One line injects 3D structure into the input or latent represen-tation, including point-cloud features, object-level 3D to-kens, depth-backprojected coordinates, camera-pose encod-ings, and BEV/object-marker cues for global-local ground-ing [6, 7, 15, 18, 20, 21, 30, 47, 49, 51]. Recent methods further incorporate reconstruction-aware priors from feed-forward geometry foundation models or introduce spatial supervision for layout, pose, correspondence, and situation modeling [13, 19, 31, 34, 35, 41, 42]. These approaches demonstrate the value of 3D structure, but spatial informa-tion is still primarily absorbed into model representations and the reasoning process remains largely implicit. In con-trast, BEV3SR maintains an explicit metric scene memory outside the VLM, making object records, reference frames, and supporting evidence queryable and controllable. 

Explicit spatial reasoning at inference time. Another line makes spatial reasoning more explicit during inference. Active or synthetic viewpoint methods acquire additional observations by selecting, moving, or synthesizing views according to the query [38, 46]. Structured prompting and chain-of-thought methods decompose spatial questions into intermediate scene descriptions or cognitive map, coordi-nates, or reasoning steps [37, 39]. Tool-augmented methods offload geometric computation to external modules [8, 17]. These methods improve interpretability by reducing purely implicit reasoning. BEV3SR is complementary but tar-gets a different setting: video-based 3D scene QA under a fixed visual budget. Instead of repeatedly acquiring new views or applying tools mainly to already provided views, or task-specified regions, BEV3SR treats finite-budget evi-dence selection itself as part of the reasoning problem. It uses a reusable BEV-indexed metric memory to support both query-specified geometric reasoning and visual evi-dence alignment for perception-dependent answers. 

## 3. Methodology 

We present the overall framework of BEV3SR in Fig. 2. BEV3SR consists of four main components. First, we con-struct a BEV-indexed metric scene memory from RGB-D video observations and camera parameters (Sec. 3.1). Sec-ond, the Spatial Task Formalizer converts each natural-language query into an evidence-aware spatial task (Sec. 3.2). Based on the task specification, geometry-solvable queries are answered by the Geometric Reasoner (Sec. 3.3), while perception-dependent queries are handled by the Visual Evidence Aligner (Sec. 3.4).

## 4. Experiments 

4.1. Experimental Setup 

We evaluate BEV3SR on three 3D spatial reasoning bench-marks: ScanQA [3], SQA3D [28], and VSI-Bench [37]. ScanQA focuses on object-centric 3D scene QA, while SQA3D introduces situation descriptions and agent-centric reasoning. We report results on ScanQA val and SQA3D test, using EM, BLEU-4, METEOR, ROUGE, and CIDEr for ScanQA, and EM/EM-R [21] for SQA3D. For VSI-Bench, we follow the static spatial evaluation setting and ex-clude route planning and appearance order. We therefore re-port Avg.-6 over the remaining six static spatial categories: object counting, absolute distance, object size, room size, relative distance, and relative direction. We use Qwen3-VL-8B as the default backbone VLM, and additionally eval-uate InternVL3.5-8B and LLaVA-OneVision-1.5-8B to as-sess cross-model generality. All experiments are conducted on a single NVIDIA A6000 GPU with 48GB memory. 

4.2. Results 

4.2.1. Generalization Across VLMs. 

Tab. 1 shows that BEV3SR consistently improves over the baseline across different VLM backbones under the same 

K=16 frame budget, where the baseline denotes uniform 16-frame prompting with the corresponding VLM back-bone. On SQA3D, BEV3SR improves EM by +11.0, +8.3, and +11.1 for Qwen3-VL-8B, InternVL3.5-8B, and LLaVA-OneVision-1.5-8B, respectively, with similar gains in EM-R. On ScanQA, BEV3SR also improves EM across all backbones, although captioning-style metrics such as CIDEr and METEOR are more sensitive to answer style and paraphrasing. These results suggest that the gains come from the proposed evidence-aware framework and modu-lar geometric reasoning / visual evidence alignment, rather than model-specific behavior.

4.3. Ablation Study 

4.3.1. Effectiveness of Geometric Reasoner. 

Table 5(a) evaluates Geometric Reasoner (GR) on the geometry-solvable subset of SQA3D. Compared with base-line, GR improves EM/EM-R from 43.2/44.0 to 65.5/65.9, yielding +22.3/+21.9 gains. Baseline denotes 16-frame uni-formly sampled RGB prompting with Qwen3VL-8B. We also compare with BEV, that provides relevant object in-stance IDs and labels to the VLM without explicit geomet-ric computation. This cue-injection baseline does not con-sistently improve performance and even degrades EM from 43.2 to 41.7, indicating that geometry-intensive queries can-not be reliably solved by additional BEV text hints alone. Overall, the ablation confirms that explicit geometric com-putation in GR over BEV-indexed metric records is crucial for query-specified spatial reasoning.

4.3.3. End-to-End Contribution on Full Validation Set 

Since STF provides the common task specification required by both downstream pathways, we isolate the end-to-end contributions of GR and VEA while keeping STF fixed. To compare both pathways under the same evaluation set-ting, we aggregate their pathway-specific results over the full 3,261 question SQA3D validation set. As shown in Tab. 5(b), GR alone improves overall EM/EM-R from 51.0/53.6 to 59.4/61.8. Combining both pathways reaches 61.3/63.5. 

4.4. Robustness to RGB-Estimated Geometry 

To assess BEV3SR’s dependence on sensor-provided ge-ometry, we construct an RGB-only variant that jointly es-timates depth, camera intrinsics, and camera poses from RGB using MapAnything [22], while keeping the down-stream pipeline unchanged. The estimated geometry is substantially imperfect, with an average depth AbsRel of 20.6%, focal-length error of 4.7%, and camera-pose ATE of 0.34 m. Despite these errors, Table 7 shows that BEV3SR (RGB-only) still improves the RGB baseline from 51.0 to 56.5 EM on SQA3D validation and from 27.8 to 29.0 on ScanQA validation. Sensor-provided geometry further in-creases performance to 61.3 and 30.6, respectively. These results show that BEV3SR remains effective with imperfect RGB-estimated geometry rather than requiring pristine ge-ometric inputs, while also quantifying the benefit of more accurate depth and camera parameters.

## 5. Conclusion 

In this work, we introduce BEV3SR, a training-free and backbone-agnostic framework for video-based 3D scene question answering. BEV3SR bridges the gap be-tween fragmented egocentric video observations and query-conditioned, evidence-grounded 3D spatial reasoning. It converts RGB-D videos with camera parameters into a BEV-indexed metric scene memory, and formalizes each question as an evidence-aware spatial task. Based on ev-idence, BEV3SR performs either deterministic geomet-ric reasoning in the query-specified reference frame or BEV-guided visual evidence alignment for VLM ground-ing. Experiments on ScanQA, SQA3D and VSI-Bench show consistent improvements over baseline across differ-ent VLM backbones, with strong gains on spatially inten-sive QA. These results suggest that explicit metric scene memory, query-defined reference-frame, and modular evi-dence alignment provide a practical and interpretable path toward reliable 3D spatial reasoning from video.
