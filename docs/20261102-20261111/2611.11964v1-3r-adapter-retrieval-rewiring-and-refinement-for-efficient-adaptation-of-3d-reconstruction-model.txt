Abstract 

Feed-forward 3D reconstruction models have demonstrated strong generalization under large-scale pre-training, yet they struggle in long-tail scenarios such as low-light reconstruction, joint human-scene reconstruction, and test-time adapta-tion. We observe relational attention structure collapse, manifested as the failure of cross-view correspondences, as a primary cause of these limitations. While adaptation offers a practical way to bridge this gap, existing parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) approaches mainly perform channel-wise feature adjustment and lack mechanisms to explicitly adapt relational structures, leaving correspon-dence failures unresolved. In this work, we formulate adaptation of 3D recon-struction models as a problem of correspondence recovery under domain shift and propose 3R-Adapter, a PEFT paradigm for long-tail 3D reconstruction sce-narios. Our method consists of three components: Deformable Retrieval for task-adaptive relation candidate retrieval, Relational Rewiring for reconstructing cross-view relational structures, and Consistency Refinement for injecting the rewired relations into stable predictions. Extensive experiments on low-light recon-struction, joint human-scene reconstruction, and test-time adaptation demonstrate that our approach significantly improves robustness over existing PEFT meth-ods while maintaining efficient lightweight adaptation. Our code is released at 

https://github.com/PKU-OV3-LAB/3R-Adapter .

1 Introduction 

Recent feed-forward 3D reconstruction models [1, 2, 3, 4, 5] have demonstrated strong generalization ability under large-scale pre-training. These models leverage large-scale data [6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12] to implicitly learn geometric structures through attention-based representations, enabling the estimation of camera poses, intrinsics, and dense geometry from uncalibrated image collections. Despite these advances, current models exhibit clear limitations under domain shift. In scenarios such as low-light environments [13], joint human-scene reconstruction [14, 15, 16], and test-time adaptation [17, 18], data distributions deviate significantly from pre-training conditions and reliable 3D supervision is often unavailable, as depicted in Fig. 1. As a result, pre-trained models frequently fail to generalize, leading to unstable geometric predictions. We observe that this failure is closely related to the instability of cross-view relational modeling, as shown in Fig. 2. Specifically, attention patterns that are well-structured in in-domain settings become diffuse and less informative under domain shift, making it difficult to establish reliable cross-view correspondences. This suggests that the core challenge lies not in insufficient feature representation, but in the inability to preserve and adapt relational structures across views. Addressing this issue requires effective adaptation mechanisms that can restore stable cross-view relations. 2 Observation and Insight 

Feed-forward 3D reconstruction models rely on attention-based relational structures to establish cross-view correspondences across input images [26]. While such structures are effectively learned from data-rich pre-training scenarios, their behavior in data-sparse long-tail scenarios remains less reliable. To better understand this failure mode, we visualize cross-view attention patterns of pre-trained reconstruction models in standard daytime scenes and challenging low-light scenes. As shown in Fig. 2, in standard data-rich scenarios, the attention distribution of a selected query token is concentrated around consistent physical counterparts across reference views. This indicates that the pre-trained model can form stable relational routes when the input distribution is close to the pre-training regime. In contrast, for selected queries under extreme low-light conditions, the attention distribution becomes diffuse and fragmented, making it difficult to maintain reliable cross-view correspondences. We refer to the degradation of geometrically consistent cross-view associations as 

relational attention structure collapse . The quantitative correspondence analysis in Sec. 5.6 measures this geometric degradation and its recovery after adaptation. This observation suggests that the degradation of feed-forward 3D reconstruction models in long-tail scenarios is not merely caused by weakened local feature representations. A key bottleneck lies in the instability of cross-view relational structures that support correspondence modeling. Therefore, effective adaptation should not only adjust channel-wise feature distributions, but also explicitly restore and reorganize cross-view relations. This motivates our 3R-Adapter, which retrieves task-relevant relational candidates, rewires them into structured cross-view associations, and refines predictions through consistency-preserving residual updates. 

3 Related Work 

3.1 3D Reconstruction Model 

Traditional 3D reconstruction methods like structure-from-motion (SfM) [27] and multi-view stereo (MVS) [28] rely on establishing cross-view correspondences through handcrafted or learned lo-cal features, followed by geometrically grounded optimization with strong 3D inductive priors. These pipelines remain sensitive to weak texture, limited overlap, and viewpoint changes, and are computationally costly. Feed-forward 3D reconstruction models [1, 29, 30, 2, 3, 5, 4] provide a promising alternative by learning geometric reasoning directly from data. Pioneering work DUSt3R [1] reformulates reconstruction as dense pointmap prediction from uncalibrated image pairs. CUT3R [2] introduces a persistent-state recurrent formulation, allowing the model to continuously update a shared 3D 3state from streaming observations and produce metric-scale pointmaps online. VGGT [3] further scales feed-forward reconstruction to hundreds of views, directly predicting camera parameters, depth maps, point maps, and point tracks with a unified transformer architecture. More recent approaches such as π3 [4] remove the reliance on a fixed reference view by adopting permutation-equivariant visual geometry learning, while Depth Anything 3 [5] investigates a minimal formulation based on a single transformer and depth-ray representation for joint any-view depth and pose estimation. These approaches demonstrate that correspondence and geometry can be implicitly learned at scale, marking a shift from explicit optimization to data-driven geometric reasoning. Despite this progress, existing feed-forward models are primarily optimized for in-distribution performance and exhibit limited robustness under domain shift. In scenarios such as low-light conditions, the learned cross-view correspondences often become unreliable. This highlights the need for effective adaptation mechanisms that can recover stable cross-view relations under distribution shifts. 

3.2 Parameter-efficient Fine-tuning 

With the increasing scale of models, full fine-tuning has become computationally prohibitive for downstream adaptation. To address this challenge, parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) meth-ods [19, 24, 25] have been widely explored in both vision and language domains. Existing PEFT approaches can be broadly categorized into three families. Prompt-based methods, such as visual prompt tuning [19], introduce a small set of learnable tokens to steer pretrained models without modifying backbone parameters. Adapter-based methods [25, 31] insert lightweight bottle-neck modules into intermediate layers to refine latent feature representations. Reparameterization methods, including LoRA [24] and its variants such as DoRA [32], approximate weight updates via low-rank decompositions, enabling efficient adaptation with minimal trainable parameters. Recent works have extended these paradigms to feed-forward 3D reconstruction. For example, Test3R [17] and Online3R [18] employ prompt-based adaptation with self-supervised objectives for test-time adaptation on single scenes, while Gen3R [33] introduces lightweight adapters to bridge represen-tation gaps across models. Meanwhile, methods such as VIST3A [34] and VGGRPO [35] adopt LoRA-style reparameterization to enable efficient alignment and adaptation of large geometry models. These works demonstrate that PEFT techniques provide a practical solution for adapting feed-forward reconstruction models under limited supervision. However, despite their effectiveness, existing methods primarily operate through feature-level modu-lation and rely on implicit updates of attention to capture cross-view dependencies. In multi-view 3D reconstruction, where geometric reasoning critically depends on structured cross-view relations, such implicit adaptation provides limited control over relational routing and often fails to maintain stable correspondences under domain shift. This limitation motivates our work, where we explicitly model and adapt cross-view relational structures through a relation-centric PEFT framework. 

4 Method 

In this section, we detail our method for efficiently adapting the pre-trained 3D reconstruction models, improving the correspondence capturing, thus enhancing the reconstruction quality. To explicitly guide cross-view correspondence modeling beyond feature shift in original pre-trained models, we introduce 3R-Adapter with three modules: Deformable Retrieval, Relational Rewiring, and Consistency Refinement, demonstrated in Fig. 3. Given a pre-trained model, we freeze the parameters and only update our newly inserted modules.

4.2 Deformable Retrieval 

A straightforward way to adapt this relation function is to recompute dense interactions among all tokens across all views. However, for N views with feature resolution H × W , dense all-to-all matching introduces quadratic interactions and can also amplify noisy correlations under domain shift. To this end, we design a lightweight Deformable Retrieval module to query task-relevant relational candidates. Firstly, we restrict the relational search space by introducing a structured sliding window over the view dimension. For a target view t, tokens from Xt = {xtu}HW u=1 ∈ RHW ×C serve as self-view query tokens, while tokens from neighboring views Xv are used as reference tokens. Specifically, we restrict reference views by a sliding window over the view dimension: 

N (t) = {v | | v − t| ≤ w}, (4) where w is the window radius. This reduces the number of view-level interactions from O(N 2) to 

O(N w ) and provides a coarse locality prior over the relational graph. For each self-view query token xtu with spatial position pu, we first project it into a low-dimensional latent space:

{∆ˆpku}Kk=1 = ψ∆(˜ xtu), {wku}Kk=1 = ψw(˜ xtu). (6) In parallel, reference tokens are projected into the same low-rank space: 

˜Xv = ϕref (Xv ) ∈ RHW ×r , v ∈ N (t). (7) Thus, Deformable Retrieval defines a sparse set of candidate relation locations {pku} and weights 

{wku} for each self-view token, while keeping both query and reference features in a lightweight latent space. Compared with dense attention, Deformable Retrieval introduces only a small number of trainable parameters and provides a structured search space for relational adaptation, making it well-suited for parameter-efficient fine-tuning without disrupting the pre-trained backbone. The sliding window constrains the view-level relational graph, while the deformable offsets provide fine-grained spatial flexibility within selected reference views. The resulting sparse candidates are then materialized and aggregated in the following Relational Rewiring stage.

5 Experiments 

5.1 Adaptation Tasks 

We evaluate 3R-Adapter on three representative adaptation settings: low-light reconstruction, joint human-scene reconstruction, and test-time adaptation. These tasks cover diverse forms of domain shift and long-tail scenarios where feed-forward 3D reconstruction models struggle to maintain stable cross-view relations. 

Low-light reconstruction. Under low-light conditions, severe image degradation and distribu-tion shifts from pre-training data lead to unreliable feature extraction. Consequently, cross-view correspondences become unstable, making accurate geometric reconstruction challenging. 

Joint human-scene reconstruction. This task requires reconstructing both parametric human geometry (e.g., SMPL [36]) and background scene structure within a unified framework. The presence of non-rigid motion and cross-entity interactions introduces significant complexity, making it difficult for pre-trained models to recover consistent geometry and fine-grained human meshes. 

Test-time adaptation. In this setting, models must adapt to a single scene without additional supervision, relying on self-supervised signals or internal consistency at test time. This scenario is particularly sensitive to errors in cross-view relations, as no external supervision is available to correct them. Overall, these tasks share a common challenge: maintaining stable cross-view correspondences under domain shift, making them suitable benchmarks for evaluating relational adaptation. 

5.2 Comparing Methods 

Our 3R-Adapter is evaluated across multiple feed-forward 3D reconstruction backbones, including DUSt3R [1], CUT3R [2], and π3 [4], to demonstrate its generality and backbone-agnostic design. We compare against three categories of methods. First, we include recent feed-forward reconstruction models across all tasks. Second, we consider task-specific approaches tailored to individual settings. For low-light reconstruction, we compare with Dark3R [13], which leverages contrastive learning between low-light and normal images. For joint human-scene reconstruction, we include Josh3R [16], Human3R [14], and UniSH [15], which incorporate specialized designs for modeling human-scene interactions. For test-time adaptation, we compare with Test3R [17] and Online3R [18], which refine predictions via self-supervised objectives on single scenes. Finally, we compare with representative parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods from NLP [32, 37], 2D vision [31, 38], and 3D vision [21]. 5.5 Analysis on Joint Human-Scene Reconstruction 

Joint human-scene reconstruction requires the model to go beyond standard point-level correspon-dence and capture structured associations between human proxy tokens, body regions, and sur-rounding scene context. This makes it a particularly suitable benchmark for evaluating whether relational adaptation can transfer a generic feed-forward reconstruction backbone to more structured downstream tasks. As shown in Table 3, 3R-Adapter achieves the strongest overall performance among PEFT baselines under the same CUT3R backbone, while using the fewest trainable parameters. This indicates that the improvement does not simply come from larger adaptation capacity, but from explicitly adapting relational structures through retrieval, rewiring, and refinement. Compared with channel-wise PEFT methods, our design is better aligned with the human-scene setting, where reconstruction depends on structured cross-entity relations rather than only local feature adjustment. 3R-Adapter also remains competitive with task-specific joint reconstruction methods, despite op-erating as a lightweight adaptation module for a generic reconstruction backbone. This suggests that relation-centric PEFT can effectively extend pre-trained 3D reconstruction models to structured downstream tasks without requiring a fully specialized architecture. The qualitative results in Fig. 4 further support this observation: our method produces more coherent human meshes and more con-sistent scene reconstruction under challenging human-scene interactions, indicating that the adapted relational structures benefit both human and scene modeling.

6 Conclusion 

We present 3R-Adapter, a relation-centric parameter-efficient adaptation framework for feed-forward 3D reconstruction models. Motivated by the observation that pre-trained models exhibit relational attention structure collapse in data-sparse long-tail scenarios, we reformulate adaptation as the recovery of cross-view relational structures rather than channel-wise feature adjustment. Our method retrieves task-relevant relational candidates, rewires them into structured cross-view associations, and refines predictions through consistency-preserving residual updates. Experiments on low-light reconstruction, joint human-scene reconstruction, and test-time adaptation demonstrate that 3R-Adapter improves robustness over existing PEFT methods with minimal trainable parameters. These results suggest that explicitly adapting relational structures is a promising direction for efficient and reliable adaptation of 3D reconstruction foundation models.

A.1 Analysis and Discussion 

Dense attention offers a flexible way to learn cross-view relations during large-scale pre-training, but it also introduces an unconstrained all-to-all relational search space. In data-sparse long-tail scenarios, directly adapting such dense relations is inefficient and may amplify noisy correspondences. Our Deformable Retrieval can be interpreted as a structured sparse approximation to dense cross-view attention. The sliding window mask provides a weak view-locality prior, while learnable offsets retain spatial flexibility for task-adaptive correspondence search. This design reduces the difficulty of relational discovery without imposing hard geometric constraints. This perspective also distinguishes 3R-Adapter from conventional PEFT methods. Rather than using trainable parameters only for channel-wise feature adjustment, our method uses them to adapt how cross-view relations are retrieved, rewired, and injected into predictions. Thus, the parameter efficiency of 3R-Adapter comes not from a larger adaptation capacity, but from placing lightweight trainable modules at the relational bottleneck of feed-forward 3D reconstruction. 

A.2 Limitation 

Although 3R-Adapter effectively alleviates the degradation of feed-forward reconstruction models in data-sparse long-tail scenarios, it still relies on the representational and geometric priors learned by the pre-trained backbone. Our method adapts and reorganizes existing cross-view relational structures rather than learning reconstruction ability from scratch. Thus, when the backbone fails to extract meaningful visual features or the input distribution lies far outside the pre-training regime, relational retrieval and rewiring may remain limited. In addition, the current sliding-window design improves efficiency but may restrict long-range relation modeling in extremely long sequences or scenes with large viewpoint changes. Future work may explore adaptive view selection and hierarchical relational memory for more scalable adaptation.

A.5 Implementation Details 

We build 3R-Adapter on top of pretrained feed-forward 3D reconstruction backbones, including DUSt3R [1], CUT3R [2], and π3 [4]. Across all experiments, the pretrained backbone is frozen, and only the lightweight adapter parameters are updated. This setting allows the model to preserve geometric priors learned from large-scale pre-training while adapting to data-sparse long-tail sce-narios with minimal trainable parameters. We optimize the adapter modules using AdamW. The hyperparameters of 3R-Adapter are set according to the ablation study: the number of deformable sampling points is K = 4 , the search radius is ρ = 0 .20 , the relational fusion factor is α = 0 .10 ,and the residual scaling factor is γ = 0 .30 . All experiments are conducted on 8 NVIDIA RTX 5090 GPUs. For low-light reconstruction and joint human-scene reconstruction, we train the adapters on the corresponding training splits and report results on the held-out evaluation sets. For test-time adap-tation, the adapter is optimized independently on each test scene using self-supervised reconstruction loss [17], without accessing additional training data or ground-truth geometry.

A.7 Broader Impact 

3R-Adapter aims to improve the adaptability and robustness of feed-forward 3D reconstruction models in data-sparse scenarios, such as low-light environments, joint human-scene reconstruction, and test-time adaptation. By reducing the number of trainable parameters required for downstream adaptation, our method may lower the computational and data costs of deploying 3D reconstruction systems in practical settings, including robotics, augmented reality, digital content creation, and embodied intelligence. At the same time, improved 3D reconstruction capabilities may raise privacy and safety concerns when applied to real-world environments or human-centered scenes. In particular, reconstructing human geometry or private spaces from visual inputs should be conducted with appropriate consent, data protection, and usage constraints. Our work focuses on technical improvements for efficient adaptation and does not introduce new data collection procedures. We encourage future applications of such systems to follow responsible data governance practices and to carefully consider potential misuse in surveillance or unauthorized 3D reconstruction. 17
