## Abstract 

In real homes, assistive robots must repeatedly locate tar-gets from large furniture to tiny tabletop items, as peo-ple move objects between household surfaces. Uniformly instantiating detections at different scales as 3D objects can be brittle for small on-surface items; without explicit object-state validation, stale entries may persist after scene changes. Our key insight is to separate persistent 3D fur-niture entities from revisable surface-object hypotheses, re-taining their visual evidence without requiring independent object geometry. We formalize this design as the Hypothesis Scene Graph (HSG), which preserves small-object evidence and supports local revision without rebuilding the stable en-tity topology. Building on HSG, HSGNav uses this separa-tion for multi-scale lifelong navigation: retrieval selects en-

1arXiv:2611.10547v1 [cs.RO] 7 Nov 2026 tity or hypothesis evidence according to target scale, explo-ration acquires additional surface observations, and verifi-cation confirms target presence or invalidates contradicted hypothesis records. Because existing lifelong benchmarks such as GOAT-Bench rarely require mid-episode memory invalidation or multi-scale search, we introduce FAROS-Bench (Find And Re-find Objects across Scales) for mid-episode relocation of surface objects and multi-scale find goals. Experiments show that HSGNav performs compet-itively on GOAT-Bench while substantially outperforming prior methods on FAROS-Bench. 

## 1. Introduction 

As embodied agents move from one-shot demos to persis-tent household assistance, lifelong object navigation [13] becomes central. Rather than resetting the map for ev-ery query, an agent must continuously find open-vocabulary targets[34] in the same environment while reusing and up-dating scene memory under real-world dynamics. This set-ting is essential for household robotics and assistive agents, and it is harder than single-episode object navigation[3, 16, 33, 36]: observations remain incomplete, targets span het-erogeneous scales, and memories can become stale when the scene changes. Many open-vocabulary navigation systems ground search in 3D scene graphs built from RGB-D observa-tions [6, 9, 20, 26, 30–32]. These representations sup-port object-level retrieval, but small on-surface objects of-ten yield intermittent detections and unstable depth, making consistent cross-view association difficult. Their observa-tions may therefore fail to accumulate sufficient support for a distinct 3D instance, leaving valid object evidence filtered out or incorrectly merged with surrounding furniture. Even when they are instantiated, object nodes may be retained without explicit state validation or removal, allowing stale entries to bias subsequent navigation after an object is relo-cated or disappears. Our key insight, embodied in the Hypothesis Scene Graph (HSG), is to separate persistent 3D furniture entities from revisable surface-object hypotheses that require no in-dependent 3D geometry. Furniture entities provide spatial anchors, while hypotheses retain visual evidence of objects on their surfaces. This separation allows small-object evi-dence to enter memory without reliable instance geometry and to be locally revised after relocation, while preserving the stable entity topology. Building on HSG, HSGNav uses the two layers for multi-scale search: retrieval selects en-tity or hypothesis evidence according to target scale, explo-ration inspects under-observed surfaces or unseen areas, and verification confirms target presence or invalidates contra-dicted hypothesis records. Together, these operations make hypothesis evidence available for navigation while allowing its validity to change independently of the persistent furni-ture geometry. Existing lifelong benchmarks such as GOAT-Bench [13] mainly evaluate static, furniture-dominated goals and rarely require mid-episode memory invalidation or multi-scale search within the same episode. We therefore introduce 

FAROS-Bench for the Find And Re-find Objects across Scales (FAROS) task, evaluating sequential search from tiny surface objects to large furniture, with mid-episode surface-object relocation under stable furniture anchors. Experiments show that HSGNav performs competitively on GOAT-Bench while substantially outperforming prior meth-ods on FAROS-Bench. Our contributions are summarized as follows: • We propose HSG , separating persistent 3D furniture enti-ties from revisable surface-object hypotheses without in-dependent object geometry. • Building on HSG, we develop HSGNav , which retrieves, enriches, and verifies hypothesis evidence for multi-scale lifelong search, locally revising surface memory while preserving the stable entity topology. • We introduce FAROS-Bench for multi-scale lifelong ob-ject navigation with mid-episode relocation of surface objects, and demonstrate strong results on both GOAT-Bench and FAROS-Bench. 

## 2. Related Work 

2.1. 3D Scene Graphs and Embodied Memory 

Dense 3D representations ground Vision-Language Foun-dation Model features [17, 23, 39] for open-vocabulary queries [7, 21, 22, 29, 35], but are costly to maintain and up-date. Explicit 3D scene graphs instead use compact object– relation topologies [10, 24, 27], although traditional vari-ants encode relations as restrictive text and discard visual nuance [1, 4, 6, 14]. Recent methods retain richer evidence through multi-view snapshots (3D-Mem [30]) or image-patch edges (MSGNav [9]). Contemporaneous OASIS [19] organizes mapped object relations and representative views around stable semantic anchors. However, these methods rely on instance-level 3D construction for both furniture and small objects. HSG instead retains surface-object evidence as revisable hypotheses without requiring independent 3D geometry, while preserving persistent furniture anchors. 

2.2. Lifelong Navigation under Object Relocation 

Recent map-based approaches also investigate scene-graph maintenance and object relocation [12, 18, 25]. DualMap updates its abstract map online and reselects navigation can-didates when a target is not found at its expected location. OpenIN initializes a carrier–object graph from pre-collected RGB-D data and updates carried-object states during navi-gation. OASIS [19] verifies targets at remembered view-2points and combines transient visual observations with se-mantic reasoning to re-find moved objects. Contemporane-ous IVAM-Nav [8] uses multi-view surface inspection and viewpoint-anchored memory to revisit and revalidate stale information. HSGNav separates persistent 3D furniture en-tities from revisable surface-object hypotheses without in-dependent object geometry. LiSoNav-Eval moves objects between episodes; FAROS-Bench re-queries the same tar-get after two relocations within an episode and includes furniture-scale goals. Target-conditioned verification lo-cally invalidates contradicted hypothesis descriptions, pre-serving the persistent entity topology and excluding rejected evidence from subsequent retrieval. 

## 3. Method 

3.1. Overview 

HSGNav separates persistent 3D furniture entities from re-visable surface-object hypotheses, retaining small-object evidence without independent object geometry (Sec. 3.2). Scale-adaptive memory retrieval (SAMR) selects entity or hypothesis evidence according to target scale (Fig. 2). Ahit triggers committed goal navigation (CGN) and belief-driven verification (BDV) with fresh views. BDV invali-dates contradicted description records, removing hypothe-ses only when no valid records remain while preserving the stable entity topology (Sec. 3.3.3). When retrieval fails, observation-complete exploration (OCE) selects frontiers or under-observed surfaces for inspection, also executed by CGN. New observations and SAMR enrich hypotheses, en-abling evidence acquisition and revision.

## 4. Experiments 

4.1. Experimental Setup 

4.1.1. Datasets 

We evaluate lifelong navigation on GOAT-Bench [13], with object, description, and image goals in static scenes. Fol-lowing prior work [30], GOAT-Bench evaluation uses one exploration episode per scene from the val unseen split, totaling 278 subtasks across 36 scenes. To complement GOAT-Bench’s static, furniture-dominated goals, FAROS-Bench evaluates multi-scale search with mid-episode surface-object relocation under stable furniture anchors, testing both memory reuse and stale-memory revision within continuous episodes (Fig. 3). It contains 210 subtasks across 7 HM3D validation scenes, with three 10-subtask episodes per scene. Goals are large (20.0%), small (51.9%), or tiny (28.1%). Tiny objects comprise the lowest tertile of YCB categories by collision-mesh bounding-box volume; remaining YCB categories are small, and scene-native furniture is large. Each episode places eight YCB objects on support surfaces: six serve as find targets and two as distractors. Two additional goals query furniture categories already present in the HM3D scene. The same movable target is queried at steps 1, 5, and 10, with relocations after steps 2 and 6. Steps 5 and 10 are Dynamic tasks; all others are Static tasks. Dynamic tasks span 21 YCB categories. 4.1.3. Implementation Details 

Lifelong runs use Habitat-Sim with HM3D-Semantics-v0.2 [28], a 1 m success threshold, and GOAT-Bench’s sub-task budget [13]. Baselines and ablations in Table 2 share the target vocabulary, GPT-4o ( gpt-4o-2024-08-06 ), and 1280 ×1280 RGB-D observations at 1.5 m height with 120 ◦ horizontal FOV; VLM inputs are 360 ×360. They use ConceptGraph-style association, YOLO-World [2] with full-frame confidence 0.4, and SAM [15]. +Det and Full Inst. share support-crop detections at confidence 0.1: +Det retains the original add-only graphs and navigation, while Full Inst. instantiates proposals, with +Removal adding BDV-based instance removal. OpenIN [25] and IVAM-Nav [8] are discussed qualitatively because their implemen-tations were unavailable during evaluation. Scene cate-gories define the detector vocabulary; GPT-5.2 selects an-chor categories using functional positive/negative exam-ples, and matching detections become anchors. Further de-tails appear in the supplementary material. 

4.2. Lifelong Navigation Performance 

GOAT-Bench. Table 1 evaluates static-scene lifelong navigation with category-name, natural-language descrip-tion, and reference-image goals. HSGNav achieves 71.2% SR and 50.3% SPL, exceeding 3D-Mem by 2.1 and 1.4 per-centage points. The supplementary breakdown reports at least 67.0% SR for each modality, suggesting that retrieval remains effective across different forms of goal evidence. 

FAROS-Bench. On FAROS-Bench (Table 2), HSGNav achieves 58.1% overall SR and 28.5% SPL. Its 25.7-point Small SR gain over the stronger baseline supports retain-ing surface-object evidence for small-target search. Dy-namic SR reaches 57.1%, more than doubling both base-lines, consistent with verification and memory revision mit-igating stale-memory errors. To isolate the effect of addi-tional detections, +Det supplies identical surface-crop pro-posals while retaining each baseline’s entity-instantiation pipeline and navigation. MSGNav gains only 0.9 points in overall SR, while both baselines lose SPL. These limited gains suggest that additional detections also require effec-tive representation and updating.

4.4. Revisable Hypothesis Memory 

Relocation Robustness. Table 4 evaluates relocation ro-bustness in pre-mapped scenes under matched observations, controllers, and search budgets. HSGNav outperforms OA-SIS in single-task In-/Cross-anchor search and degrades less under repeated relocation. Without Removal, verification and new evidence admission remain active, but rejected ev-idence is retained. Both versions match at R1, yet R3 SR falls to 60.8% without Removal versus 78.4% for the full system, with poorer fixed-target rechecks. On the fixed-target recheck, SR is 76.5% versus 52.9%, showing that stale records can mislead retrieval even without another re-location. The growing gap suggests that fresh observations and verification cannot fully offset stale evidence; persistent invalidation supports retrieval across repeated changes. 

Hypothesis Effectiveness. Figure 4(a) shows how Full Inst. can fuse a mug into furniture, losing object-specific retrieval evidence. HSG preserves anchor-linked labels and images, achieving 70.51% ERR versus 32.69% for Full Inst. on shared-detected placement events. The matched detec-tions control for detector recall; ERR measures retained vi-sual evidence without requiring an independent 3D target instance. Micro BDV achieves 86.8% accuracy over 212 checks (Fig. 4(b)). After erroneous invalidation, fresh ob-servations recover retrievable target-matched descriptions in 93.3% of cases, allowing current evidence to support re-newed retrieval while rejected historical records remain ex-cluded. Compared with w/o Removal, RR falls from 27.5% to 3.9% and WTR from 23.2% to 3.2% (Fig. 4(c)), link-ing persistent invalidation to fewer stale-anchor choices and less wasted travel. 85. Conclusion 

We presented HSGNav, which anchors revisable surface-object hypotheses to persistent furniture entities, retain-ing small-object evidence without independent 3D recon-struction. Scale-adaptive retrieval, surface exploration, and verification support evidence acquisition, reuse, and re-vision. Extensive experiments demonstrate performance gains, highlighting the effectiveness of revisable hypothesis memory in preserving evidence and supporting multi-scale lifelong navigation under object relocation. 

Limitations and Future Work. Our method uses persis-tent furniture anchors as spatial references for surface hy-potheses throughout continuous navigation. A natural ex-tension is to maintain and reassociate these hypotheses as support objects move, enabling memory revision to follow changes in both surface objects and their anchors. 

## LLM Usage Statement 

GPT-5.2 was used to identify semantic anchor categories from scene category lists and to generate FAROS-Bench episode scripts. Generated object placements were subse-quently verified and curated by the authors in simulation.
