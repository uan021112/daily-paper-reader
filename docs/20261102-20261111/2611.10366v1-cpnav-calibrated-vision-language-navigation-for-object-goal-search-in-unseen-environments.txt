# CPN AV : Calibrated Vision–Language Navigation for Object-Goal Search in Unseen Environments 

Qizhao Chen 1, Yuanhong Zeng 2, Shoh Nishino 1, and Anushri Dixit 1

Abstract — Directing a robot to locate a target object in unseen environments remains a central challenge in embodied AI. Vision–Language Model (VLM)-based methods provide rich semantic reasoning but often produce uncalibrated and overconfident action decisions. We introduce CPN AV , a navi-gation framework that combines collision-aware planning with statistically calibrated VLM action scoring for map-free object-goal navigation. From RGB-D input, CPN AV constructs anavigability map, generates collision-free motion primitives, and queries two VLMs for goal detection and action scor-ing. The resulting scores are calibrated offline via conformal prediction (CP) using oracle actions obtained through a tree-structured exploration procedure with backtracking, providing finite-sample guarantees at a user-specified risk level. The CP layer is model-agnostic and can wrap any VLM policy. Online, calibrated VLM scores guide action selection, while a tree-structured exploration history records alternative ac-tions and allows backtracking. We evaluate CPN AV in both simulation and real-world hardware settings. In AI Habitat simulation, compared with the uncalibrated baseline method, CPN AV achieves relative SR and SPL improvements of 6.2% and 11.4% on HM3D v0.2, and 12.4% and 8.8% on MP3D, respectively. In real-world deployment on a modified Hiwonder MentorPi robot, the corresponding improvements in SR and SPL are 24.9% and 15.2%, respectively. Code is available at 

github.com/practice-lab-ucla/CPnav .

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Robotic navigation in complex unseen indoor envi-ronments remains challenging because effective decision-making must tightly couple low-level path planning with high-level semantic understanding, while generalizing to novel layouts without prior maps or goal location information [1]. Traditional rule-based planners can handle geometric obstacle avoidance but rely on hand-tuned rules and expert knowledge, making them brittle in unforeseen situations [2]. In contrast, learning-based navigation planners, particularly those based on reinforcement learning (RL), often require painstaking reward shaping and large-scale training data [3]. Such RL-based planning approaches still struggle to generalize across diverse and unseen environments [4]. A key limitation of both rule-based and purely learning-based navigation methods is the absence of semantic un-derstanding, which is essential for efficient goal-directed behavior in realistic environments. By integrating these semantic priors, the robotic agent can effectively constrain the search process, significantly reducing the branching factor of the search tree and prioritizing paths that align with contex-tual expectations. Semantic information and common sense reasoning allow the agent to infer the probable locations of goal objects even when they are not present within the agent’s current field of view [5]. Recent Vision-Language Models (VLMs) such as Gemini [6], LLaVA [7], and GPT [8] enable injection of commonsense and scene priors into navigation, bridging geometric perception and high-level planning [9]. Through large-scale multimodal pretraining, these models acquire semantic priors that support general-ization to previously unseen environments, reducing reliance on environment-specific training [10], [11]. Although pretrained VLMs supply strong semantic cues, their internal heuristics, which are the learned rules that guide their behavior, are not calibrated for sequential de-cision making [12]. VLM outputs are optimized for token prediction or image-text alignment rather than long-horizon action selection under physical constraints. As a result, they often assign non-trivial confidence to unsafe, redundant, or goal-irrelevant actions [13], [14]. In this work, we tackle the problem of unreliable high-level action scoring in unseen environments. Without an explicit mechanism to quantify and control confidence, VLM-driven navigation can assign non-trivial scores to semantically plausible but suboptimal or unsafe actions. Over long horizons, such miscalibration can manifest as inefficient exploration, excessive backtracking, and action loops. Conformal Prediction (CP) provides a principled statistical mechanism to address this issue by converting uncalibrated model scores into prediction sets [15], [16]. Rather than trusting raw VLM confidences, CP uses a held-out calibration set to compute thresholds that control the error rate at a user-specified risk level. Under the assumption of exchangeability, this ensures that the probability of excluding the correct action is bounded by the calibration threshold, independent of the VLM architecture or training process. Building on this principle, we introduce CPN AV , illus-trated in Figure 1, a conformal prediction-augmented navi-gation framework that integrates semantic reasoning with sta-tistically calibrated decision-making. R ELATED WORK 

A. VLM-Augmented Vision-Language Navigation 

Recent work leverages the zero-shot reasoning of VLMs for embodied navigation [1], [9], [10], [17], covering end-to-end navigational question answering and spatial reasoning [18]–[21], hierarchical world-model and long-horizon plan-ning [22]–[24], task-preferenced grounding [22], and zero-shot semantic localization with frontier-based exploration [5], [10], [25]. However, these methods treat VLM outputs as absolute truths, so agents are misled by hallucinated relevance or overconfident scores for inaccessible paths [12], [13], underscoring the need to calibrate VLM uncertainty. 

B. Uncertainty Quantification for Perception and Planning 

Uncertainty in learned perception and planning modules is commonly quantified with Bayesian neural networks (BNNs) and their approximations such as Monte Carlo dropout [26], deep ensembles [27], or post-hoc calibration of softmax con-fidences [28]. However, BNNs and ensembles require access to model weights and multiple forward passes or multiple trained models, incurring substantial training and inference cost [27], [28], and their confidences remain heuristic without finite-sample guarantees. These methods are also tied to a specific architecture and training procedure, so uncertainty estimation for large language and vision-language models typically falls back on sampled or verbalized outputs [14], [29]. Formal guarantees instead come from probably approx-imately correct (PAC) techniques such as PAC-Bayes policy bounds [30] and PAC confidence sets calibrated on held-out data [31], or from robust control bounding a learned perception map’s error [32], which respectively require a good prior or known linear dynamics. Conformal prediction avoids these limitations. It is model-agnostic and applies to any pretrained model without retraining, weight access, or multiple forward passes, needing only a held-out calibration set to produce prediction sets with distribution-free coverage [16], [33] and, with a finite-sample correction, the same PAC-style guarantee [34]. 

C. Conformal Prediction for Reliable Decision-Making 

Conformal prediction (CP) provides a distribution-free framework for uncertainty quantification with finite-sample coverage guarantees [15], [16], and several recent works have integrated it into robotics pipelines. CP has been utilized in perception and object detection to transform standard classification outputs into class prediction sets, preventing autonomous systems from making overconfident but incor-rect categorical decisions [33]. Beyond perception, CP has been applied to motion prediction and trajectory forecasting, where it constructs uncertainty-aware prediction tubes around learned dynamics models, enabling downstream planners to account for bounded future-state uncertainty [35]–[38]. Existing work on high-level navigation focuses on uncertainty-aware stopping or downstream confidence esti-mation rather than sequential action calibration [12], [39], [40]. These methods decide when to stop exploration but give no statistical guarantees for navigation actions. We apply CP directly to VLM-based action scoring, constructing calibrated prediction sets that govern the navigation policy. III. M ETHOD

We represent the agent’s exploration history as a dynam-ically growing tree to support action selection and back-tracking. Each node records a visited state, its candidate actions, and their VLM scores, while each edge represents an executed action. VI. C ONCLUSION AND LIMITATIONS 

Conclusions. We proposed CPN AV , a conformal prediction-based calibration framework for vision-language navigation. By filtering low-confidence actions through conformal calibration, CPN AV improves reliability and path efficiency without modifying the underlying policy, achieving higher SR and SPL in simulation and on hardware, where strict step budgets and execution noise amplify the cost of suboptimal decisions. The calibration scores also show how well a VLM’s action scores align with successful paths. The layer uses only output scores, so it applies to any VLM-based navigation framework without retraining. 

Limitations. First, tree nodes are defined solely by spa-tial proximity without orientation, which may introduce poor estimation. Second, hardware calibration uses simulated episodes, so the guarantee relies on exchangeability between simulation and the real apartments. Finally, the method relies on scores from a VLM accessed via the ChatGPT API, whose stochasticity can introduce variability in action scoring and calibration. E. Qualitative Analysis of HM3D Navigation 

Figure 6 provides a spatial view of the exploration behav-ior underlying the two outcomes. The baseline trajectory con-tains a dense cluster of overlapping transitions near the initial region, followed by exploration of several branches. These revisits consume navigation steps without a corresponding expansion of the searched area, limiting the progress made toward the target. The CPN AV trajectory contains fewer over-lapping transitions and repeated visits to explored regions, concentrating exploration on the route to the target. This comparison highlights the importance of deciding which geometrically feasible actions warrant further explo-ration. The No Calibration baseline relies on the highest raw VLM score, whereas CPN AV restricts action selection to the calibrated prediction set and initiates backtracking when no candidate satisfies the threshold. This mechanism is intended to limit continued exploration along low-confidence branches while retaining alternative routes in the exploration tree. The observed reduction in redundant search is consistent with this design and provides a qualitative interpretation of the improvements in success rate and path efficiency reported in Table II. In particular, the example illustrates how the allocation of a finite action budget can influence both search efficiency and the eventual navigation outcome.
