ABSTRACT 

We present Align3D, an image-conditioned diffusion model that generates 3D in-door layouts that are both faithful to a single input photograph and physically plausible beyond the observed view. From the photograph we infer a confidence-calibrated scene graph and train a set-diffusion layout denoiser with image-structure co-alignment where confidence-weighted modality fusion balances im-age and scene-graph alignment using predicted confidences, while observability-aware fidelity-preserving guidance projects conflicting scene-quality gradients and reweights them with an object-level confidence mask so under-observed de-grees of freedom absorb physical priors without undoing the image-graph match. On 3D-FRONT, Align3D improves image-alignment and scene-quality metrics over recent baselines. 

1 INTRODUCTION 

Generating consistent 3D indoor scenes from a single photograph is a fundamental problem in com-puter graphics and vision, with applications in embodied AI, virtual staging, and interactive de-sign (Nie et al., 2020; Sun et al., 2018). Early methods recover multi-object geometry via shape retrieval or compositional reconstruction (Han et al., 2024; Ardelean et al., 2025), while diffusion models now synthesize entire layouts as unordered object sets (Tang et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2024b). Most layout generators condition on text, category priors, or pre-specified scene graphs (Zhai et al., 2023; Wei et al., 2024; Feng et al., 2026); image-to-scene pipelines (Huang et al., 2025; Meng et al., 1arXiv:2611.08861v1 [cs.CV] 6 Nov 2026 2026; Lin et al., 2025) ground generation in RGB input but seldom enforce joint agreement between visual appearance and inferred spatial structure. The central challenge is reconciling two complementary signals: image appearance (materials, lighting, and composition in the reference view) and scene structure (objects and relations that should remain coherent across viewpoints) (Johnson et al., 2018; Xu et al., 2017). Visual embed-dings anchor layouts to the photograph (Radford et al., 2021), and scene graphs encode editable relational constraints (Zhai et al., 2023; Wei et al., 2024); prior work often optimizes the two se-quentially or with fixed loss weights (Ardelean et al., 2025; Yang et al., 2024b), treating every detection and relation as equally reliable even though single-view perception is inherently noisy. However, neither signal alone guarantees a usable room: a layout can match the image from one camera yet still collide, float, or violate relations that were only weakly observed. Single-view conditioning sharpens this tension: visible silhouettes pin some objects tightly, while depth ordering, occlusions, and hidden clearances remain underdetermined (Nie et al., 2020; Sun et al., 2018). Physics-aware generators improve plausibility (Yang et al., 2024b; Zhai et al., 2023; Wei et al., 2024), but typically combine quality terms with conditioning through a weighted sum that can harm image alignment when objectives conflict — a familiar issue in multi-task learning (Yu et al., 2020; Liu et al., 2021; Sener & Koltun, 2018). Our insight is that observational fidelity and scene quality should not compete along the same parameter directions: strongly observed variables should be protected, while under-constrained ones should absorb physical priors without harming the observation match. In addition, confidence provides the bookkeeping for this split: down-weighting uncertain detections, camera estimates, and relations during alignment, then routing quality updates away from well-supported layout variables. To this end, we present Align3D: image-structure co-aligned 3D scene generation (Fig. 1), an image-conditioned layout diffusion model that predicts a confidence-calibrated scene graph from a photograph and trains with image-structure co-alignment under observability-aware fidelity-preserving guidance. Our contributions are: • a state-of-the-art image-to-scene pipeline evaluated with competitive baselines on geometry, lay-out fidelity, and human preference metrics; • a confidence-calibrated scene graph inference module from single-view images that attaches de-tection, depth, camera, and relational confidences to every graph node and edge, enabling super-vision without ground-truth graphs; and • end-to-end confidence propagation through confidence-weighted modality fusion (CWMF) and 

observability-aware fidelity-preserving guidance (OFG) : CWMF adaptively balances image-rendering and scene-graph alignment from predicted confidences, while OFG projects conflicting quality gradients and reweights them with an object-level confidence mask derived from the same predicted confidences. 

2 RELATED WORK 

Scene graph representation and learning. Scene graphs encode objects and their relations as structured, editable constraints (Johnson et al., 2018; Xu et al., 2017). In 3D, early work predicts semantic scene graphs from indoor reconstructions (Wald et al., 2020), while other methods generate and manipulate scenes from given graphs (Dhamo et al., 2021). Subsequent graph-conditioned generators use relational priors for controllable layouts (Zhai et al., 2023; Wei et al., 2024) or couple multimodal graphs with rectified flow (Yang et al., 2026). Related text- or instruction-driven layout systems further show that explicit relational structure improves physical plausibility (Feng et al., 2026), but typically assume language or given objects rather than an observed photograph. We instead predict confidence-calibrated scene graphs from images and use them as image-structure co-alignment constraints alongside appearance matching. 

3D scene generation from images. Recovering a multi-object 3D scene from a single RGB image has progressed from feed-forward reconstruction and shape retrieval (Nie et al., 2020; Sun et al., 2018) to compositional pipelines that segment, complete, and assemble per-object geometry with layout optimization (Ardelean et al., 2025; Han et al., 2024). 3D diffusion models for scenes. Denoising diffusion models (Ho et al., 2020; Song et al., 2021) generate samples by gradually removing noise from a corrupted signal and have become a leading generative paradigm; transformer backbones further scale this process in latent space (Peebles & Xie, 2023). Earlier indoor layouts are synthesized autoregressively (Paschalidou et al., 2021) or with diffusion over object sets and relational graphs (Tang et al., 2024; Lin & Mu, 2024; Yang et al., 2024b; Bai et al., 2025), while other diffusion models generate room-scale geometry (Ju et al., 2024), image-conditioned multi-object meshes (Huang et al., 2025; Lin et al., 2025), or vision-guided lay-outs (Zhu et al., 2025). Most layout-oriented approaches still condition on text, floor plans, or given graphs rather than jointly co-aligning an observed photograph with predicted structure. We instead train an image-conditioned set-diffusion layout model with image-structure co-alignment and observability-aware fidelity-preserving guidance, so quality updates improve under-constrained degrees of freedom without undoing the observation match. 

3 METHOD 

Align3D generates image-conditioned indoor layouts through a set-diffusion denoiser over un-ordered object configurations (Fig. 1). Figure 2 summarizes the full system: an input photograph is converted to a confidence-calibrated scene graph, combined with image tokens via fused condi-tioning, and trained with image-structure co-alignment and observability-aware fidelity-preserving guidance; after training, the model can map a previously unseen photograph to a layout via the same graph-inference and denoising stack. We report two families of automatic metrics: scene quality and image alignment. Scene-quality met-rics are CD ↓ (Chamfer distance between point sets sampled from generated and ground-truth scene surfaces) (Fan et al., 2017), F-Score ↑ (harmonic mean of precision and recall at a fixed Euclidean distance threshold) (Tatarchenko et al., 2019), IoU ↑ (matched object-box intersection-over-union vs. ground truth) (Paschalidou et al., 2021), and FID ↓ (Fr´ echet distance between inception features of matched-view generated renderings and ground-truth test-set renderings) (Heusel et al., 2017). Image-alignment metrics are CLIP ↑ and DINO ↑ (cosine similarity between global CLIP (Radford et al., 2021) and DINOv2 (Oquab et al., 2024) embeddings of the conditioning image and a matched-view rendering). In addition, we introduce survey-based metrics, defined next. Unless noted other-wise, the best result in each table is set in bold and the second best is underlined. 

Human preference study. Automatic metrics do not fully capture perceived image match or placement plausibility, so we run a pairwise preference study. Each trial shows the conditioning image and two anonymized matched-view renderings from two methods drawn uniformly at ran-dom from DiffuScene, Gen3DSR, PartCrafter, MIDI, SceneGen, and our Align3D; the participant answers two questions: (i) which layout is closer to the input image, and (ii) which placement is more physically reasonable. We recruit 40 participants; each answers 20 trials (40 binary choices), yielding 800 comparisons per criterion. Order of methods and left/right placement are randomized, and ties are disallowed. We then fit a Bradley-Terry model (Bradley & Terry, 1952) independently for each criterion: each method m receives a latent strength πm, and the model predicts that m is chosen over m′ with probability P (m ≻ m′) = πm 

> πm+πm′

. After maximum-likelihood fitting on all pairwise choices, we convert each πm to a report score on a 0–100 scale where the cohort av-erage maps to 50 . We denote the resulting scores for questions (i) and (ii) by HP-Align ↑ (human preference for image alignment) and HP-Scene ↑ (human preference for scene quality), respectively. Naively summing both losses (“Sum”) degrades image alignment relative to projection (CLIP 0.901 vs. 0.921), indicating that gradient projection is necessary whenever quality and image-structure co-alignment objectives conflict. Re-versed projection confirms the design choice: protecting Lquality rather than co-alignment yields higher IoU (0.571) at the cost of substantially worse alignment metrics, i.e. sacrificing observation fidelity for generic plausibility. Full OFG is necessary for the best overall tradeoff—it matches or exceeds projection on alignment metrics while delivering the strongest CD and F-Score, because C

Beyond accuracy, practical systems must also be efficient. In Tab. 4, we compare training and per-scene inference times on a single NVIDIA RTX 4090. At inference, Align3D runs graph prediction (∼3 s), 50-step layout diffusion ( ∼5 s), and scene composition ( ∼2 s), totaling ∼10 s per scene, the fastest among compared methods. Figure 4 also plots the metrics against training or inference time. Align3D occupies the fast regime on both axes while remaining competitive or best on scene-quality and image-alignment metrics, illustrating a favorable quality-efficiency tradeoff for layout-centric image-to-scene generation. 

5 CONCLUSION 

We presented Align3D, which trains an image-conditioned layout generator with image-structure co-alignment and OFG so that generated rooms both explain the observation and remain physically plausible. The training objective protects image-graph evidence while letting scene-quality updates act in under-observed degrees of freedom. Limitations remain: we evaluate only single-image con-ditioning (Sec. C sketches a preliminary multi-view extension), proxy rendering does not take colors into account, and errors in predicted scene graphs can propagate into image-structure co-alignment. We leave these limitations for future work. 9AI USE STATEMENT

Layout denoiser. We instantiate the set-diffusion layout denoiser following DiffuScene (Tang et al., 2024): an image-conditioned denoiser over unordered object tuples (semantics, oriented boxes, shape codes) with shape retrieval from a shape library (3D-FUTURE (Fu et al., 2021b) in our experiments). Training uses Adam with learning rate 10 −3, batch size 128 , and 60 ,000 itera-tions; the diffusion process uses a linear β schedule from 10 −4 to 2×10 −2 over T =1000 steps, with 

50 -step DDIM sampling at inference. For the DiffuScene baseline, the official unconditional layout denoiser is adapted by encoding the conditioning RGB with CLIP (Radford et al., 2021) and inject-ing the resulting image embedding as an additional condition into the U-Net (cross-attention over the layout tokens), then training on the same 3D-FRONT splits with matched rendered views while keeping the original network width, diffusion schedule, and retrieval head otherwise unchanged.
