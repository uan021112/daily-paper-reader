## RADARDINO: DISTILLING A VISUAL FOUNDATION MODEL INTO MMWAVE RADAR FOR HUMAN MESH RECONSTRUCTION 

## Furkan Mert Algan, Zhifan Ni, Eckehard Steinbach 

## Technical University of Munich School of Computation, Information and Technology Chair of Media Technology Munich Institute of Robotics and Machine Intelligence 

## {fmert.algan, zhifan.ni, eckehard.steinbach }@tum.de 

ABSTRACT 

Millimeter-wave (mmWave) radar offers a privacy-preserving and illumination-robust alternative for human mesh recon-struction (HMR). However, high-fidelity radar HMR remains challenging. Sparse point cloud-based methods suffer from information loss and missed detections, while raw radar ten-sor methods like RT-Mesh lack fine-grained semantics and ex-plicit local body modeling. In this paper, we propose RadarD-INO, a radar HMR framework that distills visual semantics from a DINOv3 teacher into 3D radar representations and introduces explicit geometric supervision. DINOv3 descrip-tors are transferred to radar voxels via calibrated RGB-radar geometry, providing voxel- and body-part-level supervision for part-aware radar representations. We further incorporate vertex- and joint-losses to directly constrain reconstructed ge-ometry. On the M4Human dataset, our method outperforms the SOTA baseline across all three evaluation splits. 

Index Terms — Millimeter-wave radar, human mesh recon-struction, raw radar tensor, knowledge distillation 

1. INTRODUCTION 

3D human mesh reconstruction is crucial for human-centric applications. While recent vision-based methods [1, 2] achieve remarkable fidelity, optical cameras are sensitive to lighting variations and raise privacy concerns. In con-trast, millimeter-wave (mmWave) radar provides a promising privacy-preserving, illumination-robust alternative for future Integrated Sensing and Communication (ISAC) systems. Despite these advantages, high-fidelity radar HMR remains challenging. Early human pose estimation (HPE) [3, 4, 5, 6] and human mesh reconstruction (HMR) [7, 8, 9] methods rely mainly on sparse radar point clouds (RPCs), where CFAR fil-tering discards informative radar responses and can introduce missed detections. Several works [10, 11, 12, 13] instead ex-ploit raw radar tensors (RTs) to preserve richer information, but remain focused on skeletal landmarks. Recently, the M4Human [14] benchmark introduced RT-Mesh, a raw-RT HMR baseline using 3D convolutions and self-attention. However, several limitations remain. First, raw RTs lack fine-grained semantics needed to distinguish body parts. Second, RT-Mesh does not explicitly model voxel- and part-level semantic correspondences, limiting localized body representation. Third, SMPL-X [15] parametric supervision does not directly constrain reconstructed vertices and joints, which may lead to geometric errors under complex motions. To address these challenges, we propose RadarDINO, a raw-radar HMR framework that combines cross-modal visual semantic distillation with explicit geometric supervision. Specifically, we transfer DINOv3 [16] visual descriptors from synchronized RGB images to 3D radar voxels through cal-ibrated radar-camera geometry, providing voxel- and body-part-level semantic supervision. The resulting part-aware radar representation is used for mesh reconstruction, while explicit vertex- and joint-level losses complement SMPL-X parameter supervision to directly constrain the recon-structed geometry. Extensive experiments on the M4Human dataset [14] demonstrate that our model consistently im-proves radar-based HMR over existing methods, particularly under complex human motions. 

2. METHODOLOGY 

As illustrated in Fig. 1, our framework follows a two-stage pipeline. We first distill DINOv3 [16] semantics into a spatio-temporal radar encoder using sparse voxel correspondences and body-part-level supervision. A mesh decoder then uses distilled representation to predict SMPL-X parameters and re-construct the human geometry from radar input only.

3. EXPERIMENT 3.1. Experimental Setup 

We conduct experiments on the M4Human dataset [14], the current largest multimodal benchmark for mmWave radar-based HMR. We adhere to the official experimental protocols and dataset splits. We train the radar student for 25 epochs and jointly fine-tune it with the mesh decoder for 20 epochs, using AdamW with learning rates of 4 × 10 −4 and 2 × 10 −4,respectively, and cosine decay. Model performance is evalu-ated using standard metrics: Mean Vertex Error (MVE, mm) across all SMPL-X vertices, Mean Joint Error (MJE, mm) on 22 body joints, Mean Rotation Error (MRE, ◦) evaluat-ing joint rotations on SO (3) , and Translation Error (TE, mm) measuring global root localization. The baseline results are taken from [14]. 

3.2. Experimental Results Quantitative Evaluation. Table 1 compares our method with reported radar-based baselines on the three M4Human splits. Our method achieves the lowest MVE across all splits, reducing RT-Mesh’s [14] MVE by 31.3%, 11.2%, and 22.0% on S1–S3, respectively, while also improving joint, rotation, and translation errors. Compared with RGB+RT fusion, our radar-only model remains competitive while requiring no RGB input at inference. In terms of efficiency, our model uses only 2.70M parameters while requiring 1.98 GFLOPs, achieving both the smallest model size and the lowest com-putational cost among RT only methods. 3.3. Ablation Study 

Table 2 evaluates DINO-guided radar pretraining on the most challenging S3 split. Compared with random initialization, DINO-guided initialization consistently improves mesh re-construction. The gain is largely retained with a frozen en-coder and further improves with joint fine-tuning, indicating that the distilled representation itself provides useful infor-mation for downstream mesh prediction. However, directly using the RGB DINO teacher features achieves a much lower MVE than using the distilled radar representation on S3, re-vealing a remaining teacher-student gap. 

4. CONCLUSION 

We present RadarDINO, a two-stage framework for radar-based human mesh reconstruction via visual feature distil-lation. A fine-tuned visual foundation model supervises the radar encoder at voxel and body-part levels, and the distilled representation is used by a mesh decoder to predict SMPL-X geometry from radar alone. Experiments on M4Human show consistent improvements over existing radar-based baselines. Future work will explore more effective cross-modal distil-lation and stronger generalization across environments and radar devices. 5. ACKNOWLEDGEMENTS
