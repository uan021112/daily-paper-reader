Abstract
Despite impressive progress, existing feed-forward large 3D reconstruction mod-
els produce a single, irrevocable prediction. When these models fail, there is no
mechanism to correct their mistakes. To address this limitation, we introduce
CLICK3R, a framework for interactive geometric correction of feed-forward mod-
els using sparse human correspondence clicks. Given an image pair and a small
set of cross-view point correspondences provided by a user, CLICK3R injects
geometric constraints into the 3D reconstruction networks through a lightweight
point correspondence adapter, enabling the model to resolve ambiguities in chal-
lenging scenarios such as repetitive textures, symmetric structures, large viewpoint
changes, or textureless regions. To evaluate interactive reconstruction, we curate a
challenging benchmark specifically designed to expose failure modes of existing
feed-forward 3D reconstruction methods. Experiments show that CLICK3R re-
duces reconstruction error with as few as a single correspondence click, improving
performance on both standard and our curated benchmarks. Our results highlight
that sparse human interaction provides an effective and practical mechanism for
correcting geometric reconstruction errors.

Introduction
Feed-forward stereo 3D reconstruction methods [44, 16, 29, 49] have transformed multi-view ge-
ometry by replacing iterative optimization [25, 34] with direct regression. Given two images, these
models predict dense 3D pointmaps in a single forward pass, achieving remarkable speed and
generalization across diverse scenes. This paradigm has recently been extended to multi-view set-
tings [42, 45, 53, 40, 3]. Despite their impressive performance, the efficiency comes at a fundamental
cost: when the prediction is wrong, there is no simple mechanism to correct it. Unlike optimization-
based methods [34] that iteratively refine their estimates using geometric constraints, feed-forward
models produce a single, irrevocable output and fail silently, particularly on challenging scenes with
repetitive textures, symmetric structures, or extreme viewpoint changes (Fig. 1a,b).
Several approaches aim to improve the reliability of feed-forward reconstruction models through
test-time optimization [24, 5]. For instance, LoRA3D [24] introduces a self-calibration pipeline
that fine-tunes a pretrained DUSt3R model [44] to a target scene. Although these methods improve
performance, iterative test-time optimization is computationally expensive and, more importantly,
cannot resolve failures when the model encounters genuinely ambiguous scenes at inference time.
Another line of work improves robustness by incorporating auxiliary geometric priors. For example,
Pow3R [10] shows that conditioning on camera intrinsics, relative camera poses, or depth maps
can significantly improve reconstruction performance. However, such auxiliary information is often
unavailable in practice. Meanwhile, classical reconstruction methods have long relied on explicit

correspondences remain unreliable in scenarios with repetitive textures or large viewpoint changes.
Nevertheless, cross-view correspondences, even just a handful of point pairs, carry powerful geomet-
ric information: they constrain the epipolar geometry, resolve depth ambiguities, and disambiguate
symmetric structures. This motivates a simple question: can sparse human-provided correspon-
dences guide feed-forward 3D reconstruction models without sacrificing the efficiency of the
feed-forward paradigm? Instead of relying solely on feed-forward models or automatically es-
timated correspondences, we allow the user to manually provide a small set of cross-view point
correspondences (Fig. 1a), when the model produces unsatisfactory reconstructions.
To unlock this capability, we introduce CLICK3R, a lightweight framework that incorporates user-
provided correspondences into feed-forward 3D reconstruction, enabling interactive geometric
correction without test-time model fine-tuning or optimization. Specifically, CLICK3R employs
a simple correspondence adapter that injects the geometric constraints imposed by user clicks into
the reconstruction network via a cross-attention mechanism (Fig. 2). We train the base model and
the adapter jointly end-to-end on widely used 3D datasets [28, 2, 57] using simulated human clicks.
During training, the number of clicks is randomly sampled to expose the model to varying levels of
user interaction, enabling it to operate under different levels of click sparsity at test time.
To systematically evaluate the proposed interactive reconstruction, we further introduce
Hard3DBench, a benchmark designed to expose failure modes of state-of-the-art feed-forward
reconstruction models. The benchmark contains image pairs and 5-view tuples with ground-truth
3D reconstructions, exhibiting challenging conditions (large viewpoint changes, low overlap, large
baselines, extreme depth ranges) where feed-forward reconstruction models often struggle. Our
experiments demonstrate that CLICK3R consistently outperforms baseline methods, particularly on
challenging scenarios from Hard3DBench (Fig. 1c).
Our contributions are summarized as follows:
• We propose CLICK3R, which injects sparse human correspondence clicks into feed-forward
stereo 3D reconstruction networks via a lightweight correspondence adapter.
• We curate Hard3DBench, a diagnostic benchmark with a detailed failure taxonomy under which
current feed-forward reconstruction models degrade substantially relative to standard evaluation
samples.
• We show that CLICK3R dramatically improves reconstruction accuracy on challenging cases. To
the best of our knowledge, this is the first work on interactive stereo feed-forward 3D reconstruc-
tion using sparse correspondence clicks.

Related Work
Feed-Forward Stereo 3D Reconstruction.
DUSt3R [44] pioneered dense 3D pointmap regression
from image pairs using a CroCo-pretrained [48, 47] ViT encoder-decoder, bypassing explicit matching
and triangulation. MASt3R [16] extended this with dense local feature heads. The paradigm has
rapidly advanced toward multi-view: Spann3R [41] and MUSt3R [3] use spatial memory across
frames; CUT3R [43] employs a recurrent model with persistent state; Fast3R [53] scales to 1000+
images in a single forward pass; and VGGT [42] and Pi3 [45] predict from arbitrary views with a
single Transformer. Despite their diversity, all these methods rely solely on learned visual priors and
cannot incorporate external geometric evidence at inference. Our work is orthogonal: CLICK3R
augments feed-forward stereo reconstruction with sparse correspondence guidance.
Conditioned 3D Reconstruction.
Several concurrent methods condition feed-forward 3D models
on external geometric signals: Pow3R [10] on camera intrinsics, depth, or pose, G-CUT3R [14]
on camera and depth modalities, Rig3R [17] on multi-camera rig metadata, and Mono3R [18] on
monocular depth. MapAnything [13] unifies a flexible mix of conditioning inputs (intrinsics, poses,
depth, partial reconstructions) into a single feed-forward model, while Any4D [12] extends conditional
reconstruction to dynamic 4D scenes with multi-modal sensor inputs. In the depth estimation domain,
PromptDA [21] prompts Depth Anything [54] with sparse LiDAR points. All these methods condition
on scene-level priors that improve metric accuracy but cannot resolve perceptual aliasing. To our
knowledge, CLICK3R is the first to introduce correspondence prompting, which conditions feed-
forward 3D reconstruction on cross-view point correspondences that directly disambiguate geometric
ambiguities, analogous to SAM’s [15, 27] click-based segmentation prompting.
Interactive Vision System.
Interactive segmentation has a long history of incorporating user
guidance into visual recognition. Early methods use bounding boxes [30] or scribbles [20], while
recent approaches adopt click-based prompting [51, 4, 23, 39], culminating in SAM [15, 27]. Beyond
segmentation, point-based interaction has been extended to image editing: DragGAN [26] and
DragDiffusion [37] enable users to manipulate content by dragging points. Recent work has also
explored interactive guidance for 3D reconstruction; for example, Toon3D [46] uses sparse user
annotations for cartoon 3D reconstruction. However, these interactive paradigms have largely
remained confined to 2D tasks or stylized 3D settings. Early interactive systems also explored
correspondence-based 3D reconstruction [9]. CLICK3R brings this idea to feed-forward stereo
models, with point correspondences as geometric constraints rather than semantic cues.

3.3
Point Correspondence Adapter
We inject correspondence information into DUSt3R’s decoder using a two-way attention adapter
inspired by SAM [15], enabling bidirectional exchange between sparse correspondence tokens and
dense image tokens (Figure 2).
Two-Way Attention.
The adapter has L = 2 layers. Each layer applies: (1) self-attention over
prompt tokens Tv; (2) prompt-to-image cross-attention, Attn(Tv, Gv, Gv); (3) an MLP on prompt
tokens; and (4) image-to-prompt cross-attention, Attn(Gv, Tv, Tv). This bidirectional design lets
prompts condition image features with correspondence constraints while allowing prompt tokens to
absorb local visual context.
Image Positional Encoding.
For cross-attention, image positional encodings are computed by
applying the same Fourier PE to normalized DUSt3R token-grid positions. Thus, prompt and image
tokens share a common coordinate space, allowing cross-attention to compare their spatial locations
consistently.

Hard3DBench
The primary value of CLICK3R is on challenging scenarios where DUSt3R fails. Standard evaluations
report only aggregate metrics, masking catastrophic failures on geometrically ambiguous scenes.
To enable systematic analysis, we introduce Hard3DBench, a curated benchmark for both 2-view
stereo and 5-view multi-view settings, accompanied by a failure taxonomy that exposes the specific
failure modes of feed-forward 3D reconstruction. The 2-view setting contains 404 in-distribution and
300 out-of-distribution pairs, and the 5-view setting 400 in-distribution and 300 out-of-distribution
tuples. In-distribution samples come from held-out test/validation splits of four datasets used to
train DUSt3R and CLICK3R: BlendedMVS [56], CO3D [28], Habitat [32], and WildRGB-D [50].
Out-of-distribution samples come from five datasets absent from training: 7Scenes [38], DTU [11],
ETH3D [35], NRGBD [1], and ScanNet [6]. Full curation details are in Appendix C.

Datasets.
For standard evaluation, we use held-out splits of three benchmarks: CO3D [28], Scan-
Net++ [57], and ARKitScenes [2]. For each dataset, we sample 1,000 image pairs with a fixed random
seed and use the same pairs across all methods. We additionally evaluate on the Hard3DBench bench-
mark (Section 5.3).
Metrics.
Following DUSt3R [44], we report two metrics: scale-and-shift-invariant pointmap error
for 3D reconstruction quality (lower is better) and mean Average Accuracy at threshold θ◦(mAA@θ,
higher is better) for relative camera pose accuracy. Tables report mAA@30°; results at 5° and 15° are
in Appendix E.2. Implementation details are in Appendix A.
Prompt selection strategies.
We evaluate several prompt selection strategies. (a) Random oracle
(Kp_bestB): sample K correspondences B independent times and select the set that minimizes
reconstruction loss. If there’s no bestB mentioned, K correspondences are sampled randomly. (b)
Grid oracle: partition view 1 into a 16 × 16 grid of 14-pixel cells (∼256 cells), select one random
correspondence per populated cell, run a batched forward pass for each candidate, and keep the single
correspondence whose cell yields the greatest pose improvement (reduction in max(Rerr, Terr)) over
the no-prompt baseline. Samples without valid correspondences fall back to the no-prompt result.

Evaluation on Standard Benchmark
Table 1 presents results on standard benchmarks. Without prompts, CLICK3R performs on par with
DUSt3R (retrained). Adding even a single well-chosen prompt (1p_best5) consistently improves
over the unprompted baseline. The grid oracle reveals substantially larger headroom, with a striking
+8.5% mAA@30° on ARKitScenes (from 84.27% to 92.75%). This confirms that prompt location
matters significantly: the model can leverage even a single correspondence to meaningfully reduce
pose error when placed in the right region. Notably, the grid oracle delivers the largest pose gains
while pointmap error improves only modestly, indicating that prompts primarily reshape the overall
scene configuration rather than uniformly tightening local surface fits.

Evaluation on Hard3DBench

mAA@30° over the retrained DUSt3R baseline across all four conditions, with the largest gain of

results suggest that even sparse correspondence prompts effectively resolve failure modes in highly

5.4
Evaluation with Human-Labeled Correspondences
The previous experiments use ground-truth correspondences derived from known 3D geometry. To
evaluate CLICK3R under realistic annotation conditions, we test on 100 image pairs with human-
labeled correspondences. The pairs are drawn from out-of-distribution set of Hard3DBench and cover
challenging geometric configurations. A human annotator, not specifically familiar with the evaluated
datasets or scenes, freely chooses sparse correspondences (3.8 per pair on average, from 1 to 7) via a
labeling interface (Figure 12), without access to ground-truth depth or camera parameters.
Table 3 demonstrates that human-provided prompts drastically reduce median rotation error compared
to the unprompted model (8.78° vs. 17.30°) and raise mAA@30 from 36.2 to 56.2, along with
significant improvement in Relative Rotation Accuracy. Compared to the random ground-truth oracle
(K =4), human labels recover about 96% of the medRRE improvement gap, showing that sparse,
imperfect human clicks capture most of the benefit of perfect correspondences. These results confirm
that CLICK3R translates effectively from synthetic oracle prompts to practical human interaction.

6
Conclusion
We presented CLICK3R, an interactive stereo 3D reconstruction framework that bridges fully au-
tomatic feed-forward methods, correspondence-based classical pipelines, and recent prompt-based
vision systems. By injecting sparse point correspondences into a feed-forward stereo 3D recon-
struction model through a lightweight correspondence adapter, CLICK3R enables users to guide
the reconstruction process without test-time model fine-tuning or optimization, resolving geometric
ambiguities that often cause catastrophic failures. Experiments on our curated Hard3DBench demon-
9


strate that even a handful of correspondences can dramatically improve reconstruction quality in
challenging scenarios. While CLICK3R has limitations (Appendix J) and failure cases (Appendix H),
we hope this work inspires further exploration of correspondence prompting as a promising direction
for interactive 3D reconstruction, analogous to how SAM [15] advanced interactive prompting for
2D segmentation.

A
Implementation Details
CLICK3R follows the DUSt3R [44] training recipe (loss function, optimizer, data augmentation,
symmetric batching) and inherits its CroCo v2 [47] ViT-Large encoder and dual asymmetric decoder.
Below we detail the components specific to CLICK3R.
Correspondence adapter.
We insert two SAM-style [15] Two-Way Transformer adapters (depth 2,
8 heads, MLP dim 2048, embedding dim 768) between the encoder and decoder (Section 3.3). The
two adapters share a single correspondence tokenizer (Section 3.2) that encodes each correspondence
point as the sum of a positional encoding (random Fourier features [15]), an image identity embedding,
and a pair identity embedding (vocabulary size 256). When prompts are provided, the adapted features
replace the encoder output; otherwise, the adapter is bypassed and the model reduces to standard
DUSt3R. The adapters and tokenizer are randomly initialized; all other weights are initialized from
the CroCo pretrained checkpoint. All parameters are trained jointly end-to-end.
Training data.
We train on nine datasets, each contributing 100,000 image pairs: BlendedMVS [56],
MegaDepth [19], CO3D [28], StaticThings3D [36], Waymo Open [52], ARKitScenes [2], WildRGB-
D [50], Habitat [32], and ScanNet++ [57]. Validation uses held-out splits from six of these datasets
with 1,000 pairs each.
Ground-truth prompt generation.
During training, correspondence prompts are generated by
projecting view 1’s 3D pointmap into view 2 and accepting correspondences where the 3D distance
to the nearest view 2 surface point is below 5 cm (which implicitly rejects occluded points). Per
sample, 30% receive zero prompts (adapter bypassed), 40% receive 1–4, and 30% receive 5–12
correspondences.
Training configuration.
We train for 100 epochs on 4 GPUs with per-GPU batch size 16, using
AdamW (β1=0.9, β2=0.95, weight decay 0.05), a learning rate of 10−4 with 10 epochs of linear
warmup followed by cosine decay to 10−6, and float16 mixed precision.
Baseline image preprocessing.
DUSt3R-family models (DUSt3R, MASt3R, CLICK3R) use images
normalized to [−1, 1]. The VGGT and Pi3 wrappers internally denormalize images to [0, 1] before
forwarding to match each model’s expected input range.
Standard benchmark data.
The held-out splits are the CO3D test sequences, the ScanNet++
validation scenes, and the ARKitScenes validation videos, each processed with the DUSt3R pipeline.
Evaluation metric details.
The scale-and-shift-invariant pointmap error uses the normalization
of DUSt3R: we divide the predicted pointmap by its mean point distance, shift both pointmaps by
their median depth, and rescale the prediction to the median distance of the ground-truth points to their
median point. We then average the per-pixel L2 distance over all valid pixels (i.e., L2 within each 3D
point, mean across pixels). For mAA@θ, relative camera pose is first estimated from each model’s
predicted pointmaps via PnP-RANSAC (cv2.solvePnPRansac with the SQPNP solver), recovering
per-view camera-to-world poses. Rotation error is the geodesic distance arccos

B
Training Details
Generating Training Correspondences.
During training, we generate prompts from ground-truth
3D geometry. For each image pair with known cameras and pointmaps, we first sample the number
of correspondence prompts according to a curriculum: 30% of samples use no prompts (K = 0),
40% use a small number of prompts (K ∈[1, 4]), and 30% use more prompts (K ∈[5, 12]). Given
the sampled value of K, we: (1) sample K 3D points from the first view; (2) project them into both
views using known intrinsics and extrinsics; (3) retain only points that are mutually visible, verified
by comparing the projected 3D position against the second view’s GT pointmap within a distance
threshold.
Training Protocol.
We initialize from CroCo v2 [47] pretrained weights and train all parameters
end-to-end, including both the base model and the adapter. Training uses 4 GPUs, AdamW (lr=10−4)
with linear warmup and cosine decay. We train on the same dataset mixture as DUSt3R: CO3D [28],
ARKitScenes [2], ScanNet++ [57], WildRGB-D [50], BlendedMVS [56], MegaDepth [19], Habi-
tat [32], Waymo [52], and StaticThings3D [36] (100K pairs each), at 224 × 224 resolution. With
batch size 16 per GPU on A100s, a training step takes 0.60 s for CLICK3R and 0.55 s for DUSt3R
(retrained), which amounts to about 234 and 215 hours for 100 epochs on 4 GPUs, with a peak
memory of 29,069 and 28,731 MiB per GPU.
C
Hard3DBench Details
Hard3DBench has two subsets: a 2-view stereo subset of 404 in-distribution and 300 out-of-
distribution pairs, and a 5-view multi-view subset of 400 in-distribution and 300 out-of-distribution
tuples. Both subsets use pretrained DUSt3R’s pose error as the difficulty signal, rather than CLICK3R
or our retrained baseline, so the benchmark is defined independently of the methods under evaluation.
They share the same source datasets: the in-distribution split uses held-out test/val splits of four
DUSt3R training datasets, while the out-of-distribution split uses five datasets entirely absent from
training (Table 6 reports the 2-view per-dataset statistics). DTU uses the cameras of MVSNet [55]
and the depth maps rendered by Spann3R [41], 7Scenes depth is registered to the color camera as in
SimpleRecon [33], ScanNet is prepared following MonST3R [58], and ETH3D following Pi3 [45].
2-view subset.
To obtain hard cases, we evaluate the pretrained DUSt3R model on up to 20,000
randomly drawn image pairs per dataset (∼69K in-distribution, ∼68K out-of-distribution), pre-
filtering pairs with insufficient co-visibility via depth reprojection in DUSt3R’s 224×224 crop space.

For each scene, we sample up to 30 5-frame tuples by combining stride-based
windows (strides 1, 2, 5, 10, 20) with random index sampling and deduplication; tuples are kept
only if all five views form a connected graph under pairwise depth-reprojection overlap ≥5%. We
then run DUSt3R on all C(5, 2)=10 symmetrized pairs and compose them via the standard global
alignment optimizer (300 iterations, msp initialization), yielding ∼16K in-distribution and ∼4K
out-of-distribution candidate tuples. We rank tuples by mean pairwise rotation error and select the
top-100 per in-distribution dataset (400 total) and top-60 per out-of-distribution dataset (300 total).
Difficulty for recent models.
Compared with random pairs from the same datasets with the same
per-dataset counts, VGGT and Pi3 at 518 resolution drop in RRA@5 from 82.6% and 85.1% to
54.7% and 57.9% on the in-distribution pairs of the 2-view subset, and from 98.2% and 97.0% to
88.7% and 81.3% on the out-of-distribution pairs, confirming that Hard3DBench remains challenging
for more recent 3D reconstruction models.

E.6
Automatic Prompts
We also consider scenarios without ground-truth correspondences, where we use automatic prompting
from RoMa v2 [7], a state-of-the-art dense feature matcher. We run it on each image pair, rank its
matches by confidence, and greedily select K of them with a spacing of 10% of the image width in
both views, so that the clicks spread over the scene. These clicks improve mAA@30 on all three
sets of Table 12: with K = 4, from 55.9 to 58.0 on the in-distribution pairs, from 49.4 to 59.6 on
the out-of-distribution pairs, and from 35.6 to 55.0 on the 100 out-of-distribution pairs with human
clicks, close to the human clicks themselves (56.8).
E.7
Transfer to MASt3R
As a controlled study of whether correspondence prompting transfers to another backbone, we add the
adapter to MASt3R [16] and train it end-to-end, along with a control without the adapter on the same
data. Both are trained on the same nine datasets as CLICK3R (CO3D, ARKitScenes, ScanNet++,
WildRGB-D, BlendedMVS, MegaDepth, Habitat, Waymo, and StaticThings3D), a smaller mixture
than the official MASt3R recipe, so their absolute numbers are not comparable with the released
model. Test-time clicks raise mAA@30 from 61.1 to 62.4 on the in-distribution pairs and from 52.1
to 54.9 on the out-of-distribution pairs (Table 13), confirming that correspondence clicks also improve
performance on a different backbone.

Limitations
Although correspondence prompts are ideally injected into a frozen pretrained model, we find that
training the base model [44] end-to-end with the adapter is necessary in practice, incurring nontrivial
training cost. Partly due to this cost, our experiments focus on two-view reconstruction; extending to
multi-view models [45, 42, 53, 40] with trajectory prompts across N views is an important direction.
Architecturally, the adapter treats correspondences as soft feature conditioning via cross-attention
rather than enforcing hard geometric constraints. Although eight correspondences suffice to determine
relative pose algebraically [8], the adapter cannot replicate such reasoning: it modulates all image
tokens through learned attention at a single injection point, and this signal is progressively diluted
across twelve subsequent decoder cross-attention blocks. Deeper injection at multiple decoder layers
and explicit geometric reasoning modules may be worth future inquiry to help close this gap.
Finally, due to computational constraints, the model is trained only at a resolution of 224px in a
stereo setting, although it has demonstrated multi-view capability through global alignment. In
addition, prompts must be co-visible in both views, so the two inputs need a shared visible region;
non-overlapping inputs could be connected through intermediate views by chaining correspondences.
Severely incorrect correspondences can degrade reconstruction quality (Table 11).

Broader Impacts
Our work enables more efficient 3D reconstruction from sparse user-provided correspondences. By
allowing a small number of clicks to anchor pairwise reconstruction, CLICK3R can lower the barrier
to 3D capture in diverse, in-the-wild scenes, which may benefit downstream applications such as
robotics, AR/VR content creation, and scientific imaging where dense ground-truth geometry is
unavailable. Our model and benchmark are built from publicly available datasets (CO3D, ScanNet++,
ARKitScenes, BlendedMVS, MegaDepth, Habitat, WildRGB-D, Waymo, and standard evaluation
benchmarks), and therefore do not raise privacy concerns. While our algorithm itself is scene-agnostic
