## ABSTRACT 

Embodied systems need geometric perception that exploits available observations beyond images alone. Recent feed-forward 3D models incorporate geometric priors, including camera poses, intrinsics, and depth. However, handling noisy poses, preserving accurate priors, and recovering physical scale require more than simply accepting these inputs. We introduce Vision-Prior Geometry Grounded Transformer (VPGGT), a VGGT-based framework that extends OmniVGGT for prior-aware embodied perception. We formulate sensor-motivated pose corrup-tions from ground-truth trajectories for training and introduce a parameter-free 

prior residual connection (PRC) to mitigate prior dilution , where predictions are less accurate than their supplied pose priors. Our noise formulation targets cam-era poses; supplied intrinsics and depth receive no additional corruption. We fur-ther introduce Metric Global Attention , which conditions a global scale token on available pose and depth scales and predicts a shared metric scaling factor for the geometric outputs. Experiments across four datasets show that PRC improves translation-direction accuracy and joint pose AUC over a matched training base-line when camera priors are provided for all views, under both exact and corrupted poses. These results support explicit prior access during refinement as a useful ad-dition to feature-level conditioning.

We study feed-forward 3D perception from a set of images and optional camera or depth priors (Fig. 1). DUSt3R (Wang et al., 2024b) and VGGT (Wang et al., 2025) have advanced image-based reconstruction by predicting multiple geometric attributes within a shared network. Their image-only inputs, however, do not exploit the camera estimates and depth observations often available

Pow3R (Jang et al., 2025), OmniVGGT (Peng et al., 2026), and MapAny-thing (Keetha et al., 2025) extend feed-forward reconstruction to incorporate such geometric infor-mation alongside images, providing a common model for different input configurations. Several challenges remain when these models are used with observations from embodied platforms. First, camera priors are estimates rather than ground truth, and their errors can accumulate over a trajectory or vary independently between frames. Although previous work has examined incorrect camera inputs, training and evaluation under structured pose errors require further study. Second, a model may produce camera estimates that are less accurate than the supplied priors, even when those priors are exact. We refer to this shortfall as prior dilution (Sec. 3.2). Third, embodied systems require geometry in physical units, while normalizing geometric inputs removes their original scale. MapAnything (Keetha et al., 2025) addresses metric reconstruction with a factored representation. Here, we study scale recovery while retaining the camera and dense-prediction heads of a pretrained VGGT-derived model. In this paper, we introduce Vision-Prior Geometry Grounded Transformer (VPGGT), a feed-forward model that combines images with optional camera and depth priors. First, we formulate sensor-motivated pose corruptions from existing ground-truth trajectories for training (Sec. 5). 1 Second, we introduce a parameter-free Prior Residual Connection (PRC) that places scale-aligned priors in the camera head’s refinement state to mitigate prior dilution (Sec. 4.2). Third, Metric Global Attention conditions a global token on available pose and depth scales and predicts a shared metric factor (Sec. 4.3). The model retains the image-only setting when geometric priors are absent and a common interface for further platform-specific training. Comprehensive experiments and systematic architecture ablations examine the effects of prior qual-ity and availability on camera and depth prediction. Under matched training configurations, PRC 

improves translation-direction accuracy and joint pose AUC on all four datasets when camera priors cover every view, for both exact and corrupted poses. The improvements are largest on TUM RGB-D and C3VD, while results at partial coverage reveal remaining limitations. We study these effects separately from metric-scale recovery to distinguish better camera refinement from better estimation of physical scale. 

## 2 RELATED WORK 

Vision-Based 3D Reconstruction Traditional reconstruction systems combine correspondence estimation, camera recovery, and sparse or dense mapping through task-specific modules (Schon-berger & Frahm, 2016; Furukawa et al., 2010; Campos et al., 2021). Learned features, matchers, depth networks, and differentiable optimization improve these components(DeTone et al., 2018; Sarlin et al., 2020; Sun et al., 2021; Teed & Deng, 2021; Yao et al., 2018; Wang et al., 2024a). VGGSfM (Wang et al., 2024a), for example, integrates learned correspondences and camera esti-mation with differentiable bundle adjustment. In contrast, DUSt3R (Wang et al., 2024b) predicts dense pointmaps from image pairs, while VGGT (Wang et al., 2025) jointly predicts camera pa-rameters and dense geometry from multiple views. These models reduce the need for separately designed reconstruction stages, but their image-only interfaces do not use external camera or depth observations. 

Embodied Perception Embodied platforms may combine cameras with IMUs, wheel encoders, LiDAR, and depth sensors(Zhao et al., 2025; Frey et al., 2026; Mao & Gu, 2025; Cullen, 2023). Visual–inertial and multi-sensor SLAM fuse their measurements through state estimation and geo-metric optimization (Qin et al., 2018; Campos et al., 2021). Their observation models, uncertainty estimates, and calibration describe how individual sensors constrain the estimated motion. Prior-conditioned feed-forward models operate at a different level, taking geometric estimates rather than raw sensor measurements. Pow3R (Jang et al., 2025), OmniVGGT (Peng et al., 2026), and MapA-nything (Keetha et al., 2025) incorporate camera and depth information into dense reconstruction, with MapAnything also predicting metric scale. Pow3R examines responses to incorrect camera parameters, and MapAnything identifies noise and uncertainty in geometric inputs as a remaining limitation. Our work studies structured pose corruption, preservation of informative camera priors during refinement, and scale conditioning within a pretrained multi-view model.

## 3 PRELIMINARIES AND PROBLEM DEFINITION 

3.1 PRELIMINARIES 

Our implementation builds on OmniVGGT (Peng et al., 2026), retaining the backbone and prediction heads inherited from VGGT (Wang et al., 2025). Each image Ii is encoded into patch tokens tIi ,one camera token tgi , and four register tokens tRi . The backbone contains L = 24 pairs of frame-wise and global self-attention blocks. OmniVGGT’s GeoAdapters add normalized depth features to image tokens and camera features to camera tokens, with stochastic prior availability during training. Depth and pointmap heads predict dense geometry, while the camera head refines a nine-dimensional camera estimate over four iterations. We retain these components and add a camera-state prior connection and a metric-scale prediction branch. 3.2 PRIOR DILUTION

## 5 EXPERIMENTS 

Datasets. We train VPGGT from the released OmniVGGT checkpoint using TartanAir (Wang et al., 2020), Virtual KITTI 2 (Cabon et al., 2020), TUM RGB-D (Sturm et al., 2012), and C3VD (Bobrow et al., 2023). These datasets include simulated navigation and driving sequences, handheld RGB-D capture, and endoscopic imagery. Each epoch contains approximately 100k sam-pled training items. We evaluate on TartanAir-V2 and held-out sequences of the other three datasets, using ten-frame windows with stride three and up to ten non-overlapping windows per sequence. The drift profiles combine sequence-level bi-ases with random motion increments, while the C3VD profile perturbs each pose independently (Table 2). Implementation Details. The backbone contains 24 frame/global attention pairs with token dimension 1024. We retain the original geometric adapters and prediction heads. PRC adds no parameters, while the scale branch adds approximately 0.447M parameters. For the prior-utilization study, we train the baseline and its PRC variant from the same released OmniVGGT checkpoint for two epochs under matched configurations. This study used three NVIDIA H800 GPUs over approximately seven days. We then initialize VPGGT from the trained PRC model and train the scale branch jointly with the network for two further epochs, using four H800 GPUs over approximately three days. Training uses AdamW, cosine learning-rate decay with warmup, bfloat16, gradient checkpointing, and gradient clipping at 1. We evaluate the effect of PRC on held-out TUM RGB-D (Sturm et al., 2012) and C3VD (Bobrow et al., 2023) sequences. The baseline and PRC variant are initialized from the same released Om-niVGGT (Peng et al., 2026) checkpoint and trained with matched data, optimization settings, and budgets. We denote them as OmniVGGT-trained and VPGGT (PRC only), respectively. Evaluation uses ten-frame windows at stride three, with images resized to width 518 and heights rounded to a multiple of 14. Camera and depth priors are supplied to the first 30%, 50%, 70%, or 100% of the views. Table 1 shows that PRC improves camera-prior utilization, particularly at full coverage. With exact camera priors and no depth input, it raises RTA@ 5◦ from 58.78 to 73.78 on TUM RGB-D and from 43.16 to 76.74 on C3VD, gains of 15.00 and 33.58 percentage points. The corresponding AUC@ 30 ◦

gains are 9.42 and 19.06 points. With noisy poses, RTA improves by 8.11 and 12.84 points, while AUC improves by 5.48 and 5.08 points. The refined output also exceeds the noisy input refer-ence on individual metrics: 67.00 versus 66.00 RTA@ 5◦ on TUM RGB-D, and 72.81 versus 71.73 AUC@ 30 ◦ on C3VD. These comparisons indicate improved refinement, but not uniform superiority over the supplied priors. Depth observations mainly improve depth prediction. At 30% depth coverage, the PRC variant re-duces AbsRel from 0.058 to 0.031 on TUM RGB-D and from 0.055 to 0.015 on C3VD. Camera gains are less consistent at partial coverage. For example, at 30% noisy camera coverage, C3VD RTA@ 5◦ decreases from 27.65 to 26.02 relative to OmniVGGT-trained. With full noisy camera and depth inputs, its C3VD AUC is also slightly lower, at 74.53 versus 74.80. PRC therefore improves prior utilization without guaranteeing preservation under every input configuration. Single-frame inference, scene-level surface accuracy and completeness, clinical use, and closed-loop control are not evaluated. 

## 6 CONCLUSION 

We present VPGGT, a feed-forward model for geometric perception from images and optional cam-era and depth priors. The model extends an OmniVGGT backbone with sensor-motivated pose-prior training, a parameter-free Prior Residual Connection , and a prior-conditioned global scale token. PRC gives the camera head direct access to supplied poses during refinement, while the scale branch converts the geometric outputs to metric units. Matched-training experiments show improved translation-direction accuracy and joint pose AUC under full camera-prior coverage, including cor-rupted poses. The results also show that prior availability alone does not ensure prior preservation, particularly at partial coverage. VPGGT provides a common model for incorporating geometric es-timates into feed-forward perception and for further adaptation to the observations available on an embodied platform. AI U SE STATEMENT

A.4 EVALUATION PROTOCOL 

Windows and input selection. Evaluation uses ten-frame windows with a frame stride of three. Each window spans 28 frames of the source sequence. Up to ten windows are distributed across each eligible sequence, without overlap between their temporal spans. Sequences shorter than the required span are excluded according to ground-truth availability. Images are resized to width 518, with heights rounded to a multiple of 14; intrinsics are adjusted accordingly. For a coverage ratio 

ρ, priors are supplied to the first round(10 ρ) views. Camera and depth coverage are controlled separately. The camera input consists of intrinsics and poses together.

Non-finite pose predictions cause evaluation failure rather than being removed from the scoring support. Pose scores pool eligible pairs across evaluation windows. 

Depth metrics. Depth validity is determined from ground truth. Pixels must have finite depth greater than 10 −4 and satisfy the dataset-specific upper-depth limit, when one is configured. Frames with fewer than 100 valid pixels are excluded. For the scale-aligned results, each predicted depth map is multiplied by the ratio of the ground-truth and predicted depth medians on these pixels. We then compute AbsRel and δ1, using the threshold 1.25. The final scores average eligible frame-level metrics. Predicted invalid depths on valid ground-truth pixels cause a failure rather than a reduction in evaluation support. We also report scores restricted to views without supplied depth. 

Trajectory alignment. The ATE values accompanying the prior-utilization experiments use Sim(3) alignment and measure trajectory shape up to scale. They are not evidence of metric-scale accuracy. Metric evaluation instead uses rigid SE(3) alignment, without fitting a scale to test ground truth. 

Metric reconstruction. Metric depth is evaluated on the same ground-truth-valid support as aligned depth, but no median alignment is applied. ATE-SE(3) is the root-mean-square camera-center error after fitting rotation and translation only. The reported value averages window-level errors. C3VD trajectory errors are converted from meters to millimeters only for display. For the point-norm scale statistic, each ground-truth and predicted reconstruction is expressed in its respective first-camera frame. We average point distances over the common valid support and compute their absolute relative difference. VPGGT points are obtained from predicted Z-depth, predicted intrinsics, and predicted poses. MapAnything uses its predicted local geometry and camera poses. This evaluation adopts the scale statistic, but not the datasets or view-sampling protocol, of the original MapAnything benchmark.

Raw depth priors. This work uses dataset depth references without additional corruption. Ex-tending the model to raw depth observations requires studying sensor-specific noise, missing values, and systematic artifacts. A dedicated depth-processing front end is one possible extension, but is not evaluated here. 

Additional sensing modalities. Other sensors may contribute through upstream estimators that produce camera or depth observations. Such integration requires appropriate calibration, coordinate conversion, and treatment of uncertainty. The current geometric interface does not by itself establish robustness to arbitrary sensor error distributions. 

Prior reliability. Detecting and rejecting unreliable priors is an important direction. The present model does not include an explicit reliability gate, and its results do not establish safe fallback behavior under severe prior corruption. 

Vision dropout. The current architecture requires images. Training and evaluating missing or degraded visual inputs would require additional design and supervision. Sensor-only inference and fallback to odometry are not demonstrated in this work. 

Streaming reconstruction. Extending window-level prior conditioning to streaming reconstruc-tion may enable longer-term geometric constraints. Maintaining a consistent state across windows and incorporating loop-closure information remain open integration problems. 21
