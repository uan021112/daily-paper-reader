ABSTRACT
We introduce ReDT, a feed-forward visual geometry transformer designed to
scale multi-view reconstruction to high-definition (HD) inputs. Existing visual
geometry transformers are typically trained at fixed, standard definition (SD)
resolutions and exhibit degraded reconstruction quality and poor computational
scaling to high input resolutions. While recent efficient architectures reduce the
complexity of inter-frame attention, expensive intra-frame and encoder attention
remain largely unaddressed, making HD reconstruction impractical with existing
architectures. ReDT addresses both limitations with a resolution-aware architecture
designed to scale to high-resolution inputs. The key idea of ReDT is to first map
each view to a fixed-size set of basis tokens, compute correspondences in this
compact constant-size basis space, and then project the outputs back to the original
resolution for dense prediction. By decoupling the internal representation from
input resolution during attention, ReDT substantially reduces the computational
cost of high-resolution 3D reconstruction and improves model performance at
all resolutions. Extensive experiments on multiple datasets spanning SD to FHD
(2K) show that ReDT outperforms state-of-the-art visual geometry transformers
in both reconstruction quality and computational efficiency. For instance, on the

Introduction
Multi-view reconstruction is a long-standing problem in computer vision, with broad applications
in virtual reality [25], robotics [5], and autonomous driving [30]. Traditional approaches, such
as Structure-from-Motion (SfM) [17] and Multi-View Stereo (MVS) [18], address this problem
through iterative pipelines that scale poorly with the number of input views. Recently, end-to-end
approaches such as VGGT and its successors have demonstrated the effectiveness of feed-forward
models for reconstructing 3D structures in a single forward pass [23, 24, 15, 27, 14]. Recent efficient
variants have further improved the scalability of these models with respect to view count by reducing
the complexity of inter-view (global) attention from quadratic to linear using test-time training
(TTT) [4, 11].
Despite these advances, a critical limitation persists: existing visual geometry transformers suffer
significant degradation in reconstruction quality on HD inputs, while their computational cost
grows quadratically with input resolution (Fig. 2). We argue that the bottleneck lies in the attention
mechanism, which becomes increasingly inefficient and ineffective as input resolution grows. In
particular, existing models mostly employ scaled dot-product attention (SDPA), whose computational
cost grows quadratically with the number of tokens, making it increasingly expensive at higher
input resolutions. Moreover, our experiments show that SDPA in visual geometry transformers
learns spatial relationships that depend on the training resolution, limiting generalization to higher
resolutions (Fig. 2c). Consequently, they not only become substantially slower as input resolution
increases, but also suffer from degraded reconstruction quality at high resolutions, failing to fully
exploit the richer spatial information provided by higher-resolution inputs.
To address this limitation, we introduce ReDT, a feed-forward visual geometry transformer designed
to scale multi-view 3D reconstruction to high-resolution inputs efficiently and effectively. Intuitively,
higher-resolution inputs provide finer spatial detail that can improve reconstruction quality, but
naively increasing the input resolution can lead to prohibitive computational costs due to the quadratic
growth of standard attention mechanisms. Through our empirical analysis (Fig. 3), we observe
that intermediate per-view representations in visual geometry transformers exhibit a nearly constant
effective rank as both input resolution and view count increase. This motivates us to use a fixed-size
set of latent basis tokens per view to efficiently capture high-resolution spatial information without
incurring the quadratic computational cost of SDPA. To this end, ReDT replaces the self-attention
blocks in the encoder and aggregator with our Discretization-Invariant Visual Attention (DIVA)
blocks. Specifically, DIVA projects patch-wise image features onto a compact set of basis tokens
using a density-aware learned projection. Cross-view correspondences are then computed over
this resolution-stable latent representation using test-time training before the resulting features are
projected back to the patch domain for decoding.
This design provides two key advantages. First, ReDT is substantially more robust to changes in
input resolution than existing 3D reconstruction models. We attribute this robustness to its attention
formulation, which uses quadrature-aware basis projections to approximate the same continuous
operator across different input resolutions. This enables ReDT to be trained at lower resolutions
and subsequently scaled to higher-resolution inputs without additional training. Second, the size
of its intermediate representation is independent of input resolution, avoiding the quadratic growth
in attention cost that typically accompanies higher-resolution inputs (as illustrated in Fig. 1). This
allows ReDT to process 100 frames up to FHD (2K) input resolutions with a maximum GPU memory
of 61 GB and processing time of 8.2 s. Through extensive experiments across nine datasets, we show
that ReDT achieves state-of-the-art performance on both HD and conventional SD benchmarks.
In summary, our contributions are as follows:
• We systematically study the effect of resolution on visual geometry transformers and show
that they suffer from performance degradations and poor scaling at higher resolutions.

Related Work
2.1
Multi-View Reconstruction
Reconstructing 3D scene geometry from image collections is a long-standing problem in computer
vision. Recent work has introduced feed-forward models that directly infer 3D geometry from image
inputs. DUSt3R [26] predicts dense point maps from image pairs in a feed-forward manner, while
VGGT [23] extends this paradigm to unordered multi-view inputs using a Visual Geometry Grounded
Transformer. Subsequent methods, including DepthAnything3 [15], π3 [27], MapAnything [14], and
VGGT-Ω[24], further advance feed-forward multi-view 3D reconstruction with improved recon-
struction quality and generalization. Despite this progress, visual geometry transformers are still
predominantly trained and evaluated at fixed, relatively low resolutions, and become increasingly
expensive as image resolution grows. This is largely due to the self-attention layers used in the
image encoder and alternating attention blocks, whose cost scales quadratically with token count.
More recently, HD-VGGT [2] attempts to address the challenge of high-resolution inputs by selec-
tively extracting high-resolution features, but it still incurs substantial computational cost, does not
fully resolve the scalability issue, and is evaluated mostly under standard-resolution settings. In
contrast, ReDT reduces attention complexity to linear in the number of views and near-constant with
respect to input resolution, while improving reconstruction quality for both SD and HD inputs under
comprehensive evaluation settings.
2.2
Efficient Multi-View Reconstruction
A major limitation of the conventional visual geometry transformers is the quadratic cost of attention
with respect to token count. Recent efforts address this limitation along two distinct directions. The
first direction focuses on reducing the number of tokens processed by attention. This can be achieved
by merging redundant tokens [20], reducing the key-value set [28], learning compact representative
tokens [8], etc. A second line of work has instead modified the attention mechanism itself, replac-
ing quadratic attention with more efficient variants, such as the test-time training (TTT) used by
VGGT3 [4] and ZipMap [11], which scale linearly with token count. While these approaches sub-
stantially improve efficiency, they mostly target global attention, leaving other resolution-dependent
self-attention mechanisms, such as frame attention and encoder attention, unchanged. As a result,
they scale efficiently with the number of views but not with input resolution. In contrast, ReDT targets
scalability in terms of both view count and view resolution by combining architectural acceleration
with token reduction. ReDT pairs TTT-based global reasoning with compression into a fixed-size
set of basis tokens per view, reducing the number of tokens processed throughout the network while
maintaining linear complexity with respect to the input token count. This design lowers the cost of
resolution-dependent self-attention while maintaining or improving reconstruction quality across
both SD and HD inputs.
2.3
Resolution-Aware Attention
Standard Transformers typically operate on a fixed discretization and therefore generalize poorly
when the sampling resolution at test time differs from that used during training. Recent work
addresses this limitation through discretization-invariant attention operators [1, 6, 29, 31, 12], which
model mappings between continuous functions rather than mappings tied to specific sample points.
More efficient variants use either fixed bases, such as Fourier modes [6], or learned latent functional
representations, as in Functional Attention [29] and LRSA [31]. However, directly applying these
techniques to visual geometry tasks is non-trivial due to the domain mismatch between 2D images
and 3D representations where 3D representations need to capture 3D structure that is not explicitly
present in the 2D input. ReDT shares the merit of discretization-invariant attention and proposes a

ReDT: resolution-Aware Visual Geometry Transformer
3.1
Preliminaries: Visual Geometry Transformers
We begin with a brief overview of visual geometry transformer architectures. ReDT builds on the
VGGT-family models [23, 24]; therefore, we focus our discussion on their underlying architecture.
VGGT [23] takes as input an ordered collection of 2D images and encodes each view using a DINO
image encoder. The resulting patch tokens are augmented with a camera token and a set of register
tokens, and are then processed by a sequence of alternating attention blocks. Alternating attention
consists of 24 layers of repeating intra-frame and inter-frame attention. Intra-frame attention (Frame
Attention) operates independently over the tokens within each view, while inter-frame attention
(Global Attention) enables information exchange across tokens from all views. Finally, the resulting
representations are passed to dense-prediction and camera heads to predict 3D scene geometry.
VGGT-Ω[24] is an improved variant of VGGT that simplifies the original architecture and enhances
its efficiency and scalability. In particular, it introduces register attention to enable efficient inter-
frame information exchange through a compact set of register tokens, while adopting a unified dense
prediction head. In this work, we primarily present empirical analysis and develop architectural
improvements based on VGGT-Ω, while the proposed approach is generally applicable to the broader
VGGT-family models.
3.2
Analysis of Scalability Bottlenecks and Resolution Sensitivity
Scalability Bottlenecks With Visual Geometry Transformers.
Feed-forward visual geometry
transformers, such as VGGT, rely on an alternating attention mechanism that supports cross-view
reasoning with Global Attention and within-view refinement with Frame Attention. While effective,
this design introduces a large number of self-attention blocks, each of which scales quadratically
with input resolution, making inference prohibitively slow and memory-intensive for HD inputs. As
shown in Fig. 2(a–b), increasing the input resolution from HD (1K) to QHD (4K) leads to a quadratic
increase in compute. Even more efficient models that replace Global Attention with TTT, such as
ZipMap, still exhibit quadratic growth in runtime and memory consumption, as their image encoders
and Frame Attention blocks continue to rely on scaled dot-product attention (SDPA). Moreover,
these attention mechanisms are not resolution-aware, making current visual geometry transformers
inherently sensitive to changes in view resolution. As shown in Fig. 2(c), although increasing
the resolution beyond HD (1K) provides richer spatial information and would ideally maintain or
improve reconstruction performance, the performance of VGGT-Ωand ZipMap instead progressively
degrades. We attribute this behavior to attention dilution, where attention becomes more dispersed
across the larger set of candidate matches introduced at higher image resolutions. Even if the correct
match retains the highest score, many weak matches can collectively receive a substantial share of
attention. Aggregating information from these less relevant locations can weaken the signal from the
correct match and destabilize learned correspondences. Together, these limitations motivate attention

Resolution Sensitivity and Effective Rank.
A natural follow-up to Fig. 2(c) is whether the
observed degradation requires additional high-resolution image content. To investigate this, we
downsample each image to the base resolution and then upsample it to 1K, 2K, and 4K. This
changes the image sampling and resulting token representations without introducing additional
source detail. As shown in Fig. 3(a), ZipMap and VGGT-Ωprogressively degrade, closely matching
their behavior on native high-resolution inputs. These results show that changes in image sampling
can adversely affect reconstruction even when no additional scene information is available. We
further examine this sensitivity in feature space using the mean one-dimensional Wasserstein distance
across principal components relative to the base-resolution features. Fig. 3(b) shows larger feature-
distribution differences at higher resolutions, with greater differences in later VGGT-Ωaggregator
layers. Together, these observations motivate an architecture that reduces the dependence of geometric
reasoning on the spatial discretization of its inputs by providing the models a mechanism to decouple
the resolution from the representation. Fig. 3(c) provides a complementary motivation: intermediate
representations remain compact despite substantial increases in spatial token count. We perform
per-view SVD on intermediate VGGT-Ωfeatures and measure the number of components required to
explain 99% of their energy. Across the examined datasets, resolutions, and view counts, this effective
rank remains nearly constant for DINO, frame-attention, and global-attention features. This empirical
regularity motivates a fixed-size latent representation for feature interactions, while retaining spatial
representations for dense prediction.
Summary.
Our analysis identifies increasing computational cost and sensitivity to changes in image
sampling as two challenges when scaling visual geometry transformers to high-resolution inputs.
Meanwhile, the approximately stable effective rank of intermediate features suggests that compact
representations can support efficient feature interactions. These findings motivate ReDT’s separation
of spatial token count from the per-view latent interaction budget. Its area-aware aggregation
additionally accounts for the unequal spatial support of tokens produced by adaptive patch merging.
3.3
ReDT Model Architecture
Overview.
We introduce ReDT, a resolution-aware visual geometry transformer for efficient HD
reconstruction. We begin by summarizing its three key architectural changes over existing visual
geometry transformers (see Fig. 4). First, ReDT uses a variable-patch-size encoder that adaptively
merges nearby low-entropy patches in a quadtree-like manner, reducing spatial redundancy and
producing a compact set of variable-size tokens. Second, it replaces standard VGGT-Ωattention
with our discretization invariant attention (DIVA) blocks, which compresses features into K input-
dependent basis tokens, performs correspondence reasoning in this compact space, and projects them
back to the original resolution. Finally, DIVA replaces the remaining correspondence mechanisms in
the encoder and aggregator with test-time training (TTT). Together, these changes improve robustness
to resolution shifts while substantially reducing memory and runtime. As illustrated in Fig. 2(a–b),
the run-time complexity of ReDT remains nearly constant as input resolution increases. Peak memory
grows slightly because higher-resolution images must still occupy memory, while runtime remains

Variable Patch Size Encoder.
ReDT employs a variable-patch-size tokenizer and encoder to reduce
spatial token count while preserving fine-grained information. Given an input image I, we first extract
features on the encoder’s native patch grid and compute an image-space entropy score for each patch.
A quadtree then adaptively determines the spatial resolution by recursively merging neighboring
low-entropy patches into larger 2×, 4×, and 8× patch regions, while high-entropy regions retain
the native patch resolution. The corresponding features are aggregated according to this partition,
producing a variable-length token sequence that allocates more tokens to information-dense regions
and fewer to locally redundant regions before ViT encoding.
Discretization Invariant Attention Blocks.
ReDT replaces all attention blocks within the VGGT-
Ω’s image encoder and backbone with DIVA blocks. We adopt the term discretization-invariant
following established usage in the literature [1, 29, 31]. For attention formulations such as DIVA,
the intended notion is asymptotic discretization consistency, which does not require exact equality
across finite resolutions. At each transformer layer and attention head, with the exception of register-
attention blocks, DIVA constructs token-to-basis assignment operators that map N spatial tokens to
K latent basis vectors. Specifically, following the query and key/value projections (fQ and fKV ), we
predict assignment matrices AQ, AKV ∈RN×K:
AQ = softmaxK(fQ(Q)) ,

Experiments
We evaluate ReDT on point-cloud reconstruction using summed Chamfer Distance (CD), camera-
pose estimation using Relative Translation Accuracy (RTA), Relative Rotation Accuracy (RRA), and
AUC@30◦, and depth estimation using Absolute Relative Error (AbsRel) and threshold accuracy
(δ < 1.25), following Wang et al. [23], Shen et al. [20], Wang et al. [24]. We also evaluate
ReDT, which applies ReDT to the original VGGT backbone for direct comparison with FastVGGT,
FlashVGGT, and ZipMap. Our HD benchmark includes seven datasets spanning approximately
1K-8K resolution to assess reconstruction quality and scalability. Given the limited coverage of
high-resolution 3D reconstruction in literature, we also evaluate on ScanNetv2 and 7-Scenes for
comparison with prior work. Further experimental details are in the appendix.

Comparison on Standard Sub-HD Benchmarks
While our primary focus is high-resolution reconstruction, we additionally evaluate both ReDT
variants on standard sub-HD benchmarks to facilitate comparison with prior work. Specifically, we
assess reconstruction quality and runtime on ScanNet [3] and 7-Scenes [21], noting that ScanNet is
distinct from ScanNet++. Table 3 shows that both variants achieve competitive reconstruction quality
with consistently low inference time across sequence lengths and sampling densities. With the VGGT
backbone, ReDT preserves reconstruction quality on ScanNet while substantially reducing runtime,
achieving speedups of up to 12.7×. It also consistently achieves lower Chamfer Distance (CD) than
ZipMap while matching or reducing its runtime. These results demonstrate that the benefits of our
approach extend to standard sub-HD benchmarks with the VGGT backbone, particularly as sequence
length increases. Our primary model, ReDT-Ω, improves both reconstruction quality and runtime
over its VGGT-Ωbackbone on ScanNet, and consistently outperforms ZipMap in both measures. On
7-Scenes, both ReDT variants achieve the lowest reported CD (0.032) under sparse sampling and
closely approach the best reconstruction quality under dense sampling, with substantially shorter
runtimes than π3, the most accurate method in the dense setting. Although FlashVGGT retains a small
runtime advantage on ScanNet-100 and sparse 7-Scenes, both ReDT variants are faster on ScanNet-
300, ScanNet-500, and dense 7-Scenes. Together, these results show that our approach remains
effective beyond high-resolution reconstruction, combining competitive accuracy with favorable
runtime scaling across both backbones.

4.3
Ablation Study
Component Ablation.
We evaluate the effectiveness of the adaptive tokenizer, basis projection, and
test-time training by removing each component individually from the full ReDT-Ωmodel. Table 4
reports results on ETH3D at 1K, 2K, and 4K resolutions. The full model achieves the best performance
across all metrics and resolutions. Removing the adaptive tokenizer consistently degrades accuracy,
with the largest increase in AbsRel at 4K, from 0.0425 to 0.0943, highlighting its importance for
high-resolution inputs. Removing basis projection produces the largest degradation at 1K and 2K
in both CD and AbsRel and leads to an out-of-memory failure at 4K, demonstrating its importance
for both accuracy and memory efficiency. Finally, removing test-time training modestly increases
CD and AbsRel but reduces AUC@30 by 4.16-4.52 points across resolutions, indicating a consistent
benefit from test-time adaptation.
Basis Count Ablation.
We investigate the sensitivity of ReDT to the number of basis elements in
the encoder and aggregator. Starting from the default configuration of Kenc = 512 and Kagg = 64,
we vary each count independently while keeping the other fixed. For the encoder, we reduce Kenc to
256 to assess whether a more compact basis preserves reconstruction quality. For the aggregator, we
increase Kagg to 128 to evaluate whether additional basis capacity improves performance. As shown
in Table 5, reducing the encoder basis count increases CD from 0.1137 to 0.3737 and decreases
AUC@30 from 86.09 to 36.18, while AbsRel increases slightly from 0.0356 to 0.0367. Increasing
the aggregator basis count also degrades all three metrics, yielding a CD of 0.1420, an AbsRel of
0.0430, and an AUC@30 of 81.79. These results support the default configuration, which achieves
the best performance across all three metrics among the tested settings, and indicate that increasing
aggregator capacity alone does not necessarily improve reconstruction quality.
5
Conclusion
In this work, we propose ReDT for efficient and robust multi-view 3D reconstruction from high-
definition inputs. Our approach is motivated by VGGT’s poor scaling with input resolution and the
nearly constant effective rank of its intermediate representations. ReDT exploits this structure by
projecting backbone features into a fixed-size basis space for efficient geometric reasoning. Attention
is then computed through test-time training in this compact space before the outputs are mapped back
to the full token space. Extensive experiments on standard-definition (SD) and high-definition (HD)
benchmarks demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach. The VGGT-based ReDT variant improves
reconstruction accuracy and efficiency over competing VGGT-based methods, while our primary
model, ReDT-Ω, achieves the best overall HD reconstruction quality and lowest computational cost
among evaluated methods, alongside competitive reconstruction quality and efficiency on SD inputs.

6.1
Implementation Details
We use a patch size of 16, with descriptor budgets of 512 and 64 tokens for the image encoder and
global aggregation module, respectively. The camera and depth prediction heads are enabled, while
the alignment head is disabled. All model computation is performed in BF16. For global test-time
training (TTT), we perform five internal Muon optimization steps during each forward pass using a
TTT learning rate of 0.01.
We fine-tune all benchmarked models from their publicly available pretrained checkpoints within a
unified multi-view geometry training framework, using a mixture of synthetic and real-world datasets.
Training follows a mixed-resolution, mixed-view sampling strategy, with each distributed batch drawn
from either high-definition (HD) inputs with 2–4 views or standard-definition (SD) inputs with 10–20
views. The number of input views and image aspect ratio are randomly sampled within the selected
regime. Images are resized to the target resolution, with camera intrinsics adjusted accordingly, and
standard photometric augmentations are applied. This strategy exposes the models to both fine spatial
detail and longer multi-view sequences, supporting generalization to HD inputs while maintaining
long-view reasoning.
All methods are trained for 10 epochs using AdamW with a base learning rate of 1 × 10−5, β =
(0.9, 0.95), and a weight decay of 0.05. The pretrained image encoder uses a reduced learning rate of
5 × 10−7. Learning rates increase linearly during the first epoch and subsequently decay according
to a cosine schedule. Gradients are accumulated over six micro-batches and clipped to a global norm
of 1.0 before each optimizer update. Training uses distributed data parallelism and BF16 mixed
precision, with activation checkpointing enabled where applicable to reduce memory consumption.
Predictions and ground truth are expressed relative to the first input camera and independently
normalized by their respective multi-view scene scales, making the training objective largely invariant
to the global coordinate frame and metric scale. Model forward passes run under autocast, while
geometric transformations, normalization, and loss computation are performed in FP32 for numerical
stability.
6.2
Training Losses
We supervise both global scene geometry and local surface structure during training. The overall
objective is shown below, where λo controls the strength of the orthonormal regularization:
L = Lgeometry + λoLorthonormal.
(1)
Geometry Loss.
The geometry loss jointly supervises the predicted 3D structure, depth, camera
pose, and local surface geometry. Specifically, we optimize losses on the predicted 3D points in
each camera coordinate system (cam_pts3d), their z-coordinate (depth_z), relative camera rotation
(pose_quats), and scale-normalized relative camera translation (pose_trans). For quaternion
supervision, we account for the sign ambiguity by taking the minimum error with respect to q and
−q, since both represent the same rotation.
Surface Geometry Loss.
We additionally supervise local geometric consistency using normal and
depth-gradient losses. The normal loss (normal) measures the angular discrepancy between surface
normals constructed from neighboring predicted and ground-truth 3D points. The gradient-matching
loss (gradient_matching) compares log-depth gradients at multiple spatial scales, using strides 1,
2, 4, and 8, encouraging the model to preserve both fine- and coarse-scale depth structure.

6.6
Benchmarking Details
Baselines.
In this study, we considered two general classes of 3D reconstruction models: base
foundation models and training-free efficient models. The base foundation models include VGGT [23],
VGGT-Omega [24], π3 [27], and DepthAnything3 [15]. The efficient variants are all derived from the
base VGGT backbone foundation models include FastVGGT [20], FlashVGGT [28], and ZipMap [11].
Where applicable, we used the hyperparameter settings recommended by the original authors. All
baselines (including efficient variants) were fine-tuned from their publicly available checkpoints
using the same training protocol as ReDT, with early stopping based on validation loss, to ensure a
fair and consistent comparison.
Benchmark Codebase.
Our benchmarking is adapted from MapAnything’s publicly available
benchmark code. The benchmark has been adapted for additional dataset processing and efficient
ground truth postprocessing. Additionally, timing code was adapted from FastVGGT code base.
Datasets.
We evaluate across a diverse set of real and synthetic multi-view benchmarks spanning
indoor and outdoor scenes, static and dynamic content, and a wide range of native image resolutions.
DynamicReplica provides synthetic dynamic indoor scenes with dense depth supervision at 1280 ×
720, while SAIL-VOS3D evaluates dynamic and cluttered indoor/outdoor sequences at 1280 × 800.
MVS-Synth and Spring provide synthetic FHD sequences at 1920 × 1080, covering urban multi-view
stereo and dynamic stereo settings, respectively. ScanNet++ evaluates real high-fidelity indoor
reconstruction using registered imagery and laser/mesh geometry at 1752 × 1168. To assess higher-
resolution generalization, we additionally use UnrealStereo4K, which provides synthetic 4K stereo
imagery at 3840×2160, and ETH3D, which contains real high-resolution indoor and outdoor imagery
with laser-scanner ground truth at resolutions exceeding 4K.

6.7
Qualitative Outputs
We compare ReDT with VGGT, VGGT-Ω, and ZipMap on static HD sequences from ETH3D
and dynamic HD sequences captured from Marvel’s Spider-Man 2 and Marvel’s Spider-Man Re-
mastered [10], evaluating depth maps, camera trajectories, and reconstructed point clouds. The
Spider-Man sequences are captured at HD resolution and sampled at 10 FPS. On ETH3D, all meth-
ods recover similar overall scene layouts and camera trajectories. ReDT preserves distinct depth
boundaries around the foreground furniture and background windows, while producing a coherent
reconstruction of the courtyard and building façade. These results demonstrate that ReDT maintains
reconstruction quality in static scenes.
The differences are more pronounced in the dynamic Spider-Man sequence. VGGT produces
inconsistent depth within the moving character, an irregular camera trajectory, and a severely dispersed
point cloud. ZipMap separates the character from the background more clearly, but loses the thin web
strand and exhibits local irregularities in its trajectory. ReDT preserves both the character silhouette
and the web strand, together with sharper background depth transitions. Its smooth camera trajectory
and well-organized building geometry are visually comparable to VGGT-Ω, with clearer recovery
of fine foreground detail and the recessed background region in the displayed depth map. Together,
these observations suggest that ReDT handles dynamic foreground content effectively while retaining
coherent background geometry and camera motion estimates.
