Generating 3D-consistent novel views from 2D diffusion models remains a fundamental challenge
in computer vision. In this work, we introduce a framework that enhances geometric alignment for
monocular view synthesis under drastic camera changes by jointly generating a target view and its
corresponding depth map. By conditioning the diffusion pipeline on the single source image, source
depth, and target camera rays, our approach forces the model to inherently reason in 3D space
even under extreme viewpoint shifts. To guarantee spatial fidelity, we propose a novel per-pixel
depth consistency constraint, which verifies and enforces 3D consistency across generated views. Our
framework establishes a robust pipeline for geometrically consistent view synthesis, yielding superior
visual accuracy and spatial alignment compared to current state-of-the-art methods. With this work,
we pave the road towards robust 3D world models capable of understanding complex environments
from a single view image.

Introduction
Monocular novel view synthesis (NVS) is a fundamental yet highly challenging problem in computer vision,
requiring the generation of unseen views of a 3D scene from only a single 2D image. This setting is inherently
ambiguous: a single observation provides limited evidence about scene depth, object geometry, and occluded re-
gions, making accurate 3D reasoning difficult without additional geometric constraints. This difficulty increases
with extreme viewpoint changes, where local pixel interpolation is insufficient. Because large portions of the
target view may be completely unobserved in the source image, prediction becomes an ill-posed extrapolation
problem that requires the model to synthesize both scene structure and appearance. To bridge this gap, recent
approaches have increasingly leveraged diffusion models (Ho et al., 2020; Rombach et al., 2021; Lipman et al.,
2023), whose strong generative priors provide plausible visual completions when direct image evidence is missing.
However, relying primarily on generative priors introduces a fundamental tension between photorealistic syn-
thesis and geometric validity. A prevalent strategy for addressing this tension follows a “geometry-warping and
2D-completion” pipeline: pixels or features from the source view are first projected into the target view using es-
timated scene geometry, and a 2D diffusion model is then employed to complete the newly exposed regions (Ren
et al., 2025; Lin et al., 2026; Huang et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2026; Rahary et al., 2026; Cao et al., 2024; Yu et al.,
2024). Although depth-aware warping provides a geometrically grounded initialization, the central challenge of
NVS remains in the regions it cannot resolve: projection transfers only visible surfaces and cannot recover con-
tent occluded in the source view. A 2D completion model must therefore infer target-view visibility, depth dis-
continuities, and severe distortions under large camera shifts without explicit 3D awareness. Treating these am-
biguous regions as ordinary missing pixels provides no spatial constraints, often yielding locally plausible but ge-
ometrically invalid structures with broken occlusion boundaries, inconsistent depth, and severe floating artifacts.
Escaping these geometric ambiguities requires reasoning beyond the 2D canvas. Rather than relying on purely

Related Work
Approaches to Novel View Synthesis. Recent NVS approaches can be broadly categorized into feed-forward
regression models and diffusion-based generative architectures. Feed-forward regressors, such as OVIE (Rahary
et al., 2026) and LagerNVS (Szymanowicz et al., 2026), predict the target view in a single network pass,
prioritizing inference speed at the cost of photorealism and fine-grained texture.
Conversely, diffusion-
based approaches achieve superior visual fidelity through iterative denoising. Notable examples include
PhotoNVS (Yu et al., 2023), a pixel-space DDPM requiring extensive denoising steps, and VIVID (Elata
et al., 2024), a latent diffusion model that utilizes classifier-free guidance. To tackle higher resolutions and
multi-view consistency, GLD (Jang et al., 2026) further advances this paradigm with a multi-stage cascade
diffusion design. Our work builds on the generative paradigm.
Depth-Aware and Ray-Conditioned Synthesis. Recent advances in NVS highlight the critical role of explicit
depth priors and precise camera conditioning for geometric fidelity- see e.g., Sharp (Mescheder et al., 2026),
Flash3D (Szymanowicz et al., 2025) and Voyager (Huang et al., 2025). Among recent 3D Gaussian Splatting
frameworks (Kerbl et al., 2023; Mescheder et al., 2026; Szymanowicz et al., 2025), depth estimation is heavily
leveraged to explicitly place or initialize Gaussian primitives along target camera rays, effectively anchoring
the rendering process in physical space. We adopt the same geometric principle within a diffusion backbone.
In particular, we conceptualize NVS as a ray-conditioned generative process anchored at specific depths,
which allows us to enforce rigorous constraints such as our Projection Consistency Constraint (PCC)-ensuring
that the generated textures adhere strictly to the underlying 3D structure. To this end, we build on the
Qwen-Edit architecture (Wu et al., 2025). While recent efforts (fal.ai, 2025; dx8152, 2025) have adapted Qwen
for multi-view generation via Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) (Hu et al., 2021) with natural language pose
prompts, such text-based conditioning is inherently ambiguous and lacks geometric precision. We instead

Geometric Foundation Models for 3D Reconstruction. Recent advances in 3D computer vision have shifted from
highly specialized, task-isolated pipelines (Schonberger and Frahm, 2016a; Bhat et al., 2023; Bochkovskii
et al., 2025) toward unified, feed-forward geometric foundation models. Pioneered by architectures like
DUSt3R (Wang et al., 2024b) and MASt3R (Leroy et al., 2024), this paradigm leverages large-scale transformer
backbones to directly infer 3D scene properties from arbitrary image collections without requiring prior
camera calibration. Building upon this paradigm, models such as the Visual Geometry Grounded Transformer
(VGGT) (Wang et al., 2025) and Depth Anything 3 (DA3) (Lin et al., 2025) establish a new state-of-the-art
by enabling the single-pass joint inference of dense depth and camera parameters directly from uncalibrated
viewpoints. Rather than relying on classic multi-view stereo systems, we directly exploit these models.
Specifically, we employ DA3 to extract dense depth maps and target camera poses, supplying the essential
geometric priors required to ground our generative novel view synthesis process.
Novel View Synthesis Evaluation. Standard novel-view synthesis (NVS) evaluation relies on two complementary
metric families to assess generation quality and alignment. First, 2D image-quality metrics-such as PSNR,
SSIM (Wang et al., 2004), LPIPS (Zhang et al., 2018) and FID (Heusel et al., 2017) compare predicted views
against ground-truth renderings (Rahary et al., 2026; Jang et al., 2026; Szymanowicz et al., 2026; Ren et al.,
2025; Elata et al., 2024; Szymanowicz et al., 2025; Yu et al., 2023) by quantifying pixel-level fidelity and
perceptual realism. Second, camera-pose accuracy evaluates spatial alignment by comparing Structure-from-
Motion (SfM) (Schonberger and Frahm, 2016b) poses estimated from generated views against the ground-truth
camera conditioning (He et al., 2024; Song et al., 2026; Yu et al., 2024). Together, these metrics form the
standard NVS benchmarking pipeline, but operate in 2D image space or along camera trajectories rather
than measuring intrinsic 3D coherence. As such, they have fundamental limitations for unconstrained 3D
generation. In particular, 2D metrics require ground-truth views that are often unavailable in practice and
cannot capture cross-view geometric consistency -individually photorealistic yet geometrically incoherent
views can score highly, while perfectly 3D-consistent predictions are penalized for plausible deviations in
occluded regions in the conditioning view. Furthermore, camera-pose metrics only verify alignment to the
camera conditioning prompt, not the actual structural integrity of the generated scene.
3
Method
We argue that evaluating 3D alignment between input and generated novel views must be lifted beyond 2D
image-quality and camera-pose metrics into 3D space. To operationalize this, we tackle the problem from two
complementary angles. First, in Section 3.1, we introduce Projection Consistency Constraint (PCC), a novel
3D consistency objective that evaluates geometric integrity across synthesized views. Then, in Section 3.2, we
present ESC, a geometry-aware diffusion framework that jointly synthesizes RGB and depth conditioned on
Plücker rays and leverages PCC for inference-time Best-of-N selection to enforce 3D coherence.

We present ESC, a geometry-aware diffusion framework for novel-view synthesis that simultaneously predicts
target RGB images and depth maps (Figure 3). We first describe our input representation, followed by our
parameter-efficient adaptation strategy and the overall training and inference pipeline.
Input Representation and Denoising Pipeline.
To perform geometry-aware view synthesis, our framework
ingests three primary conditioning inputs: a reference source image, its corresponding rendered depth map,
and the geometry of the source and target camera parameterized by Plücker ray maps. The framework also
ingests a noise sample representing the target image and depth map. Note that at training time, the noise
sample corresponds to a noisy version of the target groundtruth and depth.
The source image and the rendered depth map are processed and tokenized using Qwen VAE tokenizer (Wu
et al., 2025; Kingma and Welling, 2022). Similarly, the noise sample corresponding to the target image and
6


depth is fed to Qwen VAE. The resulting (source or target) depth tokens are concatenated directly to the end
of the (source or target) image tokens, creating a visual token sequence.
The Plücker rays (Hartley and Zisserman, 2004), which represent the camera poses of both source and target
views, are tokenized and fed to a Ray ControlNet module (Zhang et al., 2023) along with the visual token
sequence to guide the subsequent synthesis process with explicit spatial awareness.
The synthesis process is performed through the Qwen Multimodal-Diffusion Transformer (Qwen MM-DiT)
backbone (Peebles and Xie, 2023; Wu et al., 2025), which learns to perform iterative token denoising under
the combined conditioning of the visual token sequence and Ray ControlNet output.
Parameter-Efficient Adaptation for View Synthesis.
Optimization of the framework is performed through a
Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) (Hu et al., 2021) of the Qwen MM-DiT network and the Ray ControlNet
network. Specifically, parameter-efficient LoRA adapters are injected into the dual-stream image branch to
align the newly introduced depth tokens with native visual features, while the Ray ControlNet is utilized for
precise Plücker ray conditioning. This targeted configuration provides the necessary expressive capacity to
incorporate geometric and spatial controls without destabilizing the foundation model.

4
Experiments
In this section, we present a comprehensive evaluation of our proposed ESC framework and the Projection
Consistency Constraint (PCC) metric. First, in Section 4.1, we benchmark our novel view synthesis approach
on three complex, real-world datasets (DL3DV, RealEstate10K, and Tanks & Temples), demonstrating that our
geometry-aware ESC models consistently outperform established baselines in both perceptual realism and 3D
consistency. Subsequently, in Section 4.2, we conduct a systematic analysis of the PCC metric itself. Through
controlled geometric perturbation and occlusion experiments, we illustrate how PCC successfully isolates
multi-view geometric integrity, providing a crucial complement to standard 2D perceptual and camera-pose
metrics that often fail to penalize structural hallucinations.

Training details. We optimize our model parameters end-to-end using the AdamW optimizer (Loshchilov and
Hutter, 2017) with weight decay, coupled with a cosine learning-rate schedule and an initial warm-up phase.
To stabilize training and enhance generation quality, we maintain an exponential moving average (EMA) (Ho
et al., 2020; Polyak and Juditsky, 1992) of the trainable parameters, which is subsequently used for all
evaluations. Throughout both stages, diffusion timesteps are sampled from a logit-normal noise distribution.
For real image datasets, our model is trained on a joint mixture of the DL3DV and RE10k training sets and
evaluated on DL3DV, Re10K and T&T. All the training configurations are presented in Table 5.
Metrics. Performance is assessed across four core metric categories: (i) Synthesis Quality, measured via
PSNR and SSIM (Wang et al., 2004); (ii) Perceptual Quality, evaluated using LPIPS (Zhang et al., 2018),
DINO-Sim (Oquab et al., 2024), and DreamSim (Fu et al., 2023); (iii) Pose/View Prompt Consistency,
tracking pose-AUC, RPE-R, and RPE-T (He et al., 2024; Song et al., 2026); and (iv) Geometric Consistency,
assessed using our proposed PCC.
Models. To systematically evaluate the performance of our architecture and its ability to scale to complex,
unconstrained real-life scenes, we train and evaluate two core model configurations. Specifically, ESC-Base
represents our framework, utilizing depth conditioning to generate a single sample per target view. Finally,
ESC-PCC-BoN employs our complete framework with inference-time Best-of-N strategy, which generates
multiple candidate samples and selects the optimal output according to our PCC metric.
Baselines. We benchmark our approach against five baselines: PhotoNVS (Yu et al., 2023), VIVID (Elata
et al., 2024), OVIE (Rahary et al., 2026), GLD (Jang et al., 2026) and LagerNVS (Szymanowicz et al., 2026).
Discussions. Quantitative results are compiled in Table 1 and Table 9. ESC-Base achieves the strongest
LPIPS, DINO-Sim, and DreamSim results across the three evaluated datasets. PCC-based Best-of-N selection
provides additional improvements in these perceptual metrics and lowers PCC. However, GLD and LagerNVS
perform better on several pixel-level reconstruction and pose-consistency metrics. Taken together, these results
indicate that ESC improves perceptual similarity and depth-reprojection consistency, while some competing
approaches retain advantages under conventional reconstruction and pose metrics.
The differences across metrics reflect their complementary evaluation criteria. PSNR and SSIM emphasize
pixel-aligned reconstruction, while the pose metrics evaluate agreement with the target camera; neither
directly measures bidirectional depth-reprojection consistency. We therefore report these metrics alongside
PCC rather than treating any single metric as definitive. The corresponding qualitative comparisons are
provided in Figure 4, Figure 20, and Figure 21.
We attribute ESC’s performance to two complementary components: geometry-aware RGB–depth generation
and PCC-guided inference-time selection.
First, unlike methods that predict only RGB, ESC jointly predicts RGB and depth while conditioning on
Plücker rays through the Ray ControlNet. This design provides explicit geometric signals during generation
and encourages synthesized appearance to remain coupled to the predicted scene structure.
Second, PCC-based Best-of-N selection provides an inference-time mechanism for converting additional
sampling compute into improved depth-reprojection consistency. ESC-PCC-BoN generates N independent
diffusion samples and selects the candidate with the lowest PCC. Relative to the single-sample ESC-Base
setting, this requires approximately N times the denoising compute, plus the comparatively small cost of
PCC evaluation. ESC-PCC-BoN should therefore be interpreted as a test-time scaling result rather than a
compute-matched comparison with single-sample baselines; ESC-Base provides the corresponding one-sample
comparison.
As shown in Table 9, this selection improves both perceptual and geometric metrics. On DL3DV, moving from
ESC-Base to ESC-PCC-BoN reduces LPIPS from 0.4448 to 0.4340, DreamSim from 0.1420 to 0.1402, and
PCC from 0.2448 to 0.2257, while increasing DINO-Sim from 0.8366 to 0.8386. Thus, PCC selection provides
a measurable quality–compute trade-off, although total sampling compute grows approximately linearly with
N.

4.2
PCC vs Standard Evaluation Metrics
A reliable 3D metric must correctly penalize geometric distortions while remaining invariant to purely visual
changes in occluded regions. To prove PCC meets these criteria, we systematically evaluate its performance
against established 2D perceptual and camera-pose metrics. Using controlled synthetic scenes, we demonstrate
that PCC captures the true spatial consistency that standard evaluation protocols fundamentally miss. Unless
stated otherwise, we perform the evaluations on a custom synthetic Blender simulation dataset featuring
simple geometric primitives (Blender Online Community, 2026; Greff et al., 2022).
4.2.1
Evaluating PCC Against 2D Perception Metrics
We design two controlled sets of experiment using synthetic scenes rendered with 15 camera views distributed
on a hemisphere around a scene of objects.

5.1
How Do Frame Gap and Camera Displacement Affect Performance?
Because the DL3DV dataset consists of continuous videos of static scenes, evaluating novel view synthesis
requires sampling discrete source and target frame pairs from a sequence (see Appendix A.4 for exact sampling
configurations). Assuming a relatively uniform camera velocity, the frame gap between the selected views
serves as a proxy for the magnitude of the camera pose baseline. A larger frame gap translates to a more
pronounced geometric shift, significantly increasing the difficulty of the synthesis task. For this ablation, we
evaluate model resilience to baseline changes by varying the frame gap from 5 to 25 frames using a stride of 5.
As plotted in Figure 9, we evaluate the impact of this frame gap across several core metrics: PSNR, SSIM,
LPIPS, DINO-Sim, DreamSim and PCC. We observe a universal degradation in performance across all models
as the frame gap widens. Notably, this performance drop is non-linear; the decline is steepest at narrower
gaps (e.g., moving from 5 to 10 frames) and gradually smooths out as the baseline expands further. This
trajectory and rate of degradation remain remarkably consistent across all evaluated architectures and metrics,
indicating a uniform response to increasing geometric difficulty. Our model outperforms all baselines across
every frame gap setting on all metrics except PSNR and SSIM, where LagerNVS scores highest. Rather
than reflecting superior geometric synthesis, this discrepancy stems from two key factors: (1) Generative
Blurring: As shown qualitatively in Figure 4, Figure 20, and Figure 21, LagerNVS systematically produces
smoother, blurred outputs. Because PSNR and SSIM heavily penalize high-frequency artifacts, they favor
smooth regressions over sharp but slightly misaligned generations. (2) Rigid Pixel Comparisons: In novel view

5.2
Escaping the 2D Cave: Does Depth Conditioning Truly Benefit Novel View Synthesis?
We first evaluate the necessity of depth tracking by contrasting ESC-shadow against our depth-aware variants.
ESC-shadow retains the exact same model architecture and adapter footprint (sharing identical LoRA and
ControlNet structures) as the other variants, but lacks the input and output channels for depth tokens.4
As demonstrated by the quantitative ablation results in Table 2, incorporating explicit depth estimation
yields substantial performance gains. Specifically, our depth-aware models (ESC-base and ESC-PCC-BoN)
consistently outperform ESC-shadow across both perceptual metrics (LPIPS, DINO-Sim, DreamSim) and
geometric consistency (PCC).

6
Conclusion
In this paper, we presented ESC, a geometry-aware diffusion framework designed to overcome the inherent
3D ambiguities of monocular novel view synthesis. By moving beyond pure 2D image-plane inpainting, ESC
explicitly grounds the generative process in 3D space through joint RGB and depth prediction, guided by
Plücker ray conditioning. To strictly enforce geometric fidelity, we introduced the PCC, a novel bidirectional
depth-reprojection mechanism that evaluates the 3D spatial validity of every generated pixel to guarantee
structural agreement between the source and target views. Our extensive evaluations demonstrate that PCC
serves as both a highly robust metric—capturing true multi-view 3D consistency where standard 2D perceptual
and camera-pose metrics fail—and an effective objective for inference-time Best-of-N selection. Ultimately, by
coupling the strong visual priors of modern diffusion models with explicit geometric verification, our framework
successfully escapes the constraints of 2D ambiguity, yielding novel views that are both photorealistic and
rigorously structurally coherent.

A.1
Overview
We train and evaluate ESC and baseline models on a mix of one synthetic and three real-world novel-view
synthesis benchmarks.
• Blender (Blender Online Community, 2026) + Kubric (Greff et al., 2022) (ours, synthetic). A procedurally
generated multi-view dataset of ∼2,000 training scenes and 100 validation scenes. Each scene contains a
small object-centered scene of solid-coloured Cube/Sphere primitives spawned with Kubric and rendered
with Blender from a fixed 15-view yaw×pitch grid.
• DL3DV-10K (Ling et al., 2023). A large in-the-wild capture collection of ∼10K trajectories covering
indoor and outdoor scenes, with COLMAP-derived intrinsics and extrinsics. We use 8,559 scenes of the
training split for finetuning and the dedicated evaluation split for testing.
• RealEstate10K (RE10K) (Zhou et al., 2018). A real-world collection of ∼72K indoor real-estate walk-
through clips with per-frame camera poses. We use a subset of the official train split for finetuning and
the held-out test videos for evaluation.
• Tanks & Temples (T&T) (Knapitsch et al., 2017). An evaluation benchmark of high-resolution outdoor
and indoor captures with COLMAP-aligned poses; we never train on T&T and only use it to probe
out-of-distribution generalization.
The synthetic Blender + Kubric dataset is used for controlled pre-training and ablation studies; the
DL3DV/RE10K mix is used for the main finetuning runs; and all three real-world benchmarks (DL3DV,
RE10K, T&T) are used for quantitative evaluation.
A.2
The Blender + Kubric Synthetic Dataset
Engine stack.
Each scene is procedurally composed by Kubric (Greff et al., 2022) — which spawns the
primitive objects, assigns their materials, and orchestrates the multi-view camera grid and then handed off to
Blender (Blender Online Community, 2026) as the underlying renderer to produce RGB, depth and camera
poses.
Assets.
We deliberately keep the scene content minimal. Each scene contains a small set of solid-coloured
Kubric primitives only: kb.Cube and kb.Sphere instances spawned on a flat ground plane. The number of
objects per scene is small and varies slightly: empirically every scene contains 5 or 6 primitives (mean ¯Nobj≈5.2
across a random sample of 50 scenes). Each primitive is assigned:
• a class drawn uniformly at random from {Cube, Sphere};
• a scale s ∼U(0.8, 1.6);
• a colour cycled through a fixed 8-colour palette (red, green, blue, yellow, pink, cyan, orange, purple);
• a Principled BSDF material with metallic ∼U(0, 1) and roughness ∼U(0.2, 0.8).
By restricting the visible content to low frequencies and providing robust geometric cues, this primitive
representation is ideally suited for diffusion-based NVS training, where every pixel can be directly mapped to
a well-defined analytic surface for depth supervision.
Placement.
Objects are arranged in a ring around the scene origin at azimuth θi = 2πi/Nobj with radius
ri ∼U(1.0, 2.5) m, and sit directly on the ground plane at z = si (so the object base touches z = 0).
Lighting.
Because the dataset uses no HDRI environment, all illumination comes from a single deterministic
light rig: the CLEVR (Johnson et al., 2017) three-point setup, instantiated via kb.assets.utils.get_clevr_-
lights(light_jitter=0.5), plus a uniform ambient term camb = 0.05 on every channel.

A.3
Mixed Real-World Training Dataset
Composition.
The primary fine-tuning runs use a weighted mixture of two real-world datasets: DL3DV and
RE10K. We train on a selected subset of these datasets, with the exact number of scenes reported in Table 3.
The mixture is governed by a single per-source weight wk (Table 3). Operationally, the data loader proceeds
as follows:
1. It converts each source weight into a per-scene weight wk/|Sk|, where |Sk| denotes the number of scenes
available from source k on disk.
2. It passes these per-scene weights to the WeightedSceneGroupedSampler, which samples the next scene
with a probability proportional to its per-scene weight.
Depth and Pose Pre-extraction.
We pre-extract depth maps and camera trajectories (intrinsics and extrinsics)
offline using the DepthAnything-3 (DA3) (Lin et al., 2025) backbone (depth-anything/da3nested-giant-large)
in “video” mode with process_res_method="upper_bound_resize". During preprocessing, we observe
that DA3 estimations exhibit greater stability in multi-view settings than in monocular contexts; specifically,
increasing the sequence length significantly enhances prediction precision and reliability. Motivated by this,

physical disparity range of [dmin, dmax] = [0.02, 2.0].

1. Anchor Selection: We randomly sample a source anchor frame fsrc from the set of frames covered by a
valid DA3 chunk.
2. CandidateFiltering: We construct a valid target candidate set C = {f : |f −fsrc| ∈[gmin, gmax]}. Critically,
this set is strictly restricted to the same chunk as fsrc to prevent mixing inconsistent coordinate frames

3. TargetSampling: The final target frame is drawn uniformly from the candidate set, denoted as ftgt ∼U(C).

B.1
Plücker Ray Map Conditioning
To condition the ControlNet, target camera rays are processed and injected via three steps:
• Plücker Mapping: Source and target rays are back-projected into a 6D Plücker coordinate map.
• Lossless Tokenization: The map is deterministically reshaped into flat geometric tokens, bypassing
learned convolutions.
• Zero-Init Injection: A zero-initialized linear layer projects these tokens to the transformer width. They
are then added element-wise to the image latents, ensuring a stable "no-op" initialization at the start of
training.

C.3
Scale Alignment for PCC
Because the multi-view geometry estimator (i.e DA3 (Lin et al., 2025)) jointly predicts depth and camera
poses from RGB input alone, its outputs are defined only up to a global similarity transformation. Since each
inference step minimizes multi-view photometric and geometric inconsistencies, processing frames from two
different versions of a scene (e.g., a reference and a perturbed variant) forces the model to artificially deform
the recovered geometry to ensure the reconstructions overlap. As a result, the estimator silently absorbs the
true 3D inconsistency into its pose and scale predictions, meaning the final measured residual reflects only the

Computing the PCC requires four primary inputs: the source depth, the target depth, and their respective

Camera Pose Configuration
While an alternative approach would be to utilize the camera poses estimated
directly by DA3, we strictly enforce the use of the dataset’s ground-truth camera poses (derived from either
COLMAP or the simulation environment) during the PCC evaluation. This design choice is critical for
alignment with our core task: since our objective is to evaluate whether the generated output maintains
3D consistency conditioned on a specific camera prompt, we must measure performance against the exact
geometric trajectory requested by that prompt.

real-world, in-the-wild datasets do not, we handle them using two distinct paradigms:

degradation from polluting our evaluation, we only utilize DA3 to estimate the depth of the generated
target view. We then align this estimated target depth with Blender’s ground-truth target depth using
the foreground-background alignment algorithm detailed in Appendix C.3. Figure 14 visualizes the final

Why the test suffices.
Although Eq. (18) is one-sided, applying it across all views systematically detects
both primary error modes in novel view generation. Let vi be a generated view containing a hallucinated
surface H ⊂R3 at pixel ui ∈Ω.
(a) Occlusion Violation (H lies behind the rest of the scene). At view vi, the reprojected depth bDvi(ui)
records the closer, real surface seen by other views, whereas the predicted depth is Dvi(ui) = depth(H).
Because H is behind the real surface, Dvi(ui) > bDvi(ui), triggering a violation directly at view i.
(b) Free-Space Violation (H lies in front of the rest of the scene; a “floater”). At view vi, Dvi(ui) =
depth(H) ≤bDvi(ui), so no violation is raised at i (from vi’s own perspective, the scene appears
self-consistent). However, for any other view vj whose ray passes through H at pixel uj first before
any other points. The z-buffer pulls the reprojected depth bDvj(uj) forward to depth(H), while the
real-scene observation Dvj(uj) corresponds to the true, farther surface. Thus, Dvj(uj) > bDvj(uj), and
the violation is successfully caught at view j.

E.2
How does ESC perform in simulation with ground-truth geometry?
A primary advantage of utilizing our synthetic Blender dataset is that it inherently provides pristine, exact
ground-truth depth maps. Unlike the noisy pseudo-ground-truth depth maps extracted from alternative
3D reconstruction or estimation frameworks, these clean structural priors facilitate exceptionally smooth
optimization dynamics and stable convergence across all model variants.
Furthermore, this controlled
simulation environment grants us the unique flexibility to generate diverse scene variations specifically within
the occluded regions of the original viewpoint. This allows us to explicitly evaluate the network’s generative
completion capabilities under severe disocclusions, with qualitative examples demonstrated in Figure 13.
In this ablation experiment, we explore using the Projection Consistency Constraint (PCC) directly as a
learning objective to fine-tune our model. Initializing from the pre-trained ESC-Base EMA checkpoint,
we incorporate the PCC regularizer (detailed further in Section 3.1) to explicitly enforce strict geometric
alignment across views5. To avoid disrupting the core flow-matching process at high noise levels, this PCC
objective is applied exclusively over a truncated timestep range. This constraint ensures that the geometric
regularization effectively shapes only the later, more structurally resolved stages of denoising.
Overall, all trained model configurations produce highly reasonable and geometrically plausible novel-view
predictions, with the supervised fine-tuning (ESC-PCC-FT) variant consistently delivering the highest visual
and structural fidelity (See Table 11 for the quantitative results and Table 8 for the qualitative results). To
rigorously assess the geometric integrity of these generations, we overlay epipolar lines across the synthesized
views in Figure 24. The precise correspondence of visual features along these epipolar lines demonstrates
that our ControlNet adapter effectively interprets Plücker tokens to generate novel views that conform to the
underlying camera geometry. This alignment highlights the model’s 3D consistency with the original scene.
