# MoRE: Motion-Regularized Encoding for Articulated 3D Generation 

Guantian Zheng 1,3,* , Shan Jin 1,2,* 

> 1

Manifest Limited 2Department of Engineering, University of Cambridge

School of Mechanical and Aerospace Engineering, Nanyang Technological University 

Abstract —Generating an articulated object from a single image requires recovering both its appearance and the mechanism governing its parts. A compact encoding of joint attributes does not by itself ensure that reconstruction preserves the resulting motion. We present MoRE, Motion-Regularized Encoding for articulated 3D generation. MoRE represents coordinate-aligned joint lines with axis classification and two axis-orthogonal Plücker moment coefficients, removing the arbitrary choice of an origin along the axis. During representation learning, decoded joint hypotheses are used to move corresponding three-dimensional anchors, and their motions are compared with those of annotated joints. A differentiable expectation over joint types and axis directions couples attribute reconstruction to the motion it induces. An image-conditioned flow learns the resulting functional latents, while a multi-state-adapted Gaussian model supplies appearance. At inference, recovered part assignments and joints articulate a single set of rest-pose Gaussians without regenerating the object for each state. Experiments on PartNet-Mobility and ACD show stronger part-box agreement, articulated-state geometry and visual similarity than the compared baselines, while joint prediction reduces axis, origin and range errors relative to ArtiLatent. Controlled ablations support the joint-line encoding and show that motion supervision reduces anchor-motion error. Code and pretrained checkpoints will be released in the future. 

Index Terms —articulated objects, 3D generation, motion regu-larization, joint estimation, Gaussian splatting 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Image-conditioned 3D generation has advanced in geometry and appearance, but an articulated object must also move correctly. A door can look plausible while rotating about the wrong hinge; a drawer can reconstruct well in its closed state while translating along an incorrect direction. Recovering static geometry and reconstructing joint attributes are therefore insufficient tests of an articulated representation. Recent methods generate articulated objects through part graphs, meshes and structured latents [1]–[4]. A central chal-lenge is to connect the representation of a joint to the motion it induces. A point on an axis is not unique: moving it along the axis leaves rotation unchanged. A joint-line representation removes this redundancy, but attribute-wise reconstruction still treats errors separately rather than measuring their combined effect on moving geometry. We introduce MoRE, Motion-Regularized Encoding , which learns sparse functional latents under both attribute and analytic motion supervision. a) Image-conditioned 3D generation: Single-image 3D generation learns priors to infer geometry and appearance beyond the visible surfaces. Structured latent models associate local features with spatial support, allowing detailed geometry and appearance to be decoded from a compact representation. TRELLIS [5] uses sparse voxel latents with multiple output decoders, while TRELLIS.2 [6] models geometry and material attributes with O-Voxels. These static representations provide useful spatial and appearance priors, but do not specify the parts and joints needed for articulation. MoRE associates functional arXiv:2610.30337v1 [cs.CV] 21 Oct 2026 attributes with sparse support and connects the resulting parts to Gaussian appearance. 

b) Articulated object generation: NAP [7] models ar-ticulated objects through part geometry and joint relations, and CAGE [8] adds control over articulation structure. SIN-GAPO [1] conditions part attributes on an image and a connectivity graph before retrieving meshes. MeshArt [2] generates meshes hierarchically, while ArtFormer [9] generates tokenized kinematic trees. More recent methods combine pretrained 3D priors with articulation: PAct [4] generates part-decomposed latents and predicts joints from part features; ArtiLatent [3] jointly encodes voxel geometry and joint attributes and adapts Gaussian appearance across states. PhysX-Anything [10] additionally models physical properties, while MonoArt [11] progressively reasons over geometry, parts and motion. MoRE studies motion-regularized encoding: joint-line attributes are supervised together with the analytic motion they induce. Plücker joint representations also appear in NAP [7]; here, a classified coordinate axis determines an orthogonal plane in which two moment coefficients specify the line. 

c) Articulated reconstruction and appearance: Recon-struction methods recover geometry and motion from obser-vations of the same object in different states. PARIS [12] jointly estimates part-level implicit geometry, appearance and motion from multi-view images in two articulation states. ArticulatedGS [13] uses a Gaussian representation and progressive optimization to recover parts and motion from such observations. These approaches exploit observed state changes, whereas single-image generation must infer unobserved structure from learned priors. MoRE uses multi-state observations to adapt its appearance model during training, and associates rest-pose Gaussians with explicit joints through part assignments. III. M ETHOD 

Given an object image I, MoRE generates an articulated representation O = ( V, P, J , G0) comprising sparse support, part assignments, part-level joints and rest-pose Gaussians. The functional stream predicts part and motion attributes; the appearance stream provides renderable geometry and color. Part assignments and joint transforms connect the streams (Figure 2).

b) Axis-orthogonal joint lines: An axis point is not unique: translating it along the axis does not change the joint. For a unit direction ℓ through o, the Plücker moment m = o × ℓ

is independent of this translation and satisfies m ⊥ ℓ. For coordinate-aligned joints, we classify ℓ ∈ { ex, e y , e z } and predict two coefficients: 

m = c1uℓ + c2vℓ, o⊥ = ℓ × m, (1) where (uℓ, v ℓ) span the selected axis’s orthogonal plane and o⊥

is the closest point on the line to the origin. This specifies the joint line without regressing a redundant along-axis coordinate. Reversing a signed axis requires reversing both its moment and joint displacement to preserve the line and motion. The three axes use the orthogonal bases (ey , e z ), (ez , e x) and (ex, e y ),respectively.

The decoder then recovers the functional field on the generated support. 

d) Recovering parts and joints: We first group voxels by predicted semantic class and apply DBSCAN [19] to their bounding-box descriptors within each group. The resulting labels define voxel sets Pk for part instances, determining the part count through clustering. For each part, majority votes select joint type and axis; moments and limits are averaged within the part. The aggregated axis selects the orthogonal basis used to recover its joint line. This yields a joint 

Jk = ( τk, ℓ k, o k, r k) and a single rigid motion shared by the part’s voxels. The embedding-based configuration used in the functional evaluation is specified separately in the experimental setup. 

C. Articulation-aware appearance 

Opening a part exposes interior surfaces absent from a closed-state image. We therefore adapt the pretrained TRELLIS [5] appearance encoder EA and Gaussian decoder DA using multi-view observations across articulation states, a strategy also studied in articulated generation [3]. Offline DINOv2 features [22] are projected and aggregated onto rest-pose voxels, then encoded into appearance latents zA. The decoder predicts 32 Gaussians [23] per active appearance voxel. Each Gaussian inherits its voxel’s part assignment, providing an explicit correspondence between appearance and joint motion. A. Comparison with prior methods 

Table I compares MoRE with prior methods on PartNet-Mobility [26] and ACD [27], using baseline scores reported by PAct [4]. 

a) Single-image generation: MoRE ranks first on four of six metrics on PartNet-Mobility and five on ACD. Compared with PAct, AS-CD decreases from 0.0176 to 0.0092 and from 0.0218 to 0.0145, respectively; CLIP reaches 0.9545 and 0.9401. However, RS-CD remains higher than PAct on both datasets, and PartNet-Mobility AOR is not the best. These results show stronger articulated-state geometry, while rest-state surface reconstruction remains a limitation. 

b) Joint prediction: Table II compares MoRE with the joint results reported by ArtiLatent [3]. MoRE has lower axis, origin and both range errors: axis error decreases from 1.14 ◦

to 1.03 ◦, and origin error from 0.10 to 0.04. Type accuracy is 99.16%, 0.01 percentage points below ArtiLatent’s 99.17%. These results support joint prediction by the complete model; the controlled ablations below further examine encoding and motion supervision.

C. Earlier component measurements 

The attribute-only baseline obtains 96.17% type accuracy, 92.35% axis accuracy, 94.54% movable F1 and latent MSE 0.460 on 144 held-out objects ( n = 3 ,987 movable voxels). Without kinematic-chain attention, the corresponding classifica-tion scores are 96.92%, 93.35% and 95.27%. These image-and-shape-conditioned scores use a supplied part count K; they are not measurements of the new motion regularizer.

E. Limitations 

The motion model treats fixed, revolute and prismatic parts, assumes coordinate-aligned axes and evaluates part motions independently. Training with annotated part groups does not resolve clustering errors or cross-grid assignment errors at inference. Run-to-run variability is not quantified in these evaluations. Density and friction remain stored attributes rather than calibrated physical predictions. V. C ONCLUSION 

We presented MoRE, Motion-Regularized Encoding for ar-ticulated 3D generation. An axis-orthogonal joint-line encoding removes the arbitrary along-axis origin, while a differentiable motion objective compares the behavior of reconstructed joints. Image-conditioned functional latents and part-bound Gaussian appearance support generation followed by explicit articulation. Controlled ablations show that motion supervision reduces anchor-motion error with either encoding, and the combined design gives the lowest errors across all six metrics. On PartNet-Mobility and ACD, MoRE obtains the best articulated-state CD and CLIP scores among the compared methods; joint prediction also yields lower axis, origin and range errors than ArtiLatent, with comparable type accuracy.
