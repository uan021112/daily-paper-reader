# FreeView: Robust 3D Reconstruction from Uncurated Views 

Junho Kim ∗, Hyunyoung Choi ∗, and Seongwon Lee †

Fig. 1: Robust 3D reconstruction from uncurated views. VGGT [1] and π3 [2] process all input views without explicit distractor rejection. RobustVGGT and RobustPi3 [3] enable view filtering but rely on a clean reference and fixed rejection criteria. Our proposed FreeView automatically selects a representative reference and adapts rejection to each collection, preserving useful views while suppressing distractors without manual reference curation. 

Abstract — Reconstructing a coherent 3D scene from uncu-rated image collections remains challenging due to the presence of irrelevant or non-overlapping views. Recent work has shown that feed-forward reconstruction models contain internal view-consistency signals that support distractor rejection. However, reference-conditioned filtering relies on a curated clean refer-ence, which is not guaranteed in practical image collections. To address this limitation, we introduce FreeView, a training-free framework that jointly selects a filtering reference and rejects distractor views from an uncurated collection. FreeView leverages collection-level consensus to identify a representative view that serves as a reliable reference for filtering. It then adapts view rejection to each input collection by combining complementary visual and geometric evidence. This approach preserves useful views under substantial appearance and view-point variations while rejecting distractors. FreeView integrates with both VGGT and π3, removing the need for a manually curated reference or manual calibration of view rejection. Extensive experiments across multiple datasets demonstrate high clean-view retention and distractor rejection rates, leading to reliable 3D reconstruction from uncurated views.

In practical settings, however, images collected across differ-ent capture sessions or retrieved from large databases may contain observations of unrelated scenes. Such collections require a reconstruction system to determine which views support a common scene, while excluding distractors that can compromise the recovered geometry. Recent advances in feed-forward reconstruction [9], [10], [11], [12], [13] have enabled direct prediction of camera poses and scene geometry from multiple images. The Vi-sual Geometry Grounded Transformer (VGGT) [1] jointly predicts camera parameters and dense scene geometry from multiple input views, while π3 [2] introduces permutation-equivariant geometric prediction without a fixed reference view. These models provide efficient foundations for multi-view scene understanding and motivate the use of feed-forward reconstruction beyond carefully prepared image sets. Their application to uncurated collections, however, also requires mechanisms for identifying images that should not participate in the reconstruction. RobustVGGT [3] addresses this challenge by exploiting the view-consistency signals encoded in VGGT’s internal representations, with its RobustPi3 variants extending the approach to π3. These signals provide evidence of a view’s arXiv:2610.33656v1 [cs.CV] 24 Oct 2026 relevance to a given reference, enabling distractor rejection without additional training. This finding demonstrates that pretrained geometric models contain useful information for deciding which observations belong together. However, ap-plying reference-conditioned filtering to uncurated collec-tions requires establishing a trustworthy reference without prior knowledge of which input views belong to the target scene. The filtering reference plays a central role because it determines the scene against which the remaining views are assessed. When a distractor is used as this reference, clean views may appear inconsistent with it, while images of an unrelated scene may receive stronger support. Consequently, an effective relevance signal can still produce an unsuitable selection if it is conditioned on the wrong reference. This dependence arises in view filtering, even when the recon-struction model does not require a fixed reference frame. Thus, π3’s permutation-equivariant geometry prediction does not eliminate the need for a reliable reference when filtering views. In an uncurated collection, this reference must itself be inferred from the relationships among the inputs. A second challenge is balancing distractor rejection with the preservation of useful scene observations. Clean views can differ substantially in appearance and viewpoint, and their relevance may not be equally evident from every visual or geometric cue. An overly restrictive decision can discard valid observations and reduce scene coverage, whereas a permissive decision can retain distractors that degrade recon-struction. Moreover, the degree of agreement among views varies across collections, and different reconstruction back-bones can yield different relevance-score distributions. These considerations motivate a rejection strategy that accounts for the evidence available in each input set while preserving views that remain useful for reconstruction. In this paper, we introduce FreeView, a training-free framework for robust 3D reconstruction without a pre-verified reference view. FreeView addresses reference selec-tion and view rejection within a unified inference pipeline that can be paired with either VGGT or π3. We focus on collections in which the target scene forms a coherent group with stronger local visual support than competing distractor groups. Our approach uses consensus within the collection to establish a representative reference for filtering and adapts the subsequent rejection process to the available visual and geometric evidence. First, we introduce Consensus Reference Selection, which identifies a representative view through the strength of its agreement with other images. The underlying intuition is that clean views tend to reinforce one another through shared scene content, whereas unrelated distractors receive weaker support. By assessing this support before reference-conditioned filtering, FreeView establishes a basis for inter-preting the remaining views without relying on a designated input position. This makes reference selection an explicit part of processing uncurated inputs and removes the need to supply a clean image in advance. Second, we introduce Adaptive Dual-Cue Rejection, which combines visual evidence with geometry-aware evidence from the selected reconstruction backbone to determine which views should be retained. The rejection process adapts to each collection, accommodating differences in appearance, viewpoint, and cross-view agreement.Views supported by either cue are retained, while those unsupported by both are rejected. This allows useful observations to survive filtering even when their relevance is not equally apparent from both cues. The selected views are then passed to the same backbone for final reconstruction. Together, the two components support both VGGT and π3 without additional model training or manual calibration of the rejection process. Experiments across multiple datasets demonstrate high clean-view retention and distractor rejection rates without manual reference selection. These results show that ad-dressing reference selection together with adaptive rejection enables reliable 3D reconstruction from uncurated image collections. A. Feed-Forward 3D Reconstruction 

Recent learning-based approaches [9], [10], [11] have substantially simplified multi-view 3D reconstruction through learned geometric prediction. DUSt3R [14] introduced pointmap regression from image pairs, while MASt3R [15] strengthened learned correspondences and MASt3R-SfM [16] incorporated these representations into a scalable reconstruction pipeline. VGGSfM [17] combined learned visual geometry with classical structure-from-motion components, while VGGT [1] unified camera, depth, and pointmap prediction within a transformer-based feed-forward architecture. π3 [2] introduced permutation-equivariant geometric reasoning, removing dependence on a fixed reference view in geometry prediction. These methods primarily focus on improving geometric prediction and reconstruction architectures. Our work ad-dresses the complementary problem of input-set robustness: given an uncurated collection containing unrelated views, we aim to identify a coherent subset before final reconstruction. In particular, permutation equivariance addresses changes in input ordering, while view curation determines which observations should participate in reconstruction. FreeView provides this curation through a common framework that integrates with both VGGT and π3.

B. Robust View Filtering for 3D Reconstruction 

Robustness to erroneous observations has long been an in-tegral part of classical structure-from-motion pipelines [18]. Conventional systems use image retrieval, local feature matching, geometric verification, robust estimation [19], and view-graph optimization to prevent inconsistent observations from corrupting reconstruction. Although effective, these procedures rely on multiple stages, many of which recent feed-forward reconstruction models bypass. RobustVGGT [3] demonstrated that VGGT implicitly en-codes view-relevance information in its internal attention and feature representations. Its RobustPi3 variants further explored the use of analogous signals in π3. By measuring a view’s relevance to a reference, distractors can be rejected without additional training. However, this filtering process is conditioned on a predefined reference view. When the reference itself is unrelated to the target scene, the resulting relevance scores can favor an unsuitable subset. In addition, a fixed rejection criterion may not adapt well to collection-specific variations in appearance, viewpoint, and view con-sistency. FreeView addresses these limitations by treating reference selection as part of the robust reconstruction problem itself. We first infer a representative anchor from collection-level consensus and then perform adaptive view rejection using complementary visual and geometry-aware cues. The geo-metric evidence is obtained from the selected reconstruc-tion backbone, allowing the same reference-selection and rejection framework to be used with VGGT [1] and π3 [2]. This removes the need for a pre-verified clean reference and manual calibration of the rejection process. 

C. Reference Selection and Visual Retrieval 

Selecting representative or relevant images is closely re-lated to visual place recognition and image retrieval [20]. Classical retrieval methods and recent learned systems such as MegaLoc [21] provide global image representations for identifying visually related views [20], [22], [23], [24]. Self-supervised visual representations such as DINOv2 [25] and DINOv3 [26] likewise provide descriptors that capture visual and semantic similarity. While visual retrieval primarily aims to identify images related to a query, our goal is to establish a reliable filtering reference from a collection containing both relevant and unrelated views. FreeView uses pairwise DINOv2 similarities to estimate collection-level consensus and selects an anchor supported by a coherent group of views. FreeView thus uses visual similarity both to establish the filtering reference and to support view retention alongside geometry-aware evidence. III. M ETHODOLOGY 

A. Preliminaries 

Feed-forward 3D reconstruction. Given a set of N input images I = {Ii}Ni=1 , we consider two representative feed-forward reconstruction models, VGGT [1] and π3 [2]. They jointly predict 

{(Pi, Di, Xi, Ci)}Ni=1 , (1) where Pi denotes the camera parameters, Di the per-pixel depth map, Xi the 3D pointmap, and Ci the corresponding confidence map. VGGT anchors its reconstruction to the coordinate system of the first input camera, making the first view a reconstruction reference. In contrast, π3 adopts permutation-equivariant multi-view reasoning and represents geometry in relative coordinate systems without relying on the first input view as the reconstruction reference. Both models encode cross-view geometric information in their internal representations that can be exploited for view rel-evance estimation. 

Reference-conditioned filtering. RobustVGGT [3] and its 

π3 variants designate one input view as a filtering reference. Given a reference image Ir , they measure the relevance of each remaining view Ij to Ir using internal representations of the corresponding reconstruction backbone and reject views whose relevance falls below a fixed threshold. Accordingly, the retained view set is 

Vr = {Ij ∈ I | s(Ir , I j ) ≥ τ } , (2) where s denotes a reference-conditioned relevance score, τ is a fixed rejection threshold, and Vr denotes the subset retained with respect to Ir . Thus, regardless of the reconstruction backbone, the retained set Vr depends on the choice of filtering reference Ir .

IV. E XPERIMENTS 

A. Experimental Setup 

Datasets and uncurated benchmarks. We evaluate Free-View on three multi-view reconstruction datasets: Photo-tourism [8], On-the-Go [28], and ETH3D [29]. For each dataset, we construct controlled uncurated collections by combining views of a target scene with unrelated views sampled from other scenes. For Phototourism and On-the-Go, each collection contains Nc = 30 clean views and 

Nd ∈ { 10 , 30 , 50 } distractors for the Small , Medium , and 

Large settings, respectively, resulting in 40 , 60 , and 80 input views. For ETH3D, where fewer views are available per scene, we use Nc = 14 clean views and Nd ∈ { 5, 14 , 30 }

distractors, resulting in 19 , 28 , and 44 input views. The clean subset is fixed across noise levels, while the distractors are progressively added such that DSmall ⊂ D Medium ⊂ D Large .

Evaluation protocol. We evaluate four settings: Clean-only , Clean Reference , Distractor Reference , and automatic reference selection with FreeView. Clean-only uses only target-scene views and serves as an oracle setting. For reference-conditioned methods, Clean Reference and Dis-tractor Reference use the same mixed collection and differ only in whether the filtering reference is a target-scene view or a distractor. We evaluate five paired reference choices per target scene. FreeView receives the same mixed collection without a designated reference and selects one automatically. For fixed-threshold retrieval baselines (MegaLoc [21] and DINO [25], [26]), we tune the threshold per dataset on the Clean Reference setting by maximizing the mean of CK and DR, and keep it fixed for Distractor Reference evaluation. 

Evaluation metrics. We evaluate filtering using the clean-view keep rate (CK) and distractor rejection rate (DR), defined as CK = | ˆC ∩ C| /|C| and DR = |D \ ˆC| /|D| ,respectively. Severe rejection of clean views can leave too few camera poses for valid alignment, preventing meaningful ATE evaluation. Even when alignment is possible, ATE and RPE computed only on the retained views can favor methods that discard difficult but valid observations. We therefore report coverage-aware pose recall over all clean ground-truth views, counting rejected views as failures. The retained clean camera poses are aligned to the ground truth using a Umeyama alignment [30]. Translation error is normalized by the scene radius ρ =

B. Experimental Results 

Camera pose estimation. Quantitative results are sum-marized in Table I. (i) VGGT-based methods. With a clean filtering refer-ence, retrieval-based methods and RobustVGGT effectively remove distractors and maintain strong pose recall, showing that reference-conditioned relevance is reliable when the reference belongs to the target scene. However, when the same methods are given a distractor reference, clean-view retention and pose recall collapse across all datasets, despite using the same underlying image collections. This demon-strates that strong visual or geometry-aware relevance alone is insufficient when conditioned on an unreliable reference. FreeView avoids this failure by automatically selecting the filtering reference, achieving consistently strong filtering and camera reconstruction across all noise levels. (ii) π3-based methods. Although π3 itself is permutation-equivariant, RobustPi3 still depends on a designated filtering reference and degrades substantially when a distractor is used as the reference. The consistent improvement on π3 indicates that the benefits of FreeView are not specific to VGGT’s first-view-anchored reconstruction. (iii) Robustness to distractors. Across Phototourism, ETH3D, and On-the-Go, both FreeView variants remain robust as the distractor level increases from Small to Large, while avoiding the catastrophic failures observed with in-correct references. Overall, FreeView provides consistent reference selection and view filtering across both VGGT and 

π3.

Qualitative results. Figure 4 illustrates the effect of ref-erence selection on view filtering and reconstruction. VGGT retains distractor observations, resulting in spurious geome-try, while RobustVGGT substantially improves filtering with a clean reference, although residual views from an unrelated landmark still contaminate the reconstruction. With a dis-tractor reference, RobustVGGT instead rejects valid target views and produces a degraded reconstruction. FreeView automatically selects a target-consistent reference, preserving clean views while suppressing distractors and recovering a coherent reconstruction without manual reference selection. 

C. Ablation and Analysis 

We conduct all ablations under the Large setting to exam-ine the design choices at the highest distractor level in our benchmark. 

Model component design. Table II examines the con-tribution of Consensus Reference Selection and Adaptive Dual-Cue Rejection under the Large-noise setting with a distractor reference. Adding reference selection alone yields a substantial performance recovery across all three datasets, highlighting the effectiveness of collection-level consensus for establishing a reliable filtering anchor. Adaptive rejection then adjusts the rejection criterion to the score distribution of each collection, yielding dataset-dependent improvements in filtering and pose reconstruction. Together, the two compo-nents provide robust view selection across collections with different visual and geometric characteristics. 

Top-K sensitivity. Figure 3 studies the effect of the neighborhood size used for consensus reference selection on Phototourism under the Large-noise setting. Using only the minimum number of neighbors leads to noticeably lower reference-selection accuracy and clean-view retention, indi-cating that overly local consensus is sensitive to small ac-cidental clusters. Performance improves substantially as the neighborhood expands and remains stable over a broad inter-mediate range. Among these settings, K = max(3 , ⌊N/ 3⌋)

provides the most consistent overall performance across reference selection and view filtering. We therefore use N/ 3

as the default setting in all experiments. 

Dual-cue fusion filtering. Table III compares the indi-vidual relevance cues and alternative fusion strategies under the Large-noise setting. The relative effectiveness of the two cues varies across datasets: DINOv2 appearance relevance performs particularly well on Phototourism and On-the-Go, while the geometry-aware cue remains competitive on ETH3D metrics. These results indicate that the two cues capture different aspects of view relevance. We therefore use a conservative AND rejection rule, removing a view only when both cues indicate low relevance. This design achieves consistently high clean-view retention while maintaining strong distractor rejection across all datasets. In contrast, OR rejection and direct score averaging are more aggressive and frequently discard valid target-scene views, reducing scene coverage. V. CONCLUSIONS In this paper, we presented FreeView, a training-free framework for robust 3D reconstruction from uncurated image collections. FreeView combines Consensus Reference Selection with Adaptive Dual-Cue Rejection to establish a representative filtering reference and preserve useful views through complementary visual and geometric evidence. This design removes the need for a pre-verified clean reference and manual calibration of view rejection. By integrating with both VGGT and π3, FreeView provides a common view-curation framework for different reconstruction backbones. Experiments across multiple datasets demonstrate high clean-view retention and distractor rejection rates, enabling reliable reconstruction across varying distractor levels and reference conditions.
