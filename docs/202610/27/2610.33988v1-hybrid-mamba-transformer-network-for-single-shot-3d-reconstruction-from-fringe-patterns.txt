# Hybrid Mamba-Transformer Network for Single-Shot 3D Reconstruction from Fringe Patterns 

# Xinjie Zhou *a , XiaoLei Li** b

# a. Aulin College, Northeast Forestry University, Harbin,150040, China b.School of Mechanical Engineering, Hebei University of Technology, Tianjin, 300130, China *Corresponding author: zhouxinjie@nefu.edu.cn; **Corresponding author: lixiaolei_hebut@163.com 

Abstract: Deep learning facilitates rapid single-shot three-dimensional (3D) reconstruction using fringe projection profilometry (FPP), but recovering accurate surface height from a single high-frequency fringe image remains challenging because both long-range spatial consistency and fine geometric details must be preserved. Convolutional networks are limited in modeling long-range dependencies, whereas window-based Transformers establish global interactions only progressively with increasing depth. To address these limitations, a hybrid Mamba –Transformer network is proposed for fringe-to-height regression. The network adopts a Swin-based U-shaped architecture and introduces visual state-space modeling at the intermediate-resolution encoder stage. An axial – dilated vision state-space (ADVSS) block is further developed to enhance pre-scan feature encoding through local, dilated, and axial depth-wise convolutions while retaining four-directional selective scanning. This design combines linear-complexity long-range modeling with direction-aware preservation of local fringe structures. Experiments on a publicly available FPP dataset show that the proposed method outperforms hNet, SwinConvUNet, UHRNet, and HiDNet in mean absolute error(MAE), root-mean-square error (RMSE), absolute relative error, and structural similarity index measure (SSIM). Using only one deformed fringe image, the proposed method achieves an RMSE of 0.548 mm and an SSIM of 0.9985, reducing RMSE by 65.2% and 59.6% relative to UHRNet and HiDNet, respectively. These results demonstrate the effectiveness of combining selective state-space modeling with hierarchical window-based attention for accurate single-shot FPP reconstruction. 

Keywords: 3D reconstruction; Fringe Projection Profilometry; single-shot fringe-to-depth; Vision Mamba; Swin Transformer; hybrid Mamba –Transformer 

1. Introduction 

Fringe projection profilometry (FPP) is a well-established optical metrology technique for noncontact, high-precision three-dimensional (3D) surface measurement [1 – 3] . In a typical FPP system, sinusoidal fringe patterns are projected onto the measured object, and the phase distortions caused by the surface topography are analyzed to recover depth through geometric triangulation. Conventional FPP methods achieve high measurement accuracy using multistep phase shifting and temporal phase unwrapping [4] . However, the acquisition of multiple sequential fringe images makes these methods susceptible to motion-induced errors and limits their applicability to dynamic scenes and real-time measurements. Single-shot FPP has been developed to overcome this limitation by recovering 3D geometry from only one fringe image. Traditional single-shot approaches, including Fourier transform profilometry (FTP) [5] and other spatial phase-demodulation techniques, are often affected by spectral leakage, frequency aliasing, and phase errors near discontinuities and geometrically complex regions. In recent years, deep learning has provided an alternative solution by learning a direct mapping from a single fringe pattern to phase or depth [6 –11] , thereby reducing or eliminating the need for explicit phase demodulation and unwrapping. Most existing methods rely on convolutional neural networks (CNNs), which are effective at extracting local spatial features. Nevertheless, convolution is inherently a local operation, and its effective receptive field expands only gradually with network depth. As high-frequency fringe patterns contain periodic structures extending across the entire field of view, insufficient long-range interaction may lead to inconsistencies in depth recovery, particularly in regions containing discontinuities, steep slopes, shadows, or low fringe modulation. Transformers [12] provide a potential solution by modeling long-range dependencies through self-attention. However, the quadratic computational and memory complexity of global self-attention with respect to the number of image tokens restricts its application to high-resolution optical images. The Swin Transformer [13] alleviates this problem by alternately applying window-based and shifted-window multi-head self-attention (W-MSA and SW-MSA), resulting in approximately linear computational complexity while enabling information exchange across neighboring windows. Swin-based U-Net architectures have subsequently been introduced into single-shot structured-light profilometry and fringe-phase retrieval [14,15] , demonstrating improved contextual modeling compared with purely convolutional networks. Despite these advantages, interactions between distant image regions must still be established progressively through multiple shifted-window blocks. Consequently, the effective receptive field may remain insufficient for maintaining image-wide consistency in high-resolution fringe images. Moreover, conventional square attention windows do not explicitly exploit the pronounced directional and periodic characteristics of projected fringe patterns. State-space models (SSMs) [16] have recently emerged as computationally efficient alternatives for long-range dependency modeling. Mamba combines an input-dependent selective mechanism with linear-complexity sequence scanning. Its vision-oriented variants, such as VMamba, Vision Mamba (Vim), and VM-UNet [17 – 19] , extend this mechanism to images through bidirectional or multidirectional scanning. These characteristics make visual SSMs particularly attractive for modeling the long-range continuity of fringe patterns. However, directly employing a pure Mamba encoder may not fully satisfy the requirements of high-precision fringe-to-depth regression. Recovering metric depth from a single high-frequency fringe image, for example, one containing 128 vertical fringes, requires both long-range consistency and accurate preservation of local geometric details. Although multidirectional selective scanning can efficiently propagate global information, it may insufficiently emphasize fine structures around object boundaries, steep surface variations, shadowed areas, and low-modulation regions. Conversely, Swin Transformer blocks provide effective hierarchical feature refinement but require multiple stages of shifted-window propagation to establish global relationships. Therefore, neither a purely window-based Transformer nor an entirely SSM-based architecture provides an optimal balance between global dependency modeling and local-detail preservation for this task. Hybrid Mamba – Transformer architectures have recently demonstrated that selective state-space modeling and attention can provide complementary global and local representations [20  

> –22].

Motivated by this observation, this study proposes a hybrid Mamba –Transformer network for single-shot fringe-to-depth reconstruction. The proposed network retains a Swin-based U-Net architecture and incorporates visual state-space (VSS) blocks into intermediate encoder stages. At these stages, the reduced spatial resolution offers a favorable balance between computational efficiency and long-range feature modeling. The VSS blocks employ linear-complexity multidirectional selective scanning to propagate fringe information over extended spatial ranges and improve global consistency. Meanwhile, the Swin-based components perform hierarchical local aggregation and facilitate the preservation and reconstruction of fine geometric details. To further adapt selective state-space modeling to the characteristics of fringe images, an axial- and dilation-aware visual state-space (ADVSS) module is developed. Before multidirectional selective scanning, axial convolutions enhance orientation-dependent features along the horizontal and vertical directions, while dilated convolutions enlarge the local receptive field and capture multiscale spatial structures. This front-end enhances the directional and periodic features of high-frequency fringes without modifying the underlying selective-scan mechanism. Consequently, the proposed architecture combines the efficient global modeling capability of Mamba with the local inductive bias and hierarchical feature representation of the Swin Transformer. The main contributions of this study are summarized as follows: 1.A hybrid Mamba –Transformer architecture is proposed for direct depth reconstruction from a single high-frequency fringe image. VSS blocks are incorporated into selected encoder stages of a Swin-based U-Net to balance long-range dependency modeling, local-detail preservation, and computational cost. 2.An ADVSS module is developed to adapt visual state-space modeling to the directional and periodic characteristics of fringe patterns. Axial and dilated convolutions enhance orientation-sensitive and multi-scale local features before multi-directional selective scanning. The proposed method is comprehensively evaluated against hNet, SwinConvUNet, UHRNet, and HiDNet under a unified training and evaluation protocol. The experiments include quantitative comparisons, qualitative evaluations, and ablation studies to assess the effectiveness of the hybrid architecture and the ADVSS module. 3.For a fair comparison, all baseline networks are reimplemented using the same training protocol and evaluated on a common dataset constructed from the publicly available fringe-projection data reported by Wang et al. [7,8]. The proposed method consistently outperforms the compared approaches in terms of mean absolute error (MAE), root-mean-square error (RMSE), absolute relative error (AbsRel), and structural similarity index measure (SSIM). It achieves an RMSE of 0.548 mm and an SSIM of 0.9985, corresponding to RMSE reductions of 65.2% and 59.6% relative to UHRNet and HiDNet, respectively. These results demonstrate the effectiveness of integrating selective state-space modeling with hierarchical window-based attention for high-precision single-shot FPP. The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 presents the proposed network architecture and loss formulation. Section 3 describes the experimental setup and reports the comparative and ablation results. Section 4 summarizes the main findings and discusses directions for future research. 

2. Method 

The proposed framework consists of two stages: (i) offline training of the hybrid Mamba –

Transformer network for fringe-to-depth regression and (ii) online inference for reconstructing a metric depth map from a single captured fringe pattern. The overall framework is illustrated in Fig. 1. Specifically, Fig. 1(a) presents the offline training procedure, whereas Fig. 1(b) shows the online 3D reconstruction process for a target object. During offline training, as shown in Fig. 1(a), fringe patterns from the training dataset are fed into the proposed network to generate the corresponding depth maps. The discrepancy between each predicted depth map and its ground-truth counterpart is quantified using a loss function. The network parameters are then iteratively updated by an optimizer to minimize the training loss. During online inference, as illustrated in Fig. 1(b), a single fringe image captured by the camera of the FPP system is processed by the trained network. The corresponding metric depth map is generated in a feed-forward manner and subsequently used to recover the 3D surface of the target object. The proposed network is designed to improve the accuracy of single-shot fringe-to-depth regression by jointly modeling long-range fringe continuity and fine local geometry. To achieve this objective, visual state-space (VSS) blocks are incorporated into the intermediate encoder stages of a Swin-based U-Net. At these stages, multi-directional selective scanning captures long-range spatial dependencies with linear computational complexity. An axial- and dilation-aware visual state-space (ADVSS) module is further introduced to adapt the Mamba branch to the directional and periodic characteristics of high-frequency vertical fringes. Meanwhile, the Swin Transformer blocks at the bottleneck and decoder stages perform hierarchical local feature aggregation and facilitate fine-detail recovery during depth decoding. The network is optimized using a compound objective that combines the mean squared error (MSE) and structural similarity index measure (SSIM) losses. Sections 2.1 – 2.4 describe the network architecture and explain the motivation for each component, while Section 2.5 presents the training objective. Although global self-attention enables Transformers to model long-range dependencies, its computational complexity increases quadratically with the number of input tokens. Its direct application to high-resolution optical images therefore entails substantial memory consumption and computational cost. With a fixed window size, this hierarchical attention mechanism achieves approximately linear computational complexity with respect to the number of image patches. 2.3 Details of ADVSS block 

Although alternating W-MSA and SW-MSA facilitates information exchange across neighboring windows, the effective receptive field of a Swin Transformer expands progressively with network depth. This mechanism may limit the direct modeling of long-range spatial dependencies in high-resolution fringe patterns. To overcome this limitation, an axial–dilated vision state-space (ADVSS) block is introduced. The ADVSS block preserves the linear-complexity selective scanning of the standard vision state-space (VSS) block [19] while enhancing its pre-scan representation through multi-scale and direction-aware depth-wise convolutions. As shown in Fig. 3, the ADVSS block is derived from the standard VSS block in Fig. 3(a). Its SS2D operator, gating branch, and outer residual connection are retained. The original depth-wise convolution is replaced by three parallel branches, as illustrated in Fig. 3(b): a 3×3 depth-wise convolution for local feature extraction, a dilated 3×3 depth-wise convolution with a dilation rate of 2 for enlarged-context modeling, and a pair of axial 1×7 and 7×1 depth-wise convolutions for capturing horizontally and vertically extended fringe structures. The resulting features are concatenated and fused using a 1×1 convolution before being processed by SS2D. In addition, the local 3×3 convolution response is injected into the scan output before normalization and gating, thereby preserving fine-scale fringe details during global feature aggregation. 2.4 Details of Attention Gate 

Skip connections in encoder–decoder architectures recover spatial information lost during downsampling. However, directly concatenating encoder and decoder features may introduce redundant or task-irrelevant information. An attention gate (AG) is therefore applied to adaptively refine the encoder features before feature fusion, as illustrated in Fig. 5. The AG receives the encoder skip tokens and the decoder gating tokens. Both inputs are projected into a common intermediate dimension using 1×1 convolutions followed by layer normalization.The resulting feature maps are then combined by element-wise addition and passed through a ReLU activation. A subsequent 1×1 convolution , followed by a sigmoid function, produces a spatial attention map with coefficients in [0,1]. Finally, this attention map is applied to the encoder skip tokens via element-wise multiplication, yielding the gated skip features.Guided by the decoder representation, the AG attenuates less relevant encoder responses while retaining informative spatial features, thereby enabling more selective multi-scale fusion and facilitating the recovery of fine geometric details.

3.1 Dataset and training 

The dataset reported in Ref. [8] was used to train and evaluate the proposed method. It contains fringe images of objects with diverse geometries, dimensions, and surface characteristics, providing representative samples for fringe-to-depth reconstruction. The images were acquired using a structured-light projection system at a spatial resolution of 768×512 pixels. The original dataset comprises 3,070 samples, with each sample containing 60 images acquired by the left and right cameras—30 images per camera—and the corresponding depth labels. Only the data acquired by the left camera were used in this study, resulting in 3,070 fringe–depth pairs. For each sample, the ninth frame, corresponding to a high-frequency vertical sinusoidal pattern containing 128 fringes, was selected as the network input. This frame is the first pattern in the eight-step phase-shifting sequence. The 29th frame was used as the reference pattern. The ground-truth depth maps were generated from multiple images within each acquisition sequence using a rational-fraction-based reconstruction method without an intermediate matching procedure. Representative samples are presented in Fig. 6, where the first and second columns show the selected input fringe patterns and their corresponding ground-truth depth maps, respectively. Of the 3,070 samples, a fixed subset of 74 samples (2.4%) was reserved as the independent test set and stored in fringe_test.h5 and Z_test.h5. The remaining 2,996 samples were stored in fringe.h5 and Z.h5 and randomly divided into 2,697 training samples (87.9% of the complete dataset) and 299 validation samples (9.7%). The test samples were excluded from both network optimization and model selection. The training data were stored as chunked and compressed HDF5 arrays rather than PyTorch .pt files. During dataset initialization, only metadata, including the array dimensions and number of samples, was read; individual fringe images and depth maps were subsequently loaded on demand by index. Each DataLoader worker independently opened the corresponding HDF5 files, preventing complete float32 arrays from being duplicated across worker processes. Compared with loading the entire dataset into memory, which would require approximately 9 GB, this sample-wise loading strategy substantially reduced memory consumption and supported efficient parallel data loading. During training, the network parameters were optimized through back-propagation using the training set. Performance on the validation set was monitored throughout training, and the model yielding the lowest validation loss was retained for subsequent evaluation. The selected model was then evaluated on the independent test set. Because the test samples were not involved in either parameter optimization or checkpoint selection, the resulting performance provides an independent assessment of the generalization capability of the proposed method. All experiments were performed on a workstation equipped with an Intel Xeon Platinum 8375C processor (2.90 GHz), 251 GB of RAM, and an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090 graphics card with 24 GB of memory.The software environment comprised Ubuntu 24.04.2, Python 3.10.21 The training code was implemented in PyTorch 2.0.1 with CUDA 11.8 and optimized with the Adam optimizer. 3.3 Ablation experiment 

A series of ablation experiments was conducted to investigate how the principal architectural components affect single-shot 3D reconstruction and to determine the most effective configuration. All variants were trained and evaluated on the same fringe-pattern dataset using an identical experimental protocol and evaluation criteria. With reference to the hybrid Mamba –Transformer architecture shown in Fig. 2, the decoder configuration was kept unchanged. The ablations focused on the encoder stages (L 0 and L 1), the bottleneck (L 2), and the skip-connection strategy. The evaluated configurations are described below. 

Architecture ablations Mode A: L0, L 1, and L 2 contain 2, 4, and 6 Swin Transformer blocks, respectively. Attention-gated skip connections are employed. 

Mode B: L0 and L 2 contain 2 and 6 Swin Transformer blocks, respectively, whereas L 1

contains 4 VSS blocks. Attention-gated skip connections are retained. 

Mode C: L0 and L 1 contain 2 and 4 VSS blocks, respectively, whereas L 2 contains 6 Swin Transformer blocks. Attention-gated skip connections are retained. 

Mode D: L0 and L 1 contain 2 and 4 Swin Transformer blocks, respectively, whereas L 2

contains 6 VSS blocks. Attention-gated skip connections are retained. 

Mode E: The VSS blocks in L 1 of Mode B are replaced with 4 proposed ADVSS blocks. L 0

and L2 retain 2 and 6 Swin Transformer blocks, respectively, and attention-gated skip connections are used. This configuration represents the complete proposed model. 

Mode F: Mode F has the same encoder and bottleneck configuration as Mode E, but the attention gates are removed. The encoder features are therefore concatenated directly with the corresponding decoder features. 

Component ablations of the ADVSS block 

The following variants were derived from Mode E to evaluate the individual components of the ADVSS block. 

Mode G: The axial-convolution branch is removed from each ADVSS block in L 1.

Mode H: The dilated-convolution branch is removed from each ADVSS block in L 1.

Mode I: The post-scan local residual injection is removed from each ADVSS block in L 1,while the local 3×3 branch used for pre-scan feature fusion is retained. All other architectural and training settings in Modes G–I are identical to those of Mode E. Modes A–I use a single deformed fringe pattern as the network input.

3.4 Quantitative and qualitative comparisons with representative methods 

The proposed method was compared with four representative reconstruction networks: hNet, SwinConvUNet, UHRNet, and HiDNet. All baseline models were re-implemented according to their original architectures, with their input layers adapted where necessary to accept the single-channel fringe images used in this study. Each model takes one deformed fringe pattern as input and predicts a normalized height map, which is subsequently converted to millimetres for quantitative evaluation. All models were trained and tested using the dataset constructed from the publicly available dataset described in Section 3.1 The experiments were conducted on a workstation equipped with an Intel Xeon Platinum 8375C CPU at 2.90 GHz, 251 GB of system memory, and one NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090 GPU with 24 GB of memory. The software environment comprised Ubuntu 24.04.2, Python 3.10.21, and PyTorch 2.0.1 with CUDA 11.8.Unless otherwise specified, all methods were trained using Adam with an initial learning rate of 1 ×10 −4 , a batch size of 4, and 400 epochs. The learning rate was adjusted using a cosine annealing schedule and decreased to 6 ×10 − 5 by the end of training. The remaining experimental settings were kept consistent with those used in the ablation studies. Figure 7 presents the training and validation RMSE curves. All methods exhibit a rapid reduction in RMSE during the initial training stage and broadly approach convergence after approximately 250 epochs, although occasional fluctuations remain. The proposed method achieves the lowest final RMSE on both the training and validation sets, at 0.236 and 0.607 mm, respectively. Its validation curve is also comparatively stable, particularly during the first 80 epochs, when the baseline methods exhibit several pronounced fluctuations. These results indicate that the proposed architecture provides more accurate and stable optimization under the common training protocol. A quantitative comparison was further performed by evaluating the reconstructed height maps against their corresponding ground-truth maps on the test set. Four complementary metrics were employed: mean absolute error (MAE), root mean square error (RMSE), absolute relative error (AbsRel), and structural similarity index measure (SSIM). The inference time was also recorded . It was measured on the same NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090 GPU with a batch size of 1. Before timing, each model was executed for 50 warm-up iterations, followed by 200 timed iterations in evaluation mode with gradient computation disabled. torch.cuda.synchronize() was called immediately before and after each timed forward pass to ensure completion of all asynchronous CUDA operations. The reported inference time is the mean time required to process one fringe image, excluding data loading and result post-processing.The quantitative comparison results are summarized in Table 5.

4. Discussions and conclusion 

This study presents a hybrid Mamba –Transformer network for single-shot surface-height reconstruction from a deformed fringe pattern. The proposed architecture integrates selective state-space modeling with hierarchical window-based attention in a U-shaped encoder –decoder framework. At the intermediate-resolution L 1 stage, the proposed axial –dilated vision state-space (ADVSS) blocks combine four-directional selective scanning with local, dilated, and axial depth-wise convolutions. This design captures long-range spatial dependencies while retaining the directional and multiscale characteristics of fringe patterns without modifying the underlying selective-scan operator. Swin Transformer blocks are retained at the high-resolution encoder stage, bottleneck, and decoder to provide complementary window-based feature refinement. The architecture ablations demonstrate that the allocation of VSS and Swin Transformer blocks across the network substantially affects reconstruction performance. Replacing the L 1 Swin Transformer blocks with VSS blocks improves all four evaluation metrics relative to the all-Swin baseline, whereas extending VSS blocks to the high-resolution L 0 stage does not provide an additional benefit. Replacing the L 1 VSS blocks with ADVSS blocks produces the best overall configuration. Furthermore, removing the axial, dilated, or post-scan local residual component degrades performance, supporting the complementary roles of directional encoding, enlarged-context extraction, and local-detail preservation. The input-channel experiments also show that the five-channel representation does not improve the VSS- or ADVSS-based models under the present training protocol. The proposed single-channel configuration can therefore recover surface geometry directly from one deformed fringe image without requiring reference-fringe or coordinate channels. Under the unified evaluation protocol, the proposed method outperforms hNet, SwinConvUNet, UHRNet, and HiDNet in MAE, RMSE, AbsRel, and SSIM. It achieves an MAE of 0.195 mm, an RMSE of 0.548 mm, an AbsRel of 0.043, and an SSIM of 0.9985. Compared with UHRNet and HiDNet, its RMSE is reduced by 65.2% and 59.6%, respectively. These findings indicate that combining selective scanning with hierarchical window-based feature processing is effective for single-shot fringe-based surface reconstruction. The improvement in accuracy is accompanied by increased computational cost relative to lightweight CNN-based models. The proposed network contains 30.225 M parameters and requires 0.0246 s per image on an RTX 4090, compared with 8.631 M parameters and 0.0092 s for hNet. Although its latency is only moderately higher than those of SwinConvUNet and UHRNet, further optimization is required for resource-constrained platforms and high-speed measurement applications. Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the experiments were conducted using data acquired under a fixed imaging configuration and fringe spatial frequency. Generalization across different cameras, projection systems, working distances, fringe densities, and object materials therefore remains to be established. Second, highly reflective or non-Lambertian surfaces, shadows, steep slopes, and regions with low fringe modulation may still cause locally elevated errors. Future work will evaluate the proposed framework on more diverse real-world datasets and across different measurement systems. Physics-informed constraints, uncertainty estimation, and degraded-fringe augmentation will be investigated to improve reliability in low-quality fringe regions. Lightweight architectural variants, model compression, and hardware-oriented acceleration will also be explored to support real-time implementation. In conclusion, the results demonstrate that integrating ADVSS-based selective state-space modeling with hierarchical window-based attention provides an effective approach to accurate single-shot fringe-projection surface reconstruction.
