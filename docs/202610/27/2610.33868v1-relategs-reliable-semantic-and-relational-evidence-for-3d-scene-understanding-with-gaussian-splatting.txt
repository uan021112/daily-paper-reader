## Abstract 

Language-aware 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has ad-vanced language-driven 3D scene understanding by incor-porating semantic and relational cues into reconstructed scenes. However, relation-aware grounding depends not only on representing such information, but also on whether the underlying scene-side evidence is reliable and sufficient. Multi-view semantic aggregation can incorporate weak observations, while object relations can remain under-observed along the available camera trajectory, leading to ambiguous evidence for target disambiguation. We present RelateGS, a structured language-aware Gaussian represen-tation that selectively refines semantic and relational evi-dence for 3D visual grounding. RelateGS suppresses un-reliable observations during semantic feature uplifting and consolidates multi-view object observations into a persis-tent 3D graph. The graph further identifies under-observed object relations and guides targeted re-querying of infor-mative existing frames to complement their evidence. Ex-periments on Sr3D+ and Nr3D demonstrate competitive 3D grounding performance, improving the previous best by 7.9 and 1.7 percentage points at Acc@0.25, respectively.

Despite these advances, many referring expressions identify a target not only by what it is, but also by how 

it relates to surrounding objects. While language-aware 3DGS can retrieve semantically relevant objects, multiple instances may appear equally plausible, yet only one may satisfy a description such as “the chair next to the table.” Resolving such expressions therefore requires object-level relational context beyond semantic similarity alone. Recent approaches have extended language-aware 3D representations from dense semantics to structured rela-tional representations by organizing reconstructed scenes into persistent object instances and modeling their pairwise arXiv:2610.33868v1 [cs.CV] 24 Oct 2026 relations [10, 17, 22]. However, constructing an object-centric relational representation does not by itself guarantee reliable relational grounding, as shown in Fig. 1. The re-sulting representation is ultimately constrained by the qual-ity and coverage of the visual observations from which its semantics and relations are constructed. First, semantic ev-idence can be unreliable because language features are ag-gregated from observations affected by viewpoint changes, partial occlusion, object boundaries, and limited visibility. Weak observations can therefore corrupt the semantic repre-sentation of a Gaussian and increase ambiguity among simi-lar object candidates. Second, relational evidence can be in-sufficient because pairwise relations are inferred only from the object configurations exposed by the available camera views. When a grounding-relevant object pair is poorly ob-served, the relation needed to distinguish the target may re-main weak or missing. Consequently, even an explicitly structured scene can fail to resolve relational language when its semantic evidence is unreliable or its relational evidence is under-observed. Our key insight is that relation-aware grounding benefits from selectively refining the scene-side evidence supporting the representation, rather than treating all available obser-vations as equally informative. Specifically, weak semantic observations should be suppressed during Gaussian feature construction, while under-observed object relations should be complemented using informative existing observations. Based on this insight, we introduce RelateGS, a structured language-aware Gaussian representation that selectively re-fines the scene-side evidence required for relation-aware 3D grounding. Specifically, RelateGS realizes this principle through complementary semantic and relational refinement. First, 

confidence-aware semantic uplifting estimates the contribu-tion of each image observation to individual Gaussians and selectively aggregates reliable language evidence, reducing semantic ambiguity introduced by weak observations. The resulting multi-view semantics are consolidated into a per-sistent 3D object graph that maintains object identities and spatial relationships across views. Second, graph-guided relational re-querying reasons over the current graph to identify under-observed object pairs and selects informative frames from the existing camera trajectory for targeted re-querying. The recovered relational evidence is fused back into the persistent graph, allowing RelateGS to comple-ment missing relations rather than treating the initially con-structed graph as a fixed output. Extensive experiments demonstrate that RelateGS sub-stantially improves relation-aware 3D visual grounding. On Sr3D+ and Nr3D [1], RelateGS achieves competitive over-all grounding performance, showing the effectiveness of selectively refining scene-side semantic and relational evi-dence. Additional semantic evaluations and controlled abla-tions further validate the underlying representation and the contribution of each refinement strategy. Our contributions are summarized as follows: • We formulate relation-aware 3D grounding from the per-spective of scene-side evidence quality, considering both the reliability of Gaussian-level semantic evidence and the sufficiency of object-level relational evidence. • We introduce RelateGS, which realizes selective evidence refinement through confidence-aware semantic uplifting and graph-guided relational re-querying over a persistent 3D object graph. • RelateGS improves grounding performance on Sr3D+ and Nr3D, while complementary semantic evaluations and controlled ablations validate the proposed represen-tation design. 

## 2. Related Work 

2.1. Language-Embedded 3D Gaussian Splatting 

Recent advances in 3DGS [7] have extended its role from photorealistic scene reconstruction to language-aware 3D understanding. Building on 2D foundation models such as SAM [8] and CLIP [15], recent approaches extract ob-ject masks and language-aligned features from multi-view images and transfer this semantic evidence to Gaussian primitives. LangSplat [14] and LEGaussians [18] embed language-aligned features into Gaussian scenes for open-vocabulary localization. OpenGaussian [26] further tar-gets point-level open-vocabulary understanding through im-proved semantic representation and 2D–3D association. GAGS [13] improves multi-view semantic consistency through granularity-aware segmentation and feature distil-lation. Instead of optimizing additional semantic parame-ters, LUDVIG [11] and Occam’s LGS [2] transfer multi-view language features to pre-trained Gaussian primitives according to their rendering contributions. More recently, ReasonGrounder [10] introduces hierarchical Gaussian fea-ture fields together with large vision-language models for open-vocabulary grounding and reasoning. These methods demonstrate the effectiveness of dense language-aligned Gaussian representations for connecting natural language with reconstructed 3D scenes. However, the resulting Gaussian-level semantics ultimately depend on the multi-view observations from which they are con-structed. Observations can vary substantially in reliability due to visibility, occlusion, and object boundaries, yet this variation is not always explicitly considered during seman-tic aggregation. RelateGS builds on language-embedded Gaussians from this perspective, focusing on how reliable semantic evidence can be selectively preserved for down-stream relation-aware grounding. 2.2. 3D Scene Graph Generation 

3D scene graphs provide an object-centric abstraction of a scene by representing objects as nodes and their semantic or spatial relationships as edges. 3DSSG [21] establishes object and relation prediction from 3D point clouds, while SceneGraphFusion [25] incrementally constructs scene graphs from RGB-D sequences. Subsequent works improve the quality and consistency of object-level representations: VL-SAT [23] incorporates visual-linguistic context for 3D scene graph prediction, and SGAligner [16] exploits cross-modal alignment for object association across 3D scenes. With the emergence of 3DGS, GaussianGraph [22] extends this object-centric perspective to Gaussian scenes by orga-nizing reconstructed primitives into objects with semantic attributes and spatial relationships. These works demonstrate that object-centric graphs complement dense semantic representations by explicitly modeling inter-object relationships. However, the presence of a scene graph does not guarantee sufficient observational support for relation-aware grounding. In particular, lim-ited visibility and co-visibility along the camera trajectory can leave relational evidence incomplete, even when the corresponding objects are represented in the graph. Re-lateGS therefore leverages a persistent scene graph not only to represent object relationships but also to identify under-observed object pairs and guide targeted retrieval of com-plementary evidence from multi-view observations. 

2.3. 3D Visual Grounding 

3D visual grounding aims to localize a target object from a natural-language referring expression. When multiple in-stances share the same semantic category, grounding often requires spatial and contextual relationships with surround-ing objects. Point-cloud-based approaches such as CORE-3DVG [29] and ViewSRD [5] exploit contextual and rela-tional cues to resolve such ambiguity. ConcreteNet [20] improves dense 3D grounding through relation-aware atten-tion, contrastive learning, viewpoint modeling, and multi-view ensembling. More recently, EG-3DVG [12] introduces an expression- and geometry-aware grounding decoder that improves text–vision alignment and selectively aggregates geometry-consistent visual cues. Recent methods exploit relational scene representations to disambiguate referred objects [10, 22]. Chain of Se-mantics Programming [17] formulates grounding as zero-shot neuro-symbolic reasoning, constructing a relationship graph and chain of semantics while leveraging multi-view 3DGS rendering for complementary semantic observations. In contrast, RelateGS focuses on whether the scene rep-resentation provides reliable and sufficient grounding ev-idence, rather than introducing new decoders, reasoning programs, or rendered viewpoints. It selectively preserves reliable Gaussian-level semantics and complements under-observed object-level relations before downstream reason-ing. 

## 3. Method 

3.1. Overview 

We present RelateGS, a structured language-aware Gaus-sian representation for relation-aware 3D grounding. Given posed RGB-D observations and a reconstructed 3D Gaus-sian scene, we construct semantic and relational evidence to disambiguate the referred object through its relations to other instances. We first construct reliable Gaussian-level semantic ev-idence from multi-view observations and associate object observations across views to form an initial object graph. We then identify under-observed object pairs and selec-tively re-query informative observations to complement missing relational evidence, yielding a refined object graph for language grounding.

Relational evidence fusion. Frame-level relations are transferred to the persistent graph through the object asso-ciations established above. Specifically, a relation predicted between two frame-local objects is remapped to their cor-responding persistent node IDs and accumulated as a triplet 

(vi, r, v j ) across views. Thus, repeated observations of the same physical object pair contribute relational evidence to the same persistent endpoints. After all frame-level observations are associated, we complement the accumulated VLM relations with geomet-ric relations inferred from the final persistent node geom-etry. Relations sharing the same subject, predicate, and object are fused across views using the relation confidence provided by the VLM and their multi-view support. Weak or geometrically inconsistent edges are subsequently fil-tered to obtain Eobs . The resulting graph therefore preserves multi-view relational evidence while maintaining consis-tency with the consolidated 3D object representation. How-ever, relational evidence can remain incomplete for object pairs that are insufficiently observed across the input views. 

3.4. Graph-Guided Relational Refinement 

To complement such insufficient relational evidence, Re-lateGS uses the current object graph to determine which ob-ject pairs require additional relational evidence and scene geometry to determine which existing frames are informa-tive for those pairs . The refinement consists of under-observed pair identification, informative frame retrieval, and targeted relational re-querying. 

Identifying under-observed object pairs. We consider spatially nearby objects because their pairwise relations are likely to provide useful cues for relational grounding. Among these pairs, we identify those for which the accu-mulated VLM relation is missing or remains below a confi-dence threshold. Formally, 

Punder = {(vi, v j ) | d(vi, v j ) < τ d, c ij < τ r } , (6) where d(vi, v j ) denotes the distance between the two persis-tent objects and cij denotes the accumulated relation confi-dence obtained by fusing VLM-provided confidence across views. Pairs without observed relational evidence are as-signed zero confidence. This criterion focuses subsequent refinement on nearby object pairs for which the current graph provides insufficient relational evidence. 

Retrieving informative existing frames. For each pair 

(vi, v j ) ∈ P under , we search the original camera trajectory for a frame that provides a favorable joint view of both ob-jects. We first project their 3D extents into each camera and retain only frames with sufficient joint frustum cover-age. Among these candidates, we rank frames based on the camera-to-pair distance and the alignment of the viewing direction with the center of the object pair. The highest-ranked frame is selected for targeted relational re-querying. 

Targeted relational re-query. The selected frame is re-queried with a pair-specific prompt asking for the spatial relationship between the two under-observed objects. Un-like the generic frame-level extraction used during initial graph construction, this query explicitly targets a relational deficiency identified in the current object graph. The resulting observations are processed through the same 3D uplifting, persistent association, and relation-fusion procedure used during initial graph construction. This ensures that the newly acquired relational evidence is associated with the corresponding persistent object pair rather than treated as an independent prediction. After re-finement, we obtain the refined object graph 

Sref = ( V, Eref ), (7) where Eref contains the original relational evidence together with the selectively acquired re-query observations. 

3.5. Relation-Aware 3D Grounding 

The final RelateGS representation combines language-aligned Gaussian features with persistent object identities and their refined relational evidence. Given a referring ex-pression, this representation provides both semantic cues for identifying plausible objects and relational cues for dis-ambiguating among competing instances. For downstream grounding, we adopt the LLM-based graph matching protocol of GaussianGraph [22]. The matcher compares the referring expression with the seman-tic attributes and relations stored in the object graph and selects the object node that best satisfies the description. Using the same grounding procedure across all variants iso-lates the effect of our scene-side evidence refinement: im-provements arise from the representation provided to the matcher rather than from introducing a new task-specific grounding module. 

## 4. Experiments 

We evaluate RelateGS primarily on relation-aware 3D visual grounding, where both semantic object evidence and inter-object relations are required to resolve natural-language referring expressions. We further evaluate the un-derlying semantic representation and conduct controlled ab-lations to examine how each form of selective evidence re-finement contributes to grounding performance.

4.4. Ablation Studies 

We conduct controlled ablations on Sr3D+ to isolate the two forms of selective evidence refinement in RelateGS. All variants use the same underlying Gaussian reconstruction and downstream grounding protocol. For variants involv-ing relational re-querying, we additionally fix the number of VLM queries so that improvements cannot be attributed simply to additional inference. 

Effects of confidence-aware semantic uplifting. Adding confidence-aware observation selection to the initial ob-ject graph improves Acc@0.1 from 21.34 to 21.72 and Acc@0.25 from 12.12 to 13.10. The improvement there-fore isolates the effect of filtering weak semantic observa-tions during Gaussian-level feature uplifting. This result supports our premise that the quality of the multi-view ev-idence, rather than merely its quantity, affects downstream grounding. 

Effects of additional relational re-querying. We next test whether additional VLM queries alone are sufficient to im-prove grounding. Random Frame Re-query uses the same additional query budget as our full method but selects visi-ble object pairs and observations without considering defi-ciencies in the current graph. Despite the additional obser-vations, performance decreases to 19.78/11.62. This result shows that uniformly adding relational observations is not necessarily beneficial and may introduce evidence that is ir-relevant or ambiguous for target disambiguation. 

Effects of graph-guided relational refinement. Us-ing the persistent graph to identify under-observed ob-ject pairs and geometry to retrieve informative observa-tions yields the best performance, reaching 22.22/15.28 Acc@0.1/Acc@0.25. Compared with Random Frame Re-query under the same VLM query budget, the graph-guided strategy improves Acc@0.1 by 2.44 points and Acc@0.25 by 3.66 points. These results directly support the central de-sign of RelateGS: additional evidence is most useful when its acquisition is conditioned on what the current scene rep-resentation is missing.

## 5. Conclusion 

We presented RelateGS, a structured language-aware Gaus-sian representation for relation-aware 3D visual ground-ing. Our key insight is that effective relational ground-ing requires not only explicit object-level structure, but also reliable semantic and relational evidence supporting that structure. RelateGS addresses these complementary evidence failures by suppressing weak multi-view obser-vations during semantic feature uplifting and by using a persistent object graph to identify and complement under-observed relations through targeted re-querying of infor-mative existing frames. Experiments on Sr3D+ and Nr3D demonstrate the effectiveness of the resulting representation for 3D visual grounding, while semantic segmentation and open-vocabulary localization further validate the underlying language-aware Gaussian representation. Together, these results demonstrate the value of selectively refining scene-side evidence for extending language-aware Gaussian rep-resentations from semantic localization toward structured, relation-aware 3D scene understanding.
