# No Cones, No Duals: A Fast and Scalable Solver for NRS f M

## Suryansh Kumar Visual and Spatial AI Lab, VCCM Section College of PVFA, Department of ECEN, Department of CSCE, Texas A&M University, College Station, Texas, USA 

## Abstract 

Non-rigid structure from motion (NRSfM) aims to recover camera motion and time-varying 3D shape from tracked 2D image measurements. Recent high-performing methods show that shape-space factorization remains highly effec-tive for this task. These methods often use costly solvers for two key steps. First, a semidefinite programming (SDP) cone solver to perform Euclidean correction. Next, an al-ternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM) solver to recover the low-rank shape. These steps often scale poorly to long sequences and dense point tracks. We propose a fast, scalable NRSfM solver that avoids these costly steps. For rotation estimation, we introduce a differentiable Eu-clidean correction that directly optimizes small factors for each sequence. A row-polar projection then maps each esti-mated camera block to the closest row-orthonormal matrix. For shape estimation, we introduce an unrolled spectral-proximal solver. It combines reprojection-gradient steps with weighted singular-value shrinkage in the reshuffled shape domain. This solver further uses FISTA-style restart and continuation. The spectral shrinkage operator itself is clas-sical, but we embed it in a fixed differentiable solver, while preserving NRSfM’s geometric constraints. The proposed approach is category-agnostic, requires no external training data, and maps naturally to modern accelerator-friendly lin-ear algebra primitives. Across sparse, dense, noisy, missing-data, and long-sequence benchmarks, our solver achieves substantial speedups with competitive reconstruction accu-racy, including 7 ×–1000 × runtime improvements on dense sequences with 28,887 tracked points. 

## 1. Introduction 

This paper concerns a matrix-factorization approach to solve Non-Rigid Structure from Motion (NRS f M). It is a long-standing inverse problem in geometric computer vision [30]. It seeks to recover camera motion and the 3D shape of a dynamically deforming object from tracked 2D image mea-surements. NRS f M applications include medical imaging

[18, 31, 56], deformable and soft robotic perception [41], motion capture [5, 34, 36, 38], and visual effects [23, 51]. NRS f M factorization remains difficult because many plausible motion-shape decompositions can often explain the same 2D tracks, and both the camera motion and the deform-ing 3D shape are unknown [32, 40]. Prior work also stud-ies matrix factorization approaches under weak-perspective [20, 28, 62] and perspective camera models [14, 42]. This pa-per focuses on the classical orthographic setting with tracked image points. The measurement matrix is modeled as the product of a block-orthographic camera matrix and a shape matrix. Each camera block has orthonormal rows, while the deforming shape is assumed to span a low-rank space. Factorized NRS f M representations include trajectory-space [6], shape-space [11], and shape-trajectory models [35]. Other approaches use force-based models [3], unions of subspaces [34, 35, 69], unions of piecewise curve subspaces [2], or probabilistic point-trajectory models [4]. Among these, trajectory-space and shape-space methods are par-ticularly influential and widely used. Akhter et al. [5, 6] arXiv:2610.34403v1 [cs.CV] 25 Oct 2026 pioneered the trajectory-space view. It represents each 3D point’s motion with a small set of temporal basis functions. Naturally, the popular choice here is the discrete cosine trans-form (DCT) basis. This representation is fast to compute. However, a small fixed temporal basis may poorly capture complex or high-frequency deformations [53, 68]. This can limit reconstruction accuracy [26]. A dual view to this prob-lem is shape-space approaches. Bregler et al. [11] introduced this in early 2000. Dai et al. [15] and Kumar and Van Gool [33] later advanced this approach and showed outstanding re-sults. These methods model shape at each frame and impose a low-rank constraint across time. With careful Euclidean correction, they often achieve higher reconstruction accuracy than trajectory-space methods [26, 33]. Nevertheless, this accuracy comes at a computational price. Recent shape-space methods [33, 57] achieve excellent reconstruction results but rely on two costly solver com-ponents. First, Euclidean correction estimates or camera rotation initialization. This step often uses semidefinite pro-gramming (SDP) or related global optimization over Gram matrices [15, 33, 57]. Second, a low-rank recovery step esti-mates shape with the rotations fixed. It typically uses nuclear or weighted nuclear penalties and ADMM-style updates [10, 33, 57]. SDP cone solvers used for rotation estimation are difficult to parallelize efficiently. Meanwhile, ADMM introduces auxiliary variables, dual updates, and large sub-problem solves. Moreover, it is sensitive to the method’s stopping criteria. These costs become a practical bottleneck as video grow longer or point tracks become denser. We ask whether a fast and scalable solver can preserve the algebraic structure behind accurate shape-space NRS f M. Our answer is the No Cones, No Duals solver. No Cones means that rotation recovery avoids SDP cone solvers. It optimizes a small factor parameterization directly rather than solving for a positive semidefinite Gram matrix. No Duals means that shape recovery avoids ADMM dual variables and large shape-subproblem solves. It replaces alternating primal, auxiliary, and dual updates with a fixed unrolled spectral-proximal loop. The solver retains the algebraic and geometric structure of classical NRS f M. It expresses this structure through differentiable linear algebra operations suited to efficient computation on accelerators. 

Rotation Solver. Classical Euclidean correction solves for Gram matrices satisfying positive semidefiniteness, rank, and orthonormality constraints [15]. We instead parameter-ize each triplet-side Gram matrix to enforce positive semidef-initeness and rank at most 3, while avoiding the explicit SDP cone solver. The factors are optimized using first-order gradi-ent descent with the introduced rotation loss, which measures row-norm equality and row orthogonality after multiplication by the factorized motion matrix. Shape Solver. We optimize the shape objective function via an ADMM-free spectral-proximal solver. We embed the spectral-proximal shrinkage operator in a fixed differ-entiable NRS f M solver layer that preserves the low-rank shape prior while removing ADMM’s auxiliary and dual variables 1. Each layer performs a reprojection-gradient step, reshapes the estimate into the standard shape-space matrix, applies weighted singular-value shrinkage, reshapes back, and uses FISTA-style restart and continuation for stable progress. This gives predictable runtime for a fixed number of layers (deterministic), while retaining the option to run more layers when accuracy is preferred over speed. Our paper’s positioning also differs from supervised deep learning-based NRS f M methods [17, 29, 49, 54, 58]. Super-vised deep NRS f M methods learn category-level priors from training sequences and are therefore complementary to clas-sical factorization. Since our goal is a category-agnostic per-sequence solver, we focus our comparison on factorization-based NRS f M methods, while reporting additional com-parisons to learning-based approaches. Our solver remains category-agnostic and sequence-specific. It uses differen-tiable optimization modules, but it does not learn an external object prior. Meanwhile, it is also useful to distinguish our shape solver from dense variational NRS f M, especially Garg et al. [18], as it was an earlier attempt at an accelerator-friendly approach. Garg et al. [18] introduced a dense surface-reconstruction formulation that combines trace-norm regularization with edge-preserving total-variation spatial smoothness on an image-grid surface, and alternates rota-tion refinement with shape optimization through auxiliary variables, primal-dual TV updates, linear-system solves, and soft-impute-style shrinkage. On the contrary, our method estimates rotations through factor-side Euclidean correction and then applies a fixed no-dual shape solver. The solver, sequential approach pose and shape estimation, and the opti-mization procedure are different.

Overall, this paper makes the following contributions: • We introduce an SDP-free factor-side Euclidean correction for NRS f M rotation estimation. The method optimizes small per-sequence factors and obtains valid orthographic camera blocks via row-polar projection, avoiding explicit cone solvers for Gram recovery. • We introduce an ADMM-free unrolled spectral-proximal solver for low-rank shape recovery. Our approach removes ADMM dual variables and large shape-subproblem solves. • We put forward a fast and scalable approach, which is category-agnostic, requires no external training data, and maps to accelerator-friendly linear algebra primitives. • We evaluate the solver across sparse, dense, missing-data, noisy, and long-sequence benchmarks, showing substantial runtime gains with competitive reconstruction accuracy. The central contribution is not a new low-rank prior or a new spectral shrinkage operator or the first scalable or gradient-based NRS f M method, it is a new solver pathway for shape-space NRS f M that replaces the prevailing conic and dual-variable solver machinery. 

## 2. Preliminaries 

Problem setup. We are given a mean-centered measurement matrix W ∈ R2F ×P containing the tracked 2D coordinates of P points across F frames [11, 60]. Each frame contributes two rows corresponding to the image x–y coordinates of all tracked points. Under the orthographic camera model, the standard NRS f M factorization writes W ≈ RS , where 

R ∈ R2F ×3F is block diagonal and S ∈ R3F ×P stacks the time-varying 3D shapes. The unknowns are constrained in two different ways. (i) each camera block must have orthonormal rows, i.e., RiRTi = I2, and (ii) the deforming 3D shape must admit a low-dimensional representation over time [5, 13]. The difficulty of NRS f M comes from satisfying these constraints while explaining the same 2D observations through the bilinear product RS .

3.1. Rotation Estimation 

The classical Euclidean-correction step estimates Gram matrices Ψk satisfying positive semidefiniteness, low-rank structure, and the frame-wise orthonormality constraints in Eq. (2). This is principled, but it leads to a cone-constrained optimization problem over Ψk. Our rotation estimation takes a different approach. We change the optimization variable. Instead of optimizing the Gram matrix directly, we optimize its factor. Let Θ = {Yk}Kk=1 , Yk ∈ R3K×3, be the set of learnable per-sequence factor variables. We parameterize

(iii) No-dual solver interpretation. The shape stage re-places the ADMM split variables and dual updates with a direct feed-forward sequence layer of gradient and spectral-shrinkage operations. Each layer uses only matrix multipli-cation, masking, reshaping, SVD, scalar shrinkage, and a FISTA-style skip/restart [9, 64]. Thus, the method preserves the shape’s low-rank without ADMM dual variables, large shape-subproblem linear solves, and dependence on the stop-ping criterion. ## 5. Limitations and Closing Remarks 

Limitations. The proposed solver operates in the classical orthographic, tracked-point NRS f M setting. It assumes re-liable 2D tracks, known visibility masks for missing data, and mean-centered measurements. Its shape stage approach does not use image-grid smoothness [18, 38, 45], explicit camera-trajectory or temporal deformation priors [39, 53], or category-level training [17, 46, 49]. This makes the solver broadly applicable, but specialized methods can be more accurate when their additional priors match the data, e.g., smooth dense surfaces or challenging camera motion. Ex-tending our approach to perspective cameras and lightweight spatial or temporal priors is a natural future direction. 

Closing Remarks. This work shows that shape-space NRS f M factorization need not depend on SDP cone solvers or ADMM dual variables for accurate rotation and shape recovery. It is shown that we can retain the well-founded algebraic principle of NRS f M factorization while making its computations simple, scalable, and accelerator-friendly by replacing Gram-matrix cone optimization with factor-side differentiable correction, and replacing ADMM with an unrolled spectral-proximal solver. More broadly, the paper points to a practical direction for geometric vision, i.e., modernize the solver while preserving the algebraic principle. References

orders of magnitude slower. Empirically, our unrolled solver achieves identical objective values with less runtime and lower memory overhead, specifically for dense datasets. 

11.3. Discussion 

For typical benchmarks ( F ≈ 300 , P ≈ 30 − 100 , K ≤ 13 ), the proposed approach runs in a few seconds on NVIDIA A6000 GPU or Apple M3 processor, compared to several minutes for SDP/ADMM pipelines on CPU/GPU’s. Our introduced improvement transforms NRS f M matrix factor-ization approach from a slow, research-oriented method into a practical and scalable tool for non-rigid deformable object 3D reconstruction so that it can further be used in down-stream computer vision tasks which at present is mostly machine-learning driven. ## 13. Additional Discussion and Results 

1. Sequence-wise accuracy tradeoffs. We use a sequen-tial rotation-then-shape design to preserve the main goal of the solver. A coordinate-descent refinement that alternates between camera and shape updates may further improve accuracy on some sequences, especially when the initial camera estimate is imperfect, but it would reintroduce an outer iterative loop and increase runtime. We therefore treat such refinement as an optional accuracy-oriented extension rather than part of the fast no-cone, no-dual solver. 

2. Accuracy limit: coordinate descent. The sequence-wise behavior of our apporach is consistent with the priors used by competing methods. Our solver performs strongly when the factor-side rotation estimate is stable and the low-rank shape-space prior is sufficient, e.g., Walking and Stretch. Larger gaps appear on sequences such as Drink, Dance, and Shark, where methods such as TSM and OPM benefit from additional temporal, Procrustean, rotation-averaging, or deformation priors. Our method deliberately avoids these priors to keep the solver fast and broadly applicable, which explains the observed accuracy-runtime tradeoff.
