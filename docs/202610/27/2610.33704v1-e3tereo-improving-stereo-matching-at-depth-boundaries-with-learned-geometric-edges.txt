# E3Stereo: Improving Stereo Matching at Depth Boundaries with Learned Geometric Edges 

## Qi Xiong 1 Qinghua Zhao 2,∗ Yongqiang Xie 11Xidian University 2Hefei University

## Abstract 

Depth boundaries remain a major source of error in stereo matching. For IGEV on Scene Flow, geometric edge pixels account for only 6% of evaluated pixels but about half of the total absolute disparity error. Photometric edges can respond to texture changes without corresponding depth discontinuities, making them unreliable cues for boundary refinement. We propose E 3Stereo, which learns geomet-ric edges from disparity supervision and combines these boundary cues with the backbone disparity and image de-tails to predict full-resolution disparity corrections. The same edge map also emphasizes boundary errors during training, while joint optimization allows the edge predic-tor to adapt to disparity refinement. On the Scene Flow dataset, E 3Stereo reduces end-point error (EPE) at depth boundaries by 9.5% ∼ 14 .3% across RAFT-Stereo, IGEV, and MonSter, while also reducing overall EPE. After fine-tuning, our IGEV-based model improves upon IGEV on all eleven reported metrics across ETH3D, KITTI 2012, and KITTI 2015. The source code will be made publicly avail-able upon acceptance. 

## 1. Introduction 

Stereo matching estimates disparity by finding pixel corre-spondences between rectified image pairs of the same scene, providing the geometric basis for recovering scene depth. It supports applications such as autonomous driving and 3D reconstruction [10, 27]. Recent stereo methods have improved disparity estima-tion [7, 13, 17, 19, 22, 40]. Previous work has addressed boundary accuracy through image-guided disparity refine-ment [18], joint stereo matching and edge detection [32], and supervision that accounts for multiple disparity modes at edge pixels [45]. Nevertheless, our evaluation of seven existing stereo methods on Scene Flow [25] shows that dis-parity errors remain higher at depth boundaries than in non-boundary regions. We propose E 3Stereo, in which GeoEdgeNet learns to predict geometric edges using labels derived from ground-truth disparity, without requiring addi-tional edge annotations. Boundary locations alone do not specify the direction or magnitude of disparity corrections. GeoRestore there-fore combines the predicted edges with the backbone dis-parity and full-resolution image details to estimate a dis-parity residual. The same edge map also increases dispar-ity error penalties at predicted boundaries during training. Rather than freezing a separately pretrained edge predictor, we jointly optimize edge prediction and disparity estima-tion so that the edge representation remains supervised by geometry while adapting to its use in disparity refinement. On the Scene Flow dataset, E 3Stereo reduces EPE at depth boundaries by 13.4%, 14.3%, and 9.5% for RAFT-Stereo [22], IGEV, and MonSter, respectively, while lower-ing their overall EPE from 0.72 to 0.67, 0.47 to 0.43, and 0.37 to 0.35 pixels. For example, ETH3D Bad1.0 (Noc) decreases from 1.12% to 0.48%, and KITTI 2015 D1-all (All) decreases from 1.59% to 1.53%. 

## 2. Related Work 

Recent stereo matching methods improve disparity estima-tion through cost-volume construction, iterative refinement, and auxiliary priors. 

Cost-Volume-Based Stereo Matching. Cost-volume-based methods construct matching costs from learned im-age features and aggregate them to estimate disparity [1, 4, 15, 16, 39, 47]. Representative approaches include PSM-Net [4], which captures multi-scale context through spatial pyramid pooling; GwcNet [15], which constructs group-wise correlation volumes; and ACVNet [39], which uses at-tention to filter matching information. Coarse-to-fine meth-ods further reduce computation by progressively narrowing the disparity search range [11, 30]. Related transformer-based approaches model dense correspondences through at-tention [14, 21, 44]. Other aggregation and correlation de-signs exploit local structure to improve matching and detail recovery, including adaptive aggregation in AANet [43], bi-lateral aggregation in BANet [41], and motif correlations in MoCha-Stereo [5]. We use learned geometric edges to guide disparity refinement. 

Iterative Stereo Matching. Iterative methods progres-sively refine disparity estimates through repeated up-dates [9, 19, 22, 33, 40, 42, 46, 50]. RAFT-Stereo [22] uses a recurrent update operator with multi-level correla-tion features, while IGEV and IGEV++ [40, 42] incorpo-rate geometry encoding volumes to improve initialization and subsequent updates. CREStereo [19] adopts cascaded recurrent refinement, and other methods improve match-ing representations or the update process [9, 46, 48, 50]. Selective-Stereo [34] and DLNR [49] further exploit fre-quency information to recover disparity details. A related optimization-based approach, NMRF-Stereo [12], learns a Markov random field to preserve sharp disparity transi-tions. Our method uses predicted geometric edges for full-resolution disparity refinement and training. 

Prior-Guided Stereo Matching. Auxiliary priors pro-vide complementary information for stereo matching in ambiguous regions. Monocular depth and pretrained vi-sual representations have been incorporated to strengthen matching features and disparity estimation [2, 6, 7, 13, 17, 23, 38], while selective expert models improve robust-ness across diverse scenes [36]. LoS [20] exploits local structure, and StereoNet [18] uses image-guided refine-ment to recover high-resolution disparity. ADL [45] mod-els multiple disparity modes at edge pixels during training. EdgeStereo [32] uses photometric edges to guide disparity estimation and regularization. However, appearance con-tours can occur within a continuous surface and need not correspond to depth discontinuities. Our framework learns geometric edges from disparity supervision and uses the same predicted map for residual disparity refinement and boundary-focused training. 

## 3. Method 

Improving disparity at depth boundaries requires identify-ing relevant boundaries, converting these cues into disparity corrections, and optimizing the cues for their downstream use. As shown in Fig. 2, E 3Stereo addresses these re-quirements through a shared geometric edge map. GeoEd-geNet learns boundary cues from disparity supervision, and GeoRestore combines them with the backbone disparity and image details to predict full-resolution residual cor-rections. Joint training retains geometric edge supervision while using the same map to guide refinement and empha-size boundary errors.

3.3. Joint Training 

A separately pretrained and frozen edge predictor provides fixed guidance but cannot adapt its parameters to dispar-ity refinement. ## 4. Experiments 

4.1. Experimental Setup 

Datasets. We use Scene Flow [25] for pre-training and evaluation, with 35,454 training stereo pairs and 4,370 test stereo pairs providing dense ground-truth disparity. The real-world datasets are ETH3D [29], Middlebury 2014 [28], KITTI 2015 [26], and KITTI 2012 [10]. 

Metrics. On the Scene Flow dataset, we report end-point error (EPE) and EPE at depth boundaries ( EPE Edge ). For fine-tuned test-set evaluation, we submit predicted dispar-ity maps to the official evaluation servers and report the re-turned Bad1.0 and RMSE metrics for ETH3D, D1-bg and D1-all for KITTI 2015, and Out-2 and Out-3 for KITTI 2012. The Middlebury comparison uses the official Bad2.0 and Bad4.0 metrics, but our official test results are not re-ported in this version. Zero-shot errors are computed locally using thresholds of 3 pixels on KITTI, 2 pixels on Middle-bury at quarter and half resolution, and 1 pixel on ETH3D. To assess the quality of geometric edge guidance, we also evaluate edge predictions against the geometric edge labels using precision, recall, and F1, with a matching tol-erance of one pixel. Precision and recall measure the frac-tions of predicted and labeled edge pixels that are matched, respectively, and F1 is their harmonic mean. ## 5. Conclusion 

We presented E 3Stereo, which uses a shared geometric edge map learned from disparity supervision to guide full-resolution residual disparity refinement and edge-weighted supervision. Our analysis quantifies the concentration of disparity errors at depth boundaries. On Scene Flow, E3Stereo reduces boundary EPE by 9.5% –14 .3% across RAFT-Stereo, IGEV, and MonSter, while also reducing overall EPE. After fine-tuning, its IGEV-based implemen-tation improves upon IGEV on all eleven reported metrics across ETH3D, KITTI 2012, and KITTI 2015. Direct trans-fer from Scene Flow to real-world datasets remains a limi-tation, despite the improvements obtained after fine-tuning. References
