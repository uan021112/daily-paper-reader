# SG-Splat: Stable Geometry Learning under Pose Uncertainty for Pose-Free 3D Gaussian Splatting 

Xiaohan Lu, Jiaqi Zhang, Weijia Jiang, Siwei Ma, Fellow, IEEE 

Abstract —3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has become a promis-ing representation for real-time novel view synthesis and 3D scene reconstruction. For practical deployment, recent studies have explored pose-free feed-forward 3DGS, which reconstructs Gaussian representations directly from unposed input images. However, these methods remain sensitive to residual pose errors, which perturb canonical-space Gaussian transformation, intro-ducing noisy supervision for geometry and attribute learning. To address this problem, we present SG-Splat, a pose-free feed-forward 3DGS framework that improves robustness to pose uncertainty and stabilizes the learning of Gaussian primitives. SG-Splat decouples geometry estimation and Gaussian attribute prediction into two dedicated branches, reducing error coupling between pose-sensitive geometry and Gaussian attributes. We further introduce a coarse-to-fine refinement pipeline, consisting of Flow-Guided Pose Refinement (FGPR) and Residual-Guided Geometry Correction (RGC), to improve geometric alignment before Gaussian attribute reconstruction. Specifically, FGPR refines camera poses with flow-based correlation features and differentiable bundle adjustment. RGC then corrects initial point maps using depth residuals derived from FGPR, producing more reliable Gaussian centers. To stabilize training, we introduce a dual-path strategy that mixes ground-truth and predicted poses for canonical transformation. It provides clean supervision for Gaussian attributes while improving geometry robustness to residual pose errors. Extensive experiments on DL3DV and RealEstate10K show that SG-Splat achieves state-of-the-art per-formance in both novel view synthesis and pose estimation. 

Index Terms —Feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting, Pose-free, Novel View synthesis, Pose Estimation. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) [4] has become a powerful representation for real-time photorealistic rendering and high-fidelity scene reconstruction, with growing potential in world modeling [5], autonomous driving [6], and virtual reality [7]. However, conventional 3DGS is limited by its reliance on per-scene optimization and calibrated poses, which limits scalable and efficient 3D reconstruction. This has motivated recent interest in pose-free feed-forward 3DGS, which seeks to predict camera poses and Gaussian parameters directly from input images in a single forward pass. However, directly constructing a canonical Gaussian representation becomes increasingly challenging as the number of input views grows [10], due to the difficulty of maintaining cross-view geometric consistency without explicit pose-guided aggregation. To scale to multi-view settings, recent methods such as AnySplat [1] and WorldMirror [2] predict per-view Gaussian primitives in camera coordinate systems and transform them into a canonical space using camera poses of each view, as illustrated in Fig. 1(a) and (b). At inference time, this transformation must rely on predicted poses, whose errors directly affect the alignment of Gaussian primitives across views. trivial, as the poses used for transformation affect both op-timization stability and the consistency between training and inference. AnySplat uses predicted poses for transformation during training, improving consistency with inference but introducing scene-dependent pose perturbations that desta-bilize the learning of geometry and Gaussian attributes. In contrast, WorldMirror uses ground-truth poses for canonical transformation, which stabilizes Gaussian parameter learning but introduces a discrepancy between training and inference. In addition, both methods directly couple pose supervision with Gaussian reconstruction, introducing competing optimization objectives. This coupling becomes especially detrimental when residual pose errors perturb the canonical transformation used for rendering supervision. To remove this coupling, recent methods [3], [11] adopt a self-supervised formulation. As shown in Fig. 1(c), they discard explicit pose supervision and optimize Gaussian reconstruction solely through rendering supervision. Although this design removes the need for pose supervision, it still relies on predicted poses for canonical transformation during training. Consequently, pose errors still perturb the optimization of Gaussian geometry and attributes. In addition, without direct 3D constraints, these methods may produce visually plausible but geometrically unreliable reconstructions [12], restricting their applicability in geometry-sensitive tasks. These observations reveal a key challenge: how can pose-free feed-forward 3DGS effectively exploit pose supervision during training while maintaining stable learning under residual pose errors? Although YoNoSplat [13] mixes pre-dicted and ground-truth poses during training, predicted poses are sampled with a low probability. As a result, this strategy only weakly exposes the model to inference-time pose errors and still allows noisy supervision to affect Gaussian attributes when predicted poses are selected. To address this challenge, we propose SG-Splat, a pose-free feed-forward 3DGS framework with decoupled geometry and attribute branches, as shown in Fig. 1(d). This design sepa-rates geometry and camera estimation from Gaussian attribute prediction, reducing the interference between pose errors and attribute learning. We further introduce a progressive refine-ment pipeline with Flow-Guided Pose Refinement (FGPR) and Residual-Guided Geometry Correction (RGC). FGPR refines the initial camera poses using flow-based correlation features and differentiable bundle adjustment (DBA). RGC corrects the initial point maps using depth residuals produced by FGPR and intermediate geometry features. The refined poses stabilize canonical transformations, and the corrected point maps improve Gaussian center accuracy. Together, they reduce the discrepancy between training and inference and improve the consistency between the refined camera poses and the Gaussian centers. In addition, we design a pose-aware dual-path training strategy that prevents the attribute branch from overfitting to alignments corrupted by residual pose errors. Specifically, either ground-truth poses or predicted poses are selected to transform Gaussian primitives into a canonical coordinate system during training. When ground-truth poses are used, the model learns geometry reconstruction and Gaussian attribute prediction under reliable alignment. When predicted poses are used, we mainly optimize the geometry branch and pose estimation modules, preventing the attribute branch from over-fitting to residual pose errors. This asymmetric optimization stabilizes Gaussian attribute learning and improves geometry robustness under pose uncertainty. Our contributions are sum-marized as follows:

By separating geometry and camera estimation from Gaussian attribute prediction, the framework suppresses error propagation from inaccurate pose estimates and improves the reliability of Gaussian parameter generation.  

> •

We introduce a coarse-to-fine refinement pipeline consist-ing of FGPR and RGC to enhance pose and Gaussian alignment. FGPR iteratively refines camera poses by combining flow-based correlation features with differen-tiable bundle adjustment. RGC then corrects local point maps using depth residuals from FGPR, improving the consistency between refined camera poses and Gaussian centers.  

> •

This strategy uses ground-truth poses to provide reliable attribute supervi-sion, while using predicted poses to regularize geometry learning against scene-dependent pose errors. II. R ELATED WORK 

A. Novel View Synthesis with Feed-Forward 3D Gaussian Splatting 

Early image-based rendering methods [14]–[16] synthesize novel views by resampling or blending images captured from nearby viewpoints. These methods demonstrated the possi-bility of photorealistic view synthesis without fully explicit 3D reconstruction, but they often require dense image cap-ture, calibrated cameras, or approximate geometry to handle large viewpoint changes and occlusions. With the advent of deep learning, learning-based NVS methods [17], [18]learn the geometry of scenes and appearance priors from data, improving flexibility under sparse-view inputs. NeRF [19] represents a scene as a continuous radiance field optimized from posed images and renders novel views via volumetric ray marching, which incurs substantial computational cost. To improve efficiency, follow-up works [20]–[22] introduced compact encodings or hybrid scene representations to accel-erate rendering, but they still struggle to achieve real-time performance. Motivated by this limitation, 3DGS [4] and its variants [23]–[25] adopt explicit Gaussian primitives for scene representation, achieving high-fidelity novel view synthesis with real-time rendering efficiency. However, both NeRF and 3DGS pipelines typically rely on per-scene optimization. Re-cent methods extend 3DGS to sparse input views by introduc-ing diffusion priors [26] or geometric constraints [27]. This has motivated recent feed-forward methods for NeRF [28]–[30] as well as feed-forward 3DGS methods [31]–[34]. B. 3D Foundation Models for Geometry and Pose Priors 

Recent 3D foundation models have substantially advanced feed-forward geometry and pose estimation from unposed images. DUSt3R [40] reformulates multi-view reconstruction as point map regression from unconstrained image pairs. MASt3R [41] and its variants [42], [43] further improve dense matching accuracy and geometric consistency. Building on this line of work, VGGT [44], π3 [45], and Depth Anything 3 (DA3) [46] directly predict camera poses, point maps, depth maps, and geometric tracks from a few to hundreds of views in a single forward pass, offering scalable geometry priors for downstream applications. These foundation models have also been increasingly adopted in feed-forward 3DGS. Splatt3R [9] and NoPoS-plat [8] build upon MASt3R to obtain geometric priors and predict Gaussians in a global coordinate system from unposed images. AnySplat [1] uses VGGT as a teacher to super-vise pose and geometry prediction during training. YoNoS-plat [13] extends this paradigm with a π3-based architecture and introduces an intrinsic condition embedding to handle unknown camera intrinsics. AirSplat [47] leverages DA3 as a frozen geometry foundation model and trains a Gaussian generation module on top of its priors. Collectively, these methods demonstrate that 3D foundation models have become a powerful source of geometric priors for pose-free feed-forward 3DGS. 

C. Pose-Free Feed-Forward 3D Gaussian Splatting 

Pose-free feed-forward 3DGS reconstructs Gaussian rep-resentations directly from unposed images, avoiding camera calibration at inference time. Existing methods mainly fall into two categories. The first line exploits 3D foundation models to provide geometry and camera priors. Methods built on DUSt3R [48] or MASt3R [8]–[10], [49], [50] recover geom-etry and camera poses before Gaussian prediction. However, these methods are mostly tailored to sparse input views, which limits their scalability to denser multi-view reconstruction. Recent methods [1], [13], [47], [51] improve scalability by using visual geometry foundation models that jointly predict point maps and camera poses in a single forward pass [44]– [46]. However, their Gaussian reconstruction remains sensitive to pose accuracy, because residual pose errors can perturb the transformation of Gaussian centers and introduce noisy supervision into the optimization of Gaussian geometry and attributes. Another line of work removes 3D supervision and trains pose-free models with self-supervised rendering objectives [3], [11], [52], [53]. Although these methods avoid explicit pose supervision, pose estimation, geometry prediction, and Gaus-sian attribute generation are optimized in a coupled manner through rendering losses. Consequently, pose and geometry errors may be absorbed by Gaussian attributes, leading to visually plausible but geometrically unreliable reconstruc-tions [12]. In contrast, our work focuses on the practical setting where explicit 3D supervision is available. We study how to ef-fectively exploit 3D foundation priors for dense-view pose-free feed-forward 3DGS while preventing pose-corrupted su-pervision from destabilizing Gaussian geometry and attribute learning. To avoid corrupting attribute learning, we detach the rendering gradients from the attribute branch and use them only to optimize the geometry branch. This asymmetric design combines the advantages of both paths. The ground-truth-pose path provides reliable supervi-sion for Gaussian attribute prediction, while the predicted-pose path exposes the geometry branch to the pose distribution used during inference. By alternating between them, the proposed strategy reduces the discrepancy between training and infer-ence and prevents the attribute branch from absorbing pose-induced noise.

D. Ablation Study 

We conduct ablation experiments on DL3DV under the 24-view setting. The base model adopts the same architecture but excludes the proposed Flow-Guided Pose Refinement (FGPR) and Residual-Guided Geometry Correction (RGC) modules, JOURNAL OF L ATEX CLASS FILES, VOL. 14, NO. 8, AUGUST 2021 11 

and is trained with ground-truth (GT) poses. We progressively incorporate FGPR, different pose selection strategies, and RGC to evaluate their individual contributions to rendering performance. 

a) Effect of FGPR: As shown in Tab. IV, introducing FGPR alone (FGPR*) leads to only marginal improvements over the base model. This suggests that while pose refinement is beneficial, its effectiveness is limited when optimized in isolation with all other components frozen, and cannot fully compensate for pose inaccuracies during training. 

b) Effect of Dual-Branch Pose Selection Strategy: We further investigate different pose selection strategies for train-ing FGPR. Training with predicted poses (Pred Pose) leads to a noticeable performance degradation, indicating that noisy pose estimates introduce instability and hinder effective opti-mization. In contrast, using only GT poses achieves strong performance but suffers from exposure bias, as the model is not exposed to pose perturbations during training. Our proposed dual-branch strategy (Mix Pose), which adaptively combines predicted and GT poses, achieves the best results. This demonstrates that jointly leveraging accurate poses and realistic pose perturbations effectively reduces the discrepancy between training and inference, resulting in more robust ren-dering quality. 

c) Effect of RGC: Building upon FGPR with the pro-posed dual-path training strategy, further incorporating RGC consistently improves all metrics. This suggests that point-map correction driven by depth residuals improves the reliability of Gaussian centers, thereby enhancing rendering fidelity. 

d) Effect of the Number of Iterations: Tab. V evaluates the effect of the number of FGPR iterations T . A single iteration ( T = 1 ) yields suboptimal performance, indicating that one-step refinement is insufficient to fully correct pose errors. Increasing T to 2 significantly improves all metrics, demonstrating that iterative refinement is effective in enhanc-ing pose quality and rendering accuracy. Increasing T from 2 to 3 slightly improves LPIPS and AUC@5 ◦, but reduces PSNR and SSIM while increasing computational cost. We therefore set T = 2 as a practical trade-off between reconstruction quality, pose accuracy, and efficiency. V. C ONCLUSION 

In this paper, we presented SG-Splat, a pose-free feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting framework for robust recon-struction under inaccurate pose estimation. To reduce the impact of residual pose errors, SG-Splat decouples geometry estimation from Gaussian attribute prediction and introduces a coarse-to-fine refinement pipeline with Flow-Guided Pose Refinement and Residual-Guided Geometry Correction. We further proposed a dual-path training strategy that mixes ground-truth and predicted poses with asymmetric optimiza-tion, improving robustness to pose errors while maintaining reliable pose supervision. Extensive experiments on DL3DV and RealEstate10K show that SG-Splat achieves improved novel view synthesis and relative pose estimation under both sparse and dense input-view settings. Despite these improvements, several limitations remain. First, our method assumes known camera intrinsics, limit-ing its applicability to in-the-wild images with unavailable or unreliable intrinsics. Second, pixel-aligned feed-forward reconstruction tends to produce view-dependent and redundant Gaussian primitives, resulting in less compact representations. Third, high-resolution reconstruction still incurs substantial training and inference costs. Future work will explore intrinsic-free reconstruction, more efficient high-resolution architec-tures, and non-pixel-aligned Gaussian prediction to obtain more compact and effective 3DGS representations.
