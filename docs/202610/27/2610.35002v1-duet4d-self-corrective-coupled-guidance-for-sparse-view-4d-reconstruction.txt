Abstract 

Sparse-view 4D reconstruction is highly under-constrained when only a few cameras are available. Generative priors can recover missing appearances, but video models are com-putationally impractical for iterative reconstruction, whereas single-view models produce temporally inconsistent pseudo-observations. Moreover, directly absorbing uncertain genera-tive outputs into Gaussian optimization often creates redun-dant primitives and floating artifacts. To address these chal-lenges, we propose DUET4D, a self-corrective coupled guid-ance framework for sparse-view 4D Gaussian splatting. The core of our approach is a self-corrective framework where the 4D representation guides generative prior for spatio-temporal consistent supervisions. We first construct sequential align-ments from current Gaussian evidence to steer the latent restoration. Then we use Sequential Fidelity Resampling to lift generative supervisions into Gaussian space to selectively dis-tinguish repeatedly supported primitives. The corrected rep-resentation then provides more reliable Gaussian evidence for the next restoration cycle. Extensive experiments demonstrate that DUET4D significantly improves rendering quality and rendering efficiency.

scenes with high visual quality (Cai et al. 2022; Du et al. 2021; Fang et al. 2022; Kratimenos, Lei, and Daniilidis 2024; Li et al. 2022). However, most existing methods rely on suf-ficiently dense multi-view observations (Shi et al. 2026; Pan et al. 2026; Zhou et al. 2026). When the input is reduced to only a few cameras, reconstruction quality degrades severely. Many regions of the scene are never directly observed, while dynamic objects are only partially constrained across time. As a result, sparse-view 4D Gaussian reconstruction often suffers from distorted artifacts, floating Gaussians, and tem-porally inconsistent motion. Existing works (Shi et al. 2026; Zhou et al. 2026) introduce texture regularization and Gaus-sian decaying to constrain sparse-view reconstruction. How-ever, they are insufficient to recover stable dynamic appear-ance under large viewpoint drifts. To introduce stronger generative priors, Sparse-Cam4D (Pan et al. 2026) utilizes video diffusion models (VDM) to synthesize pseudo-observations, but iteratively querying a VDM is computationally impractical for multi-view 4D optimization. This presents the first challenge: single-view diffusion models (SDMs) provide a more effi-cient alternative, yet their independent predictions lack cross-view and temporal awareness, leading to temporal incoher-ent motions. However, temporal coherence alone does not make pseudo-supervision uniformly reliable. There remains a spatial drift between the generative supervisions and the sparse observations. Blindly densifying these locations leads to redundant primitives and floating Gaussians. A second challenge is therefore to selectively lift pseudo supervision into reliable representation-side decisions. arXiv:2610.35002v1 [cs.CV] 26 Oct 2026 To address these challenges, we propose DUET4D, a self-corrective framework that couples a frozen single-view diffu-sion model with an evolving 4D Gaussian representation. In-stead of relying on a computationally heavy VDM or directly using independently restored views as supervision, DUET4D exploits the current 4D Gaussians as a spatiotemporal cor-respondence carrier. The resulting Gaussian-derived align-ments and confidence estimates construct a reliable anchor that steers the latent denoising direction of a frozen SDM, enabling temporally coherent pseudo-observations without training or invoking an additional video model. Crucially, DUET4D does not indiscriminately absorb these generated observations into the representation. The Sequential Fidelity Resampling distinguishes reliable recon-struction deficits from persistent inconsistencies by jointly considering confidence-gated pseudo supervisions and real observations, and then lifts these 2D signals to individual 3D primitives. The updated representation consequently pro-vides cleaner correspondence evidence for the next restora-tion round, forming a closed self-corrective cycle that im-proves reconstruction fidelity while suppressing redundant Gaussians. Our contributions are summarized as follows: 

• We propose DUET4D, a unified framework for sparse-view 4D reconstruction, establishing a self-corrective framework where the 4D representation guides genera-tive prior for spatio-temporal consistent supervisions. 

• We introduce a training-free anchor-driven latent steer-ing mechanism that uses Gaussian-derived correspon-dences to make a frozen SDM produce temporally co-herent pseudo-supervisions. 

• We design Sequential Fidelity Resampling to selectively lift confidence-supported pseudo-view discrepancies into Gaussian densification while pruning primitives that re-main inconsistent with real observations, producing a cleaner and more compact representation. 

## 2 Related Work 

Sparse-view Dynamic Reconstruction Dynamic scene reconstruction requires temporally coherent modeling of ap-pearance and motion. While NeRF-based methods rely on time-conditioned fields or feature factorization (Mildenhall et al. 2021; Pumarola et al. 2021; Park et al. 2021a,b; Cao and Johnson 2023; Fridovich-Keil et al. 2023),4DGS-based approaches significantly improve efficiency via deformation fields, tracking, or direct 4D representations (Kerbl et al. 2023; Yang et al. 2024a; Li et al. 2024; Luiten et al. 2024; Lin et al. 2024; Wu et al. 2024; Yang et al. 2024b; Duan et al. 2024). However, these methods typically require dense, synchronized multi-view videos and degrade heavily under sparse camera setups. To tackle the under-constrained sparse-view setting, prior works exploit category-specific priors like SMPL for humans (Peng et al. 2021a,b; Weng et al. 2022; Hu, Hu, and Liu 2024) or monocular depth and tracking cues (Lei et al. 2025; Wang et al. 2025; Pradeep et al. 2013). Though providing useful temporal constraints, recent methods (Shi et al. 2026; Zhou et al. 2026)still suffer from unstable ap-pearance under large viewpoint changes. 

Diffusion Models for Novel-View Synthesis Diffusion models serve as powerful priors for 3D/4D generation. While object-centric models (Liu et al. 2023, 2024; Shi et al. 2024; Long et al. 2024) achieve strong multi-view consistency, they cannot directly handle scene-level dynamic reconstruction. Recent camera-controlled video diffusion models (Wang et al. 2024; He et al. 2024; Xu et al. 2024a; Yu et al. 2024; Bai et al. 2025; Wu et al. 2025) synthesize novel-view dynamic observations, offering richer supervision. However, they rely on expensive multi-frame denoising and often produce cross-view or temporal inconsistencies that harm 4D optimization. To incorporate diffusion into sparse dynamic reconstruction, recent methods such as Diffuman4D (Jin et al. 2025) and SparseCam4D (Pan et al. 2026) query video diffusion models for better rendering quality, which take 40 hours for a single dynamic scene, facing a strict trade-off between consistency and efficiency. We address this through a unified framework that constructing a self-corrective coupled pipeline where the 4D representation guide generative prior for spatio-temporal consistent supervisions.

## 4 Experiments 

4.1 Experiment Setup 

Datasets. Following previous works (Pan et al. 2026; Li et al. 2024), we evaluate the proposed DUET4D on three widely-used sparse-view 4D reconstruction benchmarks: Neu3d (Li et al. 2022), Nvidia Dynamic (Yoon et al. 2020) and Google Immersive Dataset (Broxton et al. 2020) dataset. 

Evaluation Metrics. Following standard practices for novel view synthesis, we employ established quantita-tive metrics to assess rendering quality, including PSNR ,

SSIM (Wang et al. 2004), and LPIPS (Zhang et al. 2018). We additionally report the temporal-PSNR ( tPSNR ) to eval-uate the temporal consistency for the ablation study. To fur-ther evaluate rendering efficiency, we report the number of Gaussians ( NGS ) and rendering frames per second ( FPS ). 

Baselines. We compare DUET4D with representative dynamic Gaussian reconstruction methods, including 4DGS (Wu et al. 2024), 4D-Rotor (Duan et al. 2024), Real-Time4DGS (Yang et al. 2024b), Ex4DGS (Lee et al. 2024), and Grid4D (Xu et al. 2024b), as well as recent sparse-view methods 4C4D (Zhou et al. 2026), and SparseCam4D (Pan et al. 2026). For methods whose official sparse-view re-sults are unavailable, we reproduce them using the same input cameras and evaluation protocol. Notably, Sparse-Cam4D adopts a dedicated preprocessing pipeline. To ex-clude improvements caused by data processing, we addi-tionally construct a strictly controlled comparison. Specifi-cally, DUET4D ∗ uses exactly the same preprocessed inputs as SparseCam4D, while all model components and optimiza-tion settings remain unchanged. 

4.2 Evaluation 

Quantitative Comparison. As reported in Table 1, DUET4D consistently achieves the highest average PSNR across all evaluated datasets. This performance is particularly notable in dynamic scenes exhibiting complex, non-rigid de-formations. The superiority of our approach is attributed to the self-corrective coupled guidance, which effectively constrains the restoration process and filters out unreliable pseudo-supervision. Consequently, DUET4D significantly boosts PSNR while maintaining highly competitive SSIM and LPIPS scores. Furthermore, to ensure a fair evaluation against SparseCam4D, we adopt an identical preprocessing pipeline. Our method not only outperforms SparseCam4D in reconstruction fidelity but also operates at a significantly higher frame rate. 

Qualitative Comparison. As illustrated in Fig. 3, whereas baseline methods inherently suffer from incomplete geome-try, blurred textures, or floating artifacts in heavily occluded dynamic regions, DUET4D produces sharper and tempo-rally coherent renderings. This robustness validates that our core designs—sequential anchor alignment, latent steering, and fidelity resampling—collectively cooperation to sup-press frame-wise drift, preserve high-frequency details, and eliminate weakly supported primitives. 

Efficiency and Compactness. Beyond synthesis quality, DUET4D demonstrates a significant advantage in both ren-dering efficiency and Gaussian compactness. As detailed in Table 1, our method achieves up to a 49 × acceleration in rendering speed compared to state-of-the-art baselines, re-markably utilizing only about 20% of their Gaussian primi-tive count on Neural 3D dataset. The compactness is driven by the proposed Sequential Fidelity Resampling strategy. Rather than blindly proliferating primitives to fit dynamic regions, this mechanism allocates capacity: it densifies areas that are repeatedly under-reconstructed while aggressively pruning primitives that are persistently inconsistent or re-dundant across time. This dynamic regularization balanced the trade-off between quality and speed, reducing both the storage overhead and rasterization cost without compromis-ing the fidelity of the reconstructed 4D dynamics.

Moderate parameter choices provide a better bal-ance between temporal consistency and spatial fidelity. Ex-cessive latent guidance may over-constrain the restoration process and weaken local appearance details. Similarly, a loose confidence threshold may introduce unreliable pseudo-supervision, while a strict threshold may discard useful correspondence evidence. The performance remains stable within a reasonable range, demonstrating the robustness of DUET4D and supporting the system design. 

4.4 Limitations 

DUET4D relies on the current Gaussian representation to construct spatiotemporal correspondences. When the initial geometry is severely corrupted, or when an object undergoes extremely fast motion and long-term occlusion, the avail-able Gaussian evidence may be insufficient to produce re-liable anchors. In such regions, the confidence mechanism conservatively falls back to independent single-view restora-tion, which limits error propagation but cannot fully recover content that is never observed. Future work may explore long-range correspondence aggregation and more efficient on-demand pseudo-view scheduling. 

## 5 Conclusion 

We presented DUET4D, a self-corrective framework for sparse-view 4D Gaussian reconstruction. This design is mo-tivated by how to mitigate the spatiotemporal inconsistency inherent in the generative prior to improve the reconstruction quality. By using the evolving Gaussian representation to guide temporally coherent restoration with a frozen single-view diffusion model, and selectively converting confidence-filtered discrepancies into Gaussian densification and prun-ing policies, DUET4D enables the generative prior and ex-plicit representation to progressively refine each other. Exten-sive experiments on three widely-used datasets demonstrate that our method improves novel-view rendering quality while maintaining high rendering efficiency and a compact Gaus-sian representation.
