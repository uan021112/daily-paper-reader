ABSTRACT
Existing neural 3D representations separately address continuous surface modeling,
efficient image synthesis, or object-specific compression. They do not jointly
provide the explicit surface queries required by ray tracing and a structured
output space that can be predicted across objects. We introduce NeuOctree,
a neural explicit representation that directly predicts whether and where a ray
intersects a surface, together with its normal and albedo.
NeuOctree stores
shared features at the corners of sparse octree voxels and interpolates them into
a continuous local field. A lightweight decoder with recurrent intersection-guided
sampling refines each query near the predicted surface, enabling direct surface
rendering without mesh extraction or texture baking and accommodating open,
non-watertight geometry.
The same octree structure supports both compact
per-object optimization and a shared octree CNN that constructs renderable assets
from colored point clouds without per-object fitting. Across seven meshes and
four storage budgets, NeuOctree achieves the lowest positional error in 24 of
28 comparisons while retaining competitive normal accuracy, and substantially
improves joint geometry–appearance reconstruction over N-BVH and VQAD.
The feedforward model constructs a representation in 0.05 seconds and transfers
from Objaverse to ShapeNet without retraining. We further demonstrate ray-traced
rendering and image-conditioned generation, showing that NeuOctree provides
a common output space for neural inference and direct surface rendering. Code
is available at https://github.com/octree-nn/neural-octree.

INTRODUCTION
Learning compact 3D assets requires a representation that connects neural prediction with geometric
computation. A ray-tracing renderer needs to determine where a ray intersects an object and recover
the normal and material attributes at that point. A network trained across objects benefits from a

structured output space in which to predict geometry and appearance. Bringing these requirements
together would allow learned assets to pass directly from a neural network to a ray-tracing renderer.
Existing representations address different parts of this problem. Signed distance fields (Park et al.,
2019; Chabra et al., 2020) and occupancy fields (Mescheder et al., 2019) encode surfaces as level sets
of learned scalar functions. NeRF (Mildenhall et al., 2020) jointly models density and radiance, with
hash encodings (M¨uller et al., 2022) and sparse voxel structures (Yu et al., 2021; Fridovich-Keil et al.,
2022; Liu et al., 2020b) reducing the cost of field evaluation and rendering. SDF-based volume ren-
dering (Wang et al., 2021) improves surface reconstruction, while Gaussian splatting and sparse voxel
rasterization (Huang et al., 2024; Sun et al., 2025) enable efficient image synthesis. These advances
illustrate the value of compact, spatially organized representations. However, density or occupancy
values do not directly specify the intersection and material attributes required by a surface ray tracer:
these quantities must be recovered through additional surface localization or geometry extraction.
Neural compression methods bring learned representations closer to conventional surface rendering.
Neural Geometry Fields (Sivaram et al., 2024) encode detailed geometry through displacements
of coarse mesh patches, while N-BVH (Weier et al., 2024) directly predicts ray intersections and
appearance within a bounding volume hierarchy. Both construct their representations through object-
specific optimization. Building on the direct ray-query interface, we seek a representation whose
geometry and appearance can also be predicted by a shared network across objects.
We introduce NeuOctree, a neural explicit shape representation that couples direct ray–surface
intersection prediction with an octree-based sparse voxel structure. The octree organizes local
geometry, skips empty space during traversal, and provides a structured domain for 3D neural
networks. Adjacent voxels share corner features, defining a continuous feature field through trilinear
interpolation. A lightweight, shared MLP decodes the ray and interpolated features into a hit
probability, intersection position, surface normal, and albedo. These outputs supply the surface
queries needed for ray tracing without mesh extraction or texture baking. Because the representation
does not require an inside–outside labeling, it also accommodates open, non-watertight surfaces.
Accurate intersection prediction requires querying the right part of the feature field. Features sampled
only at a voxel’s boundary may be far from the surface, and rays observing the same surface point can
enter the voxel at different locations. We therefore introduce a recurrent sampling strategy that uses
predicted intersections to guide subsequent feature queries. This refinement brings queries closer to
the surface and encourages rays from different views to access features near the same location. The
ablation shows improved intersection and normal accuracy over endpoint-only and uniform sampling.
The same structure enables us to amortize representation construction across objects. We build a
feedforward 3D U-Net on octree-based CNNs (Wang et al., 2017) to predict voxel-corner features
from colored point clouds. Averaging predictions at shared vertices produces a consistent feature
field, which is decoded by the shared intersection MLP. Training the feature extractor and decoder
jointly with rendering losses allows a single checkpoint to construct geometry and appearance for
new inputs without per-object optimization. NeuOctree thus supports both per-object compression
and feedforward construction within the same representation and renderer.
We evaluate per-object compression on seven meshes from Thingi10K and the Stanford 3D Scanning
Repository at four storage budgets. NeuOctree achieves the lowest positional error in 24 of 28
model–budget comparisons and competitive normal accuracy compared with geometry-only methods,
and significantly overperforms N-BVH and VQAD which jointly represent geometry and appearance.
On the separate feedforward task, a shared checkpoint constructs a representation in 0.05 seconds
with lower intersection error, compared with approximately 3.5 minutes for N-BVH fitting under
the reported settings. The same checkpoint, trained on Objaverse, transfers to ShapeNet without
retraining. We further demonstrate direct ray-traced rendering with reshading, reflection, refraction,
and fisheye projection, and show image-conditioned generation of NeuOctree assets as a downstream
application. Our contributions are:
- A structured neural surface representation that jointly encodes geometry and appearance in
shared voxel-corner features and supports direct ray-traced rendering.
- Recurrent intersection refinement that adaptively queries the continuous feature field to improve
the accuracy of predicted surface intersections and normals.

RELATED WORK
Neural Surface and Radiance Representations.
Neural implicit fields provide a compact and
differentiable alternative to explicit geometry. Early methods represented shapes with coordinate-
based multilayer perceptrons, using signed distance functions (SDFs) (Park et al., 2019; Chabra et al.,
2020) or occupancy fields (Mescheder et al., 2019). Subsequent approaches introduced octrees and
adaptive spatial partitions (Takikawa et al., 2021; Martel et al., 2021), while neural radiance fields
jointly modeled density and appearance through volume rendering (Mildenhall et al., 2020). Octree
and hash-grid variants (Yu et al., 2021; Fridovich-Keil et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2020b; M¨uller et al.,
2022), SDF–density hybrids (Wang et al., 2021; Li et al., 2023), and explicit rasterization-based
representations (Huang et al., 2024; Sun et al., 2025) have substantially improved memory use or
image-rendering speed.
These methods nevertheless expose a mismatch with surface ray tracing. A density or occupancy
value does not directly specify a ray–surface intersection, normal, or material; obtaining these
quantities typically requires repeated field evaluations, surface localization, or mesh extraction. The
resulting geometric ambiguity can compromise fine detail, and level-set formulations often rely on an
inside–outside convention that is awkward for open or non-watertight surfaces. NeuOctree instead
makes the ray–surface query the representation’s primary interface: a shared decoder predicts the hit,
position, normal, and albedo from a ray and a continuous octree feature field. This design preserves
the compactness of neural representations while enabling direct ray-traced rendering without an
intermediate mesh or texture-baking stage.
Neural Mesh Compression.
Traditional mesh compression combines connectivity coding, coor-
dinate quantization, and simplification to reduce storage while preserving surface fidelity (Deering,
1995; Chow, 1997; Diaz-Gutierrez et al., 2005; Touma & Gotsman, 1998; Heckbert & Garland,
1999). Learning-based methods replace hand-designed operators with graph networks or lightweight
MLPs that upsample a coarse mesh and predict vertex displacements (Liu et al., 2020a; Chen et al.,
2023; Sivaram et al., 2024; Pentapati et al., 2025). Although effective for individual assets, these
approaches are generally optimized per mesh, focus primarily on geometry, and require a fixed coarse
topology; appearance is commonly handled by a separate texture-baking step. N-BVH (Weier et al.,
2024) is closer to our setting because it predicts ray intersections and appearance within a hierarchical
structure, but it also relies on object-specific optimization.
NeuOctree addresses these limitations with a sparse octree that supplies a global spatial prior and
shared voxel-corner features that provide local context. Geometry and appearance are decoded
through the same ray-query interface, so the representation can be optimized as a compact per-object
asset or predicted by a shared network across objects. The distinction is therefore not only a different
compression structure, but also a shift from per-mesh fitting to a renderable representation that
supports feedforward construction.

Structured 3D Learning and Feedforward Reconstruction.
Point-, voxel-, and sparse-voxel
networks have become standard tools for 3D perception and generation (Qi et al., 2017a;b; Li et al.,
2018; Maturana & Scherer, 2015; Wu et al., 2015; Wang et al., 2017; Graham et al., 2018; Choy
et al., 2019; Wang, 2023; Wu et al., 2024). Their structured spatial domains support scalable feature
extraction, but their outputs are typically occupancies, semantic features, or task-specific predictions
rather than a representation that can be queried directly by a surface renderer. NeuOctree uses the
same structured prior for a different purpose: an octree U-Net predicts features at voxel corners
from colored point clouds, and averaging shared vertices yields a consistent continuous field for
the ray-intersection decoder. This establishes a direct bridge between feedforward 3D learning and
surface rendering: one shared checkpoint constructs geometry and appearance without per-object
optimization, while the resulting representation remains suitable for ray tracing and can serve as a
native output space for 3D generation.

METHOD
Overview.
NeuOctree is built around a direct surface query. An asset is represented by an occupied
sparse octree and a continuous feature field defined on its voxel corners. Given a ray segment inside an
occupied voxel, a shared decoder predicts whether the ray hits the surface and, if so, the intersection
position, normal, and albedo. The same decoder is used for every voxel and every ray; only the octree
topology and its feature values vary across assets. This separation lets us use the representation in
two regimes: a feature table can be optimized for one asset, or an octree network can predict the
feature field in a single feedforward pass. In both cases the output is directly consumable by a surface
ray tracer and does not require mesh extraction or texture baking.

refinement state. A separate material branch uses the same spatial feature field and ray information
together with the current position and color state. Both branches are lightweight residual MLPs

The per-object geometry compression protocol uses the equivalent ray-projection parameterization,
which projects the predicted local point onto the current ray line. When the segment-bounded option
is enabled, this projection is restricted to the current segment. Both forms preserve the key property
that the decoder predicts a surface intersection rather than a density level set. Fourier position features
are not used in the reported configurations.
3.2
RECURRENT INTERSECTION REFINEMENT
Samples taken only at a voxel boundary can be far from the surface, and the boundary point depends
on viewing direction even when rays observe the same surface point. A single boundary query
therefore makes the decoder learn an unnecessarily view-dependent mapping. We use the predicted
intersection to place later queries in the continuous feature field.
Let qk be the k-th adaptive query. The decoder produces an intersection estimate bpk and the next
query is obtained by projecting this estimate back onto the current ray parameterization,
qk+1 = Πray(bpk),
where Πray denotes ray-line projection or its segment-bounded variant, depending on the chosen
position parameterization. The recurrent decoder can maintain a chained hidden state across iterations.
Our pooled variant, used for the reported compression and sampling experiments, encodes the entry
and exit points as ordinary members of the feature set, initializes the first interior query at the segment
midpoint, and then adds further adaptively placed queries from the preceding readout. The per-query
hidden increments are averaged before a shared output head predicts the final attributes. With three
adaptive iterations this gives five feature reads per voxel segment (entry, exit, and three adaptive
queries). The fixed-sampling ablations use the same decoder modules and replace only the adaptive
positions with predetermined positions.
This construction couples all queries to the same local feature field while moving later queries toward
the surface. It therefore improves localization and normal prediction without introducing a large
per-voxel network or requiring volume samples along the entire ray. When the chained recurrent
variant is used, intermediate predictions are also supervised; at inference we use the final stage.
3.3
OCTREE TRAVERSAL AND SURFACE RENDERING
Given a camera ray, we enumerate its intersections with occupied leaf voxels in front-to-back order.
Each candidate is represented by its local entry and exit points and the index of its voxel feature
block. The decoder is evaluated on these candidates in chunks. A candidate is accepted when its
hit probability exceeds a threshold; the first accepted candidate for each ray is selected. Its local
position is converted back to octree coordinates by adding the voxel origin, while the predicted
normal and albedo are retained as surface attributes. Rays for which no candidate is accepted return
the background value. Thus octree traversal replaces the broad volume sampling used by neural
radiance fields, and the selected attributes can be passed directly to a standard ray-tracing integrator
for reshading, reflection, refraction, or other camera effects.
3.4
FEEDFORWARD REPRESENTATION CONSTRUCTION
The structured representation makes feature construction amortizable across objects. We build an
input octree from a colored point cloud and attach point color, local point offset, and normalized
global coordinates to occupied cells. An octree-based U-Net (Wang et al., 2017) processes this sparse
hierarchy with octree convolutions, downsampling and upsampling stages, residual blocks, and skip
connections. At the query depth, it predicts a tensor with eight feature slots per occupied voxel.
Reshaping these slots and averaging all slots that share a geometric vertex yields the continuous field
used by the decoder.
The feature extractor and the ray-query decoder are trained jointly with the same rendering losses
used for per-object optimization. Consequently, a single checkpoint maps a new colored point cloud
to a renderable NeuOctree without optimizing the object-specific feature table. The resulting field is
also a native output space for the image-conditioned generation pipeline in Section 4.3.

the oriented normal, and the albedo. Let m denote the hit label and let P be the positive surface pairs.

RESULTS OF PER-MESH COMPRESSION
We compare NeuOctree with the per-object optimization methods NGF (Sivaram et al., 2024), QNDF
(Pentapati et al., 2025), VQAD (Takikawa et al., 2022), and N-BVH (Weier et al., 2024) over a broad
range of storage budgets. VQAD is adapted to predict surface normals and intersection positions
under the same geometry supervision.
Dataset and Metrics.
We evaluate the seven meshes in Table 1 from the Stanford 3D Scanning
Repository (Stanford University, 1994) and Thingi10K (Zhou & Jacobson, 2016), rendering 16 fixed
512 × 512 views. We report mean ray–surface position error dpm in normalized object coordinates,
scaled by 103, and oriented normal error dnorm in degrees.
Geometry Compression.
As shown in Table 1, NeuOctree obtains the lowest dpm in 24 of 28
model–budget comparisons. It wins all 21 at 81–244 KiB and three of seven at 823 KiB, where
QNDF takes the other four. NeuOctree also wins 12 normal-error comparisons; NGF leads mainly at
the smallest payload, while QNDF is competitive only at the largest. Thus, the strongest baseline
changes with payload, whereas NeuOctree maintains the most consistent positional accuracy. The
curves in Fig. 4 and qualitative results in Fig. 3 corroborate this trend, showing cleaner silhouettes
and better preservation of fine structures.

4.2
RESULTS OF FEEDFORWARD METHODS
Dataset and Metrics.
Our feedforward model is trained on a randomly sampled subset of Objaverse
with 32k assets. We evaluate on 30 unseen Objaverse assets and 30 ShapeNet assets to compare with
per-object methods. The same metrics dpm, dnorm as in Section 4.1 are used, along with PSNR for
appearance reconstruction.
Evaluation.
We evaluate a shared feedforward checkpoint under the same rendering and scoring

CONCLUSION
We presented NeuOctree, a neural explicit surface representation for ray tracing. Shared corner
features in a sparse octree and a recurrent decoder jointly predict ray–surface hits, positions, normals,
and albedo, supporting compact geometry–appearance modeling, direct rendering, and open surfaces.
Experiments show accurate reconstruction across meshes and budgets; the shared feedforward
model constructs a renderable representation in about 50 ms, rather than minutes of per-object
optimization, and transfers from Objaverse to ShapeNet without retraining. Ray-traced rendering and
image-conditioned generation further demonstrate the versatility of this structured output space.
NeuOctree still requires costly high-fidelity per-object fitting and models albedo rather than view-
dependent materials; generation remains a proof of concept. Future work will accelerate octree
construction, extend surface queries to richer materials, lighting, and dynamics, and develop NeuOc-
tree as a native output space for 3D generative models.

A.2
FEEDFORWARD NEUOCTREE
Input representation and architecture.
The reported feedforward model is the depth-9 union
variant. For each object, its input octree is the union of a conservatively rasterized depth-9 mesh
occupancy (with colors sampled from eight rendered albedo views) and a 200k point cloud. The union
is capped at 900,000 depth-9 cells; this cap limits the encoder memory without changing the depth-7
coverage used by the renderer. The octree uses full depth=4 and query depth 7. Its nine LPF
input channels encode position, within-cell offset, and local color. A sparse octree U-Net maps the
depth-9 input to 128 channels at query depth 7. These channels are reshaped into eight 16-dimensional
corner features per voxel and averaged at shared corners, yielding the continuous vertex feature field
used by the renderer. The renderer has hidden width 128, four recurrent refinements, 8-dimensional
voxel features, RMSNorm, and segment-position encoding. Its hit threshold is 0.4, and the forward
pass is chunked at 65,536 candidates (with a maximum render batch of 262,144 queries).
Figure 9 details the 3D U-Net used by the feedforward encoder. The sparse octree input and point-
cloud features enter a multiscale encoder, are fused at the bottleneck, and are decoded with skip
connections to produce the output octree and point features. Each octree residual block uses two
1 × 1 convolutions with RMSNorm and ReLU, followed by a residual addition and a final ReLU, as
shown in the lower panel.
Losses.
The hit term uses balanced binary cross-entropy with positive weight 1.5, Tversky weight
0.2 and (α, β, γ) = (0.3, 0.7, 1.0). Hole, floater, and boundary penalties have weights 0.1, 0.1, and
0.2. Color uses weight 2.0, a Charbonnier penalty, gradient weight 0.2, and masked SSIM weight 0.5.

The generation demonstration uses NeuOctree itself as the output domain. A sparse-octree VAE
encodes the occupancy and feature tensors of a renderable NeuOctree into an 83 latent grid. An
image-conditioned flow-matching DiT predicts both latent features and occupancy; the VAE decoder
then reconstructs the octree feature field, which can be queried by the same surface renderer as the
reconstructed assets. No mesh extraction or post-hoc texture baking is required, and the paper does
not use this experiment for a quantitative comparison with external generation systems.
The generation setup uses 5,000 ShapeNet objects from ten categories. The VAE receives a 1283
representation, downsamples it four times to 83, and uses a latent dimension of 32. It is trained
with AdamW (learning rate 10−4, weight decay 0.01), batch size 32, on four RTX 5090 GPUs. For
the image-conditioned model, 16 random Blender views are rendered per asset, with one retained
for testing. The DiT has approximately 0.7B parameters: width 1,536, 16 transformer blocks, 12
attention heads, and MLP width 8,192. It uses the same optimizer settings, batch size 16, and four
RTX 5090 GPUs.

REPRODUCIBILITY NOTES AND LIMITATIONS
The capacity tables report measured payloads rather than inferring size from a compression-ratio label.
The actual capacity controls are the values in Tables 5 to 9. The VQAD quality table uses the tested
configuration listed for each cell, whereas its timing table uses a uniform 28-cell protocol. QNDF’s
Dragon 823 KiB remeshing exception and N-BVH’s excluded calibration probe are disclosed above.
Finally, the feedforward inference time measures representation construction only; image rendering
and evaluation are separate downstream operations.
