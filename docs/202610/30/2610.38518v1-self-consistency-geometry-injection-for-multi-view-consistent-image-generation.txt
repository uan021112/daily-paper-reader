Abstract. Recent diffusion models have achieved impressive progress in single image to multi-view generation by synthesizing consistent novel views efficiently. Existing methods usually explicitly or implicitly couple geometry inference and texture synthesis, making generation vulnera-ble to inaccurate geometry prediction. Although several geometry-guided methods have explored decoupling, they usually treat predicted geometry as a fixed condition and do not explicitly model whether the geometry is reliable. We therefore propose Reliability-Guided Multi-View Generation (RGMV), which comprises a parameter-free Self-Consistency Geometry Gate (SCG) and a Reliability-aware Geometry Injection (RGI) module. Specifically, a geometry proposal generator produces multiple geometry predictions for the same reference image and target viewpoints under different stochastic sampling configurations. SCG measures their spatial agreement to derive a reliability map, which RGI uses to modulate the in-jection strength of geometry-conditioned residuals in the RGB synthesis model. Moreover, RGMV can adapt to different geometry proposal gener-ators. As a result, RGMV preserves stronger geometric guidance in stable regions while attenuating unreliable geometry cues before they propa-gate into multi-view RGB generation. Extensive experiments show that reliability-aware geometry injection improves the geometric quality of multi-view generation. Code: https://github.com/WISLab-GDUT/RGMV .

Keywords: Multi-view image generation · Geometry-conditioned diffu-sion · Self-consistency reliability estimation 

## 1 Introduction 

Multi-view Consistent Image Generation aims to synthesize visually coherent images of an object from novel viewpoints given only one reference image. Re-cent diffusion-based approaches have substantially improved the realism and efficiency of multi-view synthesis [4, 7, 8, 25, 27, 29, 31, 33, 39, 43] by leveraging large-scale image priors and joint view generation [16, 21, 28, 37, 38, 44]. Never-theless, a single reference image provides only partial observations of the under-lying object. The geometry of occluded and unseen regions therefore remains

To reduce this ambiguity, geometry-guided methods [6,13,15,19,20,26,42] in-troduce normal maps, depth maps, or other structural representations as explicit conditions for multi-view RGB synthesis. When the geometry condition is accu-rate, it provides view-aware structural cues that help constrain object shape and appearance in unseen regions. Figure 1(a,b) highlights two alternative formula-tions for multi-view generation. Direct methods do not expose geometry as an independently controllable signal, whereas geometry-guided pipelines expose it without accounting for its non-uniform reliability. However, geometry predicted from a single reference image is not uniformly reliable. Ambiguity in occluded regions, thin structures, and local boundaries can lead to spatially and view-wise varying errors in the predicted geometry. Existing geometry-conditioned pipelines [19, 20, 25, 26, 45] typically inject predicted geometry with a globally fixed strength, implicitly treating all geometric cues as equally trustworthy. This design creates an unavoidable trade-off that weak conditioning underuses reli-able geometry, whereas strong conditioning propagates local geometric errors into RGB synthesis. Because the reliability of predicted geometry varies across spatial locations and target views, no single global injection scale can simulta-neously preserve trustworthy structural cues and suppress unreliable ones. This limitation motivates a selective geometry-conditioning mechanism that preserves stable geometric cues while suppressing unreliable ones. Therefore, we propose RGMV, a reliability-guided framework for geometry-guided multi-view generation. Figure 1(c) summarizes the resulting alternative. RGMV retains the decoupled pipeline but selectively controls the influence of predicted geometry rather than modifying the geometry itself. geometry proposal generator under different stochastic seeds. SCG converts their agreement into spatial reliability maps, and RGI uses these maps to modulate the geometry residuals injected into the multi-view RGB synthesis model. This design preserves stronger geometry guidance in stable regions while limiting the influence of inconsistent predictions. Experiments show that SCG-guided RGI improves over spatially uniform injection with a frozen RGB synthesis model, and parameter-efficient adaptation further improves the full RGMV framework. The main contributions of this work are as follows: 

– We propose RGMV, which couples a parameter-free Self-Consistency Geom-etry Gate (SCG) for estimating spatial reliability from stochastic geometry agreement with a Reliability-aware Geometry Injection (RGI) module for modulating normal-conditioned residuals. 

– We introduce a parameter-efficient adaptation scheme for the RGI-equipped RGB synthesis model, which uses ground-truth normals during training and SCG-derived spatial variation only at inference, without dependence on a particular geometry proposal generator. 

– Extensive experiments on Objaverse and GSO demonstrate that RGMV con-sistently improves multiple geometry proposal generators in both multi-view RGB synthesis and downstream 3D reconstruction. Qualitative results show more stable structures on rendered objects and real captured images. 

## 2 Related Work 

2.1 Multi-view Consistent Image Generation 

Diffusion-based models [17, 18, 32, 33, 35, 36] have become a major approach for synthesizing novel views from a single reference image. Zero-1-to-3 [17] introduces camera-conditioned diffusion, enabling novel-view generation under specified rel-ative camera transformations. SyncDreamer [18] further models the joint distri-bution of multiple target views and synchronizes their denoising process through cross-view feature interaction to improve multi-view consistency. SV3D [36] adapts latent video diffusion to orbital multi-view synthesis and incorporates explicit camera control for image-conditioned view generation. Era3D [14] im-proves high-resolution multi-view generation through camera prediction and ef-ficient row-wise attention that exploits epipolar correspondence across views. More recently, MV-Adapter [10] introduces a lightweight adapter-based design that equips pretrained text-to-image diffusion models with multi-view generation capability while preserving the original backbone. These methods [1,7,34,35,47] improve the realism, camera controllability, and cross-view coherence of image-based multi-view synthesis. Their primary focus is on constructing stronger multi-view generation priors or more effective cross-view interactions. In this work, we study how external geometry predicted from a single reference im-age should be used within an existing multi-view diffusion pipeline when the reliability varies across spatial regions and target views. 4 Y. Xiao et al. 

2.2 Geometry-conditioned Diffusion Models 

Geometry conditions have been widely used to provide explicit spatial control for diffusion models [2,9,22,24,25,40,43,45]. ControlNet [45] introduces an addi-tional trainable branch that injects spatial conditions, such as edges, depth maps, and normal maps, into a pretrained diffusion backbone. T2I-Adapter [25] instead learns lightweight adapters for external structural signals while keeping the orig-inal text-to-image model frozen. These methods establish effective mechanisms for incorporating geometry into image diffusion. Several works further incorpo-rate geometry into multi-view or 3D generation [6, 13, 15, 19, 20]. Wonder3D [19] jointly generates multi-view RGB images and normal maps through cross-domain diffusion and multi-view cross-domain attention. Direct2.5 [20] models multi-view normal maps as a 2.5D representation and uses normal-conditioned multi-view image generation for appearance synthesis. PointmapDiff [26] uses predicted pointmaps as geometric conditions for single-image novel-view synthesis through reference attention and ControlNet-based conditioning. In a related texture-generation setting, CaliTex [15] calibrates attention with known 3D structure to improve view-coherent texture synthesis. These methods demonstrate that explicit geometry can substantially improve view synthesis and appearance gen-eration. However, they generally use the available geometric representation di-rectly or condition generation with a globally fixed mechanism [19, 20, 26, 45]. Regarding this issue, RGMV uses multi-sample agreement to adapt the strength of geometry-conditioned residual injection at inference time. 

2.3 Consistency-based Reliability Estimation 

Multi-sample consistency has been explored as an inference-time signal for as-sessing the reliability of generative outputs [11, 12, 23]. Generative Uncertainty in Diffusion Models [12] estimates the uncertainty of diffusion samples to iden-tify potentially low-quality generations. Self-Refining Video Sampling [11] uses self-consistency to identify regions that benefit from selective refinement during video generation. These studies show that repeated stochastic generations can provide useful evidence about output stability without requiring an additional manually annotated reliability label. RGMV evaluates the stability of the geom-etry condition before it is injected into downstream multi-view RGB synthesis. Specifically, SCG measures agreement among normal predictions generated un-der different stochastic seeds and converts it into a spatial reliability map. This parameter-free signal directly modulates normal-conditioned residuals at infer-ence time, allowing the multi-view diffusion model to retain stable geometric cues while attenuating geometry conditions that are locally inconsistent.

## 4 Experiments 

4.1 Dataset and Metric 

We train RGMV on a curated Objaverse [3] subset of approximately 100 K ob-jects, with more than 60 K for training and the remainder for validation. Abla-tions use a fixed 100 -object validation subset, and the Google Scanned Objects (GSO) [5] provides a fixed 100 -object test set. Both of them are selected with seed 42 . For 2D synthesis, we report PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS [46], which mea-sure pixel fidelity, structural similarity, and perceptual similarity respectively. For 3D reconstruction, lower Chamfer Distance (CD) and higher Volume IoU indicate better geometric quality. 

4.2 Implementation Details 

Our multi-view RGB synthesis model is built on Stable Diffusion v2.1-base [30]. We use MV-Adapter [10] as the multi-view consistency module and a normal-conditioned ControlNet [45] for geometry residual injection. We use K = 3 ,

γ = 0 .20 , αscg = 0 .08 , and [rmin , r max ] = [0 , 1] , selected in Sec. 4.4. During training, we optimize only the parameters of MV-Adapter for 120 ,000 steps with batch size 3 and learning rate 1 × 10 −5, while freezing the diffusion backbone and ControlNet, and the Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) scale is 3.0. For each reference image, the proposal generator produces K stochastic sets of six-view normal maps, and RGMV synthesizes the corresponding RGB views at 0◦ (front), 

45 ◦ (front-right), 90 ◦ (right), 180 ◦ (back), 270 ◦ (left), and 315 ◦ (front-left). SCG assumes no specific camera angles, but arbitrary or continuous pose evaluation is beyond the scope of this work. Peak GPU memory is approximately 45 GB for training. Unless specified otherwise, the default geometry proposal generator of RGMV is a separately trained MV-Adapter for multi-view normal generation, trained with the same optimization settings and parameter-efficient adaptation strategy as the RGB synthesis model. 

4.3 Performance Comparison Compared Methods. We compare RGMV with two representative multi-view image generation baselines, Zero-1-to-3 [17] and SyncDreamer [18], as well as three recent systems with distinct multi-view generation designs, including Won-der3D++ [41], MV-Adapter [10], and Era3D [14]. Zero-1-to-3 performs camera-conditioned novel-view synthesis from a single reference image, whereas Sync-Dreamer [18] explicitly models the joint distribution of multiple target views. Wonder3D++ [41], MV-Adapter, and Era3D [14] represent complementary ap-proaches based on joint RGB–geometry generation, lightweight multi-view adap-tation, and epipolar-aware multi-view generation, respectively. The latter three methods can additionally provide multi-view geometry proposals for RGMV. We therefore evaluate each of them both as a standalone multi-view generation method and as a geometry proposal generator integrated with RGMV. ## 5 Conclusion 

In this paper, we presented RGMV, a reliability-guided framework for multi-view image generation. RGMV considers geometry predicted from a single reference image useful but not uniformly reliable across spatial regions and target views. The proposed SCG is parameter-free and operates at inference time to construct a reliability map from the agreement among multiple stochastic geometry predic-tions, while RGI uses this map to selectively modulate geometry residuals during RGB synthesis. Experiments demonstrate that RGMV improves multiple geom-etry proposal generators on both multi-view image synthesis and downstream 3D reconstruction. Qualitative results further show that reliability-guided con-ditioning reduces geometry-related artifacts and produces more stable object structures across viewpoints.
