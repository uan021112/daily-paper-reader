ABSTRACT 

Recent advances in physical 3D generation have enabled visually faithful as-sets with simulatable mechanical behavior. However, existing methods provide limited control over material behavior beyond appearance-based inference and struggle to estimate physical properties from complex interactions between ob-jects and semantic parts. Therefore, we present P HYS FORGE , a Video Diffusion Model (VDM)-based framework for addressing these challenges, comprising two complementary modules for material-controllable physical fitting and component-level interaction modeling. The first, P HYS FIT , leverages material-conditioned VDM dynamics as references for physical-property fitting, introducing windowed fitting to focus on material-revealing interactions and Hallucinated Forcing to dis-entangle nonphysical motion hallucinated by the VDM from material responses. The second, P HYS COMP decomposes complex interactions into individual objects and semantic components, enabling their physical parameters to be optimized in-dependently and flexibly composed for joint simulation. Experiments on PhysAs-sets and Google Scanned Objects demonstrate that, compared with existing meth-ods, P HYS FORGE provides stronger control over material-dependent physical re-sponses while supporting component-level physical modeling for complex inter-actions. It achieves the best CLIP and physical controllability scores on both com-mon and uncommon materials, with qualitative comparisons, component-level ab-lations, and a user study further supporting these improvements. Our project page is available at https://masterwanna.github.io/PhysForge.html. 

1 INTRODUCTION 

Recent advances in 3D generation and reconstruction have made it possible to create assets with increasingly realistic geometry and appearance (Xiang et al., 2026; Liang et al., 2026). For physical simulation, visual fidelity alone is insufficient: meaningful interaction also requires modeling how objects made of different materials respond to forces, contact, and deformation. This requires 3D assets to be endowed with physical properties that govern their mechanical behavior and enable physically meaningful responses to interaction (Zhong et al., 2024; Jiang et al., 2025).

Toward this goal, existing methods augment reconstructed or generated 3D assets with physi-cal properties inferred using Vision–Language Models (VLMs) or discriminative models trained on physical-property data (Cao et al., 2026b;a). However, two key challenges remain. First, appearance-based inference provides limited control over material behavior. Such predictions are largely shaped by appearance–material correlations learned from training data, making it difficult to assign a user-specified material to the same object and obtain the corresponding physical re-sponse (Xu et al., 2025; Li et al., 2023). Second, existing methods have limited ability to infer physical properties from complex interactions between objects or semantic parts (Lin et al., 2025b; Xu et al., 2026). In most scenes, material behavior is often manifested through contact and deforma-tion among these interacting entities, whereas existing methods mainly rely on object-level visual observations and struggle to capture such interaction-dependent behavior (Zhang et al., 2024). To address these limitations, we propose P HYS FORGE , a video diffusion model (VDM)-based framework for constructing physically simulatable 3D assets with separable semantic components, as shown in Figure 1. Our framework consists of two modules, P HYS FIT and P HYS COMP . P HYS FIT 

leverages the ability of VDMs to generate material-conditioned dynamics and introduces a robust generated-video fitting mechanism that mitigates the influence of nonphysical motion when estimat-ing physical properties for user-specified materials. P HYS COMP decomposes complex interactions into individual objects and semantic components, enabling their physical properties to be analyzed separately and flexibly composed for joint simulation. Specifically, P HYS FIT exploits the ability of VDMs to generate different material-dependent dy-namics for the same object and treats these material-conditioned outputs as reference dynamics for physical-property fitting. To further streamline the fitting process, we develop a windowed fitting paradigm that optimizes physical parameters only over informative intervals that reveal material properties, such as contact, collision, and deformation events. An unavoidable limitation is that VDM-generated dynamics does not fully obey physical laws, making direct fitting to the entire gen-erated sequence unreliable. Therefore, we introduce Hallucinated Forcing to account for gener-ated motion that cannot be explained solely by the underlying physical parameters while separating it from the material response used for fitting. For complex interactions, P HYS COMP propagates object–part identities from video to reconstructed 3D geometry, allowing the physical parameters of individual objects and semantic parts to be optimized independently. It then determines simu-lation groups based on their relative motion, enabling independently optimized components to be composed for joint simulation while preserving their semantic identities. We conduct extensive experiments on comparing P HYS FORGE to other baseline methods. Qualita-tive results show that the material controllability and component separability enabled by our design lead to more physically plausible responses and interactions; a human study further finds that partic-ipants prefer our results. In quantitative evaluations, P HYS FORGE achieves the best CLIP score and 2Under review as a conference paper physical controllability score for both common and uncommon materials. Finally, ablation studies demonstrate the effectiveness of P HYS COMP and P HYS FIT .Our contributions are as follows: • We introduce P HYS FORGE , a VDM-based framework for constructing physically simu-latable 3D assets with controllable material behavior and separable semantic components, enabling material specification beyond appearance-based physical-property inference. • We propose P HYS FIT , which leverages material-conditioned VDM dynamics for control-lable physical-property estimation and introduces robust generated-video fitting to account for nonphysical motion and focus optimization on physically informative intervals. • We develop P HYS COMP to enable independent physical-parameter optimization for indi-vidual objects and semantic components, followed by flexible composition into simulation groups based on their relative motion. • Extensive experiments show that P HYS FORGE generates visually faithful 3D assets with plausible material-dependent responses. Ablation studies further validate the effectiveness of P HYS FIT and P HYS COMP .

2 RELATED WORK 

2.1 PHYSICAL -P ROPERTY ESTIMATION 

Observation-Driven Inference. In common cases, physical properties can be inferred from an object’s appearance or identified from its observed motion and deformation. GaussianProperty uses segmentation and multimodal reasoning to assign physical attributes, while PhysGM jointly predicts geometry and physical parameters (Xu et al., 2025; Lv et al., 2026). PhysX-3D and PhysX-Anything further incorporate physical attributes into asset generation and reconstruction (Cao et al., 2026a;b). Complementing these visual and semantic cues, PAC-NeRF couples neural reconstruction with con-tinuum simulation for parameter identification, and Spring-Gaus recovers deformable dynamics with a spring-based Gaussian representation (Li et al., 2023; Zhong et al., 2024). Static images alone pro-vide limited evidence of how an object responds to forces and contact. P HYS FORGE therefore uses material-conditioned motion, collisions, and deformation as dynamic evidence, providing additional constraints for more accurate physical-property estimation. 

Video-Prior-Guided Fitting. Video generation provides dynamic priors for physical modeling. MotionCraft, PhysGen, and PSIVG incorporate simulation guidance (Montanaro et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2024b; Foo et al., 2026), while PhyT2V uses physics-oriented prompt refinement (Xue et al., 2025). For physical-property estimation, PhysDreamer fits parameters to generated motion, while DreamPhysics and Physics3D distill video diffusion priors through simulation (Zhang et al., 2024; Huang et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2024a). PhyMAGIC further incorporates motion-aware frame se-lection and confidence-guided reasoning (Meng et al., 2026). Fitting an entire generated sequence can mix material-dependent responses with nonphysical motion, since generated videos can vio-late physical constraints (Bansal et al., 2025). In particular, we observe Hallucinated Forcing that produces visually plausible but physically incorrect motion, which can bias the fitted material pa-rameters. P HYS FORGE uses window selection to fit physical-property estimation which limits the influence of this nonphysical motion. 2.2 COMPONENT -A WARE 3D A SSET CONSTRUCTION

PartField and P3-SAM recover seman-tic regions in 3D assets (Liu et al., 2025; Ma et al., 2025), while PartCrafter and PartGen generate decomposed geometry with coherent component structure (Lin et al., 2026; Chen et al., 2025). Ge-ometric decomposition alone does not establish how the resulting components respond to physical interactions. Physical modeling of object and component interactions. PhysGaussian couples Gaussian ap-pearance with continuum mechanics (Xie et al., 2024), PhysTwin reconstructs interactive physical twins from observations (Jiang et al., 2025), and OmniPhysGS learns constitutive behavior under video guidance (Lin et al., 2025b). 3 METHOD 

Our goal is to reconstruct physically simulatable 3D assets with controllable material behavior and separable semantic components. To achieve this goal, P HYS FORGE couples two complementary modules: P HYS FIT uses material-conditioned VDM dynamics to fit physical properties while ac-counting for nonphysical motion, and P HYS COMP decomposes the scene into persistent objects and semantic components for separate estimation and joint simulation. Their outputs are com-bined to produce components that can be composed in a shared physical simulation. We first introduce 3DGS and the particle–grid mechanics underlying the component-level simulator, then present video-guided physical-property fitting and component-aware physical modeling; Figure 2 summarizes the framework. 3.1 PRELIMINARY

Existing methods often simulate each segmented object as a single body, suppressing relative motion and interactions among its semantic components. Therefore, P HYS COMP focuses on component-level identification and segmentation, organizing the resulting components into simulation bodies to preserve their distinct motion and physical interactions. 3.3.1 OBJECT –C OMPONENT TRACKING , R ECONSTRUCTION , AND PLACEMENT 

To obtain component-level semantic information, a vision–language agent converts generated frames into object-level and component inventories. SAM3 (Carion et al., 2026) initializes and propagates the corresponding masks across frames and views, preserving identities for repeated or attached components. The masked views are converted into a 3DGS representation G using SAM3D (Chen et al., 2026), TRELLIS (Xiang et al., 2025), or other 3D generation or reconstruc-tion backends. Depth Anything (Lin et al., 2025a) provides depth and camera-consistent scale; P3-SAM (Ma et al., 2025) decomposes the reconstructed geometry into semantic component re-gions and transfers their labels to the corresponding Gaussians. FoundationPose (Wen et al., 2024) restores the object pose and scale and replaces the labelled representation in the shared scene coor-dinate system, yielding a 3DGS asset from which each semantic component can be extracted. 3.3.2 SIMULATION BODY ASSIGNMENT AND JOINT MATERIAL REFINEMENT

Each simulation body is represented as Bb = ( Gb, πb, qb), where Gb is its labelled 3DGS subset, πb is its material– physical-parameter pair, and qb is its dynamic state. The labelled Gaussians are converted to surface and interior particles, all bodies are assembled in the shared component-level simulator, and each window initializes qb before the state is propagated through the response. 4 EXPERIMENTS 

4.1 EXPERIMENTAL SETUP 

Datasets and Implementation. We evaluate P HYS FORGE on PhysAssets (Lv et al., 2026) and Google Scanned Objects (GSO) (Downs et al., 2022). PhysAssets contains over 24,000 3D assets annotated with physical properties and accompanied by guiding videos, providing diverse objects for evaluating material-dependent dynamics. GSO provides over 1,000 high-quality 3D scans of household objects, capturing realistic geometry and appearance across diverse object categories. Our framework is implemented on two NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPUs. 

Baselines. Our qualitative and quantitative comparisons include 6 baselines: DreamPhysics (Huang et al., 2025), PSIVG (Foo et al., 2026), PhysRVG (Zhang et al., 2026), WMReward (Yuan et al., 2026), DeforM (Li et al., 2026), and PhysGM (Lv et al., 2026). DreamPhysics learns material prop-erties from video diffusion priors through Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) and MPM simulation. PhysGM jointly predicts 3D Gaussians and physical properties from multiview images, whereas PSIVG guides video generation with simulated trajectories reconstructed from an initial generated video. DeforM uses VLM reasoning and spatial–temporal masks to focus generation on regions undergoing physical deformation. Simulation. Our component-level simulator uses Taichi-based MPM and rigid-body solvers (Hu et al., 2018; 2019). For rigid bodies, contact friction controls sliding and restitution controls rebound while the shape remains fixed. For MPM, we generate 150-frame videos at 30 FPS with a maximum of 8 substeps per frame. Elastic bodies use Young’s modulus to control stiffness and Poisson’s ratio to control lateral deformation under compression or stretching. Sand uses Young’s modulus and Poisson’s ratio for its elastic response and an internal friction angle for Drucker–Prager yielding, which determines its resistance to shearing and spreading. Liquids use a weakly compressible fluid model in which the bulk modulus controls the pressure response to compression. 

Evaluation Metrics. We evaluate text alignment, physical material controllability, and human pref-erence. The CLIP score uses ViT-L/14 to encode the text prompt and video frames, and measures their cosine similarity averaged over sampled frames to assess semantic agreement with the re-quested content. Physical controllability (PC), rated on a five-point scale by VLM, assesses whether the simulated motion, contact, and deformation express the requested material behavior. For the user study, each trial presents seven anonymized videos from P HYS FORGE , DreamPhysics (Huang et al., 2025), PSIVG (Foo et al., 2026), PhysRVG (Zhang et al., 2026), WMReward (Yuan et al., 2026), DeforM (Li et al., 2026), and PhysGM (Lv et al., 2026) in randomized order, together with the text prompts and input images. We also conduct user study on qualitive results. Participants rank the videos by how well they match the requested material while maintaining plausible dynamics. 4.2 RESULTS AND ANALYSIS

5 CONCLUSION 

In this paper, we analyzed existing methods and found that insufficient material controllability and semantic component separability in reconstructed 3D assets lead to unrealistic physical simula-tions. To address these limitations, we proposed P HYS FORGE with two key modules: P HYS FIT 

and P HYS COMP . P HYS FIT improved the efficiency of physical-property fitting through response-window selection and handling of Hallucinated Forcing, while P HYS COMP used dynamic and se-mantic information to decompose reconstructed 3D assets so that individual components retain dis-tinct material properties. Together, these modules enable the reconstruction of different material-conditioned 3D assets from the same input object under different material prompts, while keeping their semantic components extractable. Quantitative and qualitative evaluations show that material controllability and component separability yield more realistic simulations of 3D assets than existing methods. Ablation studies further confirm the effectiveness of both modules. However, P HYS FORGE still has some limitations. P HYS COMP uses generated videos to segment objects based on their dynamic and semantic information. Changing the material condition requires additional video generation and physical fitting for each material-condition change. The perfor-mance of P HYS FORGE also depends on how well the generated video represents physical behavior, although P HYS FIT can mitigate hallucinations from video generation to some extent. In future works, more advanced non-physical motion separation techniques can be employed to provide a cleaner window with less nonphysical motion for physical parameter fitting.

A APPENDIX 

A.1 IMPLEMENTATION DETAILS 

We use Qwen3.5-4B for object and component analysis and initial material estimation, with SAM3 providing framewise masks. Depth Anything 3 processes at most 24 images per inference call, including shared reference frames spanning the video. Each chunk is aligned to these references using high-confidence depth pixels, and overlapping predictions are blended. Physical simulation of MPM uses a shared grid with 100–300 cells per axis, selected according to scene extent, object size, particle spacing, and motion constraints. Each video frame is integrated with eight substeps, giving 

∆t = 1 /(8 f ) for frame rate f . Material fitting runs for up to 20 rounds over the detected collision events, initialized from the positions and velocities obtained by video-force fitting. Young’s modulus is updated from deformation errors, while candidate selection also accounts for contact duration, penetration, and a material-prior regularizer weighted by 0.01 . Particle volumes are assigned by dividing each body’s particle-cloud convex-hull volume by its particle count, and particle masses follow from the assigned density. Ground contact uses a planar boundary with restitution 0.5 and Coulomb friction coefficient 0.2.A.2 MORE RESULTS ON MATERIAL -C ONTROLLABLE
