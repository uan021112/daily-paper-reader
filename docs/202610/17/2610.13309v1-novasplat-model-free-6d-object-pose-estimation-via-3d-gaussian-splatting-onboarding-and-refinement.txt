Abstract
6D pose estimation of novel objects without CAD models
remains an open challenge: model-free methods must build
the object representation from scratch during onboarding
and have long trailed their model-based counterparts. We
present NovaSplat, a model-free pipeline that closes this
gap. From a short smartphone video captured at onboard-
ing, we train a 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) model that
serves both as a template source for detection and coarse
pose estimation and as a differentiable backbone for pose
refinement. Our refiner optimizes the object-to-camera pose
with combined photometric and geometric losses under a
continuous rotation parametrization free of gradient singu-
larities. On a household-object benchmark, NovaSplat sur-
passes all model-based methods. On a challenging indus-
trial benchmark with textureless and specular surfaces, it
outperforms all model-free competitors while substantially
narrowing the gap to model-based ones.
We further in-
troduce NovaSplat-Lite, an inference variant that is 3.1×
faster at negligible accuracy cost. Code will be made avail-
able at https://github.com/POSE-Lab/novasplat.
1. Introduction
6D object pose estimation, recovering the full position and
orientation of an object relative to a camera, is a core capa-
bility for robotic manipulation, augmented reality, and in-
dustrial inspection. The highest-performing methods rely
on a 3D CAD model of the object: they render templates at
many viewpoints and extract geometric point clouds, which
are then matched against the query image using powerful
frozen foundation models [5, 35].
In practice, however,
CAD models are rarely available outside controlled envi-
ronments, making these approaches impractical for real-
world deployment. This motivates the model-free setting:
given only a short video of a novel object captured during
an onboarding phase, estimate its 6D pose in new scenes

Model-free methods face a fundamental reconstruction
problem. Where model-based methods receive the object
geometry by definition, model-free methods must recover
it from video, a noisier and less complete substitute that
degrades every downstream stage, from template rendering
to correspondence matching. As illustrated in Figure 1, this
has historically resulted in a substantial performance gap
between the two paradigms, limiting the practical utility of
model-free approaches.
We propose NovaSplat, a complete model-free pipeline
that closes this gap and in some cases reverses it. During
onboarding, we reconstruct a dense 3D representation of the
object from video and train a 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)
model. This representation serves two roles simultaneously:
rendering high-quality templates for object detection and

coarse pose estimation and acting as a differentiable back-
bone for gradient-based pose refinement. Our 3DGS refiner
freezes the learned scene representation and directly opti-
mizes the object-to-camera pose by jointly minimizing ap-
pearance and geometric losses, using a continuous rotation
parameterization that avoids singularities. This joint opti-
mization proves considerably more robust than geometry-
only refinement under the noisy reconstructions typical of
video-based onboarding.
We make the following contributions:
• A complete model-free onboarding pipeline that produces
a high-quality 3DGS object representation from a short
video, enabling model-based pose estimation methods to
operate without a CAD model.
• A novel 3DGS-based pose refiner that jointly optimizes
appearance and geometry, advancing model-free pose re-
finement.
• State-of-the-art results on two benchmarks spanning
household and industrial objects, demonstrating that
model-free methods can match and exceed model-based
performance without CAD models.
2. Related Work
2.1. Taxonomy of 6D Object Pose Estimation
Existing 6D object pose estimation methods can be broadly
categorized by the level of prior object knowledge they re-
quire. Instance-based methods [15, 43, 47] leverage 3D
object models during training to achieve high accuracy but
are restricted to previously seen objects.
Novel object
methods [4, 5, 11, 18, 21, 23, 25, 29, 32, 35, 49, 55, 56]
generalize to unseen objects and can be further divided
into category-based [46, 50] and unseen object approaches.
Within the unseen object paradigm, model-based meth-
ods [5, 28, 29, 32, 35, 44] require a CAD model at inference
time, exploiting geometric priors from rendered templates
and 3D point clouds, while model-free methods build an
object representation from a short video sequence during an
onboarding phase, without any 3D model. This work fo-
cuses on the model-free unseen object setting.
2.2. Model-Based Novel Object Methods
Several recent methods follow a correspondence-based
pipeline over CAD-rendered templates.
FoundPose [35]
matches DINOv2 [34] descriptors between the query and
rendered templates but relies solely on visual features.
FreeZe [5] and FreeZeV2 [6] extend to depth input by
fusing visual and geometric (GeDi [39]) features for 3D-
3D correspondence matching and refine the final pose
using the Iterative Closest Point (ICP) algorithm, rank-
ing among the top BOP methods.
We build directly on
FreeZeV2 as our coarse pose estimator.
GigaPose [32]
is fast and supports both model-based and model-free set-
tings on single RGB frames, though lacking depth lim-
its its performance on small or occluded objects.
Gen-
Flow [28] and its successor Co-op [29] refine poses via
optical flow and semi-dense correspondences, respectively,
the latter using a CroCo-based [53] ViT with a differen-
tiable PnP layer. Pos3R [9] likewise matches over CAD-
rendered templates, employing MASt3R [22], a 3D re-
construction foundation model, to establish dense corre-
spondences, select the best template by feature similarity,
and recover pose via PnP-RANSAC, with optional refine-
ment via render-and-compare. WALDO [36] extends the
detect-segment-hypothesize-refine pipeline with dynamic
non-uniform sampling, multi-hypothesis inference, and it-
erative refinement to exhibit robustness under occlusions.
All these methods require a 3D CAD model at inference
time. NovaSplat operates without one at any stage, replaces
ICP with a differentiable 3DGS-based refiner, and surpasses
those of them evaluated on HOPEv2 (Table 1).
2.3. Model-Free Novel Object Methods
Model-free methods can be organized into three categories.
Visual localization approaches [11, 44] apply Structure-
from-Motion (SfM) during onboarding to build a sparse 3D
point cloud, recovering pose via 2D-3D correspondences.
They struggle, however, with low-texture objects and large
viewpoint changes. Template-based methods [25] use view-
point retrieval over posed reference images for a coarse hy-
pothesis before refinement but are sensitive to detection and
bounding box quality. Few-shot methods operate from only
one or a handful of RGB or RGB-D images: [18] generate
a geometry-aware encoding from a few RGB views, [24]
compress a single RGB-D reference into a pose embedding
via latent diffusion, and [21] combine 2D-3D alignment
with render-and-compare refinement from a single RGB-D
anchor. Despite their practical appeal, these methods rely
on strong assumptions, such as symmetry priors or scale
constraints, to compensate for limited onboarding data, re-
stricting generalization across object geometries.
2.4. Neural Rendering for Object Pose Estimation
Neural rendering approaches encode objects into differ-
entiable implicit representations for CAD-free novel-view
synthesis. Early NeRF-based methods such as LatentFu-
sion [38] and NeRF-Pose [23] use latent voxel grids and
radiance fields, respectively. BundleSDF [55] builds a Neu-
ral Object Field on-the-fly from an RGB-D stream, concur-
rently optimizing pose and a 3D representation. Founda-
tionPose [56] extends this into a unified foundation model
supporting both model-based and model-free setups via
large-scale synthetic training.
More recently, 3D Gaus-
sian Splatting (3DGS) [19] has largely displaced NeRF-
based representations owing to superior rendering qual-
ity and real-time speed. GS-Pose [3], iG-6DoF [4], and

3. Method
3.1. Overview
Our pipeline consists of two stages: onboarding and infer-
ence (Figure 2).
During onboarding (Section 3.2), the goal is to build
an object representation from a short video of the object
captured from multiple viewpoints with a handheld device
such as a smartphone. Given the video frames and corre-
sponding object masks, we run a classical 3D reconstruc-
tion pipeline comprising SfM, Multi-View Stereo (MVS),
and 3DGS training to produce a 3D mesh and an optimized
3DGS object representation.
During inference, given an RGB-D query image con-
taining instances of onboarded objects, the goal is to de-
tect each object and estimate its 6D pose. We first run a
modified CNOS [31] detection and segmentation module
(Section 3.3) to localize each object and produce its seg-
mentation mask. We then employ the coarse pose estima-
tion module of FreeZeV2 [5, 6] (Section 3.4), adapted to
use templates rendered from the 3DGS representation and a
3D point cloud sampled from the mesh model, to obtain an
initial 6D pose estimate. Finally, each coarse pose is passed
to our 3DGS-based hybrid refiner (Section 3.5), which pro-
duces the final 6D pose. Implementation details of the on-
boarding and inference stages are provided in Section 4.3.
3.2. Onboarding
We assume access to a set of RGB video frames depicting
the object from all viewpoints, along with corresponding
object segmentation masks. When masks are unavailable,


they can be obtained automatically using an off-the-shelf
video segmentation model such as SAM3 [7].
Structure-from-Motion. The raw frames are passed to a
SfM pipeline [37, 41] to estimate camera poses and a sparse
3D point cloud. The object masks are then applied to isolate
the object point cloud from the background.
3D Gaussian Splatting.
The masked frames, camera
poses, and object point cloud are used to train a 3DGS
model [19]. The 3DGS representation consists of a set of
3D Gaussians, each parameterized by its mean, covariance,
opacity, and view-dependent color encoded as Spherical
Harmonics (SH) coefficients. The model is optimized by
differentiably rendering the Gaussians onto the image plane
and minimizing the photometric error against the masked
training frames.
Multi-View Stereo.
The SfM point cloud is often too
sparse to initialize stereo depth estimation reliably, particu-
larly over untextured object regions where few or no feature
correspondences can be established. Since 3DGS densifica-
tion fills in these regions during optimization, we exploit
the Gaussian centers as a semi-dense point cloud. Equipped
with camera visibility information, this denser prior serves
as input to our MVS pipeline. We run PatchMatchStereo [1]
to estimate per-view depth maps from neighboring views,
which are then fused into a dense point cloud. From this
point cloud we recover a 3D mesh by following [16], which
discretizes space via Delaunay tetrahedralization and for-
mulates surface extraction as a graph optimization problem
that incorporates visibility constraints.
Outputs. At the end of onboarding we have two assets: a
3DGS representation from which novel views of the object
can be rendered, and a 3D mesh that captures the object ge-
ometry. From the 3DGS representation, we render Ntempl

3.4. Coarse Pose Estimation
We build our coarse pose estimation module on the work-
flow of FreeZeV2 [6], adapting it to our model-free set-
ting. Visual features are extracted from {Ii

ing a frozen DINOv3 encoder, while geometric features are
extracted from T (Section 3.2) using a frozen GeDi en-
coder [39]. Both feature types are extracted on the template
and query sides, then fused into a unified per-point descrip-
tor that encodes appearance and geometry jointly. Match-
ing these descriptors yields a set of 3D-3D correspondences,
which are passed to a RANSAC framework to produce the
coarse pose Pcoarse.
Compared to the original FreeZe pipeline, we relo-
cate the symmetry-aware augmentation from the refinement
stage to the coarse pose estimation stage. At this stage the
full set of Ntempl views is available, providing substantially
more viewpoint variation for the augmentation to exploit
than the single rendered view used during refinement.
3.5. 3DGS-Based Pose Refinement
The coarse pose Pcoarse is further refined in the final stage
of our pipeline through a hybrid optimization scheme that
jointly considers appearance and geometric cues. The back-
bone of this optimization is the differentiable 3DGS rep-
resentation learned during onboarding. During refinement,
the Gaussian parameters are frozen, and only the object-to-
camera pose is optimized.
We parameterize the rotational component using the con-
tinuous 6D representation of Zhou et al. [59], which is de-
fined over SO(3) without singularities or discontinuities,
allowing gradients to flow freely throughout the optimiza-
tion. The translational component t is parameterized as a
standard 3D vector. Both are initialized from Pcoarse.
At each iteration, we render an RGB image Irend and a bi-
nary mask Mrend obtained by thresholding the alpha channel
at τα.
Photometric Loss. Following GS-Pose [3], we define the
photometric loss as the sum of the image structural similar-
ity (SSIM) [51] and the multi-scale SSIM (MS-SSIM) [52],
computed over the region defined by Mrend the following:
Lphoto = (1 −SSIM) + (1 −MS-SSIM).

We use 3 scales for MS-SSIM rather than the standard 5, as
the latter requires a minimum image resolution that small

a query point cloud Q. Q is backprojected from the depth
channel of the query image, retaining only points that fall

within a range drange of the transformed T . This filtering ex-

Optimization.
We use separate Adam optimizers for ro-
tational and translational components, as we found that a
shared learning rate was too restrictive for the translation
parameters when tuned appropriately for rotation. The two
optimizers alternate every Nalt iterations. Per-iteration cor-
rections are clamped to an angular threshold αthresh for rota-
tion and tthresh for translation to prevent large updates from
destabilizing the optimization. We retain the pose with the
lowest total loss over Nref iterations and apply early stop-
ping if no improvement occurs for Nhalt iterations.
4. Experiments
4.1. Datasets
We evaluate NovaSplat on two complementary benchmarks
spanning household and industrial objects.
HOPEv2 [48] is the BOP version of NVIDIA’s Household
Objects for Pose Estimation dataset, comprising 28 toy gro-
cery objects across 50 cluttered scenes from 10 household
and office environments. For the BOP Challenge 2024 [33],
the dataset was extended with multi-view test images cap-
tured by a moving camera and provides a standardized
leaderboard for direct comparison with both model-free and
model-based methods under identical conditions. We note
that we encountered some inconsistencies in this dataset,
which are detailed in Sec. G.
IndustryShapes [40] is an RGB-D benchmark designed
for industrial robotics, with two splits: classic and extended.
It features five object categories with properties that are par-
ticularly challenging for pose estimation: textureless sur-
faces, specular reflections, thin geometries, and small scale.
Scenes are captured in realistic industrial assembly settings
with multiple instances per object and inter-object occlu-
sions. The extended split provides onboarding sequences,
directly matching our model-free paradigm.

First and foremost, LM provides no onboarding videos,
which are a fundamental requirement of our model-free set-
ting and cannot be substituted by the reference images the
dataset supplies. Second, its capture protocol covers only

4.2. Evaluation Metrics
For each of the benchmarks, we follow the proposed eval-
uation protocol to ensure fair comparison with the re-
ported baselines. In particular, for HOPEv2, we adopt the
BOP detection evaluation protocol [33], computing Aver-
age Precision (AP or mAP) for the Maximum Symmetry-
aware Surface Distance (MSSD) APMSSD and the Maximum
Symmetry-aware Projection Distance (MSPD) APMSPD.
For IndustryShapes, we adopt the BOP localization pro-
tocol, computing the Average Recall (AR) for MSSD
(ARMSSD), for MSPD (ARMSPD) and for the Visible Sur-
face Discrepancy (VSD) ARVSD. The overall accuracy is
measured for both datasets as an average over the different
metrics. We refer to Sec. 3.4 of [33] for details.

0.365) and leads OPFormer (0.509 vs. 0.243) and NeMO
(0.509 vs. 0.291) by similarly large margins.
Against
model-based methods, NovaSplat achieves gains of 2.9%
on AP and 17.0% on APMSSD over Co-op (MUSE), the best
CAD-model-assisted baseline, and outperforms GenFlow
(APMSSD: 0.509 vs. 0.346) and GigaPose (0.509 vs. 0.046)
by substantial margins.
The sole exception is APMSPD,
where Co-op (MUSE) leads by 8.2% (0.593 vs. 0.548),
while NovaSplat remains competitive with Co-op (CNOS)
(0.549 vs. 0.548). Additional per-object quantitative break-
downs as well as qualitative results are provided in Sec. F.
The distinction between APMSSD and APMSPD is mean-
ingful here: as noted by Hodaˇn et al. [14], MSSD measures
3D surface distance and is most relevant for robotic ma-
nipulation, whereas MSPD measures 2D projection error
and is more relevant for AR. Since our target application
is manipulation, APMSSD is our primary metric, and No-
vaSplat’s lead here, over both model-free and model-based
methods, is the most consequential result in the table. We
attribute this to NovaSplat’s end-to-end geometric ground-
ing: GeDi descriptors drive the coarse stage and Lgeo con-
strains refinement, an integration absent in all competing
model-free methods that rely on appearance cues, which ex-
plains the disproportionately large gains on APMSSD relative
to APMSPD.
Figure 11 corroborates these findings: GFreeDet2 as-
signs multiple hypotheses per instance, OPFormer shows
systematic scale errors, and NeMO misses many instances,
whereas NovaSplat produces accurate and complete esti-
mates whose residual errors trace to upstream detection
rather than to pose estimation. Sec. F expands on these fail-
ure modes.
IndustryShapes Results. Table 2 evaluates NovaSplat
on a qualitatively different challenge. Unlike HOPEv2’s
textured household objects, IndustryShapes consists of
texture-less, black industrial parts with specular surfaces,
thin geometry, and small scale, conditions that stress-
test both 3D reconstruction quality and appearance-based
matching.
Despite this domain shift, NovaSplat consis-
tently surpasses all three model-free baselines, Foundation-
Pose [56], OnePose [44], and OnePose++ [11], on both
splits, demonstrating generalization across object domains.
OnePose and OnePose++ rely on SfM-based 2D-3D corre-
spondence matching (Sec. 2.3) and are especially hindered
by IndustryShapes’ texture-less, specular surfaces, reaching
only 0.07/0.02 and 0.14/0.20 AR on the classic/extended
splits, respectively. NovaSplat improves over model-free
FoundationPose by 44.0% and 21.2% on overall AR for
the classic and extended splits, with the largest margin on
ARMSSD (+64.5% on classic).
Model-based methods, however, retain a clear advantage
here, with FoundationPose [56] achieving AR scores of 0.67
and 0.69 on the classic and extended splits, respectively,
compared to NovaSplat’s 0.36 and 0.40. That said, the gap
narrows considerably on ARMSSD: model-free Foundation-
Pose trails the best model-based result by 0.43 on the clas-
sic split, whereas NovaSplat reduces this gap to 0.23, nearly
halving it. The same trend holds on the extended split (gap
reduced from 0.37 to 0.27). This is consistent with our find-
ings on HOPEv2 and reinforces that NovaSplat’s geometric
grounding is the key driver of progress, as ARMSSD is the
metric most sensitive to 3D accuracy.
The overall performance gap relative to model-based
methods on this dataset is larger than on HOPEv2, and we
attribute this to the fundamental difficulty of the objects’
nature. Texture-less, uniformly black surfaces with specu-
lar highlights provide little signal for appearance-based fea-
ture matching and yield degraded 3D reconstructions that
fail to capture fine geometric detail. This explanation is
further supported by the qualitative results reported in the
appendix. Improving NovaSplat’s robustness to such ob-
jects, for instance, through geometry-only reconstruction
pipelines, is a natural direction for future work.

4.5. Ablation Study
Table 3 isolates the contribution of each component of our
refinement module on the HOPEv2 validation set.
The
coarse pose estimation (CPE) stage alone achieves a strong
baseline of 0.724 AP and 0.703 APMSSD. Refining this ini-
tialization with standard Iterative Closest Point (ICP), as
employed by FreeZe [5], severely degrades performance
across all metrics: APMSSD drops by 45.4% (0.703 to 0.384)
and AP by 34.9% (0.724 to 0.471). This highlights the un-
reliability of zero-shot geometry extracted from monocular
video for standard point-to-point registration: the resulting
point clouds are too noisy for ICP, which is highly suscep-
tible to local minima under such conditions.
Our 3DGS-based refinement takes a fundamentally dif-
ferent approach by directly optimizing the pose against the
captured scene rather than relying on point cloud alignment.
Using only the photometric loss Lphoto yields a marginal im-
provement over CPE (APMSSD: 0.703 to 0.706), as texture
ambiguities can still trap the optimizer in poor local min-
ima. Isolating the geometric term, CPE + 3DGS (only Lgeo)
degrades performance below CPE (APMSSD: 0.703 to 0.651;
AP: 0.724 to 0.666), mirroring the ICP result: without the
complementary photometric signal to anchor it, geometric
alignment alone inherits the same sensitivity to noisy zero-
shot geometry. Introducing the full dual loss, combining
photometric and geometric (depth) terms, effectively con-
strains the optimization and outperforms both individual
components, achieving the best performance across all met-
rics (APMSSD: 0.718, AP: 0.736). The qualitative improve-
ment is illustrated in Figure 4, where the Ketchup and Mus-
tard objects show a clear correction of misaligned coarse
estimates after refinement. Together, these results validate
our design choice to use the 3DGS representation not only
for template rendering but also as a differentiable backbone
for pose refinement.

5. Conclusions
We presented NovaSplat, a model-free pipeline for 6D ob-
ject pose estimation that replaces the CAD model require-
ment with a compact onboarding procedure: SfM and MVS
recover object geometry from a short smartphone video,
which is then used to train a 3D Gaussian Splatting rep-
resentation for both template rendering and differentiable
pose refinement. NovaSplat is, to our knowledge, the first
model-free method to surpass model-based competitors on
a BOP benchmark, exceeding the best model-based baseline
on HOPEv2 by 2.9% on AP and 17.0% on APMSSD, and out-
performs the best model-free competitor on IndustryShapes
while narrowing the gap to model-based methods. We ad-
ditionally provide NovaSplat-Lite, an inference variant that
is 3.1× faster at a cost of 0.001 AP, bringing the pipeline
within the reach of practical deployment.
Our primary limitation is sensitivity to object appear-
ance:
on IndustryShapes, textureless, specular surfaces
degrade both reconstruction quality and feature matching,
leaving a gap to model-based methods.
Future work
includes improving robustness to such objects, explor-
ing our refiner as a tracker, and replacing our separate
MVS meshing step with direct mesh extraction from
3DGS, as in recent approaches like MeshSplatting [12].

A. Full Implementation Details
We specify two inference configurations.
NovaSplat is
the full configuration and produces all results reported in
the main paper unless stated otherwise. NovaSplat-Lite is
an inference-only variant that streamlines coarse pose es-
timation and photometric refinement for a 3.1× speed-up
(Sec. B). The two share the same onboarding, the same
learned object representation, and the same detection mod-
ule and differ only in the settings listed in Table 4.
Onboarding.
We use the gsplat library [57] to train the
splat for 14k iterations, allowing densification for the first
7k iterations and have the depth loss enabled. Our MVS

C. Onboarding Reconstruction Budget
Sec. B shows that onboarding costs 468.2 s per object,
of which 3DGS training accounts for 326.7 s, or 70%.
We therefore study how the 3DGS training budget trades
against downstream pose accuracy, re-running onboarding
at iteration counts from 500 to our 14k default and re-
evaluating the full pipeline on the HOPEv2 validation set.
Table 7 reports the result.
Accuracy improves steadily up to 5k iterations and then
plateaus.
Relative to the 14k default, the 5k setting is
marginally better on all three metrics (AP 0.741 vs. 0.736;
APMSSD 0.722 vs. 0.718; APMSPD 0.761 vs. 0.754), dif-
ferences of under one percent that we regard as within run-
to-run variation rather than a genuine gain from training
less. Below 5k the picture changes sharply: 1k loses 4.1%
AP relative to the default and 500 iterations loses 11.0%,
as the reconstruction is no longer faithful enough to support
template matching and photometric refinement.
The practical consequence is that the onboarding budget
can be cut substantially at no measurable accuracy cost. At
5k iterations the estimated per-object onboarding time falls
to 258 s, bringing it within the BOP protocol’s five-minute
per-object budget, against 468.2 s at the 14k default.

E. Onboarding Poses from Structure-from-
Motion
The onboarding protocol of BOP-H3 supplies camera poses
together with the onboarding videos, and all results in the
main paper are produced with those poses. This raises a
fair question about how much of our performance depends
on having them. This section shows that the poses can be
recovered from the onboarding video alone and that this re-
mains true for the textureless, specular objects the method
targets, the case in which SfM is most often expected to fail.
E.1. Protocol
We discard the provided poses entirely and re-estimate the
onboarding trajectory from the RGB frames with COLMAP
features and matches [41] followed by the GLOMAP global
mapper [37]. Every onboarding frame is used (no temporal
subsampling), images are not masked to the object, loop
detection is disabled, and the camera model is PINHOLE
with the intrinsics shipped with each dataset, held fixed (no
intrinsic refinement).
We run this on two objects chosen to bracket the diffi-
culty range of the benchmark: obj 000001 of HOPEv2,
a textured grocery item, and obj 000005 of Indus-
tryShapes, the most textureless and specular object in that
dataset.
The estimated trajectory lives in an arbitrary gauge (the
global mapper fixes the world frame and the overall scale),
so it cannot be compared to the reference poses directly.
We recover the similarity transform between the two sets
of camera centers in closed form with the Umeyama algo-
rithm, apply it to the estimated poses, and report the follow-
ing:
• Absolute rotation error: the geodesic angle between the
reference camera orientation and the aligned estimate.
• Absolute position error: the Euclidean distance between
the reference and aligned camera centers, in millimeters.
• Relative rotation error: the same angle computed on the
relative pose between consecutive frames. This quantity
is invariant to the alignment and measures the local multi-
view consistency on which the MVS and 3DGS stages

G. HOPEv2 Onboarding Data Inconsistencies
The HOPEv2 dataset contains object instances whose phys-
ical configuration differs between the onboarding and query
images. In particular, articulated or loosely attached com-
ponents are not always in the same relative pose across the
two splits. Figure 10 shows an example in which the cap
orientation relative to the printed label differs between the
images from the onboarding and validation sets. In the on-
boarding image, the cap is rotated sideways, whereas in the
image from the validation set, it is aligned above the label.
This inconsistency creates a mismatch between the ob-
ject geometry reconstructed from the onboarding sequence
and the object in the query images. For methods that build
an explicit 3D reference representation from the onboarding
data, such as 3DGS-based refinement approaches, this mis-
match can affect pose refinement. The rendered reference
model may contain local geometric structures whose rela-
tive arrangement does not correspond to the query obser-
vation, causing the refinement objective to optimize against
inconsistent geometry.
Methods that rely primarily on global learned features or
2D image-space cues may be less affected by these local
3D discrepancies. Consequently, results on such instances
should be interpreted with care, as the object in the query
images may not exactly match the onboarding-derived ref-

H. Qualitative Comparisons
Figure 11 shows additional qualitative comparisons on the
HOPEv2 test set, complementing the results presented in
the main paper. The examples include challenging scenes
with multiple objects, partial occlusions, clutter, and visu-
ally similar packaging, where accurate 6D pose estimation
requires both reliable object localization and precise pose
refinement.
Across these examples, NovaSplat produces predictions
that are better aligned with the visible object extent and ori-
entation than the competing model-free baselines. By op-
timizing the pose against the reconstructed 3D representa-
tion, NovaSplat yields tighter alignment between the pro-
jected object geometry and the image evidence.
The comparison also highlights common failure modes
of the baseline methods. GFreeDet, OPFormer, and NeMO-
Pose can produce plausible detections, but their predicted
poses are often less consistent with the object boundaries,
especially under occlusion or when only a limited portion
of the object is visible. In cluttered scenes, these methods
may also exhibit larger orientation errors or less stable box
projections. In contrast, NovaSplat maintains more geomet-
rically consistent predictions, suggesting that the 3DGS-
based reference representation provides useful constraints
during refinement.
Like all evaluated methods, NovaSplat may occasionally
produce false positive predictions. These predictions are
primarily inherited from misdetections made by the initial
object detection. Our method attempts to align these er-
roneous proposals, ultimately preserving them in the final
scene prediction. While NovaSplat mitigates many of the
failure modes seen in competing methods, explicitly filter-
ing out these inherited misdetections remains an interesting
direction for future work.
Furthermore, Figures 12 and 13 present the qualita-
tive results on the IndustryShapes Classic and Extended
datasets, respectively.
These visualizations demonstrate
that our model-free NovaSplat robustly handles challeng-
ing, textureless industrial parts, achieving highly accurate
pose alignments that are competitive with state-of-the-art
model-based methods, even under extreme viewpoint vari-
ations.
