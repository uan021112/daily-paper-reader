Abstract
3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has been becoming a promising 3D recon-
struction technique because of its advantage of high-fidelity rendering effect
with high efficiency. However, it still suffers from coarse density control and
semantically blind loss functions when reconstructing the fine-grained struc-
tures in complex urban scenes. Thus, this study proposes SG-GS, a semantic-
guided 3DGS method for fine-grained structure reconstruction. It takes the
semantic segmentation deduced by the Segment Anything Model (SAM) as a
constraint and systematically integrates it into both Gaussian density control
and loss function design. On the one hand, a semantic-constrained adaptive
density control mechanism is constructed, which dynamically allocates gra-
dient thresholds to different semantic regions of the scene and introduces an
additional boundary-enhanced densification strategy, so that new Gaussians
are directed to structurally critical regions.
On the other hand, a multi-
feature guided loss function is designed, and fine-grained supervision signals
are gradually introduced through a phased progressive training strategy, al-
leviating the artifacts caused by the lack of semantic constraints in the basic
loss. Experiments on three datasets show that the proposed method consis-

1. Introduction
With the advancement of refined urban management, higher requirements
are being placed on the accuracy, completeness and richness of detail of 3D
urban scene models (Jiang et al., 2021, 2020; Liu et al., 2024). Urban scenes
are highly complex and contain many semantic categories such as historical
buildings, metal towers, vegetation and road markings. The reconstruction
targets in different regions differ significantly in geometric features, texture
features and spatial structure, which poses a severe challenge to the fine-
grained expressive ability of 3D reconstruction methods.
The 3D reconstruction pipeline based on Structure from Motion (SfM)
and Multi-View Stereo (MVS) (Jiang et al., 2024; Schönberger and Frahm,
2016; Furukawa and Hernández, 2015; Jiang et al., 2022) usually executes
a sequence of tedious steps, including image orientation, dense point cloud
generation, Delaunay meshing and texture mapping. The pipeline is long
and computationally heavy, and the resulting mesh models fall short in detail
fidelity and novel view synthesis quality. In recent years, Neural Radiance
Fields (NeRF) (Mildenhall et al., 2021) have attracted wide attention for
their implicit neural representation and photo-realistic novel view synthesis.
However, their computational intensity and long training time (Barron et al.,
2022; Turki et al., 2022), together with an implicit representation that is
difficult to edit, limit their large-scale applications.
Currently, 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) (Kerbl et al., 2023) represents
a scene with explicit Gaussian primitives and, combined with highly paral-
lel differentiable rasterization, achieves real-time novel view synthesis while
maintaining high rendering quality (Yu et al., 2026a). 3DGS has significant
advantages over NeRF in rendering efficiency and editability and has become
the mainstream method for 3D reconstruction and novel view synthesis (Yu
et al., 2026b). Nevertheless, 3DGS still has two core limitations when fac-
ing complex urban structures. First, its Adaptive Density Control (ADC)
2


adopts a globally uniform gradient threshold and cannot apply differentiated
densification to thin linear structures (e.g., metal towers and railings) and
flat regions (e.g., sky and roads), which leads to under-reconstruction of thin
structures and redundant Gaussians in flat regions (Ye et al., 2024b; Zhang
et al., 2024b). Second, the training loss imposes the same constraint on all
pixels of an image and cannot guide the optimization towards key regions
such as building edges and texture details (Zhang et al., 2024a).
Existing research therefore mainly follows two technical routes, i.e., den-
sity control optimization and semantic information fusion. The first cate-
gory improves representation efficiency by optimizing Gaussian cloning and
pruning strategies.
AbsGS (Ye et al., 2024b) revealed the gradient colli-
sion problem in the original ADC and proposed replacing the vector norm
with the sum of absolute gradients. Pixel-GS (Zhang et al., 2024b) proposed
pixel-area-weighted gradient statistics. 3DGS-MCMC (Kheradmand et al.,
2024) introduced Markov chain Monte Carlo sampling to replace the tra-
ditional ADC. In general, however, these methods adopt globally uniform
strategies, ignoring the geometric differences between semantic regions, and
can hardly enhance thin structures while compressing flat regions at the
same time. The second category focuses on fusing semantic information with
3DGS. Feature 3DGS (Zhou et al., 2024b) attaches extra feature dimen-
sions to each Gaussian and distills 2D features extracted by large models
such as SAM/CLIP/DINOv2 into a 3DGS feature field for feature rendering.
LangSplat (Qin et al., 2024) encodes CLIP language features into Gaus-
sians for open-vocabulary querying. Gaussian Grouping (Ye et al., 2024a)
groups Gaussians with the instance masks generated by the Segment Any-
thing Model (SAM) (Kirillov et al., 2023) to support object tracking and
scene editing. These methods, however, mainly attach semantic attributes
to the Gaussians for downstream understanding and editing tasks, which in-
creases the number of parameters and memory footprint without effectively
improving the reconstruction accuracy metrics.
To address the insufficient structure preservation, the lack of semantic
guidance and the single geometric constraint of existing 3DGS methods in
fine-grained urban reconstruction, this paper builds a semantic-guided fine-
grained reconstruction framework that jointly optimizes density control and
loss supervision. The main contributions are as follows. (1) To address the
inability of globally uniform density control to adapt to regional differences
in urban scenes, SAM semantic segmentation constraints are introduced into
the Gaussian densification process. Gradient thresholds are allocated accord-
3


ing to semantic regions and a boundary-enhanced densification mechanism
is added, so that newly added Gaussians concentrate on structurally crit-
ical regions while redundant generation in flat regions is reduced. (2) To
address the inability of a uniform loss function to guide the optimization of
key regions, a multi-feature guided loss function is constructed that fuses
feature-gradient attention, edge preservation and high-frequency constraints.
Combined with a phased progressive training strategy that introduces super-
vision signals step by step, it improves the fidelity of geometric details while
ensuring stable convergence.
This study is organized as follows. Section 2 presents the literature review
from density control optimization and semantic information fusion. Section 3
gives the details of the proposed solution for semantic-guided density control
and loss design, which is followed by tests in terms of viual analysis of key
steps, rendering quality evaluation, and ablation study in Section 4. Finally,
Section 5 presents conclusions and future studies.
2. Related work
2.1. Density control optimization
In 3DGS, Adaptive Density Control decides where Gaussians are added
and removed during optimization (Kerbl et al., 2023): the view-space posi-
tional gradient of each Gaussian is accumulated over a fixed interval, primi-
tives whose mean gradient exceeds a global threshold are cloned or split, and
those with negligible opacity or excessive extent are pruned. This heuristic
has well-documented deficiencies. Gradients from different views may cancel
out, so that large Gaussians covering complex regions are never densified (Ye
et al., 2024b); the statistics are biased by the number of pixels a Gaussian
covers (Zhang et al., 2024b); and because the decision is driven by photomet-
ric error alone, flat regions tend to be over-reconstructed while thin structures
remain under-reconstructed (Rota Bulò et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2024a).
Subsequent work improves ADC along two lines. The first refines the
densification criterion. AbsGS (Ye et al., 2024b) replaces the norm of the
summed gradient with the sum of absolute gradient components to resolve
gradient collision. Pixel-GS (Zhang et al., 2024b) weights per-view gradients
by pixel coverage and camera distance. Rota Bulò et al. (2024) reformulate
densification from an error-driven perspective and introduce an explicit prim-
itive budget. Taming 3DGS (Mallick et al., 2024) combines gradient, cover-
age, saliency and depth cues to reach a target budget in a controlled manner.
4


The second line treats density control as a sampling or compression prob-
lem. 3DGS-MCMC (Kheradmand et al., 2024) replaces cloning and splitting
with stochastic Langevin updates and relocation, Mini-Splatting (Fang and
Wang, 2024) and LightGaussian (Fan et al., 2024) prune primitives accord-
ing to importance scores to represent a scene with a constrained number of
Gaussians, while Scaffold-GS (Lu et al., 2024) and CityGaussian (Liu et al.,
2024) rely on anchor- or block-based structures to keep the primitive count
tractable in large scenes.
Despite their diversity, these methods decide to densify or prune exclu-
sively from image-space statistics, such as gradient magnitude, pixel coverage
or photometric error, and apply the same rule uniformly over the scene. In
urban environments, however, the required primitive density varies drasti-
cally with the semantic category of the surface. Thin towers, railings and
cables need many small Gaussians that a global threshold rarely triggers,
whereas sky, roads and bare walls are adequately represented by a few large
primitives. Globally uniform strategies therefore cannot enhance thin struc-
tures and compress flat regions simultaneously, which motivates the semantic-
constrained density control proposed in this study.
2.2. Semantic information fusion
Semantic information was first fused into radiance fields in the NeRF
literature, where Semantic-NeRF (Zhi et al., 2021) appends a semantic head
to the radiance field and propagates sparse or noisy 2D labels through multi-
view consistency. Vision foundation models such as CLIP (Radford et al.,
2021), DINOv2 (Oquab et al., 2024) and the Segment Anything Model (SAM)
(Kirillov et al., 2023) subsequently made dense 2D semantic priors available
for arbitrary scenes, and LERF (Kerr et al., 2023) distilled language-aligned
features into a NeRF for open-vocabulary querying.
The explicit nature of 3DGS makes it convenient to attach such features
to individual primitives. Feature 3DGS (Zhou et al., 2024b) augments each
Gaussian with a learnable feature vector and distills SAM, CLIP or DINOv2
features into a 3D feature field.
LangSplat (Qin et al., 2024) uses SAM
to define hierarchical semantic levels and compresses CLIP features with a
scene-wise autoencoder. LEGaussians (Shi et al., 2024) and OpenGaussian
(Wu et al., 2024) pursue memory-efficient and point-level open-vocabulary
understanding. For segmentation and editing, Gaussian Grouping (Ye et al.,
2024a) supervises a per-Gaussian identity encoding with video-tracked SAM
masks, and SAGA (Cen et al., 2025) distills SAM masks into 3D affinity

features. In urban scenes, HUGS (Zhou et al., 2024a) and Street Gaussians
(Yan et al., 2024) jointly model geometry, appearance, semantics and motion
to decompose driving sequences into static and dynamic components.
Common to these works is that semantics are treated as an additional
output of the representation: extra attributes are stored per Gaussian so
that semantic maps or language features can be rendered for downstream
understanding and editing. This increases the parameter count and memory
footprint. A less explored direction uses external priors as additional super-
vision, e.g. monocular depth for geometric regularization (Turkulainen et al.,
2025; Yang et al., 2024) or progressive frequency regularization against over-
reconstruction (Zhang et al., 2024a). These priors, however, are semantic-
agnostic and constrain every pixel in the same way.
To the best of our
knowledge, using semantic segmentation priors to decide where Gaussians are
densified and how strongly individual pixels are supervised has not been sys-
tematically investigated for 3DGS. The proposed SG-GS (Semantic-Guided
3DGS ) fills this gap by converting SAM region masks into region-wise den-
sification thresholds and a pixel importance weight map, so that semantics
guide training without adding any attribute to the final representation.

3. Methodology
This paper aims to resolve the conflict between insufficient detail repre-
sentation, accumulation of redundant Gaussians and artifacts in 3DGS re-
construction of urban scenes. Most existing methods optimize from a single
perspective such as density control, loss function or regularization, and are
prone to sparse reconstruction of linear structures, blurred boundaries and
semi-transparent floaters in complex urban scenes. To this end, this study
introduces semantic guidance and structural constraints and build the 3DGS
framework SG-GS (Semantic-guided Gaussian Splatting), as shown in Fig. 1.

3.3. Multi-feature guided loss and progressive training
The original 3DGS uses a fixed weighted combination of L1 and D-SSIM
as the reconstruction loss, applying supervision of equal strength to all pixels,
which is semantically blind: low-information regions such as sky and road
surfaces usually occupy most pixels of urban images, so the optimization
capacity is spread over these regions, while key details that carry visual
quality, such as building contours, receive insufficient supervision, resulting
in blurred edges and lost high-frequency textures.
We design the Multi-Feature Guided Loss (MF-Loss).
In the prepro-
cessing stage, a pixel importance weight map is built offline from the
12


multi-granularity features of SAM; during training, the reconstruction loss
is weighted per pixel accordingly, an edge-preservation constraint and a
frequency-domain high-frequency constraint are added, and a phased pro-
gressive training strategy controls when and how strongly each supervision
signal is introduced. Unlike methods that attach learnable semantic features
to the Gaussian primitives, this design places all guidance on the loss side,
adds no model parameters or rendering overhead, and is thus a lightweight
external guidance.
3.3.1. Pixel importance weight map
The key idea of the weight map is that the reconstruction difficulty dif-
fers significantly between semantic regions: linear structures and semantic
boundaries are where errors concentrate and supervision should be biased,
whereas flat regions such as sky and roads only need basic constraints.
First, a base importance is assigned to every pixel according to its region
type:

4. Experiments and Analysis
4.1. Datasets and configuration
To verify the effectiveness of the proposed method, experiments are con-
ducted on the public Zeche (Nex et al., 2015) and DroneSplat (Tang et al.,
2025) datasets and on the self-collected Derrick dataset.
Details of the
datasets are listed in Table 1. The Zeche dataset contains 147 aerial images
with many object instances and complex texture structures, and is used to
verify the detail reconstruction ability in complex urban scenes. The Derrick
dataset contains 292 aerial images with a large number of steel linear struc-
tures, and is used to verify the reconstruction of thin geometric structures.
The DroneSplat dataset contains 24 sequences captured by drones, covering
dynamic and static scenes, and is used to verify static scene reconstruction
from drone imagery in the presence of dynamic distractors. For all datasets,
COLMAP is used for feature extraction, incremental SfM reconstruction and
camera pose estimation, and the resulting sparse point clouds serve as the
initialization of 3DGS. Example images of the datasets are shown in Fig. 6.
The experimental server runs Ubuntu 22.04 with a single NVIDIA
GeForce RTX 4090 GPU; the deep learning framework is PyTorch 2.1.2
with CUDA 11.8. The model is trained for 30,000 iterations. The original
density control is used for the first 3,000 iterations to establish a stable
geometric skeleton.

4.2. Visual analysis of key individual modules
4.2.1. SAM segmentation and region classification
Fig. 7 shows the intermediate results of each stage of semantic prepro-
cessing on the three datasets. SAM generates instance-level masks image by
image (Fig. 7(b)). The masks are divided into four region types (Fig. 7(c)) by
the classification rule of Section 3.2 (Eq. (10)). The steel beams and cables
of the derrick in the Derrick scene are stably recognized as linear structures
(red) owing to their low compactness and large aspect ratio; high-gradient
regions such as trees in the Zeche scene are assigned to the textured re-
gion (green); sky and road surfaces in the DroneSplat scene are assigned to
the flat region (purple). The boundary map (Fig. 7(d)) forms continuous
high-response bands at the junctions of adjacent masks, with the strongest
responses on both sides of linear structures, which are the places where the
original 3DGS most often produces “broken lines” and “blurred edges”.
It should be noted that the goal of region classification is not pixel-
accurate semantics. A small number of misclassifications (e.g., recognizing a
thin shadow as a linear structure) only slightly changes the local threshold
multiplier and does not alter the basic “gradient-triggered” logic of densifica-
tion, so the influence on the final reconstruction is limited. Moreover, both
the region map and the boundary map are generated offline.

4.3.2. Qualitative evaluation
Fig. 11 shows the results on the Derrick dataset; from left to right are the
ground truth, 3DGS, Scaffold-GS and SG-GS. The scene contains many linear
structures such as transmission towers and pipelines. Such structures occupy
few pixels and have weak rendering gradients, so they can hardly trigger den-
sification under a globally uniform threshold; 3DGS can only approximate
the thin geometry with a few large Gaussians, resulting in semi-transparent
floaters and discontinuous structures. SG-GS lowers the gradient threshold
of linear-structure regions to 0.5 times the base threshold and explicitly pe-
nalizes deviations of linear edges with the edge-preservation term (Eq. (17)),
almost eliminating artifacts and blur and verifying the effectiveness of the
semantic adaptive threshold mechanism.
For the Zeche dataset, Fig. 12 shows the visual results. The scene has
many object instances and complex textures; 3DGS is clearly blurred at the
junction between the small house and the background and at object bound-
aries, because the uniform loss spreads the optimization capacity over large

4.3.3. Efficiency analysis
Fig. 14 plots the evolution of the number of Gaussian primitives and
the test PSNR with the number of iterations: (a) compares the number of
Gaussians of 3DGS and SG-GS, and (b) compares their PSNR. The number
of Gaussians of SG-GS is higher than that of 3DGS throughout training:
the lowered thresholds in linear-structure and boundary regions make weak-
gradient key regions trigger cloning and splitting earlier and more frequently.
Importantly, these additional Gaussians are not indiscriminate redundant
clones but are directed by the semantic prior to the regions where errors
concentrate, such as linear structures and semantic boundaries. The PSNR
curve shows that SG-GS leads 3DGS continuously once semantic guidance
is enabled (iteration 3,000), and the lead widens further as the edge and
frequency supervision ramp up, indicating that the additional Gaussians are
effectively converted into reconstruction quality and that the quality gain per
Gaussian is higher than the blind cloning of the baseline.
In terms of training time, under the same hardware and number of it-
erations, 3DGS takes 32.08 mins and SG-GS takes 44.9 mins, an increase
of about 40%. The extra cost comes from two sources: the increased to-
tal number of Gaussians raises the computation of rasterization and back-

propagation in each iteration, and the edge convolution and FFT in MF-Loss
introduce additional overhead. It should be noted that our method attaches
no learnable features to the Gaussian primitives and introduces no decoding
network at rendering time, which is fundamentally different from existing se-
mantic fusion methods (Zhou et al., 2024b; Qin et al., 2024; Ye et al., 2024a)
that continuously pay feature storage and decoding costs at the rendering
stage. Thus, our method trades about 40% more training time for stable ac-

modeling applications where reconstruction quality is the primary goal.
Taking the quantitative and qualitative results on the three datasets to-
gether, the proposed method stably improves the reconstruction quality of
urban scenes without attaching extra learnable features to the Gaussian prim-
itives or introducing a decoding network, with especially significant improve-
ments in linear structures and semantic boundary regions, which verifies the
effectiveness and generalization ability of the semantic-guided framework.

Taking the L1+D-SSIM baseline loss (with both SG-ADC and MF-Loss
disabled, i.e., the 3DGS baseline) as the reference, the weight-map weight-
ing, the edge-preservation loss and the frequency-domain high-frequency loss

Table 7. The weight-map weighting contributes the largest gain, improv-
ing PSNR by 0.52 dB over the baseline and verifying the redistribution of
optimization capacity by the pixel importance weight map. Adding the edge-
preservation loss on top slightly lowers PSNR, but LPIPS further decreases
from 0.19 to 0.18, indicating that its benefit lies more in structure and per-

the complete MF-Loss raises PSNR back to 24.93 dB while keeping LPIPS
at its best, indicating that the frequency constraint effectively recovers the
lost high-frequency details. The three losses act on different levels and are
ultimately complementary, jointly constituting the complete multi-feature
guided supervision.

5. Conclusion
To address the coarse density control strategy and semantically blind
loss function of the original 3DGS in reconstructing complex urban struc-
tures, this paper proposes SG-GS, a semantic-guided fine-grained 3DGS re-
construction method that jointly optimizes density control and loss super-
vision. A semantic-constrained adaptive density control mechanism is built,
31


which allocates gradient thresholds according to region type and adds a
boundary-enhanced densification strategy, so that new Gaussians concentrate
on key regions such as linear structures and semantic boundaries. A multi-
feature guided loss function is designed, which weights the reconstruction loss
per pixel with a pixel importance weight map, adds edge-preservation and
frequency-domain high-frequency constraints, and ensures convergence sta-
bility through a phased progressive training strategy. Experiments on three
urban scene datasets show that the proposed method stably outperforms
the original 3DGS and effectively mitigates artifacts on linear structures and
boundary blurring. The method places semantic guidance entirely on the
training side, attaches no learnable parameters to the Gaussian primitives,
introduces no decoding overhead at rendering time.
The method also has limitations. First, appearance modeling still relies
on static spherical harmonics, so its PSNR on datasets with obvious illumina-
tion and exposure changes is inferior to Scaffold-GS, which has view-adaptive
appearance capability. Second, region classification depends on the segmen-
tation quality of SAM; in weakly textured or strongly reflective regions where
segmentation fails, the guidance signal may degrade to the baseline behav-
ior. Future work will explore the joint optimization of semantic guidance
with view-adaptive appearance modeling and surface reconstruction, as well
as block-wise training strategies for large-scale scenes.
