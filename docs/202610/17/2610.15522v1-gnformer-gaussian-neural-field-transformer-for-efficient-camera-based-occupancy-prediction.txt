# GNFormer: Gaussian Neural Field Transformer for Efficient Camera-based Occupancy Prediction 

Guangcheng Chen 1,2, Li He 1,2, and Hong Zhang 1,2, Life Fellow, IEEE 

Abstract — Camera-based occupancy prediction provides ge-ometric and semantic scene understanding for robot perception and has been adopted in autonomous vehicle and UAV percep-tion systems. Gaussian primitives offer a promising alternative by potentially representing only occupied regions, thereby concentrating computation and representational capacity where needed and reducing wasted computation and memory in empty space. However, existing Gaussian-based methods suffer from poor primitive utilization, as ineffective training objectives leave some Gaussians in empty space, while the limited expressiveness of Gaussian functions restricts their ability to model complex shapes. To address these limitations, we propose a novel 3D representation, Gaussian Neural Field (GNF), which can accurately map voxel positions to semantic occupancies. In GNF, Gaussians and a neural network jointly parameterize the field, where the Gaussians define its spatial support and provide features for neural decoding, while the neural network enables more expressive occupancy modeling than Gaussian functions alone. We further develop the Gaussian Neural Field Transformer (GNFormer), a streamlined and flexible method that predicts GNFs for camera-based occupancy prediction. Across Occ-ScanNet, SSCBench-KITTI-360, and nuScenes-SurroundOcc benchmarks, GNFormer achieves the highest IoU and mIoU and the fastest inference among the compared single-frame methods. In the experiments, GNFormer can represent indoor and outdoor scenes with only 256 and 1024 Gaussians, respectively, substantially fewer primitives than prior Gaussian-based methods. The code will be released.

A. Gaussian-based Occupancy Prediction 

Gaussian-based methods represent semantic occupancy with a set of geometric primitives [4], [7]–[10], [12]. Gaus-sianFormer [7] proposes the first Gaussian-based occupancy prediction framework, but it can still allocate Gaussians to empty regions. Subsequent methods explore two repre-sentative directions: probabilistic modeling, introduced by GaussianFormer-2 [8], and density-based modeling, adopted by FLM-Occ [10]. GaussianFormer-2 models voxel occu-pancy through probabilistic Gaussian composition, and sev-eral follow-up works [4], [11], [12] further develop this formulation. Across the probabilistic and density-based for-mulations, Gaussian supports approximate occupancy geom-etry, while posterior weights derived from their responses aggregate Gaussian semantics into voxel semantics. However, probabilistic modeling may still leave isolated Gaussians in empty regions, resulting in ineffective primi-tive utilization. FLM-Occ attributes this issue to vanishing gradients with respect to Gaussian primitives and instead optimizes their spatial allocation through Gaussian-induced density. Its likelihood-based regularization encourages Gaus-sians to concentrate around occupied regions, improving Gaussian allocation. However, the voxel grid is still decoded directly from Gaussian density and posterior semantics, limiting representational capacity and occupancy accuracy. Because the spatial supports and semantic posterior weights depend on the same Gaussian parameters, Gaussian alloca-tion remains coupled with semantic decoding. In contrast, GNF decouples these roles by retaining Gaussian supports for occupancy geometry while replacing posterior semantic aggregation with neural-field decoding. 

B. Neural Field-based Occupancy Prediction 

Neural fields provide a continuous mapping from coordi-nates to scene properties and have gained popularity in 3D vision tasks such as novel view synthesis [18] and surface reconstruction [19]. Their neural decoding provides strong capacity for modeling complex geometry and semantic boundaries. TPVFormer [2] combines tri-perspective features with a neural field for fine-grained occupancy prediction. HybridOcc [20] models binary occupancy with a neural field and supervises it through depth rendering, while subsequent works [21]–[23] extend neural fields to semantic occupancy under 2D image supervision. Despite their expressive decod-ing, existing neural-field-based methods commonly require dense or grid-structured queries over large spatial regions, limiting their efficiency for 3D occupancy prediction. GNF addresses this limitation by using Gaussian local supports to define the effective domain of a neural field. GNF restricts neural decoding to voxels covered by Gaussian supports, where the decoder models semantic occupancy. III. M ETHODOLOGY

B. Implementation Details 

For all datasets, we set the number of decoder blocks per refinement Nblk to 3, the number of refinements B to 4,and the Gaussian regularization weight λ to 0.2. We use the AdamW optimizer [32] with a learning rate of 2 × 10 −4,

β1 = 0 .9, β2 = 0 .999 , and a weight decay of 10 −2. The learning rate of the encoder is set to 2 × 10 −5. The initial quaternions of Gaussians are set to (1 , 0, 0, 0) . The support radius τ is set to 3 for feature aggregation Eq. (3) and 6

for Gaussian regularization Eq. (5). We initialize the ViT-B [24] encoder with the weights from DepthAnythingV2 [33] and the ViT-L encoder with the weights from VGGT [34]. V. C ONCLUSION 

We presented GNF, a novel 3D representation for com-pactly parameterizing a semantic occupancy grid. It lever-ages Gaussian regularization and the local support property of Gaussians to restrict neural decoding around occupied regions. Together with a neural decoder, GNF achieves higher accuracy than pure Gaussians while reducing prim-itive budget, computation, and memory costs. Leveraging GNF, we were able to develop GNFormer, a novel camera-based occupancy prediction method that leverages Trans-former blocks predicts GNFs for accurate and efficient inference. Thanks to its streamlined architecture, it can be flexibly trained for different scenarios and camera setups. Across Occ-ScanNet, SSCBench-KITTI-360, and nuScenes-SurroundOcc, GNFormer achieves leading IoU, mIoU, and inference efficiency among single-frame methods with only 256 Gaussians indoors and 1,024 outdoors, demonstrating its effectiveness and efficiency for robot perception. Despite its strong performance among single-frame meth-ods, GNFormer relies solely on single-frame inputs and therefore requires a larger model to achieve high accuracy. As a result, its inference speed remains lower than that of recent streaming-based methods.
