# COMPLEXITY-CALIBRATED RECONSTRUCTION DISCREPANCY FOR NO-REFERENCE POINT CLOUD QUALITY ASSESSMENT 

# Haocheng Wu, Xiangjie Sui † , and Xin Sun 

# City University of Macau, Macau SAR, China 

ABSTRACT 

The discrepancy induced by surface reconstruction in a distorted point cloud is jointly influenced by its intrinsic structural complex-ity and signal degradation. This observation motivates a reference-free point cloud quality assessment (PCQA) strategy that estimates and compensates for the complexity-induced component. We im-plement this strategy through successive reconstruction. Specifi-cally, reconstructing the distorted point cloud produces an input-to-reconstruction discrepancy that combines the effects of signal degra-dation and content complexity. Reapplying the reconstruction oper-ator with stronger smoothing produces a discrepancy between suc-cessive reconstructions, which serves as a proxy for the complexity-induced component. This proxy is then used to calibrate the input-to-reconstruction discrepancy. We apply the same strategy to 3D geometry patches and their aligned 2D regions, enabling local com-parison of the resulting discrepancies. The calibrated geometric and projected-view discrepancies are then fused into a final degrada-tion score. For both branches, the discrepancies are measured using frozen pretrained perceptual representations. Our method does not require PCQA-specific training or fine-tuning. Experiments on three public databases show that the proposed method consistently outper-forms most generic baselines and achieves performance comparable to several full-reference metrics.The code is available at https: //github.com/HC-W-whc/CCRD-NR-PCQA.git 

Index Terms — Point cloud quality assessment, no-reference, surface reconstruction. 

1. INTRODUCTION 

Point clouds are irregular 3D signals whose geometry and appear-ance may be degraded during acquisition, compression, transmis-sion, and rendering [1]. Quantifying the perceptual impact of these impairments is essential for developing and optimizing point cloud processing systems. Subjective assessment provides reliable percep-tual scores [2, 3], but is impractical for automatic quality monitor-ing. Objective quality metrics provide a practical alternative. Full-reference (FR) quality metrics [4, 5] compare a distorted point cloud with its aligned pristine counterpart. However, such a reference is often unavailable in practical applications, motivating no-reference point cloud quality assessment (NR-PCQA) [6, 7]. Most NR-PCQA methods address the missing-reference prob-lem by learning a direct mapping from statistical or deep represen-tations to subjective scores [8, 9, 10]. Projection-based methods fur-ther use 2D views to model the perceptual effects of point cloud distortions [2, 11, 12, 13]. Other methods combine geometric and projected-view information through multimodal architectures [14]. Probabilistic models account for the variability of subjective judg-ments [15]. When evaluated on a local patch or projected region, the same notation is indexed by the local anchor and view. 

2.2. Successive Surface Reconstruction 

We use a pretrained reconstruction model [18] to instantiate the operator Φ, without additional training or fine-tuning on PCQA data. The discrepancy ∆i2r reflects both signal degradation and the structural complexity of the underlying content. In contrast, the discrepancy ∆r2r measures the additional change induced by stronger regularization. Since this change is induced after the initial reconstruction, it provides a content-dependent cue for estimating the complexity-induced component of the initial discrepancy. Note that neither P′ nor P′′ is treated as a pristine reference. Instead, they form a sequence of observations under increasing regularization. 

2.3. Local 3D–2D Correspondence 

To obtain local quality cues, we sample K anchors from the dis-torted point cloud P using farthest-point sampling. Each anchor de-fines a local geometry patch formed by its M neighboring points.

3. EXPERIMENTS 3.1. Experimental setup Databases. We evaluate the proposed method on three public PCQA databases. SJTU-PCQA [2] contains 9 reference point clouds and 

378 distorted samples generated from 7 distortion types at 6 severity levels, including compression, color noise, geometry noise, down-sampling, and their combinations. WPC2.0 [21] contains 16 refer-ence point clouds and 400 V-PCC samples produced by combining 

5 geometry and 5 attribute quantization levels, providing controlled geometry–attribute compression distortions. ICIP2020 [22] contains 

6 reference point clouds and 90 samples encoded by G-PCC Octree, G-PCC Trisoup, and V-PCC at 5 quality levels, primarily covering codec-induced compression artifacts. 

Evaluation criteria. We use the Spearman rank-order correla-tion coefficient (SRCC) and the Pearson linear correlation coefficient (PLCC) to measure the agreement between the predicted degradation and the mean opinion scores (MOS). Since a larger distance indi-cates lower perceptual quality, the predicted distance typically has a negative correlation with MOS. We therefore report |SRCC |. Be-fore computing PLCC, we apply the standard five-parameter logistic mapping. To fairly compare methods that require training, we use a nine-fold evaluation protocol for each database. In each fold, super-vised NR-PCQA methods are trained on the corresponding training split and evaluated on the held-out test split. All methods are evalu-ated on the same nine test splits, and the reported SRCC and PLCC values are averaged over the nine folds. 

Implementation details. The surface-reconstruction operator, geometric feature extractor, and projected-view quality model are initialized with publicly available pretrained weights and kept frozen throughout the experiments. No PCQA-specific training or fine-tuning is performed. We sample K = 64 anchors from each dis-torted point cloud and extract M = 32 points around each an-chor to form a local geometry patch. Each local patch is associ-ated with six orthographic views. A projected view is retained when at least Nmin = 32 points in the corresponding patch is visible. The geometric discrepancy combines the directional and magnitude terms with weights wdir = 0 .2 and wmag = 0 .8. The complexity-calibration coefficient is fixed to λ = 0 .25 , and the geometric and projected-view responses are fused with weights w3D = 0 .6 and 

w2D = 0 .4. All parameters are fixed across the three databases.

4. CONCLUSION 

We presented a reference-free point cloud quality assessment framework that uses successive surface reconstruction to calibrate reconstruction-induced discrepancies. The key idea is to use the change caused by stronger regularization as a content-dependent proxy for the component associated with intrinsic structural com-plexity. By combining calibrated geometric and projected-view cues, the proposed method achieves competitive performance with-out PCQA-specific training or fine-tuning. Current geometric and appearance discrepancies are measured in frozen pretrained perceptual spaces. Although this simple in-stantiation provides an initial validation that reconstruction-induced discrepancies contain useful cues for PCQA, the calibration may not fully capture distortion types with reconstruction responses that differ substantially from those considered in the evaluated databases. Future work will investigate point-cloud-specific mea-sures of reconstruction-induced discrepancies, as well as content-adaptive reconstruction and calibration strategies. 5. REFERENCES
