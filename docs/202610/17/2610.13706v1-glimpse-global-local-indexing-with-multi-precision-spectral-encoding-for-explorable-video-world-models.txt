ABSTRACT
A camera-controlled video world model must keep a scene consistent when the
camera returns to it. Memories that render stored features into the target frame can
turn depth and pose errors into ghosting or duplicated structure. Positional transport
avoids that resampling by relocating a historical patch’s address rather than its
content, but assigns full positional precision even to inaccurate projections. We
present GLIMPSE, a global–local memory interface with band-wise multi-precision
coordinates and geometry-conditioned spectral attenuation to reduce sensitivity
to inaccurate projected addresses. Local patch memory supplies view-specific
appearance, while recurrent global memory provides scene context. Footprint-
guided retrieval selects historical patches under a fixed local read budget. Under
matched retrieval and read budgets, GLIMPSE improves geometric correspondence
and photometric consistency over deterministic positional transport.

INTRODUCTION
An explorable video world model generates the next video window along a requested camera
trajectory. During extended exploration it must also remain self-consistent: surfaces should retain
their appearance when the camera returns to them. Revisiting previously observed regions requires
retrieving historical appearance and addressing it in the requested view. Yet geometric estimates are
imperfect, making the reliability of these positional addresses critical to consistent generation.
Prior work follows three main approaches. Frame- and view-retrieval memories retain selected
historical observations (Yu et al., 2025a; Xiao et al., 2025; Li et al., 2025b). A limited set of views

duplicated structure. Positional transport avoids this resampling by leaving each historical patch
exactly as it was observed and relocating only its address within attention (Xu et al., 2026b; Yu et al.,
2026). Each token remains associated with its original patch, without resampling historical features
onto the target grid.
Positional transport preserves historical appearance, but relies on estimated geometry to locate it in
the target view. Errors in depth or camera pose can misplace these positional addresses, with high-
frequency rotary bands being particularly sensitive to small coordinate errors. Standard positional
transport does not account for this varying geometric reliability. We therefore adapt the precision
and frequency content of each address to the reliability of its underlying geometry, preserving fine
positional detail when reliable and reducing sensitivity to uncertain projections otherwise (Figure 2).
We introduce GLIMPSE, Global–Local Indexing with Multi-Precision Spectral Encoding, a global–
local memory framework for explorable video generation (Figure 1). In our framework, a frozen
recurrent memory provides global context, while a local pointer memory retrieves original historical
patches through projected footprint coverage under a fixed per-frame read budget. The two memories
are accessed through separately gated readouts. To address retrieved patches in the requested view,
we introduce Spatio-Temporal Aligned Positional Encoding (STaPE), which represents projected
locations at band-specific coordinate precisions and regulates their spectral strength using geometric
reliability cues. This design reduces sensitivity to projection errors without resampling historical
appearance.
Our contributions are threefold:
• We propose a geometry-addressed global–local memory interface that separates scene-
level recurrent context from view-specific appearance. Footprint-guided retrieval selects
original historical patches under a fixed local read budget. Separate gated readouts combine
the two memories without making global context compete with local evidence in the same
attention normalization.
• We introduce multi-precision positional addressing, assigning cell, half-cell, and con-
tinuous coordinates to different rotary frequency bands. Fine-frequency addresses remain
unchanged under perturbations within a bin, while continuous lower-frequency coordinates
retain sub-cell placement without adding memory tokens.
• We develop geometry-conditioned spectral attenuation, using depth smoothness and
temporal lag to regulate spatial frequency components of memory keys and values. To-
gether with multi-precision addressing, it controls the precision and strength of transported
addresses. Matched ablations establish their complementary benefits over deterministic
positional transport in geometric correspondence and photometric consistency.

RELATED WORK
2.1
INTERACTIVE VIDEO WORLD MODELS
Interactive video world models generate observations in response to user actions while keeping the
environment coherent across interaction steps. In camera-directed systems, the action specifies a
trajectory and the observation is the next video window. InSpatio-World develops this autoregressive
trajectory-control interface, while AlayaWorld, Wonder, DreamX-World, and Infinite-World target
long-running playable or general-purpose environments (InSpatio Team et al., 2026; AlayaWorld
Team et al., 2026; Xu et al., 2026a; DreamX Team et al., 2026; Wu et al., 2026a). GLIMPSE
focuses on retaining historical appearance and retrieving it for a new camera query. Vid2World
adapts pretrained video diffusion models to causal interactive generation (Huang et al., 2026), and
MiniWorld instead studies a reproducible streaming training recipe from scratch (Zhao et al., 2026).
GLIMPSE studies the memory interface within a pretrained video generator.
2.2
CAMERA CONTROL AND POSITIONAL ALIGNMENT
Camera-controllable generation conditions synthesis on an explicit viewpoint trajectory. CameraCtrl
injects camera-ray conditioning (He et al., 2024), whereas TrajectoryCrafter jointly conditions on
point-cloud renders and source video through a dual-stream diffusion model (Yu et al., 2025b).
GEN3C conditions generation on a point-cloud cache rendered at the requested camera (Ren et al.,
2025). UCPE combines Relative Ray Encoding of 6-DoF pose, intrinsics, and lens distortion with
Absolute Orientation Encoding of pitch and roll through a lightweight spatial-attention adapter (Zhang
et al., 2026). PRoPE expresses relative camera geometry as projective positional encoding inside
attention (Li et al., 2025a). CameraSquad combines decoupled content and camera control with dual-
mode cross-view attention for consistent parallel multi-trajectory generation (Xu et al., 2026c). These
encode geometry to control generated target tokens. UCM applies the same VAE and patchification
to historical and target latents, then warps positional encoding in time, assigning target space-time
coordinates to clean historical tokens while leaving their content unchanged (Xu et al., 2026b).
Integrated positional encoding attenuates Fourier features according to spatial uncertainty (Barron
et al., 2021), and our work instead considers geometry-conditioned positional addressing for retrieved
video memory.
2.3
PERSISTENT AND SPATIAL MEMORY
Recent-context methods improve how a finite frame history is packed or weighted (Zhang et al., 2025;
Song et al., 2025; Shen et al., 2026). Persistent-memory methods retain information beyond that
window: Context as Memory and WorldMem retrieve stored frames, VMem retrieves views through
a surfel index of the queried surface, and SPMem combines working, spatial, and episodic stores (Yu
et al., 2025a; Xiao et al., 2025; Li et al., 2025b; Wu et al., 2025). CUT3R maintains a recurrent state
for streaming 3D perception and MeMix updates that state selectively to reduce forgetting (Wang
et al., 2025b; Dong et al., 2026). GLIMPSE uses these frozen components for its global branch.
Long-context state-space video world models combine blockwise state-space scanning with local
attention (Po et al., 2025). GIM-World compresses history into learned geometry-aware implicit
memory and prunes observations (Wei et al., 2026). In contrast to this compressed history, LPM
retains the provenance of individual source patches. WorldPlay reconstructs context from past frames
and temporally reframes old observations to maintain geometric consistency (Sun et al., 2025).
A further line of work makes spatial memory directly addressable. Mirage persists latent features at
world points and renders their values onto a target grid, MosaicMem indexes localized patches and
aligns them through warped RoPE or warped latents, and SPMem renders explicit spatial-memory
values in the target view alongside sparse episodic frames (Wang et al., 2026a; Yu et al., 2026;
Wu et al., 2025). The value-warping variants commit appearance to a target raster before attention,
whereas positional-warping variants preserve separate source features. AnchorWeave retrieves local
geometric memories by trajectory coverage and renders them as anchor conditions (Wang et al.,
2026b), and WorldTrace keeps a compressed cache addressable by assigning summary slots virtual
positions (Wu et al., 2026b). Concurrently, ReWorld bounds long-horizon memory with a pose-
indexed landmark bank under a fixed KV budget (Chen et al., 2026). LPM uses the positional-warping

Retrieval-to-read procedure.
Let Hp × Wp be the shared patch-embedding grid and M = HpWp.
For each target frame q, candidate views are jointly ranked by camera proximity and temporal lag,
prioritizing source observations over generated history. Using the occupancy masks Fi of projected
patch quadrilaterals, greedy selection adds the view covering the most uncovered cells at each round.
In selected-view order the allocator keeps the first positive-depth, in-bounds projected center for each
target cell and fills the remaining capacity up to M with unused original tokens. These fillers may be
redundant or outside the target frustum. The allocator preserves individual token features and applies
no target-depth test. Table 13 compares this allocator with center-hit and causal z-buffer controls
under a matched token budget.
At each denoising step, history and main-stream latents share the DiT patch embedding. Each
backbone layer updates pointer tokens at timestep zero using source-coordinate native RoPE and
a tanh(gℓ) gate, then processes the main stream. Target queries read the associated pointer K/V
through STaPE and GRM through a separate softmax. The two outputs form the conditioning residual
(Equation 14). Appendix A provides the implementation settings and retrieval details.

TRAINING AND DATA CONSTRUCTION
We train on a mixture of SpatialVID (Wang et al., 2025a), RealCam-Vid (Zheng et al., 2025),
ReCamMaster (Bai et al., 2025), and Context-as-Memory (Yu et al., 2025a). Cached DA3 (Lin et al.,
2025) pose, intrinsics, and depth are the only geometry source, and a cache miss skips the sample
instead of invoking online estimation. Three augmentations stress the memory interface. Future-as-
memory uses real post-target observations as training-only LPM donors with relabeled memory times.
Inference uses only source observations and completed generated windows. Synthetic revisitation
builds complementary old-view memory from multiple keyframes under a shared camera perturbation,
and fast motion temporally subsamples the target at stride two or three while keeping the dense
history. Sampling weights, offsets, probabilities, and eligibility rules are specified in Appendix B.

evaluated under the same causal inference protocol.

EXPERIMENTS
4.1
EXPERIMENTAL SETUP
GLIMPSE uses Wan2.2-TI2V-5B (Team Wan et al., 2025), cached DA3 geometry (Lin et al., 2025),
and frozen CUT3R recurrent context (Wang et al., 2025b; Dong et al., 2026). Camera control uses
trajectory errors, CLIP-T/F, FID, and FVD. WorldScore (Duan et al., 2025) evaluates quality and
consistency, while Y-traj. also measures revisit correspondences and RGB agreement. We compare
against UCM (Xu et al., 2026b), SPMem (Wu et al., 2025), GEN3C (Ren et al., 2025), VMem (Li
et al., 2025b), and the concurrent ReWorld (Chen et al., 2026), following their public inference
recipes. These comparisons evaluate complete systems, while matched internal ablations isolate the
contribution of STaPE. SPMem denotes the spatial-memory video world model of Wu et al. (2025).
Training settings, metric definitions, and comparison details are collected in Appendix B.2.
4.2
CAMERA-CONTROL EVALUATION
Table 1 summarizes camera diagnostics and video-quality metrics (Bai et al., 2025). GLIMPSE
attains the lowest rotation errors and the lowest translation-direction error, together with the best
CLIP-T, CLIP-F and FID. UCM achieves lower translation NRMSE, while ReWorld achieves lower
FVD. Table 2 reports the WorldScore components on the same subset: GLIMPSE leads six of the
nine and the nine-component mean, ReWorld ranks second overall and leads on 3D reprojection,
while UCM leads on style consistency. Figure 4 shows qualitative replay comparisons.

CONCLUSION AND LIMITATIONS
We presented GLIMPSE, a global–local memory indexing framework with multi-precision spectral
encoding for explorable video world models. Local pointer memory preserves view-specific historical
evidence, while recurrent global memory supplies complementary scene context. At the core of
this interface, STaPE addresses a challenge that persists even when historical content is preserved:
inaccurate geometry can assign that content an overly precise positional address. By combining band-
wise multi-precision coordinates with geometry-conditioned spectral modulation, STaPE improves
memory reads without resampling historical appearance or increasing the local token budget. Camera-
control and Y-trajectory experiments demonstrate improved cross-view correspondence and recovery
of previously observed content. Matched component ablations support the complementary roles of
the two addressing mechanisms and memory branches, while controlled address perturbations show
reduced sensitivity to projection errors. Together, these results highlight that consistent exploration
depends not only on retaining relevant history, but also on how its geometric addresses enter attention.
However, the current system focuses on camera-conditioned exploration and does not explicitly
model object motion or use few-step distillation for real-time inference. Extending memory updates
to track changing object states and distilling the generator to reduce denoising latency are natural
next steps toward more responsive exploration and interaction.

Three resolutions and two selections.
Let the isomorphic patch grid be Hp × Wp, after the shared
DiT patch embedding, with M = HpWp tokens per frame. Source RGB pixels, these patch cells,
and the 4Hp × 4Wp footprint scoring grid are distinct. Increasing the scoring resolution does not

chooses original tokens from those views using center-cell coverage. Consequently the area covered
by all patches in selected views is not necessarily the area supported by the final M tokens.
Candidate bank and ordering.
The bank contains decoded historical observations and their
aligned DA3 depth/pose/intrinsics, with an immutable-source versus generated-history flag. For
each target frame, camera prefiltering uses si = θi/(15◦) + ∥ti −tq∥/0.25, where translation is
in the scene-depth-normalized gauge. The nearest view is first, and subsequent views are ordered
by si −αi, with stable observation order breaking ties. The default shortlist retains at most 16
immutable-source and 16 generated views (16 total when no source flag is supplied). The all-source
inference option removes the former cap but keeps the generated-view cap. Compared retrieval
variants share this setting and the upstream history sampling schedule, and the attention budget
remains M independently of shortlist size. The shortlist is per target frame, while projection can
batch its union across a target window.
View selection scores sampled geometric footprints, whereas positional addressing uses the projected
centers of the selected tokens, and the two stages therefore consume different geometric primitives.
Projected footprint scoring.

using one depth value shared by the corners and the grid-scaled source intrinsics, then projects

4Hp × 4Wp grid, and in-bounds samples mark occupied cells. Their Boolean union defines the
projected-occupancy mask Fi, counting cross-view overlaps once. Table 13 compares this retrieval

Future donors.
The future augmentation is training-only. With target start s and length T = 20
on the latent-aligned observation clock, the trainer checks every donor index j > s + T + g. We
use g = 40, donor sampling stride 15, at most 16 donor observations, and attempt probability 0.70.
These are observation-index units, not seconds, and frame rate and temporal subsampling determine
their physical duration. The reader selects available donors beyond this guard and supplies their
indices explicitly, and the trainer does not assume a uniform continuous-time distribution. Donors are
sampled only when eligible observations are available. RGB, depth, pose, and intrinsics retain the
same true donor index. Only the timestamps used to compute memory temporal lag are relabeled,
and target RGB/cameras are unchanged. No future donor is available in causal rollout inference.
Revisit and fast-motion augmentation.
Synthetic revisit perturbs multiple selected memory views
using a shared camera-local delta, leaving target RGB and target camera commands unchanged.
Revisitation is attempted with probability 0.50, with perturbations up to 45◦yaw, 0.20 scene-depth
units laterally, and 0.12 units forward. A joint-bank option permits future donors to receive revisit
perturbations as well, and these two augmentations are not inherently mutually exclusive. Fast-motion
augmentation uses temporally subsampled targets with synchronously indexed RGB and geometry,
not a change of playback FPS alone.
Future donors provide additional visual support during training, and inference uses causal observations.
Training/test separation is defined at source-video level and includes derived clips, donor pools, and
geometry caches.

Tasks and inference settings.
Camera replay follows trajectories estimated from the corresponding
source videos. Y-traj. observes the left and right sides before moving backward at the initial heading,

observed prefix, prompt, requested cameras, seed list, resolution, sampler, and horizon. Inference
donors consist of observed source frames and completed generated windows. Generated endpoints are
retained as generated. Source-video-disjoint splits separate derived clips, donor pools, and geometry
caches.
STaPE controls.
Table 5 holds the observation pool, cameras, footprint retrieval, global branch,
and local read budget fixed. Latent/value warping resamples historical features onto the target grid.
PE warp uses continuous coordinates in every spatial band and disables spectral attenuation. The two
intermediate controls enable multi-precision coordinates or spectral attenuation separately, and the
full configuration enables both. Attenuation modulates both memory keys and values.
Memory controls.
Table 6 independently enables GRM and LPM. GRM supplies frozen chrono-
logical recurrent context, and LPM supplies geometry-indexed patches with full STaPE. Source,
text, and camera conditioning are retained in all four configurations. The local read budget is fixed
whenever LPM is enabled.
Metrics and aggregation.
The camera-control table reports mean and endpoint rotation errors,
translation NRMSE, translation-direction error, CLIP-T, CLIP-F, FID, and FVD. DA3-derived pose
scores quantify agreement with the requested trajectory in the estimated scene coordinate system.
WorldScore component scaling and composite weighting are given in the table captions. For per-
case metrics, frame or frame-pair measurements are averaged within each case, followed by an
equal-weight average over cases. Inlier fractions are computed per pair before this aggregation. For
revisit correspondences, each video pairs frames 76–80 and 156–160 with frames 236–240, using
zero-based one-to-one indices, and GIM-DKM (Shen et al., 2024; Edstedt et al., 2023) samples
15,000 correspondences per pair after a common 240×416 resize and padding, and matches above 0.1
confidence are counted (Mat. Pix.). Geometric inliers are estimated with a fundamental matrix using
OpenCV USAC MAGSAC, a 1-pixel threshold in the unpadded 240 × 416 coordinates, confidence
0.999999, and at most 10,000 iterations. Inliers are counted only among matches above 0.1 confidence,
and RGB MAE is evaluated at these accepted correspondences using bilinear sampling of RGB values
in [0, 1]. FID and FVD are computed over the evaluation set as distribution-level metrics. Motion
magnitude measures the median displacement of estimated optical flow rather than camera accuracy,
and flow consistency measures forward–backward optical-flow agreement. On the Y-traj. set, all
six methods use the full shared case set and the same evaluation protocol. Appendix B.3 defines the
counting units and aggregation details.
