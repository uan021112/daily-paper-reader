Abstract
Generating complete 3D assets from sparse, unposed images requires pre-
serving observed geometry while completing unseen regions. Joint recon-
struction and generation offers a way to address both goals: reconstruction
recovers the geometry observed in the input images, while generation con-
tributes learned shape priors. However, combining camera-relative recon-
struction with canonical-space generation introduces a coordinate mismatch
that makes direct geometric interaction more difficult. Moreover, condition-
ing each 3D location on all views without accounting for their relevance may
mix useful geometric evidence with unrelated image content, hindering local
detail recovery. We present UniForge3D, which jointly models reconstruc-
tion and generation in a shared anchor-guided space, resolving their coordi-
nate mismatch. This space follows the anchor view’s azimuth while keep-
ing the object upright. The two branches exchange features bidirectionally
to predict per-view point maps and a complete sparse voxel structure. We
further condition point-map prediction on the generated structure to establish
pixel-to-voxel correspondences and select the most informative input view for
each voxel. Features from the selected views then guide detailed shape gen-
eration, helping preserve observed geometric details. Our method achieves
state-of-the-art appearance and geometry results on Toys4K and GSO, gener-
ating complete 3D assets with improved fidelity. Project webpage: https:

Introduction
High-quality 3D assets are essential for digital content creation, gaming, augmented
and virtual reality, and embodied intelligence. In many practical scenarios, an ob-
ject is observed through only a few images with unknown camera poses. From
these sparse, unposed images, we aim to generate a complete 3D asset that faith-
fully preserves the geometry and appearance observed in every input view.
Existing multi-view 3D generation methods commonly condition native 3D
generators on features extracted from multiple input images. They incorporate
multi-view evidence by assigning images to predefined view slots or adaptively
fusing features across views (Team, 2025; Li et al., 2026b; Sun et al., 2026; Li
et al., 2026a; Zhou et al., 2026). However, image conditioning does not strictly
constrain the generated surfaces to match the observed geometry. Generative mod-
els may therefore favor plausible shapes under their learned priors over faithfully
preserving input-specific structures. This motivates using multi-view reconstruc-
tion to provide more direct geometric guidance.
A straightforward approach is to reconstruct geometry from multi-view images
and use the resulting implicit features or explicit geometry to condition genera-
tion (Chang et al., 2026; Huang et al., 2026; Siddiqui et al., 2026; Hu & Shugrina,
2026; Zhou & Tai, 2025; Liu et al., 2026). However, errors in these upstream
reconstruction estimates can propagate into generation, limiting fidelity when the
geometric guidance is unreliable.
A further direction explores joint reconstruction and generation. Mix3R (Lin
et al., 2026) connects pretrained reconstruction and generation models through a
Mixture-of-Transformers architecture, allowing the two tasks to exchange informa-
tion and improve each other. However, the two branches retain different coordinate
representations, requiring an additional predicted similarity transformation to align
the reconstructed point maps with the generated geometry.
This coordinate mismatch hinders direct geometric interaction between recon-
struction and generation, posing the first challenge. A further challenge lies in re-
covering local shape details from complex multi-view observations. As illustrated
in Fig. 2, the same region may be fully visible with clear details in one view, but
difficult to distinguish in another because of an oblique viewing angle or occlu-
sion. Combining all views without accounting for these differences can mix useful
evidence with unrelated image content, making it harder to recover details at the
corresponding 3D location.
2


To address these challenges, we propose UniForge3D, a framework that jointly
models 3D reconstruction and generation in a shared anchor-guided space and uses
structure-guided view conditioning to recover local shape details from sparse, un-
posed multi-view images.
Specifically, UniForge3D consists of two main components. First, Anchor-
Guided Joint Reconstruction and Generation defines a shared space whose hor-
izontal orientation follows the anchor view’s azimuth while preserving the object’s
upright direction, eliminating the coordinate mismatch between the two branches.
The branches exchange features bidirectionally to predict per-view point maps and
a complete sparse voxel structure in this space. The shared coordinate system al-
lows image observations to be directly mapped onto the generated structure through
the point maps. Furthermore, to establish more reliable pixel-to-voxel correspon-
dences, Structure-Guided View Selection and Conditioning uses the generated
sparse structure to guide point-map prediction. These correspondences identify the
most informative input view for each voxel, whose features then guide local shape
generation to better preserve the observed geometric details.
Extensive experiments on the Toys4K (Stojanov et al., 2021) and GSO (Downs
et al., 2022) datasets demonstrate that UniForge3D achieves state-of-the-art per-
formance across all evaluated geometry and appearance metrics, generating com-
plete 3D assets that faithfully preserve the structures and visual details observed in
sparse, unposed input views.

Related Work
2.1
Single-View 3D Generation
Single-view 3D object generation has progressed rapidly, with existing approaches
broadly categorized into methods based on 2D generative priors and native 3D
generative methods. Within the former category, one paradigm optimizes 3D rep-
resentations under supervision distilled from pretrained 2D diffusion models (Poole
et al., 2022; Lin et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2023; Melas-Kyriazi et al., 2023; Tang
et al., 2023; Qian et al., 2024; Tang et al., 2024b; Liu et al., 2023). The other
paradigm employs image or video diffusion models to synthesize multiple views of
an object and aggregates the resulting multi-view information to recover 3D repre-
sentations (Shi et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2024b; Long et al., 2024; Xu et al., 2024a;
Tang et al., 2024a; Xu et al., 2024b; Liu et al., 2024a; Li et al., 2024a; Zuo et al.,
2024; Wu et al., 2024).
Recent research has increasingly shifted toward native 3D generation, where
generative models are trained directly on large-scale 3D data. Driven by advances
in 3D latent representations, current approaches primarily follow two paradigms:
Vector Set (VecSet) (Zhang et al., 2023; Li et al., 2024b; Zhang et al., 2024; Li
et al., 2025b; Hunyuan3D et al., 2025; Li et al., 2025a) and Sparse Voxel (Ren et al.,
2024; Xiang et al., 2025; Wu et al., 2026; He et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2026b; Li
et al., 2026c; Xiang et al., 2026; Chen et al., 2026a; Ye et al., 2025). VecSet-based
methods encode 3D shapes as collections of latent vectors, providing compact rep-
resentations and effective global modeling, whereas Sparse-Voxel-based methods
retain explicit spatial organization and better support local geometric details.

2.2
Multi-View 3D Reconstruction and Generation
Classical multi-view reconstruction combines Structure-from-Motion and multi-
view stereo (Schonberger & Frahm, 2016; Furukawa et al., 2010), while learning-
based methods improve depth estimation through learned matching (Yao et al.,
2018; Gu et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2021). Neural rendering and feed-forward Gaus-
sian prediction further support reconstruction and novel-view synthesis (Mildenhall
et al., 2021; Niemeyer et al., 2022; Charatan et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2025). Re-
cent feed-forward models directly estimate point maps and other geometric quanti-
ties from unposed images (Wang et al., 2024; Leroy et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2025;
Wang et al., 2025b,a, 2026b; Keetha et al., 2026; Lin et al., 2025; Wang et al.,
2026a). However, their reconstructions primarily cover observed surfaces and may
remain incomplete in unseen regions.
Multi-view 3D generation follows two main approaches. Training-free methods
extend pretrained single-view generators by adaptively selecting or fusing informa-
tion across views (Li et al., 2026a; Zhou et al., 2026; Qu et al., 2026). Training-
based methods learn to incorporate multiple views through feature conditioning or
adaptive view routing (Team, 2025; Li et al., 2026b; Sun et al., 2026). These strate-
gies improve the use of multi-view evidence, but image conditioning alone does
not ensure that generated surfaces faithfully match the observed geometry.
To combine reconstruction with generative priors, one line of work uses esti-
mated reconstruction features or geometry as generation conditions (Chang et al.,
2026; Huang et al., 2026; Siddiqui et al., 2026; Hu & Shugrina, 2026; Zhou & Tai,
2025; Liu et al., 2026). For example, ReconViaGen uses reconstruction features,
while RecGen3D additionally incorporates canonicalized point clouds. Such guid-
ance provides geometric and cross-view information, but errors in the reconstruc-
tion estimates may propagate into generation. Another direction jointly models
reconstruction and generation. Mix3R (Lin et al., 2026) enables bidirectional in-
teraction between the two branches, but retains camera-relative reconstruction and
canonical-space generation. It therefore requires learning cross-space alignment
alongside both tasks, complicating direct geometric interaction between the two
branches. Our method instead places reconstruction and sparse-structure genera-
tion in a shared anchor-guided space, giving both branches a consistent geometric
reference. We further use structure-guided reconstruction to identify informative
input views for detailed shape generation.

3.1
Anchor-Guided Joint Reconstruction and Generation
To achieve high-fidelity multi-view 3D object generation, we seek to make recon-
struction and generation mutually beneficial. Directly conditioning generation on
reconstructed geometry or features can propagate reconstruction errors. Joint train-
ing enables mutual assistance, but their different coordinate systems complicate
geometric interaction: feed-forward reconstruction (Wang et al., 2025a) predicts
geometry in the first-camera space, whereas native 3D generation (Xiang et al.,
2026) operates in a canonical object space. We therefore introduce a shared anchor-
guided space that follows the anchor view’s azimuth while preserving the canon-
ical vertical direction. Both branches operate in this shared space and exchange
features bidirectionally, so that observed geometry guides structure generation and
complete-shape context improves reconstruction.

3.2
Structure-Guided View Selection and Conditioning
Given a generated sparse voxel structure V and the input images I, this stage gen-
erates a fine-grained shape. A surface region may be clearly visible in one view but
occluded or difficult to distinguish in another. To identify the observations relevant
to each voxel, we perform structure-guided reconstruction in the shared anchor-
guided space. The predicted point maps provide per-pixel 3D coordinates in the
same coordinate system as the generated voxels, enabling explicit pixel-to-voxel
correspondences. These correspondences allow us to select the most informative
view for each voxel based on valid pixel support and use its image features to guide
detailed shape generation.

Experiments
4.1
Experimental Setup
Data Preparation.
Our model is trained on a subset of the TRELLIS-500K dataset (Xi-
ang et al., 2025) containing 210K objects. For each object, we sample two ran-
dom azimuthal orientations and render six candidate anchor views for each ori-
entation. We additionally render 48 views from randomly sampled viewpoints to
provide additional input views for multi-view training. We randomly select 500
objects from Toys4K (Stojanov et al., 2021) and 500 from Google Scanned Objects
(GSO) (Downs et al., 2022), for a total of 1,000 evaluation objects. All test ob-
jects are held out from training. For each test object, we render four images from
randomly selected viewpoints. All methods receive the same four unposed RGB
images.
7


Evaluation Metrics.
We evaluate appearance quality using PSNR, SSIM (Wang
et al., 2004), and LPIPS (Zhang et al., 2018). Chamfer Distance (CD) (Fan et al.,
2017) is computed as the sum of the bidirectional mean nearest-neighbor distances
between points sampled from the reconstructed and ground-truth surfaces. We
compute F-score using a distance threshold of 0.01. All appearance and geometry
metrics are computed after aligning each reconstructed mesh with its ground-truth
mesh using a GICP-based Sim(3) (Segal et al., 2009) transformation. For the ab-
lation on anchor-guided joint modeling, each predicted sparse structure is aligned
with the ground truth before evaluating the 643 binary surface-occupancy grids us-
ing IoU and CD. For point-map reconstruction, predictions from all input views are
jointly aligned with the visible ground-truth position maps using a shared object-
level Umeyama Sim(3) transformation (Umeyama, 1991). Accuracy measures the
mean distance from predicted to ground-truth points, Completeness measures the
mean distance in the reverse direction, and Overall is their arithmetic mean. For the
view-conditioning ablation, PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS are computed on rendered
normal maps.
Training Details.
All models are trained on 32 NVIDIA A800 GPUs. For Anchor-
Guided Joint Reconstruction and Generation, we initialize the generation and re-
construction branches from pretrained TRELLIS.2 (Xiang et al., 2026) and VGGT (Wang
et al., 2025a), respectively, and fine-tune them using LoRA (Hu et al., 2021) with
ranks 64 and 32, while fully training the new interaction modules. We train for
60K iterations with a global batch size of 128 and a learning rate of 5 × 10−5. For
Structure-Guided Reconstruction, we fully fine-tune pretrained VGGT for 40K iter-
ations, then freeze it during subsequent generator training. For coarse-to-fine shape
generation, we fine-tune pretrained TRELLIS.2 denoisers using rank-128 LoRA
and fully train the new modules. Each model is trained for 60K iterations with a
global batch size of 64 and a learning rate of 5 × 10−5. The texture generator uses
the same training settings as the detailed shape generator.

Ablation on Anchor-Guided Joint Modeling.
Table 2 compares different condi-
tioning schemes and coordinate spaces for sparse-structure generation. DINO Con-
ditioning concatenates per-view DINOv3 (Sim´eoni et al., 2025) features along the
sequence dimension, with separate embeddings for anchor and non-anchor views.
DINO–VGGT Conditioning additionally uses VGGT (Wang et al., 2025a), fine-
tuned for point-map prediction in our anchor-guided shared space and then frozen.
An MLP adapter with a zero-initialized output projection fuses spatially aligned
features from four VGGT layers into a residual, which is added to DINOv3 fea-
tures before concatenating views. Its consistent gains over DINO Conditioning
show that reconstruction features complement DINOv3 with geometric cues and
cross-view relationships. Our full model, Anchor-Guided Joint Training, further
jointly optimizes the reconstruction and generation branches through bidirectional
feature interaction. It achieves the best results across all metrics, supporting the
benefit of joint optimization with bidirectional feature interaction.
We further compare coordinate-space choices for joint training. In Canonical-
Space Joint Training, both branches operate in the canonical object space. In Cross-
Space Joint Training, generation operates in the canonical object space while re-
construction remains in VGGT’s first-camera space. Both variants perform worse
than our anchor-guided formulation, highlighting the benefit of a shared coordinate

Ablation on Reconstruction Modeling.
We ablate the reconstruction design us-
ing three VGGT variants. Image-Only VGGT is independently fine-tuned end-to-
end using only multi-view images. Jointly Trained VGGT is jointly optimized with
the sparse-structure generator through bidirectional feature interaction in the first
stage. Structure-Conditioned VGGT further introduces the generated sparse struc-
ture as an explicit condition for point-map prediction in the second stage. As shown
in Table 3, Jointly Trained VGGT outperforms Image-Only VGGT, supporting the
benefit of interaction with the generation branch for reconstruction. Structure-
Conditioned VGGT produces the most accurate and complete point maps, demon-
strating the benefit of explicit sparse-structure conditioning for reconstruction.
Ablation on Voxel-Wise View Selection and Conditioning.
Using the same pre-
dicted sparse structure, we compare two conditioning strategies for detailed shape
generation. In Full Multi-View Conditioning, the adapter-aligned reconstruction
features are added to the corresponding DINOv3 patch tokens for each view. The
resulting feature sequences from all views are concatenated along the sequence
dimension and used to condition every denoiser layer.
Our Voxel-Wise View
Selection and Conditioning alternates full multi-view layers with voxel-specific
selected-view layers. As shown in Table 4, our design improves geometric re-
construction performance. These results show our voxel-wise view selection and
conditioning strategy improves the fidelity of generated shapes.

Limitation and Conclusion
We presented UniForge3D for joint 3D reconstruction and generation from sparse,
unposed images. The two branches exchange features in a shared anchor-guided
space to predict per-view point maps and a complete sparse structure. The gen-
erated structure then guides reconstruction to establish pixel-to-voxel correspon-
dences and select informative views for detailed shape generation. Quantitative
results on Toys4K and GSO (Table 1), qualitative comparisons in Figs. 4 and 5,
and the supplementary video demonstrate that UniForge3D generates complete 3D
assets with faithful geometry and appearance and generalizes well to real-world
images.
Limitation. Our model is trained on synthetic renderings, and severe occlu-
sion, strong reflections, and extreme lighting remain challenging. Future work will
explore training with simulations of these challenging conditions to improve real-
world robustness.

A.1.2
Detailed Network Structure
Anchor-Guided Joint Reconstruction and Generation.
The joint model com-
prises a VGGT reconstruction branch and a DiT generation branch. They exchange
features bidirectionally in the shared anchor-guided space to jointly predict per-
view point maps and a complete sparse structure.
(1) Reconstruction-to-generation. For each view v, we concatenate the frame-
wise and cross-view VGGT features into Fv, map them through an adapter ϕ, and
add the result to the spatially corresponding DINOv3 image features Dv. We then
concatenate all views along the sequence dimension:
Cv = Dv + ϕ(Fv),
Cmv = Concatv[Cv].
(9)
DiT uses generation features as queries and Cmv as keys and values in cross-
attention, allowing generation to use the observed geometry provided by the recon-
struction branch.
(2) Generation-to-reconstruction. After updating the generation features, we con-
catenate the global VGGT features across views into R and use them as queries,
with generation features G as keys and values:
R′ = R + CAt(R, G, G).
(10)
Here, CAt denotes cross-attention with time-conditioned modulation and gat-
ing, allowing the reconstruction branch to receive complete-shape information ac-
cording to the current denoising state. The updated R′ continues through recon-
struction. After repeated bidirectional interactions, the two branches output point
maps and a sparse structure through the point head and structure decoder, respec-
tively.

Thus, the selected-view condition is specific to each voxel while retaining the full

visual evidence to recover geometric details. Under these conditions, DiT progres-

The resulting latent is finally decoded into a mesh by the shape decoder.
Shape-Conditioned Texture Generation.
We encode the final mesh into a shape
latent and concatenate it with the noisy texture latent at the same sparse locations
as input to the texture DiT. The texture model follows the Structure-Guided Re-
construction and view-selection scheme of the shape stage, with an independent
adapter for constructing image conditions. During generation, the shape latent re-
mains fixed while the texture latent is progressively updated. The resulting latent is
decoded into base color, metallic, roughness, and opacity attributes and baked into
UV textures.

A.2.1
Qualitative Ablation of Structure Conditioning
Fig. 6 presents additional qualitative comparisons of different VGGT conditioning
strategies. Each row corresponds to one object. From left to right, we show four

of introducing view-specific visual evidence.
