Abstract 

Multi-view representations enable pretrained image and video vision-language models to understand 3D scenes, but their long visual token sequences make inference costly. Redundancy arises both from repeated observations of the same scene regions across views and from processing all visual tokens at every LLM layer, regardless of their relevance to the current computation. We propose GeoGR, a two-stage framework that jointly compresses scene representations and visual computation. At the projector level, GeoSemZip consolidates cross-view observations into 3D voxel tokens and combines semantic saliency with spatial coverage to construct a compact scene representation. Unselected voxel features are aggregated into contextual anchors to retain scene information that would otherwise be discarded. At the LLM level, we identify an effective visual computation window and observe that query-conditioned token relevance remains locally stable but changes over longer layer intervals. Guided by these findings, GroupRoute combines late entry and early exit with recoverable group-wise skipping. Low-relevance tokens bypass complete LLM layers while retaining their hidden states and can rejoin the active group at subsequent anchor layers selected under a computation budget. For compression-aware post-training, we construct a compact dataset of 67K samples by retaining 30% of the original training pool. With this protocol, GeoGR retains 95.2% and 96.8% of the dense post-trained models’ performance on LLaVA-OneVision-7B and Video-3D LLM-7B, respectively, while accelerating LLM prefill by 6 .8× and 6 .1×. These results demonstrate the consistent effectiveness of GeoGR across 3D VLMs with and without explicit 3D spatial encoding, highlighting a favorable performance-efficiency trade-off. The code is available at https://github.com/EIT-NLP/GeoGR. 

1 Introduction 

Enabling multimodal large language models to understand 3D scenes is an important step toward spatial intelligence [9]. Due to the scarcity of 3D-language data compared with large-scale image-text corpora [25, 39], recent studies often represent 3D scenes as sequences of posed RGB or RGB-D views, allowing pretrained vision-language models (VLMs) to transfer knowledge from 2D images and videos to 3D reasoning [5, 38, 39]. However, converting a 3D scene into multiple 2D views inevitably introduces a large number of visual tokens. For example, Video-3D LLM uses 32 views per scene, yielding 6,272 visual tokens [38]. Such long visual sequences make inference substantially more expensive, making efficiency a critical challenge for multi-view 3D VLMs. Multi-view 3D VLMs exhibit redundancy in both scene representations and visual computation. At the representation level, overlapping views repeatedly encode the same physical regions (Figure 1(a)). While geometric correspondence allows for basic consolidation [13], existing spatial pruning methods struggle to balance semantic saliency with scene coverage [16, 18, 19]. Strictly optimizing for salient objects discards the less prominent background regions essential

Studies of image-based VLMs suggest that the contribution of visual computation varies across layers [7, 8, 27], motivating us to examine how visual computation should be allocated across tokens and layers in multi-view 3D VLMs. Moreover, existing pruning strategies often make irreversible execution decisions. As we observe in 3D reasoning, a token’s relevance to the query shifts dynamically across layers (Figure 1(b)). Permanently removing tokens with low relevance at one layer may therefore discard visual evidence needed for reasoning at later layers. To address these two forms of redundancy, we propose GeoGR, a two-stage framework that compresses multi-view scene representations and selectively allocates visual computation within the LLM. At the projector level, between the vision encoder and the LLM [28], we introduce GeoSemZip to construct a compact scene representation. GeoSemZip maps cross-view observa-tions into a shared 3D coordinate system and consolidates them into voxel tokens. It retains voxel tokens with high encoder-attention saliency as dominant tokens and selects contextual anchors by sampling the remaining voxels along their 3D Morton order. These anchors complement the salient tokens by covering less salient scene regions. Unselected voxel features are aggregated into the contextual anchors according to feature similarity, retaining scene information that would otherwise be discarded. GeoSemZip thus shortens the visual sequence while preserving salient content and spatial coverage. Within the LLM, we introduce GroupRoute to reduce visual computation through Late En-try, Early Exit, and Recoverable Group-wise Skipping (RGS). Training-free interventions that separately delay visual token entry and advance visual token exit suggest a layered pattern of visual computation in multi-view 3D VLMs: portions of shallow- and deep-layer processing can be omitted with limited changes to predictions. Using next-token KL divergence relative to full visual computation, we identify candidate window boundaries, then compare the can-didates after compression-aware post-training to select the final window. Within this window, query-conditioned token relevance remains locally stable but changes over longer layer intervals (Section 3), motivating group reuse over short intervals with periodic reassessment. At anchor layers, RGS forms high- and low-priority groups using query-conditioned attention. High-priority tokens execute subsequent LLM layers, while low-priority tokens bypass them with their hidden states preserved. All visual tokens execute the next anchor layer and are rescored, allowing previously skipped tokens to rejoin the active group. Budget-constrained Anchor and Regroup-ing Search (BARS) selects anchor positions to minimize the risk of reusing groups under a computation budget.

To support compression-aware post-training, we use PRISM [2] to select the least redundant 30% of samples from the original training pool, yielding a compact dataset of 67K samples. Using this post-training protocol, we evaluate GeoGR on LLaVA-OneVision-7B and Video-3D LLM-7B, without and with explicit 3D spatial encoding, respectively. Evaluation covers two question-answering benchmarks for LLaVA-OneVision and five benchmarks spanning question answering, visual grounding, and dense captioning for Video-3D LLM. At the projector level, GeoSemZip’s cross-view consolidation and residual aggregation preserve salient content and scene context under a limited token budget. At the LLM level, GroupRoute complements this representation-level compression with query-conditioned regrouping, improving performance retention over static projector-derived grouping while keeping temporarily skipped tokens available for later reactivation. Together, the two modules enable GeoGR to retain 95.2% and 96.8% of the respective dense post-trained models’ performance, with LLM prefill speedups of 6 .8× and 6.1× and time-to-first-token (TTFT) speedups of 2 .4× and 2 .3×, respectively. Our main contributions are as follows: (1) We propose GeoSemZip, a projector-level compression module that combines cross-view geometric consolidation with semantic saliency and spatial coverage. It aggregates unselected voxel features into contextual anchors to retain scene informa-tion under compression. (2) We identify effective visual computation windows in multi-view 3D VLMs and observe locally stable but evolving query-conditioned token relevance across layers. Guided by these findings, we propose GroupRoute, which combines Late Entry and Early Exit with Recoverable Group-wise Skipping. (3) We construct a compact 67K-sample dataset for compression-aware post-training of 3D VLMs and will release it publicly. (4) We propose GeoGR, a two-stage compression framework comprising GeoSemZip and GroupRoute that achieves a favorable performance-efficiency trade-off on LLaVA-OneVision-7B and Video-3D LLM-7B. 

2 Related Work 

Token compression reduces visual sequence length within the vision encoder, at the projector between the encoder and the LLM, or within the LLM [28]. Methods select tokens by visual saliency [23] or language-conditioned relevance [29], merge similar tokens [3], or combine selection with aggregation [31]. For multi-view 3D inputs, geometric correspondence guides cross-view redundancy reduction, while spatial diversity promotes scene coverage. DTC [13] and ToSA [12] guide merging through voxel constraints and depth-derived spatial tokens, respectively. SeG-Pruner [18] and Geo3DPruner [16] select geometrically diverse tokens, while Prune2Drive [30] combines diversity-aware sampling with view-adaptive budgets. Within the LLM, Fast3D [14] assigns sample-adaptive pruning budgets, and AdaToken-3D [34] schedules layer-wise token retention using spatial information flow. HiSC [22] combines spatial graph-based merging before the LLM with hierarchical cluster-based pruning within it. Our GeoSemZip combines salient token selection with spatial coverage and aggregates unselected voxel features into contextual anchors to retain additional scene information. 

Operation compression reduces computation along LLM width and depth [10, 28]. In image-based MLLMs, RedundancyLens [17] and YOPO [37] exploit redundancy within attention and FFNs, with YOPO also pruning deep-layer visual computation. MWOP [26] further reduces computation within layers by pruning modality-interaction paths in attention heads and selecting FFN channels separately for visual and textual tokens while preserving the token sequence. Along depth, Skip-Vision [33] skips FFN updates for selected visual tokens, whereas ShortV [32] freezes visual token updates in low-contribution layers. HiDrop [27] combines late injection and early exit with progressive token pruning. ViCA [20] bypasses visual token updates and provides text access to static visual features through sparse cross-attention. Our GroupRoute extends late entry and early exit to 3D VLMs with recoverable group-wise skipping. Query-conditioned token groups bypass complete LLM layers with their states preserved and are reassessed at subsequent anchor layers for reactivation.

3 Understanding Layer-Wise Visual Token Dynamics in 3D VLMs 

Visual tokens play different roles across LLM layers, with vision–language interaction concentrated in the middle layers [27]. This motivates late visual entry and early visual exit, which restrict visual computation to a middle-layer window (Section 4.3). Within this window, token rankings may still change as vision–language interaction proceeds, making it unclear whether a single-layer ranking provides a reliable basis for permanent token pruning. Ranking Stability over Layer Distance. Figure 2 shows ranking stability through two metrics on the left y-axis: Spearman correlation measures agreement between complete token rankings, while normalized rank shift measures the mean absolute rank change divided by the maximum rank span. Nearby layers exhibit higher rank correlation and smaller rank shifts, whereas agreement generally weakens as layer distance increases. These results indicate that token rankings retain local consistency but cannot be assumed to remain stable throughout the visual computation window. This local consistency motivates reusing a token partition over a short layer interval to reduce the overhead of repeated scoring and routing. 

Low-to-High Rank Transitions. The two metrics on the right y-axis of Figure 2 quantify how initially low-ranked tokens enter the top half at later layers. The transition rate measures their fraction of the later top half, while the attention share measures their contribution to that group’s total attention. As layer distance increases, a growing portion of the later top half originates from initially low-ranked tokens. Crucially, these tokens also account for a growing, non-negligible share of the attention assigned to that group. A low rank at one layer therefore does not guarantee a low rank at subsequent layers. This motivates treating low-ranked tokens as temporarily inactive at the current stage , rather than permanently redundant in all subsequent layers , and retaining them for later reassessment.

Implications for Group-wise Computation. Together, these observations motivate reusing token partitions over short layer intervals while preserving the opportunity for reassessment. Local ranking stability motivates temporary reuse of a partition, while low-to-high transitions motivate retaining low-ranked tokens for later recovery and regrouping. This leads to the group-wise skip mechanism in Section 4.3, where low-ranked tokens temporarily skip LLM blocks while preserving their hidden states. At the next anchor layer, all visual tokens, including those previously skipped, undergo full LLM-block computation and are rescored to determine their groups for the following interval. 

4 Method 

As illustrated in Figure 3, GeoGR combines projector-level and LLM-level compression for multi-view 3D VLMs. GeoSemZip consolidates cross-view observations into compact tokens preserving salient content and spatial coverage. GroupRoute combines Late Entry and Early Exit to define a visual computation window with Recoverable Group-wise Skipping to selectively allocate computation according to query relevance while preserving skipped token states for later reactivation. We first present the distillation dataset and post-training protocol, followed by the two compression stages.

4.3 GroupRoute: Late Entry, Early Exit, and Recoverable Group-wise Skipping 

After GeoSemZip , GroupRoute uses Late Entry and Early Exit to define a visual computation win-dow, within which Recoverable Group-wise Skipping (RGS) selectively updates tokens according to query relevance while preserving skipped token states for later reactivation. Budget-constrained Anchor and Regrouping Search (BARS) selects the regrouping anchors under a computation budget. 

Late Entry and Early Exit. Following HiDrop [27], we adapt late entry and early exit to multi-view 3D VLMs. Text tokens pass through all LLM layers, while visual tokens participate only within a window [ ℓin , ℓ out ]. They enter at layer ℓin with their original full-prompt position IDs and exit after layer ℓout . Both boundaries use one-based indexing. To identify candidate boundaries, we independently vary the number of initial blocks skipped and the last block retaining visual tokens, measuring next-token KL divergence relative to full visual computation. As shown in Figure 4, skipping the first K = 5–7 blocks yields the final consecutive low-mean-KL region before divergence increases. For early exit, KL drops sharply between retained blocks 21 and 22 and remains low from block 23 onward. These pre-adaptation measurements define candidate ranges, which we refine through compression-aware post-training. The resulting window for LLaVA-OneVision is [8 , 23], as evaluated in the window ablation study. 

Recoverable Group-wise Skipping. Within the visual-computation window, we partition visual tokens at designated anchor layers according to their relevance to the query. The query is the last prompt token for question answering and captioning, or the grounding token for localization. Budget-constrained Anchor Selection. BARS selects anchor positions under a computation budget using layer-wise attention scores normalized over retained visual tokens. For a partition formed at anchor a and reused at layer ℓ, we quantify three risks: low-priority attention mass, migration from the low-priority to high-priority group, and mean normalized rank displacement. Risks are weighted by the remaining visual-computation depth, averaged within benchmarks, and then equally across benchmarks. For each feasible anchor count m, dynamic programming finds the minimum-risk schedule Am under a normalized visual token–layer budget and minimum anchor gap. Starting with A1, BARS considers increasing anchor counts, accepting a candidate only if it reduces both aggregate risk and each calibration subset’s risk by at least τ = 5% relative to the current schedule. With κ = 0 .5, BARS selects anchors [9 , 13] on both 3D VLMs. The schedule is fixed at inference, while token groups remain query-dependent. 5.1 Experimental setup Models. We evaluate GeoGR on two representative multi-view 3D VLMs: LLaVA-OneVision-7B [15], which independently encodes views without explicit 3D representations, and Video-3D LLM-7B [38], which augments view features with depth- and pose-derived 3D positional embeddings in a shared scene coordinate frame. Both models take 32 uniformly sampled RGB views per scene. Architectural details are provided in Appendix C.1.

We evaluate GeoGR on five ScanNet-based benchmarks covering visual ground-ing (ScanRefer [4] and Multi3DRefer [36]), dense captioning (Scan2Cap [6]), and 3D question answering (ScanQA [1] and SQA3D [21]). Video-3D LLM is evaluated on all five, while LLaVA-OneVision is evaluated only on the two QA benchmarks because it does not support the required 3D localization outputs. 6 Conclusion 

We presented GeoGR, a two-stage compression framework for multi-view 3D VLMs that addresses redundancy in scene representations and visual computation. GeoSemZip consolidates cross-view observations while preserving salient content, spatial coverage, and information from unselected regions. Guided by effective visual computation windows and locally stable but evolving token relevance, GroupRoute combines Late Entry and Early Exit with Recoverable Group-wise Skipping, allowing temporarily inactive tokens to resume computation. A compact, selected dataset supports compression-aware post-training. Experiments demonstrate that GeoGR achieves a favorable performance–efficiency trade-off across 3D VLMs with and without explicit 3D spatial encoding.

A.1 Training and Evaluation Protocol Input construction. For each of the 32 sampled RGB views, we bilinearly pool projector features and vision-encoder attention scores to a 14 × 14 grid. GeoSemZip reconstructs world coordinates from aligned depth maps, camera intrinsics, and camera-to-world poses. 

Evaluation. Evaluation metrics are summarized in Table 9. For ScanQA, predictions and references are lowercased, processed with the benchmark caption-text cleaner, whitespace-normalized, and stripped of trailing punctuation. SQA3D EM follows benchmark-specific normalization: case and punctuation normalization, digit-to-word conversion, article removal, and spelling correction. A prediction is correct if its normalized form matches any normalized reference answer. Evaluation uses batch size 1. 

Post-training. For both 3D VLMs, compression-aware post-training consists of projector adaptation followed by late-entry/early-exit adaptation. Each stage runs for one epoch with a global batch size of 16 and learning rate 10 −5. The second stage starts from the projector-adapted checkpoint. The vision encoder remains frozen in both stages; the projector is updated only in the first stage, while the LLM backbone is updated in both. We use zero weight decay, cosine learning rate decay, a warm-up ratio of 0.03, BF16 precision, and DeepSpeed ZeRO-3 in both stages.

C More Experimental Setup Details 

C.1 3D VLMs LLaVA-OneVision-7B. LLaVA-OneVision [15] provides a unified framework for single-image, multi-image, and video understanding. Its SigLIP visual encoder extracts patch-level visual features, which are further projected into the language embedding space via a two-layer MLP. Each sampled scene view is independently encoded, and the output visual tokens are concatenated with textual instructions to form the multimodal input sequence. Under our 32-view setting, each view yields a 14 × 14 pooled visual grid, producing 32 × 14 × 14 = 6 ,272 visual content tokens before compression. This 3D VLM takes only RGB-based visual features as input, while depth maps and camera calibration parameters are exclusively fed into our GeoSemZip module. 

Video-3D LLM-7B. Built upon LLaVA-Video-7B, Video-3D LLM [38] explicitly integrates 3D positional information into multi-view visual representations. Specifically, depth maps, camera intrinsics, and camera poses project observations from different views into a unified world coordinate system. The obtained patch-level spatial coordinates are encoded and fused with visual features, endowing the model with robust spatial awareness. In addition to standard visual question answering, Video-3D LLM supports spatial reasoning tasks, including object-conditioned captioning and visual grounding. For object-conditioned captioning, the center coordinates of target objects are adopted to specify query instances. For visual grounding, the hidden representations of a dedicated grounding token are matched with object-level features to locate target objects.

SQA3D. SQA3D [21] adds a textual description of an agent’s situation, including its position and orientation, to a question about the surrounding scene. Answering requires interpreting the question from that situated perspective. The benchmark therefore tests whether the compressed representation preserves scene layout and spatial relations as well as object appearance. We evaluate the test split using normalized EM, with the situation description and question supplied together in the task prompt. 

Scan2Cap. Scan2Cap [6] evaluates dense captioning in 3D scenes, using object descriptions from ScanRefer. A caption must describe the localized object and may refer to its attributes and surrounding objects. For Video-3D, the target location is supplied through its coordinate-conditioned captioning prompt. We report BLEU-4 and CIDEr at a 3D IoU threshold of 0.5,which couples language quality with adequate object localization.
