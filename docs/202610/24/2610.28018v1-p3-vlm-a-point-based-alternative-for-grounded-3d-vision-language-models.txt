# Abstract 

Examining existing benchmarks for grounding in 3D VLMs reveals a pervasive “semantic leakage" in the evaluation and prompting protocols: a heavy reliance on bounding boxes as spatial prompts or representation primitives. Because bounding boxes are highly informative of object categories, they introduce a shortcut for the models to neglect visual information during 3D VLM training. We demonstrate this with a “blind" LLM that, prompted only with the box coordinates, can per-form surprisingly well. To further analyze this shortcut, we propose point-based grounding prompting – querying models with a single 3D point instead of a box. Under this protocol, the accuracy of the blind LLM drops from 36.4% to 18.0% 

on ScanNet’s Nr3D, and similarly degrades for 3D VLMs (e.g. LL3DA). Building on this insight, we introduce P3-VLM , a novel tri-pathway 3D VLM that supports point-based prompts and achieves 52.1% state-of-the-art accuracy on location-grounded captioning under this setting. To achieve this, we redesign the architecture while keeping it detector-free, scene-centric, and suitable for 3D GS inputs. P 3-VLM utilizes three complementary pathways (location-, task-, and context-aware), enabling strong reasoning about scenes without any bounding box dependencies. The key benefit of point-based learning is a consistent improvement across settings: P 3-VLM achieves state-of-the-art performance under point-based grounding while also improving robustness, OOD generalization, and performance on ungrounded scene-centric 3D VQA benchmarks, reflecting its reduced reliance on bounding-box shortcuts and resulting stronger visual-spatial reasoning.

# 1 Introduction 

3D Vision-Language Models (VLMs) hold the promise of generalist scene understanding: given a 3D representation of an environment, such a model should be able to answer both grounded queries – those anchored to a specific location or object in the scene – and ungrounded queries about the scene at large [8, 24, 8, 20, 23]. Significant progress has been made in this direction, yet a fundamental question remains open: to what extent do current 3D VLMs actually observe the scene, rather than exploit contextual shortcuts? We investigate this question through grounded object understanding: identifying the correct object given a 3D spatial prompt. This requires jointly reasoning about geometry and semantics, including localizing the queried region, interpreting its visual content, and producing a semantically accurate response [11, 6, 19, 18]. We find that existing object- and scene-centric paradigms share a common issue of “semantic leakage” in evaluation protocols, arising from the widespread use of bounding boxes as either prompts or representation primitives. Because bounding boxes carry strong categorical cues through their size, shape, and aspect ratio, they enable shortcut solutions (e.g., a tall narrow box often corresponds to a door; beds have standardized measurements) that reduce the need for true visual-spatial reasoning. They also implicitly assume accurate object localization, which is usually unrealistic in real-world 3D applications. The box dependency appears across both object-centric VLMs [24, 23, 58], which inherit box-based object representations from detectors, and scene-centric models [19, 8], which still rely on bounding boxes as grounding prompts at inference time. As a result, models can exploit categorical priors encoded in box geometry rather than visual evidence. We demonstrate this effect by showing that a blind LLM trained only on bounding box coordinates, without any visual input, achieves 36 .4% 

accuracy on Nr3D as shown in Fig. 1. We further show that the overreliance on bounding boxes makes the existing 3D VLMs fragile, leading to significant degradation in scenarios with atypical object sizes, noisy reconstructions, or poorly aligned bounding boxes in Fig. 2 (robustness analysis). These findings motivate both a new evaluation protocol and a new model design. We propose point-based grounding prompting , where queries are specified using only a single 3D point on the target object. Unlike bounding boxes, point prompts do not reveal object shape or extent, substantially reducing category-specific geometric cues and incentivizing models to rely on genuine visual-spatial understanding. Under this stricter protocol, the shortcut enabled by bounding boxes largely disappears: the accuracy of the blind LLM drops sharply from 36 .4% to 18 .0% on Nr3D. Existing 3D VLMs, including scene-centric models such as [19, 8], also degrade substantially, further exposing their overreliance on bounding-box cues (e.g. LL3DA drops from 47 .7% to 33 .1% , see Fig. 1). To address this new challenging setting, where existing scene-centric 3D VLMs struggle, we introduce 

P3-VLM , a detector-free, scene-centric 3D VLM built on 3D Gaussian scene representations (3D GS) and designed specifically for point-based grounding. P 3-VLM utilizes a tri-pathway architecture consisting of: (1) a location-aware pathway that encodes compact positional cues from the query point, (2) a task-aware pathway that sparsifies and refines query-relevant volumetric features, and (3) a context-aware pathway that summarizes the global scene for grounded reasoning. By anchoring visual features directly to scene voxels rather than detector-derived geometry, P 3-VLM enables spatially grounded reasoning under the proposed point-based alternative. Under point-based grounding, P 3-VLM achieves 52 .4% class accuracy on Nr3D, substantially outperforming both the blind LLM baseline ( 18 .0% ) and previous 3D VLMs adapted to this setting (33 .1% and 29 .3% for LL3DA and GaussianVLM, respectively; see Tab. 2). This large margin demonstrates that its visual encoder is genuinely load-bearing. Moreover, by avoiding bounding-box shortcuts, P 3-VLM is not only more robust but also exhibits stronger scene-level OOD generalization (see Fig. 2), while maintaining consistent gains on ungrounded scene understanding benchmarks such as ScanQA [3] and SQA3D [33]. Overall, this work makes the following contributions: • We identify bounding box leakage as a pervasive shortcut in current 3D VLMs, hurting visual-spatial understanding, and introduce point-based grounding as a stricter protocol. • We present P 3-VLM, a detector-free, scene-centric 3D VLM operating on 3D Gaussian representations via three complementary pathways (location, task, and context aware). • We show that P 3-VLM achieves SOTA performance under point-based grounding while improving robustness, OOD generalization, and performance on VQA benchmarks. 2Figure 2: Performance, robustness, and generalization. # 2 Related Work 

2.1 3D-Grounded Scene Understanding 

3D-grounded scene understanding refers to tasks involving visual grounding in 3D space, which can manifest on either the input or the output side of the model [6, 1, 19, 34, 30]. On the output side, the model receives a reference to an object or category and must predict a spatial specification for the matching object(s) – a 3D bounding box or segmentation mask [34, 28, 30, 32, 2, 52, 27, 42]. Specialist models for this setting [28, 30], including those built on language backbones [34, 52, 2], achieve strong performance by combining task-specific pretraining, structured output modeling, and dedicated detection heads. However, because of this specialization, the state-of-the-art methods sacrifice their free-form answer and thus generalist capabilities. Generalist models with multi-task open-language capabilities [8, 23, 24] have been unable to match specialist performance on output-side grounding, lacking, for instance, the structured output modeling and full-scale task-specific supervision. Furthermore, existing free-form benchmarks for diverse scene understanding are largely restricted to ScanNet-scale datasets [3, 33, 20, 24, 25, 6, 1, 19], hindering the training of generalist models for output-side grounding at the scale necessary to achieve state-of-the-art performance [34]. Input-side grounded understanding entails providing the grounding on the input side through a 3D location query, with model output – a caption of the referenced object [19, 8, 18, 58, 24, 56]. This setting is more compatible with open-form answer models. Some free-form VLMs also report bounding box outputs in this setting, yet those are obtained via independent, frozen detectors [9, 41] entirely separate from their core architecture [8, 23, 24], making the box prediction not a product of the model’s learned visual understanding. Exploring free-form 3D generalist VLMs, we operate in the input-side captioning setting [19, 18, 56]. The captioning quality directly measures the VLM’s visual understanding, not focusing on box predictions, obtainable through an external module. 

2.2 3D VLMs for Multi-Task Scene Reasoning 

VLMs for 3D scenes aim to support a broad range of tasks, including object captioning [6, 1], question answering [3, 20], spatial reasoning [33, 54] and beyond [19, 20], through a single unified model [19, 8, 55, 39]. The dominant paradigm in this setting has been object-centric: detect a set of object proposals, encode their point cloud features to separate tokens, and align them with language [54, 24, 23, 29, 57, 47, 53, 10, 46, 31, 10]. Prior research suggests that this approach is fundamentally limited as scene-level understanding is bounded by detector recall [19], and is emergent rather than by design [19, 56, 36, 16]. Thus, a parallel line of work moves away from detector dependencies, spanning video VLMs for 3D understanding [17, 55, 39, 22, 7, 49, 45], and native 3D models. The latter processes 3D scenes either (1) in a region-based manner requiring incorporation of advanced methods for instilling cross-regional awareness (e.g., GCNs [18]) or (2) via scene-centric strategies that encode the environment at once as a whole [19, 8, 56]. GaussianVLM [19] pioneered the use of 3D GS for scene-centric understanding and acts as a baseline for this work. However, it suffers from reduced robustness and generalization due to reliance on semantic shortcuts (Sec. 3.2,5.2, D.3). 

# 3 From Bounding Box Semantic Leakage to Point Grounding 

A foundational assumption underlying 3D VLM evaluation is that grounded query performance reflects genuine visual-spatial reasoning – an assumption which we investigate in this section. When 3models are queried about a specific object via a bounding box prompt – the universal convention across 3D VLMs – the box itself encodes strong categorical information: its dimensions, aspect ratio, and volume are highly predictive of object class. A tall, narrow box standing up from floor level implies a door; a box with measurements of a queen-sized bed – a bed, etc. We term this phenomenon 

bounding box semantic leakage and demonstrate that it acts as a dominant signal that can bypass visual perception in 3D VLMs when it comes to grounding tasks. 

3D-Grounded Object Captioning. To investigate this phenomenon, we focus on the task of 3D-Grounded Object Captioning, which requires a model to generate a natural language description for a specific object given a spatial query. We evaluate various 3D VLMs on two ScanNet-based benchmarks: Nr3D [1] and ScanRefer [6]. Firstly, we evaluate models under the standard prompting protocol, bounding-box prompting , specifying the query location as a box center and extent.

We aim to isolate the contribution of geometric priors from other key aspects of 3D grounding such as semantics and context awareness. For this, we conduct two controlled experiments on Nr3D. Specifically, we examine the effect of the strong reliance on bounding boxes within the tex-tual and/or the visual component of the prompt, highlighting that the leakage can occur via both information channels and cause over-reliance on this geometric prior rather than utilization of rich visual features. 

Leakage via Textual Prompting. First, we examine the effect of bounding boxes as grounding prompts within the textual LLM task prompt. We fine-tune a "blind" LLM (Qwen3-VL 4B Instruct LLM [4]) via LoRA to predict object classes from box center, yaw and extent alone, with no visual encoder of any kind. The fine-tuned blind LLM achieves 36% class accuracy – a significant result that establishes box geometry as a strong categorical signal independent of any visual observation. 

Leakage via Architectural Priors. Leakage can also occur through the visual channel, particularly in Object-Detector VLMs (OD-VLMs). These models typically use a decoupled architecture: a detector segments the scene, and a 3D encoder transforms each instance’s point cloud into tokens for the LLM. We investigate whether the 3D encoder contributes meaningful semantic understanding beyond what the detector’s box metadata already provides. We ask whether the visual encoder in a full object-centric VLM is strongly utilized on top of this prior. For this, we evaluate an OD-VLM (LEO [24]) against a modified version where the visual features are simply encodings of the bounding boxes (center and extent) of the objects within the scene, derived with a lightweight 2-layer MLP. We observe very close performance of the two models (Tab. 1). These results suggest that, under bounding-box-based visual prompting, object class predictions are once again driven primarily by geometric priors rather than visual features. 

3.2 Point-Based Grounding 

Our findings indicate that the prevalent use of bounding boxes as spatial prompts may provide geometric priors that simplify the grounding task. To isolate visual perception from these structural cues, we propose point-based grounding prompting : a protocol where location queries are specified as a single 3D coordinate (x, y, z ). By removing information about the target’s extent or aspect ratio, it serves as a more faithful benchmark for a model’s ability to extract semantics from visual features. The effect of removing box geometry is immediate. We fine-tune two variants of the blind LLM: one trained on bounding box prompts and evaluated accordingly, and one trained and evaluated on point-based prompts (Supp. Sec. A.1). Class accuracy drops from 36.4% to 18.0% (Tab. 2) – a 50% relative reduction achieved by changing only the prompt format, with no modification to the model architecture. This confirms the prior inherent in the bounding-box grounding prompts, and its reduced effect in point prompts. 4Figure 3: P3-VLM architecture overview. Three complementary pathways process the voxelized 3D scene: the location-aware pathway extracts a single token at the queried point; the task-aware pathway produces prompt-guided scene tokens by first refining the task prompt tokens and subsequently utilizing them for visual refinement of the sparsified features; the context-aware pathway distills 8 global scene summary tokens via unconditional cross-attention. Notably, the context and task pathways operate on the sparsified features for efficiency gains. We then ask whether existing scene-centric VLMs can close this gap when adapted to the new prompt. For each model, we fine-tune on point-based grounding and re-evaluate under the stricter protocol (training details in the Supplementary A.1). The results in Tab. 2 show that fine-tuning does recover some performance, yet a substantial gap relative to the box-based baseline persists across all models: on Nr3D LL3DA drops from 47.7% to 33.1%, and GaussianVLM from 46.2% to 29.3%. The fact that explicit training on point prompts does not close this gap suggests that the limitation is not one of prompt formatting, but of visual capacity – these models have not developed the dense visual representations that point-based grounding demands.

P3-VLM is a scene-centric 3D VLM, that takes as input 3D Gaussian Splats (GS) and a natu-ral language query and produces a free-form language response relying on point-based spa-tial prompting. An overview of the architec-ture is shown in Fig. 3. The scene is first vox-elized: a sparse 3D GS encoder processes the raw Gaussian splats into a set of voxel features 

F ∈ RN ×D with associated 3D coordinates 

C ∈ RN ×3, where each feature is anchored to a specific spatial location. These voxel fea-tures then feed three complementary pathways: location-aware, task-aware, and context-aware, which together produce the visual token se-quence as input to the LLM. To the visual tokens, we append the user language task tokens L.

4.1 Tri-Pathway Visual Processing 

The 3D visual encoder produces rich, yet dense, visual features. Even for our downsampled scans, this can produce more than 10k tokens, which are computationally heavy to be processed as a sequence in the attention mechanism. To alleviate this, we sparsify the dense visual features by aggregation and refinement, which additionally increases their spatial awareness, to make them consumable by the context and task pathways. Notably, the location pathway operates on raw encoder features as it focuses exclusively on locality rather than global awareness. Detailed implementation specifications and hyperparameter choices are provided in Supp. Sec. D. 5Sparsification. The raw voxel features are spatially downsampled to at most 2048 tokens per scene via average pooling on a uniform 3D grid, making subsequent self-attention tractable. The downsampled features are then spatially enriched and lifted to LLM space by a stack of spatially-aware self-attention layers where both queries and keys are rotated by 3D Rotary Position Embeddings (3D RoPE) [43]. Applying 3D RoPE to both Q and K encodes pairwise relative 3D distances between voxels directly in the attention scores, giving the projector spatial awareness without positional embedding lookups. The output is S ∈ RN ×Dlm with LLM hidden dimension Dlm .

Location-Aware Pathway. The location-aware pathway produces a single token that encodes the visual content at the queried 3D point. Given the query location p ∈ R3, we identify the closest voxel center (with voxel id i). The voxel feature Fi is concatenated with the normalized query location and passed through a four-layer MLP projecting to LLM hidden dimension: 

Tloc = MLP([ Fi ; p]) ∈ RDlm . (1) The concatenation of raw spatial coordinates with the voxel feature allows the MLP to jointly reason about what is at a location and where that location is, without a separate conditioning mechanism. When no query location is provided the pathway is suppressed and no location token is injected. 

Context-Aware Pathway. The context-aware pathway produces a compact, prompt-independent scene summary. A set of K=8 learnable query tokens ( Qlearn ) cross-attends over the sparsified and spatially enriched features S through two cross-attention layers 

Tctx = XAttn( Qlearn , S) ∈ RK×Dlm . (2) Because the queries are unconditional, they learn to summarize scene-level properties (object layout, room type, spatial extent) useful regardless of the query. We observe that this global context allows the task-aware pathway to specialize more sharply: without context tokens, the compressor attends broadly across all voxels; with them, attention concentrates on query-relevant spatial regions. 

Task-Aware Pathway. The sparsified visual representation is consumed by the task-aware pathway for further sparsification of the tokens with respect to the user task query. The language query L is processed by two self-attention layers to Lrefined . Subsequently, we add a 3D RoPE embedding [43] to each voxel token, which then cross-attends to the text tokens. This asymmetry encodes the relevance of each prompt token for each spatial location in the attention score, allowing selective refinement of spatially relevant voxels. The update is applied as a residual with zero-initialized output projection for stable training: 

Stask = LayerNorm( S + Wout · XAttn 3DRoPE (S, Lrefined )) . (3) The task-refined scene Stask is compressed via 128 learnable tokens to Ttask LLM-ready tokens. 

# 5 Experiments 

5.1 Experimental Setup Datasets. We follow standard training protocols within the scene-centric 3D VLM field [8, 19] and train our models within the ScanNet [15] settings. Notably, we use 3D GS representation [30] as visual input. We perform joint training on multiple of tasks without task-specific fine-tuning: general question-answering (ScanQA [3]), situated question-answering (SQA3D [33]) and location-grounded captioning (on Nr3D [1], ScanRefer [6]). 

Evaluation. Following baseline protocols [19], we evaluate question-answering on a multitude of metrics covering linguistic similarity (METEOR (M) [5], ROUGE (R) [12], BLEU-4 (B4) [35], CIDEr (C) [44]), and exact-match accuracy. For the latter, we adopt the refined EM protocol (EM-R) [24]. For captioning, we evaluate target object identification via accuracy (Acc) and caption similarity via Sentence-BERT Similarity (Sim) [40], with metrics discussion provided in Supp. Sec. A.2. Results are reported on the validation sets of ScanRefer, Nr3D, ScanQA and test set of SQA3D. 6Training. Training is carried out in two stages. In the first stage, only the location-aware pathway is active, with the model being trained on point-based grounded captioning (Nr3D and ScanRefer), using the single location token as the sole visual input. This allows the location extractor to learn a stable voxel-to-language mapping before the full scene representation is introduced. In the second stage, all three pathways are active and the model is trained jointly on the full task mixture. Both stages are trained under the standard autoregressive language modeling objective (Supp. Sec. A.5). 

Implementation Details. P3-VLM uses Qwen3-VL-4B [4] as LLM backbone, fine-tuned with LoRA [21] with rank 16 [19, 24]. The 3D encoder is initialized from SceneSplat [30] and kept unfrozen. The three visual pathway components are trained from scratch. Training takes 10 hours on 2 NVIDIA-H200 GPUs with 5 epochs of pre-training the location pathway, followed by 12 epochs of full model training, using batch size 16 across both stages. We use the AdamW optimizer with a cosine annealing learning rate schedule, learning rate 3e-5 and weight decay of 0.05. We perform evaluation every epoch. For further training and hyperparameter details, please refer to Supp. Sec. A.5. We train and evaluate two variants of P 3-VLM differing solely in the adopted location prompting strategy - bounding box ( P3-VLM (b) with box format [center, yaw, extent] ) or point ( P3-VLM (p) ). For the latter we use the centers of the objects, derived from the ground truth segmentation [15]. To isolate language model performance from architectural contributions, we also retrain one of the baselines, GaussianVLM, using its original training protocol while replacing its language decoder with ours: Qwen3-VL-4B (denoted GaussianVLM Q). This ensures that both P 3-VLM and GaussianVLM Q

share identical 3D (SceneSplat) and language backbones.

# 6 Conclusions 

In this work, we highlight the problem of semantic leakage in current 3D VLMs, causing these models to neglect visual information. We propose a new point-based grounding prompting protocol and show that other methods struggle under this new setting. To address this, we present P 3-VLM, a novel 3D VLM based on a tri-pathway design. We demonstrate the proposed point-based grounding approach with our architecture leads to improved visual understanding, demonstrated through improved performance, generalization, and robustness on multiple 3D tasks and benchmarks. 

Limitations First, P 3-VLM requires 3D GS representations as input, introducing an extra re-construction step that adds complexity; however, this step has been performed at scale in prior works [30, 32] and industrial applications [38, 14], and is shown to yield richer visual features [19]. Second, our evaluation is primarily focused on perception-oriented benchmarks, with limited cover-age of language-intensive or dialogue-driven tasks. Extending P 3-VLM to more complex multimodal reasoning settings remains an important direction for future work. Third, our approach relies on voxelized visual features, currently obtained via the SceneSplat encoder. Evaluating how well the method generalizes to alternative 3D visual encoders is an interesting avenue for further investigation. 

Impact We envision this work as a step toward enabling real-world applications such as high-level planning for navigation and visual assistance systems. Additionally, we hope it draws attention to the issue of information leakage in 3D grounding, encouraging the development of more robust and general-purpose 3D vision-language models. 10 Acknowledgments

A.2 N-gram Metrics Conceal Failures in Spatial Understanding 

Standard 3D scene understanding benchmarks such as ScanRefer and Nr3D evaluate dense captioning models using n-gram overlap metrics – CIDEr, METEOR, ROUGE – gated by an IoU threshold of 0.25 or 0.50. A response that fluently describes the wrong object category can achieve a high n-gram score if it shares common words with the reference caption [19]. A.7 Training with Surface-Point Grounding 

We acknowledge the center point being a canonical point: its height is a stable, category-specific statistic. To escape this prior, we also analyze prompting with random surface points instead. A point sampled uniformly on the object surface may lie anywhere within the object’s vertical extent, so its height no longer encodes a consistent per-category value. We retrain P 3-VLM with random surface points as the grounding signal during training. Small or thin objects (pillows, boxes, monitors, keyboards, cups) improve much less, by 6.2 points on average; this difference is statistically significant (Mann-Whitney U test, p = 0 .014 ). We attribute this behavior to the feature pooling at the query point. Prompting a large object by its center forces the visual features to be pooled from deep inside the object’s interior, or even from empty space, e.g., below a table top or inside a cabinet, yielding uninformative features. # C Further Ablations 

Complementary to the tri-pathway design ablation provided in Sec. 5.5, we also evaluate specific design decisions within the location pathway and sparsifier components of P 3-VLM. 

Location Pathway Design. For the location pathway we consider only the location-based cap-tioning task, as the other tasks do not utilize the pathway. First, we ablate whether scene-context information improves local information extraction. The results demonstrate better location under-standing on raw voxel features ( F ) compared to utilization of already sparsified and refined features (S).We attribute this to the sparsifier smoothing voxel features through spatial self-attention, which reduces their locality – precisely the property the location pathway relies on. We also ablate the extraction strategy comparing our MLP-based approach to FiLM location conditioning, with exact details on the FiLM alternative provided in the Supplementary. Results show that processing raw voxel features ( F ) with an MLP strategy delivers best results. 

Sparsification of Visual Tokens. We also ablate the role of positional encodings (PE) for increasing scene-level awareness within the sparsification process. We evaluate the sparsifier module with 3D RoPE PE, learned absolute PE added to voxel features, and no PE. 3D RoPE yields the strongest performance, confirming that explicit spatial structure in the global features is critical. 

# D P3-VLM Architecture Details 

D.1 Location Pathway Designs. 

We investigated two different location pathway designs – FiLM- and MLP-based, Both first identify the nearest voxel to the target location, retrieving its feature vector as a local scene descriptor. The first strategy, FiLM-based extraction , conditions this voxel feature on the queried location via Feature-wise Linear Modulation [37]: we use a shallow location encoder MLP (2 linear layers) to map the 3D point coordinates p to affine parameters (γ, β ) ∈ RD × RD . Those are afterwards used to modulate the retrieved voxel feature Fi ∈ RD as Tloc = σ(γ) ⊙ Fi + β, where σ(·) denotes the sigmoid function, applied to γ to act as a soft selection mask over feature dimensions. We note that all notation is introduced in Sec. 4. We note that the MLP-based variant is introduced in Sec. 4. The MLP structure we adopt is four-layer MLP with GELU activations.

D.3 Comparison to the GaussianVLM Architecture 

P3-VLM and GaussianVLM [19] are both scene-centric 3D GS VLMs, but are built around different priorities, from which their architectural differences follow. GaussianVLM is semantics-oriented : it fuses language into the 3D backbone itself (per-Gaussian SigLIP2 features), so every downstream token is semantically grounded before sparsification. This favors scene-level tasks and semantic VQA, where broad semantic understanding matters more than precise spatial extent. P 3-VLM is 

geometry-oriented , designed to strengthen grounding, which leads to the following design decisions: • Context pathway. A dedicated, unconditional pathway explicitly recovers global scene semantics; GaussianVLM has no counterpart. • Task pathway. In P 3-VLM, voxel tokens query the text, and the result is applied as a residual correction to the visual features before compression, i.e., language reshapes geometry. In GaussianVLM, text tokens query and directly select visual tokens, i.e., language selects semantics. • Location pathway. P3-VLM derives the local token with an MLP over the concatenated geometric and semantic features at the queried point, explicitly learning location understand-ing jointly with content. GaussianVLM retrieves the per-Gaussian semantic feature nearest to the query point, without a comparable geometric grounding mechanism. • Positional information. P3-VLM fuses positional information early via 3D RoPE, rein-forcing geometry throughout the visual processing; GaussianVLM adds position as a final Fourier embedding, secondary to semantics. 

D.4 Point Cloud Encoder Variant 

To assess whether P 3-VLM generalizes beyond 3D GS inputs, we replace the SceneSplat encoder with the self-supervised point cloud encoder Sonata [48] (P 3-VLM-PC), keeping the tri-pathway design and training protocol unchanged. Results are shown in Tab. 15. Owing to Sonata’s large-scale pre-training, P 3-VLM-PC achieves strong location-grounded captioning results under point prompts (Nr3D: 58 .1% , ScanRefer: 63 .3% Acc), outperforming the 3D GS variant. However, Sonata features are not language-aligned, unlike SceneSplat features, which results in lower performance on the VQA benchmarks. This shows that the tri-pathway design transfers to other 3D representations, with the choice of encoder trading off grounding against language-aligned scene understanding. # E Datasets Information 

Point Cloud and 3D GS Representations. Our method uses 3D GS scene input, differing from the point cloud input consumed by other methods. To ensure maximal similarity in setup to the point cloud-based methods, we use 3D GS representations with Gaussian centers initialized at the point locations from the original point clouds ("Fixed XYZ" strategy). This guarantees the exact same geometrical properties under the two setups. Our 3D GS data is consistent to the one utilized within the GaussianVLM experiments and is obtained from the GaussianWorld dataset [30].

# F Broader Impact 

Our work aims to make a step towards VLMs with strong visual understanding capabilities, showing strong robustness and out-of-distribution generalization. With this we invision real-world utilization of 3D VLMs for applications that require free-form, open-world, spatially grounded reasoning, such as robotics, assistive technologies, and AR/VR systems. We also note the potential environmental implications of training VLM models such as ours. While P3-VLM is designed with sparsification modules and scene downsampling, its training still requires significant compute resources. With this in mind, we underscore the necessity of research into environmentally-friendly training approaches.
