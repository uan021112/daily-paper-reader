Introduction
3D scanning is widely used on construction sites to capture the
geometric structure of real-world buildings and environments. The
resulting point clouds often provide noisy, incomplete, and purely
geometric surface measurements. In contrast, Building Information
Modeling (BIM) represents buildings as structured collections of
semantic component instances with volumetric geometry, paramet-
ric attributes, and explicit relationships[Liu et al. 2025a; Zbirovský
and Nežerka 2025]. Consequently, BIM aims to reconstruct editable
as-built architectural components rather than merely producing a
geometric reconstruction of the scanned scene. For architectural
components, this requires inferring wall thickness, wall-opening
relationships, and editable instance identities that are not directly
observable from raw scans.
Current methods rarely recover such BIM components directly
from point clouds. Manual modeling remains labor-intensive, while
classical Scan-to-BIM pipelines rely on multi-stage processing, in-
cluding segmentation, primitive fitting, plane extraction, and topol-
ogy optimization, often requiring scene-specific assumptions and
carefully tuned parameters [Liu et al. 2025a; Zbirovský and Nežerka
2025]. Learning-based approaches improve automation, but most fo-
cus on intermediate geometric abstractions such as floor plans, room
polygons [Yue et al. 2023], or 3D planes [Mao et al. 2025]. Although
these representations support spatial understanding, they describe
layouts or surfaces rather than explicit volumetric component in-
stances. They do not directly couple component geometry, limiting
their extensibility to other BIM attributes such as load-bearing sta-
tus and materials. A reconstruction representation should therefore
expose these attributes as primary variables, rather than deriving
them from layouts or planes after prediction.
This mismatch calls for a volumetric, component-aware repre-
sentation. Cuboid and primitive assemblies are a natural candidate
because they encode compact solids with interpretable parameters
and support editable 3D modeling. However, existing cuboid-based
methods primarily target object decomposition[Ye et al. 2025a],
shape generation [Jones et al. 2020], or furniture arrangement [Feng
et al. 2025], rather than architectural reconstruction from incomplete
scene-level point clouds with ambiguous component boundaries.
Moreover, their generic cuboid parameterizations are not tailored to
highly elongated architectural components. This motivates a BIM-
oriented volumetric representation that explicitly models parametric
component geometry and architectural relations.
To this end, we introduce CRAB (Cuboid-based Representation
for As-built BIM), a BIM-ready proposal-and-assembly framework
for indoor point clouds. CRAB is designed precisely to move beyond
layout and plane abstractions toward volumetric BIM components,
thereby demonstrating the value of a BIM-oriented cuboid repre-
sentation for AI-based Scan-to-BIM. It reconstructs walls, doors,
windows, beams, and columns in single-floor scenes and repre-
sents each scene as centerline-based semantic cuboids and explicit
topological relationships, jointly encoding parametric component
geometry, semantics, wall adjacency, and opening-wall affiliation.
To recover these cuboids and relationships from noisy and incom-
plete point clouds, CRAB adopts a two-stage design. ProposalNet
first generates coarse semantic cuboids from point cloud features,
while AssemblyNet further refines their geometry and predicts inter-
component relationships through relation-aware Flow Matching.
This refinement improves structural consistency while preserving
editable cuboid parameters for user-guided modification. The result-
ing components can be directly instantiated as Industry Foundation
Classes (IFC) elements, enabling compatibility with the open BIM
data-exchange standard.
A further challenge is the lack of cuboid-level supervision for
scan-to-BIM learning. Existing indoor datasets typically provide
floor plans, meshes, semantic labels, or layout annotations, but lack
component-wise volumetric cuboids aligned with BIM elements.
To support training and evaluation, we extend the public Struc-
tured3D [Zheng et al. 2020] dataset with cuboid annotations for
architectural components, and further construct a cuboid-based
synthetic dataset with procedurally generated scenes and simulated
point cloud acquisition.
In summary, our contributions are threefold:
• We recast scan-based indoor BIM reconstruction as semantic
cuboid assembly, using centerline-based cuboid parameters to ex-
plicitly model component semantics, wall thickness, opening height,
and editable volumetric geometry.
• We construct CrabSet, a scalable cuboid-level supervision dataset
for Scan-to-BIM, by enriching Structured3D with component-wise
cuboid annotations and procedurally synthesizing diverse architec-
tural scenes with simulated point-cloud acquisition.
• We introduce CRAB, a proposal-and-assembly framework that
couples ProposalNet for semantic cuboid generation with Assem-
blyNet for relation-aware flow-matching refinement, producing
accurate, structurally coherent, and editable BIM-ready models.

Related Work
2.1
Structured Indoor Modeling
Indoor structural modeling is often cast as floor plan or layout
estimation. Existing methods reconstruct 2D graphs, room polygons,
or polygonal loops from RGB-D scans and point-density maps [Chen
et al. 2019, 2022a; Stekovic et al. 2021; Yue et al. 2023], with recent
variants improving polygon generation, room representation, graph
reasoning, segmentation, and edge continuity [Chen et al. 2023; Liu
et al. 2025b, 2024; Sun et al. 2025; Xu et al. 2024; Ye et al. 2025b].
While compact, these floorplan-oriented outputs do not directly
specify wall thickness, vertical extent, opening height, or component
volume, which are required by CAD/BIM workflows.
Recent 3D structured modeling methods move beyond 2D layouts
by predicting walls, openings, oriented boxes, or parametric layout
quads from images or point clouds [Avetisyan et al. 2024; Bieri et al.
2025; Chen et al. 2022b; Mao et al. 2025]. For planar representa-
tions, recovering volumetric semantic component instances from
predicted paired parallel planes requires additional plane pairing
and instance association to infer wall thickness, opening affiliation,
and component identity. Such associations can be ambiguous when
a single plane spans multiple walls, limiting extensions to instance-
specific BIM attributes such as materials and load-bearing status.
We instead reconstruct cuboid assemblies for walls, doors, windows,
beams, and columns, with explicit centerline, thickness, height.

2.2
BIM-Ready Cuboid Reconstruction
Scan-to-BIM converts sensed geometry into editable building mod-
els. Existing systems recover walls, slabs, openings, rooms, and IFC-
compatible outputs from point clouds [Murali et al. 2017; Ochmann
et al. 2019; Zbirovský and Nežerka 2025], and BIMNet highlights
the need for domain-specific data and BIM-aware geometric and
topological evaluation [Liu et al. 2025a]. Yet many pipelines still
rely on geometric rules, segmentation, optimization, or software-
specific workflows, making them sensitive to clutter, occlusion, and
incomplete scans [Han et al. 2023; Wong et al. 2025; Wu et al. 2023].
Moreover, they primarily recover final component geometry with-
out explicitly modeling relations, limiting coordinated refinement.
Cuboid and primitive assemblies offer compact, editable struc-
tures. ShapeAssembly represents shapes as programs over cuboid
parts [Jones et al. 2020], while recent primitive and procedural meth-
ods learn structured decompositions or editable programs from
data [Raji et al. 2026; Ye et al. 2025a]. CASAGPT uses cuboid primi-
tives for indoor scene synthesis to reduce mesh-level collisions [Feng
et al. 2025]. Existing methods mainly target object decomposition,
shape generation, or furnished scene synthesis rather than archi-
tectural reconstruction from incomplete scene-level point clouds.
They typically use generic cuboids parameterized by center, size,
and orientation, which are not tailored to highly elongated architec-
tural components. CRAB instead adopts centerline-based semantic
cuboids and explicit relations, supporting coordinated refinement
and editable component reconstruction.
2.3
Local Structural Refinement
Recent LLM/VLM agents have moved generation and editing from
one-shot prompting to closed-loop planning, tool use, evaluation,
and revision. SceneCraft refines Blender-code scenes with rendered
feedback [Hu et al. 2024], and VIGA reconstructs editable graphics
programs through a code-render-inspect loop [Yin et al. 2026]. Sim-
ilar feedback-driven loops appear in visual editing [Yao et al. 2026],
while BIM agents invoke APIs, manipulate IFC data, or operate au-
thoring software [Deng et al. 2025; Du et al. 2026; Nithyanantham
et al. 2025]. Xie et al. [Xie et al. 2025] further show the value of local
correction for missed or misplaced 3D layout elements. Motivated
by these local correction paradigms, our cuboid representation and
AssemblyNet support user-guided refinement, enabling controllable
local edits and global correction for improved structural consistency.

door BIM reconstruction from point clouds. Each sample contains
an input point cloud, component-wise volumetric cuboid annota-

4.2
ProposalNet
We argue that the primary objective of the first stage is to generate
accurate individual architectural components, since reliable predic-
tion of each cuboid forms the foundation for subsequent relationship
reasoning and structural assembly. Therefore, this module focuses
exclusively on cuboid geometry and semantic prediction. Formally,
we aim to learn the conditional distribution 𝑝𝜃(𝐶| 𝑃), from which
the initial cuboid proposals are generated:
𝐶init ∼𝑝𝜃(𝐶| 𝑃).
Specifically, we employ Sonata [Wu et al. 2025] as the point cloud
encoder and Qwen2.5-0.5B [Yang et al. 2024] as the auto-regressive
cuboid generator. The Sonata encoder is initialized with pretrained
weights and further trained on our point cloud and cuboid pairs. For
each input scene, we voxel-downsample the point cloud at 2.5 cm
and randomly keep at most 800,000 points for training and inference.
The point cloud encoder and autoregressive cuboid decoder are
connected by a lightweight MLP Adapter, which maps point cloud
features into the decoder token embedding space. Conditioned on
the adapted point cloud tokens, the decoder sequentially generates
the initial semantic cuboids. We fine-tune the generator on cuboid
token sequences, with a maximum of 200 cuboids and an EOS token
for termination. The proposal process is formulated as:

4.3
AssemblyNet
The cuboid proposal stage produces an initial set of architectural
elements, but the predicted cuboids may still contain geometric
inaccuracies and structural inconsistencies. AssemblyNet aims to
jointly refine cuboid geometry and infer structural relationships be-
tween components through Flow Matching. It also supports effective
user-guided local editing. Formally, this stage is defined as
(𝐶revised, 𝑅) = A𝜙(𝑃,𝐶init),
where A𝜙denotes the AssemblyNet, 𝐶revised is the refined cuboids.
Specifically, for each cuboid, the input to AssemblyNet consists
of explicit cuboid parameter tokens, one relation-aware token, and
a change flag, together with point cloud features used for cross
attention. The parameter tokens encode the cuboid geometry and
semantic type, while the relation-aware token captures the struc-
tural role of this cuboid and participates in all attention operations.
The tokens are first embedded with parameter-specific linear
layer encoders and timestep embeddings. They are then processed
by an Intra-Cuboid Attention module, which models interactions
among tokens within each cuboid and helps distinguish fixed cuboids
from refinement targets according to the change flag. Next, Inter-
Cuboid Attention propagates information across cuboids, allowing
each cuboid to perceive the positions, scales, semantic categories,
and potential structural dependencies of neighboring cuboids. A
Cuboid-Point Cross Attention module further associates cuboid to-
kens with point cloud features, grounding the refinement process in
the observed scene geometry. Details of the three attention modules
are provided in the supplementary material.

4.4
Reconstruction Pipeline
CRAB converts an input point cloud into an editable as-built BIM
model through the following three-step pipeline.
Step 1: Cuboid Proposal. ProposalNet reconstructs most architec-
tural components from the input point cloud as an initial set of
semantic cuboids with coarse geometric parameters.
Step 2: Human-in-the-Loop Assembly. User interaction is optional:
users may add, delete, move, or anchor coarse cuboids before as-
sembly. AssemblyNet jointly uses the cuboid set and input point
cloud to refine component geometry and predict their relationships,
automatically correcting geometric errors, local misalignments, and
redundant components. Completely missing components require
coarse user initialization, after which AssemblyNet aligns and inte-
grates them into the existing structure.
Step 3: BIM Conversion. Refined cuboids and relations are con-
verted into editable IFC components. For walls, adjacency relations
resolve junctions by trimming or extending each wall only along
its longitudinal axis, while preserving its direction, thickness, and
centerline offset. The resulting wall-adjacency graph is searched
for closed cycles containing at least four wall segments, which are
assembled into rooms in a bottom-up manner and can be further
converted into 2D floor plans or 3D planar representations. Opening-
wall affiliations associate doors and windows with their host walls
for wall-consistent placement. Based on the refined cuboids and pre-
dicted relations, the BIM model can be instantiated with corner-joint,
cross-joint, or wall-integrated wall structures.
5
Results and Evaluation
5.1
Experimental Setup
Datasets. For training, we use 3,000 Structured3D training scenes
and 3,000 procedurally generated scenes from CrabSet. For evalua-
tion, we evaluate on the Structured3D validation and test splits (250
scenes each), with BIMNet and ISPRS for real-scene generalization.
Metrics. We evaluate reconstruction quality in two stages. Match-
F1 measures coarse component-level matching quality using the F1
score computed from precision and recall based on voxel-overlap
matching. After coarse matching, geometric metrics evaluate accu-
racy: CL-Offset reports the centerline deviation of interior walls in
centimeters, T-MAE measures the mean thickness error of walls,
and T-Acc@𝑘denotes the proportion of matched walls whose thick-
ness error is within 𝑘cm. H-MAE measures height errors for doors
and windows. Details are provided in the supplementary material.
Baselines. We compare CRAB with an open-source volumetric
Scan-to-BIM method, Cloud2BIM[Zbirovský and Nežerka 2025],
floorplan-based methods, including RoomFormer[Yue et al. 2023]
and CAGE[Liu et al. 2025b], and 3D plane-based methods, including
SceneScript[Avetisyan et al. 2024] and SpatialLM[Mao et al. 2025].
Since released plane-based models lack valid opening heights on
Structured3D, we report SceneScript∗and SpatialLM∗, fine-tuned
on corrected S3D annotations. We report Ours∗, trained only on
S3D-derived cuboids, and Ours, trained on CrabSet, to ensure fair
comparison and assess procedural cuboid supervision.

5.2
Evaluation
We evaluate CRAB from six perspectives: (i) volumetric accuracy on
Structured3D, covering component matching, wall thickness, and
opening height; (ii) layout and plane quality using the native metrics
of representative 2D floor plan and 3D plane methods; (iii) robustness
under structural ambiguity and local perturbations; (iv) real-world
generalization on noisy and incomplete BIMNet and ISPRS scans;
(v) editability and BIM conversion enabled by cuboid components
and their relations; and (vi) beam and column reconstruction on
procedurally generated scenes to assess broader coverage.
Volumetric Reconstruction Accuracy. Cuboid representations di-
rectly model architectural elements as 3D components, avoiding the
need to infer wall thickness or opening geometry from 2D layouts
or planar fragments. We evaluate these BIM-critical attributes on
the Structured3D test set in Table 2. Compared with the volumetric
baseline Cloud2BIM, CRAB produces more complete component re-
constructions on incomplete scans, with fewer fragmented walls and
unstable openings. Compared with floor-plan and plane-based al-
ternatives, CRAB achieves stronger component matching and more
accurate geometry. For openings, CRAB improves window Match-F1
from 77.90% to 88.97% over SpatialLM, and reduces height errors to
2.86 cm for doors and 9.23 cm for windows. For interior walls, CRAB
achieves a wall-thickness MAE of 2.80 cm and a T-Acc@3 of 79.50%,
substantially outperforming the strongest non-cuboid baseline at
41.62% T-Acc@3. As shown in Fig. 4, CRAB also better preserves
wall thickness, wall extent, and opening–wall consistency.
Fig. 4. Qualitative comparison with a plane-based representation. Zoomed
views compare the same local structures in GT, CRAB, and SpatialLM.
CRAB’s volumetric cuboids better preserve wall thickness, component ex-
tent, and opening-wall consistency than the plane-based representation.
Layout and Plane Quality. As shown in Table 3, we evaluate CRAB
using RoomFormer’s native 2D layout metrics [Yue et al. 2023] and
SpatialLM’s native 3D plane metrics [Mao et al. 2025]. Following
Sec. 4.4, CRAB outputs are converted into 2D room polygons and 3D
planes for evaluation. For cross-representation comparison, plane-
based outputs are grouped into rooms by matching shared wall end-
points, while floorplan outputs are lifted using point-cloud heights.
CRAB achieves notably higher Corner F1 and Angle F1 than CAGE,
demonstrating more accurate boundary geometry. Compared with
SpatialLM, CRAB achieves comparable wall-plane performance and
improves window F1 from 87.5% to 91.4% at IoU2𝐷@0.5. Overall,
CRAB achieves competitive layout and plane quality against base-
lines while providing accurate volumetric reconstruction.
Robustness to Ambiguity and Local Perturbations. Cuboids provide
more reliable refinement units because each prediction corresponds
to an explicit volumetric component. This is particularly useful in
incomplete, ambiguous, or non-Manhattan scenes, where floor-plan
methods depend on room closure and plane-based methods may
become unstable when several nearby planes describe the same
wall or opening. As shown in Fig. 7, CRAB recovers more coherent
wall structures and more consistent opening geometry by directly
predicting component-level cuboids.

5.3
Ablation Study
Effect of Centerline-based Parameterization. We ablate the pro-
posed centerline-based cuboid parameterization against a generic
oriented box representation, which can be viewed as a simplified
variant of common primitive and scene tokenizations [Feng et al.
2025; Ye et al. 2025a]. Since architectural components are vertically
extruded in our setting, the oriented box is represented by center
translation, side lengths, and a yaw rotation angle around the verti-
cal axis. Both variants are trained under the same setting, with only
the geometric representation changed.
As shown in Table 5, oriented boxes perform slightly better on
doors and achieve comparable results on windows, which are rel-
atively compact components. However, for long and thin walls,
the centerline-based cuboid explicitly separates wall extent from
wall thickness, leading to better wall reconstruction and improving
interior-wall T-Acc@3 from 71.74% to 79.50%. For highly elongated
architectural components, longitudinal extent and thickness play
strongly asymmetric geometric roles, and centerline-based parame-
terization better disentangles them, reducing geometric ambiguity
and improving wall reconstruction stability. This suggests that the
proposed parameterization is more suitable for wall-dominated ar-
chitectural reconstruction, where accurate wall geometry is critical
for downstream BIM conversion.
5.4
Limitation Analysis
CRAB still has several limitations. (1) Missing component recovery.
CRAB can automatically correct continuous geometric errors, local
misalignments, and redundant components, but entirely missing
components may still require coarse user initialization. The supple-
mentary material further shows AssemblyNet’s ability to correct
geometric errors and local misalignments, while removing redun-
dant components through component alignment. (2) Irregular non-
Manhattan geometry. While CRAB handles standard Manhattan
layouts and moderate non-Manhattan variations, highly irregular
or curved structures remain challenging: cuboids can capture the
dominant organization, but may oversimplify fine-scale deviations.
(3) Sparse infrequent components. Beams, columns, stairs, and service
shafts appear sparsely in current real-world test scenes, limiting
comprehensive evaluation. Although CRAB can model these ele-
ments in principle, robust reconstruction requires richer component-
level supervision and broader BIM benchmarks.

Conclusion and Future Work
We presented CRAB, a cuboid-based approach to indoor Scan-to-BIM
reconstruction. The key observation is that BIM reconstruction re-
quires component-level volumetric structure, not only layout curves
or planar surfaces. Accordingly, CRAB represents walls, doors, win-
dows, beams and columns as semantic cuboids with explicit center-
line, thickness, height, and opening parameters. This representation
makes architectural components directly editable and provides a
natural interface for downstream BIM conversion.
The proposed framework follows a proposal-and-assembly design.
ProposalNet predicts coarse architectural cuboids from point-cloud
observations, while AssemblyNet refines their geometry and re-
lationships through relation-aware Flow Matching. Together, the
two-stage system is capable of correcting geometric errors and lo-
cal misalignments while removing redundant components through
component alignment. To support learning and evaluation, we con-
structed CrabSet, which provides cuboid-level supervision from
Structured3D-derived annotations and procedurally generated ar-
chitectural scenes. Experiments on synthetic and real-world scans
show that cuboid assembly yields more accurate wall-thickness
estimation, more stable refinement, and more flexible BIM instan-
tiation than floor-plan- or plane-based alternatives. Importantly,
these gains in volumetric reconstruction do not come at the expense
of layout or plane quality, where CRAB remains competitive with
representation-specific baselines.
To address the limitations discussed earlier, future work will pri-
marily broaden CRAB’s practical capabilities and application scope.
We will explore (1) complex non-Manhattan geometry and richer
primitives, including cylinders and curved surfaces, (2) broader BIM
categories and structural relations, together with semantic plane
cues as auxiliary geometric evidence for robust reconstruction un-
der real-scene domain shifts, and (3) interactive or agent-assisted
workflows, reducing manual effort and enabling more automated
Scan-to-BIM reconstruction.
