## ABSTRACT 

Feed-forward models accurately estimate camera motion and 3D geometry from short video windows. However, computational and memory costs prevent direct inference on sequences containing thousands of frames. Long videos must there-fore be processed as overlapping chunks. This division produces independent local reconstructions with inconsistent coordinate frames and duplicate estimates of shared cameras. Achieving global consistency requires propagating constraints from adjacent overlaps and distant revisits across the sequence. This requirement naturally motivates pose graph optimization. However, the choice of graph level creates a trade-off. Chunk-level graphs efficiently resolve scale drift and close large loops. Yet, their rigid transformations cannot correct internal errors or con-flicting frame predictions. Conversely, frame-level optimization offers local flex-ibility, but scaling it globally is computationally expensive and can break metric scale. HIP3R resolves this trade-off using a hierarchical pose graph. HIP3R first optimizes a robust chunk-level Sim(3) graph to establish the global layout and scale. It then holds this layout fixed and estimates one shared SE(3) camera per frame, turning overlapping window predictions into a single trajectory. Ex-periments across multiple benchmarks demonstrate the effectiveness of H IP3R in improving camera trajectories and 3D reconstruction. Our method applies to both chunk-based and streaming backends without additional training or fine-tuning. 

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

Recovering globally consistent 3D geometry and camera trajectories from video is fundamental to computer vision. This capability supports autonomous navigation, robotic mapping, and spatial artificial intelligence. Recent 3D foundation models (Wang et al., 2024; 2025a; 2026b) have sig-nificantly advanced visual geometry. By jointly predicting camera poses and dense pointmaps in a single forward pass, they bypass classical handcrafted matching. These models achieve high ac-curacy on short video clips without per-scene calibration or optimization. However, the quadratic complexity of visual attention limits them to short temporal windows. Directly scaling transformers to extended multi-kilometer sequences remains too expensive. To process long videos, modern systems adopt a divide-and-conquer strategy (Deng et al., 2025). Long sequences are divided into overlapping video chunks, which are processed independently and stitched together. While chunking bounds GPU memory, sequential alignment introduces severe computational and numerical bottlenecks. Dense overlapping points share high spatial redundancy, making all-pixel alignment computationally expensive. Furthermore, pairwise alignment errors compound along the sequential chain, causing severe scale and orientation drift. Loop closures are essential to eliminate cumulative drift and enforce global topological consistency. However, when the chains are not yet aligned, applying multiple long-range loop constraints at once can make the non-convex optimization unstable. The solver may diverge or converge to poor local minima. As shown in Figure 1, even after global chunk placement is resolved, a conflict remains at the frame level. Existing alignment methods (Deng et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2026b; Xiong et al., 2026) optimize similarity transformations per chunk. However, a single similarity transform preserves

A chunk is an inference unit, whereas the true trajectory contains only one camera pose per frame. Arbitrarily selecting one prediction discards multi-view evidence and causes velocity jumps across chunk boundaries. Conversely, full bundle adjustment is too expensive, while relative motion alone drifts from the recovered global layout. To overcome these challenges, we introduce H IP3R, a Hierarchical Pose-graph optimization method for long-horizon 3D Reconstruction. H IP3R decomposes reconstruction into coarse chunk layout recovery and fine frame trajectory refinement. The coarse stage optimizes a chunk-level Sim(3) 

pose graph over adjacent overlaps and distant loop closures. This stage establishes a globally con-sistent layout and eliminates accumulated scale drift. Holding this coarse layout fixed, the fine stage optimizes a frame-level shared-camera SE(3) pose graph, representing each physical frame with a single camera variable to resolve cross-window pose inconsistency. Placement priors keep that cam-era on the recovered layout, while within-chunk motion factors resolve conflicting predictions from overlapping windows. We evaluate H IP3R across seven public benchmarks spanning diverse indoor and outdoor environments. Under matched backends and windows, H IP3R improves long-horizon trajectory accuracy over loop-enabled VGGT-Long. Controlled ablations show that bounded point sampling reduces graph construction and optimization time with little change in accuracy. Addi-tional evaluations show that the method also works with streaming backends such as LingBot-MAP. We summarize our main contributions as follows: • We show that chunk-level similarity alignment alone cannot ensure cross-window camera-pose consistency, as overlapping windows may yield conflicting estimates for the same frame. • We develop a hierarchical pose graph that first recovers a globally consistent chunk layout and scale, then estimates one shared SE(3) camera pose for each frame. • We introduce progressive loop closure and bounded point sampling to achieve stable and scalable optimization on long sequences without retraining.

Streaming and recurrent video geometry. To extend temporal context beyond static windows, another line of research develops streaming and recurrent architectures. Early methods such as Spann3R (Wang & Agapito, 2025) and CUT3R (Wang et al., 2025b) maintain persistent spatial memory states. Recent methods like StreamVGGT (Zhuo et al., 2026), STream3R (Lan et al., 2026), and WinT3R (Li et al., 2026b) process video frames causally with temporal key-value caching. LingBot-MAP (Chen et al., 2026a) structures geometric context across anchor frames, local win-dows, and trajectory memory. Other approaches explore test-time training, memory compression, and feature retrieval (Chen et al., 2026b; Zhang et al., 2026a; Xie et al., 2026; Jin et al., 2026; Liu et al., 2026; Zou et al., 2026). Causal streaming still accumulates drift on long trajectories. As post-processing, H IP3R adds global loops and per-frame pose consistency without retraining. 

Multi-window fusion and Structure-from-Motion. Scaling feed-forward predictions to extended trajectories connects to classical global Structure-from-Motion and pose graph optimization. Chunk-based methods like VGGT-Long (Deng et al., 2025), VGGT-Align (Zhang et al., 2026b), and VGGT-Motion (Xiong et al., 2026) align adjacent chunks via pairwise Sim(3) alignment. A rigid Sim(3) 

per chunk preserves internal cameras up to scale and cannot resolve overlapping-frame disagree-ments. GlueMap (Pan et al., 2026) couples feed-forward star reconstructions with global motion averaging and bundle adjustment over virtual tracks. H IP3R resolves overlapping camera predic-tions through a shared frame-level pose graph after chunk alignment, without optimizing 3D tracks. 

## 3 METHOD 

3.1 PROBLEM FORMULATION 

Let I = {It}Tt=1 denote an input video sequence of T frames. We partition I into K overlapping chunks {Ck}Kk=1 of at most C frames with overlap O. Chunk starts advance by C − O frames; the final chunk may be shorter than C. Each chunk is processed independently by a feed-forward 3D vision model (Wang et al., 2026b). For chunk Ck, the backend predicts dense pointmaps Xk,i ,confidence maps γk,i , and camera-to-chunk poses Pk,i . Here i ∈ { 1, . . . , |Ck|} indexes local frames within the chunk. Let g(k, i ) ∈ { 1, . . . , T } map local frame i in chunk Ck to its global video index. The goal is to recover a single camera trajectory F = {Ft ∈ SE(3) }Tt=1 together with consistent 3D pointmaps. Figure 2 gives an overview. 

Decoupled optimization. Directly optimizing all camera and point variables across thousands of frames is expensive. Unconstrained bundle adjustment loses the efficiency of feed-forward models. HIP3R uses a two-level hierarchical pose graph. Stage I solves a chunk-level Sim(3) pose graph 3Preprint for a globally aligned layout (Section 3.2). Stage II then optimizes a frame-level SE(3) pose graph with one shared camera variable per frame while holding the layout fixed (Section 3.3). 3.2 COARSE LAYOUT OPTIMIZATION WITH PROGRESSIVE LOOP SCHEDULING

Relative constraints determine scale only up to a global factor, so we fix A1 as the world frame and scale reference. Scale errors in independent chunk predictions accumulate on long trajectories, so we optimize the remaining sk jointly with rotation and translation. Dense overlapping 3D points are spatially redundant, so all-pixel alignment is unnecessary. For adjacent chunks, identical pixels in overlapping frames yield paired 3D predictions. We estimate relative similarities with confidence-weighted iteratively reweighted least squares (IRLS) rather than unweighted Umeyama alignment on all dense pixels (Deng et al., 2025). Each iteration runs a weighted Umeyama alignment (Umeyama, 1991) and updates Huber weights from spatial residuals. We bound alignment cost by limiting the number of point correspondences used in each fit. Visual place recognition proposes candidate loop closures across temporal separations (Izquierdo & Civera, 2024). For each selected pair of revisiting frames, we combine temporal neighborhoods from both endpoints into one backend input. The resulting joint reconstruction connects the two visits in a common local coordinate system. We align the predicted geometry at each endpoint to the corresponding original chunk, obtaining transformations Bi and Bj from the joint reconstruction to chunks i and j, respectively. ## 4 EXPERIMENTS 

We evaluate trajectory accuracy across seven public benchmarks comprising 61 diverse sequences. The benchmark spans indoor room-scale environments, including 7-Scenes (Shotton et al., 2013) and Neural RGB-D (Azinovi´ c et al., 2022). It also includes high-precision multi-scale scenes from ETH3D (Sch¨ ops et al., 2017) and Tanks and Temples (Knapitsch et al., 2017). Finally, it includes ex-tended outdoor driving and campus traversals from Oxford Spires (Tao et al., 2025), KITTI (Geiger et al., 2012), and VBR (Brizi et al., 2024). Dense surface geometry is evaluated on ETH3D, Neural RGB-D, and 7-Scenes, which provide dense 3D ground truth. Appendix E details dataset composi-tions and sequence splits. Baselines in Table 1 use released checkpoints and recommended window policies on identical sequences. VGGT-Long and VGGT-Align are evaluated from public imple-mentations, while VGGT-Motion (Xiong et al., 2026) is not publicly available. We evaluate trajectory accuracy using ATE, RPE rot , and RPE trans (lower is better). ATE is translation RMSE after a per-sequence Sim(3) alignment that corrects rotation, translation, and scale. Relative errors use all consecutive frame pairs ( ∆=1 frame). Rotation RPE is in degrees, and translation RPE applies the same Sim(3) correction. Pairwise pose AUC (higher is better) evaluates all i < j camera pairs using the maximum of relative rotation and translation-direction angular errors at thresholds of 5Preprint 

5◦, 15 ◦, and 30 ◦. Reconstructed 3D pointmaps are evaluated using the mean of accuracy and com-pleteness (Chamfer, meters) after Umeyama and ICP. F1 uses a hit distance of 0.25 m on ETH3D and 

0.05 m on Neural RGB-D and 7-Scenes. Appendix H provides per-dataset geometric breakdowns. We apply H IP3R to the camera poses and dense geometry produced by VGGT-Omega (Wang et al., 2026b) and LingBot-MAP (Chen et al., 2026a). Both backends use the same graph formulation without fine-tuning. Unless otherwise stated, we use VGGT-Omega 1B with a chunk size of 60 

frames and an overlap size of 30 frames. LingBot-MAP uses windowed inference with a chunk size of 120 frames and an overlap size of 32 frames. We use the same refinement weights across backends. For short sequences, the fine stage optimizes all camera poses; for long sequences, it optimizes a keyframe subgraph and interpolates pose cor-rections for the remaining frames. ## 5 CONCLUSION 

HIP3R turns overlapping local predictions into a globally consistent reconstruction of long video se-quences through a hierarchical pose graph. The method first recovers global layout and scale through a chunk-level Sim(3) graph, then refines a shared-camera SE(3) graph with the chunk transforms held fixed. This separation allows distant loop closures to correct accumulated drift while reconcil-ing conflicting predictions of the same camera across windows. Evaluations on indoor and outdoor benchmarks show improvements in overall trajectory accuracy and surface reconstruction with both VGGT-Omega and LingBot-MAP. Extending this hierarchy to online updates and improving its robustness to unreliable loop closures are promising directions for future work.

## B GRAPH COLORING AND CLOSED -FORM JACOBIAN BLOCKS 

This appendix provides mathematical derivations for the sparse Jacobian blocks in Section 3.3. Derivative identities follow standard Lie-group calculus (Sol` a et al., 2018). We first analyze Jaco-bian sparsity and compare finite-difference graph coloring with analytic derivatives. We then derive closed-form blocks for SE(3) residuals and describe automatic differentiation for Sim(3) residuals. 

Jacobian sparsity and manifold structure. Nonlinear least-squares iterations linearize r(x) at current poses. Jacobian J = ∂r/∂x has one row per residual coordinate and one column per pose parameter. For a 20 ,000 -frame graph, n reaches 10 5 and m spans millions. A dense J consists mostly of zeros. A relative residual between frames a and b depends only on those two cameras under fixed chunk transforms. 

Graph coloring baseline for finite differences. Without closed-form derivatives, J can be ap-proximated via finite differences. Evaluating columns individually costs n residual passes. Sparsity enables a more efficient approach. Two columns are structurally orthogonal if they share no residual rows. Perturbing orthogonal variables simultaneously isolates each column after differencing. Each residual experiences at most one perturbation (Curtis et al., 1974). Coloring the column-intersection graph groups mutually orthogonal columns together. The number of residual sweeps equals the chromatic number. This depends on graph topology instead of total parameter count. On a 735 -frame scene, coloring cuts post-processing from 10 ,228 to 172 s. The closed-form derivatives below eliminate these colored sweeps for SE(3) residuals.

## K CONTROLLED LAYOUT -SOLVER COMPARISON 

The layout-solver study uses ten long sequences: KITTI sequence 00, all seven VBR sequences, and Droid-W downtown2 and downtown5 (Li et al., 2026a). All evaluated configurations share identical model predictions, candidate revisit pairs, and initialization. Camera refinement and point correc-tions are disabled to isolate layout optimization. The full coarse solve allows at most 5000 function evaluations. The five configurations evaluate baseline pairwise alignment, bounded point sampling, residual-fit weighting, progressive loop scheduling, and the complete optimization solver. The final step combines adaptive continuation, Huber loss, and bounded restarts. Appendix D specifies the continuation and restart rules and their evaluation budgets. We test solver robustness against noisy loop measurements by perturbing loop closures. Specifi-cally, synthetic noise is injected into 25% of detected loop edges using a fixed random seed. For each selected edge, we apply a 10 ◦ rotation error. We also inject a 25% translation offset and a 0.10 

log-scale distortion. The evaluation confirms efficiency gains from bounded sampling, alongside ac-curacy and runtime gains from the complete solver. The complete configuration reduces clean mean ATE by 19 .77% relative to the baseline. This improvement concentrates in VBR, where mean error falls from 28 .6017 to 22 .4461 m. Peak host memory remains comparable across configurations. 

## L PLACEMENT AND MOTION ABLATION SETTINGS 

The four controls in Table 5 evaluate different strategies for resolving multiple camera predictions under identical optimized coarse layouts. Layout Only selects the first available coarse-placed pose for each frame in chunk order. Single selects the occurrence that maximizes the minimum dis-tance, in frames, to either boundary of its chunk. Ties are broken by the smaller chunk index. Both Layout Only and Single directly output the selected coarse-placed poses without frame-level optimization. Unary computes a robust pose average over all coarse-placed occurrences of each frame using deterministic Huber IRLS, without relative-motion factors. Motion optimizes within-chunk relative-motion factors without placement priors. It fixes the first camera to its coarse place-ment and uses λf = 1 and λp = 0 .
