# Rethinking Sparse-View 3D Gaussian Splatting: A Few-Shot Learning Perspective on Overfitting Prevention

Sparse-view novel view synthesis (NVS) is essential for AR/VR and real-time communication applications, yet remains challenging when reconstructing 3D scenes from limited viewpoints. While 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) excels in dense-view scenarios, it suffers from overfitting and geometry degradation under sparse inputs. Current approaches often tackle these problems from a local perspective, addressing either data quality or training regularization individually, lacking a unified and interpretable framework for systematic improvement. We introduce a new perspective that reframes sparse-view 3DGS from a few-shot learning viewpoint, treating each Gauss-ian primitive as a neuron and leveraging deep learning regularization tech-niques. Under this unified framework that systematically connects 3DGS optimization with deep learning regularization principles, our approach generates more effective supervision from scarce observations through two strategies: (1) data-related supervision enrichment via optical flow-guided pseudo-view synthesis and joint camera–scene optimization; and (2) training-time regularization via motion-aware dropout and early stopping, which suppress overfitting while preserving dynamic-scene stability. Extensive experiments on the SIGGRAPH Asia 2025 volumetric video challenge sparse-view dataset demonstrate significant improvements over state-of-the-art optimization-based and feed-forward baselines. Our system won first place in the sparse-view track of the SIGGRAPH Asia 2025 challenge. Additional Key Words and Phrases: 3D Gaussian Splatting, sparse-view synthesis, few-shot learning 

1 Introduction 

Sparse-view novel view synthesis (NVS) aims to reconstruct a ren-derable 3D scene from a small number of input viewpoints, a ca-pability that is critical for AR/VR and real-time communication applications. Recent advances in differentiable rendering—most no-tably Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) and 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)—have driven substantial progress; in dense-view settings, 3DGS in particular offers fast convergence and high rendering effi-ciency and has been widely adopted [Kerbl et al. 2023]. However, under sparse inputs, even 3DGS often overfits the limited viewpoints, resulting in geometry degradation and unstable, artifact-prone ap-pearance.

Existing approaches address the sparse viewpoint problem through various strategies—synthesizing pseudo-views, incorporat-ing external depth supervision, and applying training regularization [Bao et al. 2024; Li et al. 2024; Smith et al. 2024; Yu et al. 2024]—but lack a unified perspective. This motivates us to seek a principled viewpoint that organizes these strategies within a single method-ology. We therefore ask: if each Gaussian primitive could be treated as a neuron, can we borrow the well-established regu-larization toolkit from deep learning? This question leads us to reframe sparse-view 3DGS from a few-shot learning perspective, making deep learning regularization principles directly applicable. In deep learning, Ying et al. [Ying 2019] attribute overfitting to three causes—noisy data, excessive model capacity, and insufficient data. We argue that a corresponding mapping to sparse-view 3DGS challenges is: camera miscalibration introduces noise, excessive and entangled Gaussians lead to overfitting and co-adaptation, and limited viewpoints provide insufficient supervision. Correspond-ingly, the four canonical overfitting prevention strategies in deep learning—early stopping, model reduction, data augmentation, and regularization—naturally apply to 3DGS to address these challenges. We therefore group them into the two pillars that structure our method: Supervision Enhancement (data-related) and Training Regu-larization (model-training-related). Our approach instantiates these pillars through: (1) Supervision Enhancement : data expansion via optical flow-guided pseudo-view synthesis and data augmentation via joint camera–scene optimiza-tion; (2) Training Regularization : motion-aware dropout (restricted to static regions to handle dynamic scenes) paired with early stop-ping. 

Contributions. Our work makes the following contributions: 

• To the best of our knowledge, we are the first to provide a princi-pled reframing of sparse-view 3D Gaussian Splatting as a few-shot learning problem by interpreting each Gaussian primitive as a computational unit. This perspective yields an interpretable link between graphics optimization and well-established generaliza-tion mechanisms in deep learning. 

• We instantiate the Supervision Enhancement component via a supervision scheme tailored to limited viewpoints that (i) syn-thesizes pseudo-views through optical flow, and (ii) performs adaptive joint camera–scene optimization based on reprojection error. 

• We instantiate the Training Regularization component by cou-pling motion-aware dropout—selectively applied to static Gaus-sians—with early stopping, effectively reducing co-adaptation under sparse inputs.

2 Methodology 

Our overall algorithm is illustrated in Figure 2. Following the deep learning perspective outlined in the introduction, we organize our approach into two complementary categories: Supervision Enhance-ment and Training Regularization . This unified framework addresses the three fundamental causes of overfitting in sparse-view 3DGS: noisy data (corresponding to camera miscalibration), insufficient data, and excessive model capacity. In the following subsections, we detail each component. 

2.1 Supervision Enhancement

2.1.2 Joint Camera–Scene Optimization. Camera poses are part of the input data; when their calibration is inaccurate, they act as noise that degrades sparse-view reconstructions. To address this, we jointly optimize both camera poses and scene geometry based on reprojection error. We parameterize camera poses using SO (3)

rotations and R3 translations (SO3xR3), allowing the optimization to correct calibration errors while simultaneously refining the 3D Gaussian scene representation. To avoid unnecessary optimization when camera calibration is already accurate, we selectively enable camera pose optimization only for sequences with high reprojection errors, which indicates potential calibration issues. The camera op-timizer is activated after an initial warmup period and frozen before geometric constraints are applied, ensuring stable convergence. This selective joint optimization effectively reduces the impact of noisy camera parameters on sparse-view reconstruction while preserving well-calibrated camera poses. 

2.2 Training Regularization 

To address excessive model capacity and prevent overfitting, we employ two regularization strategies: motion-aware dropout and early stopping. 

2.2.1 Motion-Aware Dropout. Dropout requires that different sub-sets of Gaussians, after being dropped, yield comparable outcomes. However, this consistency assumption is often violated under fast motion. We therefore restrict dropout to static regions only. To de-fine static and dynamic regions, we leverage dense initialization that combines optical flow estimation (PDCNet[Truong et al. 2023]) and multi-view point cloud reconstruction (OpenMVS[cdcseacave 2023]). We compute the distance from each point in the PDCNet point cloud to the near-est point in the OpenMVS point cloud. Gaussians in dynamic regions are excluded from dropout to preserve rendering quality, while only static-region Gaussians are subject to dropout regularization. This selective ap-plication maintains the consistency assumption for dropout while protecting dynamic regions. 

2.2.2 Early Stopping. To prevent overfitting, we employ early stop-ping based on validation metrics. We determine the optimal early-stopping point by evaluating checkpoints throughout training and computing rankings on novel view synthesis metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS). The optimal stopping point is determined as the checkpoint with the lowest average rank across all three metrics. This stopping criterion is determined using a validation sequence and then applied consistently across all experiments.

3 Experiments 3.1 Experimental Settings 

Dataset and Evaluation Protocol We evaluate our method on the SIGGRAPH Asia 2025 Volumetric Video Challenge sparse-view track dataset [Yu et al. 2025]. The dataset consists of 2 validation se-quences (with full 16 camera viewpoints) and 5 test sequences (with 8 camera viewpoints), each containing 300 frames. All quantitative comparisons reported in this paper are conducted on the validation sequences, where we use 8 training views for reconstruction and evaluate on 8 novel test views. Test sequences are reserved for chal-lenge submissions. Metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) are computed on foreground regions only across all novel views. 

Implementation Details All experiments were conducted on an NVIDIA A100 40GB GPU, with the training phase of each run tak-ing roughly three minutes (preprocessing time is excluded). The training process consisted of 5,000 epochs with a progressive reso-lution schedule: we trained 3,000 epochs at half resolution followed by 2,000 epochs at full resolution. Although the nominal schedule budgets 30k optimization steps, we monitor PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS on a held-out validation sequence and consistently trigger early stopping around step 5k. 

Hyperparameters For the rendering loss, we set 𝜆 1 = 0.8 and 

𝜆 SSIM = 0.2. The geometric objective weights are: 𝜆 geo = 0.8, 𝜆 𝑑 =

0.05 (depth TV), 𝜆 𝑓 = 0.1 (flow TV), and 𝜆 𝑒 = 0.05 (edge-aware). Camera optimization is enabled for sequences with reprojection error exceeding 1.0, activated at step 3,000 and frozen at step 4,000. Motion-aware dropout uses a factor of 0.1 for static Gaussians, with motion detection threshold of 0.8. The geometric constraint starts at iteration 500 with translation range (0.05 , 0.2).

3.2 Comparison with SOTA Methods 

Our comparative study covers state-of-the-art optimization-based and feed-forward methods, specifically: 1 the base 3DGS method [Kerbl et al. 2023]; 2 a representative self-supervised pseudo-data approach, Binocular3DGS [Han et al. 2024]; 3 a representative feed-forward method, GPS-Gaussian [Zheng et al. 2024]; 4 the state-of-the-art for sparse reconstruction, SparseGS [Xiong et al. 2025]. Conclusion We reframe sparse-view 3D Gaussian Splatting from a few-shot learning perspective and organize the solution around two pillars: Supervision Enhancement (optical-flow pseudo views plus joint camera–scene optimization) and Training Regularization
