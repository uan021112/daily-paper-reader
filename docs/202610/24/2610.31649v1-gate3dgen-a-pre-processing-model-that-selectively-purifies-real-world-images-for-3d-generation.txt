Abstract 

Single-image 3D generation has achieved high fidelity on curated benchmarks, yet degrades sharply on real-world images with uneven illumination, blur, and occlusions. Purifying such inputs requires deciding when to intervene and preserving the visual cues needed for 3D generation, while leaving clean inputs unchanged. We introduce Gate3DGen , a selective pre-processing model that gates image inputs to existing 3D generators without retraining them. A degradation-aware router bypasses inputs classified as clean and extracts features to guide purification of the remaining inputs. Conditioned on these features, a 3D-oriented one-step purifier purifies degraded inputs with spatial losses that encourage preservation of object identity, contours, and textural cues. To provide paired supervision, we construct a synthetic data engine that renders clean views of 3D assets and synthesises composite degradations. On the synthetic benchmark, the purifier improves image fidelity and downstream 3D geometry in only 2.3 s on average, reducing TRELLIS.2 mesh distance by 61 .5% relative to de-graded inputs. VLM evaluation and human preferences on real photographs further support identity preservation and degradation correction. ## 1 Introduction 

Single-image 3D generative models have achieved remarkable progress on curated benchmarks (Hong et al., 2024; Tochilkin et al., 2024; Xu et al., 2024; Xiang et al., 2026; Li et al., 2026; Yang et al., 2026), yet degrade sharply on real-world images. This vulnerability stems from restrictive assumptions about input images. Contemporary models assume centered, sharp, and fully visible objects under uniform studio lighting (Xu et al., 2024; Xiang et al., 2025; 2026). In real-world scenarios, however, unconstrained photographs routinely exhibit blur, uneven illumination, or occlusions (Cho et al., 2025; Qu et al., 2026), corrupting visual cues and inducing severe artifacts in 3D outputs. Open-source image-to-3D pipelines commonly use background removal and recentering (Tochilkin et al., 2024; Xu et al., 2024; Xiang et al., 2025; Zhao et al., 2025), which do not directly address photometric corruptions or foreground occlusions. Specialized preprocessing also targets individual factors such as illumination through delighting (Li et al., 2025a; Zhao et al., 2025). When multiple degradations coexist, correcting one factor can leave other sources of ambiguity in the conditioning image. Our comparisons show that generic 2D image editing can alter object appearance or contours, with adverse effects on downstream geometry (Section 4.4). The objective is therefore to recover a suitable observation of the same object, retaining the visible structural evidence that the 3D generator uses to infer shape and appearance. This motivates selective input purification that preserves visual cues for 3D generation while avoiding unnecessary changes to already suitable clean inputs. We therefore introduce Gate3DGen, a selective pre-processing model that gates real-world images to existing 3D gener-ators (Figure 1). It routes each image through purification or an unchanged bypass, keeping the downstream generators frozen. Gate3DGen couples a degradation-aware router with a 3D-oriented one-step purifier . The router bypasses inputs classified as clean and selects the remaining inputs for purification, while extracting continuous degradation-aware fea-tures for the purifier. Conditioned on these features, the one-step purifier purifies degraded inputs in a single forward pass to reduce inference cost, with spatial losses that encourage preservation of object identity and essential geometric and tex-tural cues. Training requires matched ground-truth references, which are unavailable for real-world images. To provide paired training supervision without manual collection, we construct a synthetic data engine from 3D assets. Rendering multi-view anchors from 3,961 3D assets (Zhang et al., 2025) and synthesizing composite corruptions (e.g., uneven illumination, blur, and occlusions) yields 43,936 aligned image pairs and 1,082 held-out cases with 3D ground truth. We evaluate the purifier on this synthetic benchmark with two pretrained 3D generative models, TRELLIS.2 (Xiang et al., 2026) and Pixal3D (Li et al., 2026). For 2D purification, Gate3DGen reduces foreground LPIPS by 39 .1% while preserving subject silhouettes, with an inference time of 2.3 s per image. In comparison, the image editing baselines require 15 .6–100 .8 s per image and can alter object identity and reduce reconstruction fidelity (Table 2). When used to condition these 3D generative models, the purified images consistently improve all geometry and appearance metrics over the original observations. Specifically, Gate3DGen achieves a 61 .5% reduction in all-surface mesh distance, closes 

55 .5% of the geometry gap to clean references on average (up to 83 .1% on TRELLIS.2), and reduces rendering LPIPS by 10 .9% . Evaluated separately, the degradation-aware router identifies degraded inputs with 88 .45% recall and 93 .47% 

specificity on clean inputs, sending inputs classified as degraded for purification. On real photographs, VLM scores and human preferences further support identity preservation and degradation correction, complementing the reference-based evaluation on synthetic inputs. Our contributions are as follows: • We formulate robust single-image 3D generation as selective input purification, improving existing 3D generators through purified inputs without retraining them. • We introduce Gate3DGen, combining a router that bypasses inputs classified as clean with a one-step purifier. Degradation-aware features and spatial losses guide purification toward preserving object identity, contours, and geometric and textural cues for downstream 3D generation. • We construct a synthetic data engine providing paired purification supervision from 3D assets and a benchmark with 3D ground truth for 3D evaluation. Our method reduces foreground LPIPS by 39 .1% and TRELLIS.2 mesh distance by 61 .5% relative to degraded inputs, demonstrating gains in image fidelity and downstream 3D geometry.

Blur, uneven illumination, and occlusions can reduce their generation quality. Common input preprocessing operations include foreground segmentation and recentering, which do not directly correct illumination, blur, or foreground occlusions. ## 3 Method 

Gate3DGen is a selective pre-processing model that purifies real-world input images without modifying the downstream 3D generator. It targets images degraded by uneven illumination, blur, and occlusions while encouraging preservation of geometric and textural cues. Figure 2 summarizes its data engine, router, and one-step purifier. 

3.1 Problem Formulation 

Let G : X → Y be a pretrained generator mapping a photograph to a 3D representation y ∈ Y . A matched clean reference xc shows a centered, sharp, fully visible subject under uniform lighting, with G(xc) ≈ y. An observed image xo may be clean or degraded. Uneven illumination, blur, and occlusions can violate these assumptions. These degradations can distort geometry ( G(xo)̸ ≈ y). We learn to purify input images under supervision from matched clean references. The purifier maps xo toward the matched clean reference xc using reconstruction and spatial losses that encourage preservation of object identity, contours, and textural cues. 3.2 Synthetic Data Engine 

Learning Cθ requires paired images (xoi , x ci ) that share identical 3D geometry and viewpoints while differing only in capture quality, whereas learning Rϕ requires examples labeled by whether purification is necessary. Because real photographs provide neither matched clean counterparts nor controlled routing labels, we use high-fidelity 3D assets to construct this supervision (Figure 2A). 

Anchor view selection. From about 4k high-fidelity 3D assets in TexVerse (Zhang et al., 2025), we render eight views under uniform lighting. A vision-language model (VLM) ranks these views by geometric visibility and self-occlusion. The four most informative views per asset serve as multi-view anchors and matched clean references xci .

Degradation synthesis. For each anchor, instruction-guided editing produces a matched observation xoi with composite corruptions, including uneven illumination, blur, and occlusions. Each pair is audited by Qwen3-VL (Qwen Team, 2025) for structural consistency, and RMBG-2.0 extracts a soft foreground mask mi from the clean reference xci . This pipeline produces roughly 44 k image pairs Dtrain = {(xoi , x ci , m i)} for Cθ and 100 k routing pairs Droute = {(xj , γ j )} for Rϕ,where γj = 1 denotes P URIFY and γj = 0 denotes K EEP . Training and evaluation assets are disjoint. 

3.3 Degradation-Aware Router 

Purifying clean inputs can introduce unwanted changes. To guide purification of images with spatially varying degradations, we use features extracted from the input image. We therefore design the degradation-aware router Rϕ

based on Qwen2.5-VL (Bai et al., 2025). Trained on Droute , the router predicts whether purification is needed and extracts continuous degradation-aware features. 

Logit-based routing. Given input xo, the router returns (p, h ) = Rϕ(xo), where p ∈ [0 , 1] is the predicted probability that purification is needed and h denotes continuous degradation-aware features. Rather than using greedy decoding, we derive the purification probability p directly from the unnormalized logits for the routing decisions P URIFY and K EEP ,denoted by opurify and okeep .

p = σ(opurify − okeep ), (3) where σ(·) is the sigmoid function. We apply a threshold τ to obtain the routing indicator 

bγ = I[p ≥ τ ] ∈ { 0, 1}, (4) where I[·] is the indicator function. Predicted clean inputs bypass purification unchanged. 

Degradation-aware conditioning. When purification is triggered ( bγ = 1 ), we extract the final hidden states h ∈ RN ×d

from Rϕ under a fixed editing instruction. These features condition Cθ .

3.4 3D-Oriented One-Step Purifier

## 4 Experiments 

4.1 Experimental Setup 

Benchmark and evaluation protocols. Our benchmark contains 1,082 held-out DTC cases (Dong et al., 2025) with 3D ground truth and clean references sharing the degraded observations’ camera poses. We compare 3D outputs from degraded, edited, and clean-reference images against ground truth. Our purifier and all editing baselines process every degraded input. Routing is evaluated separately and does not affect these results. 

Baselines. We compare against 7 instruction-guided image editing methods, namely FLUX.1-Kontext (Black Forest Labs et al., 2025), FireRed-Image-Edit (1.1) (Qiao et al., 2026), Mage-Flow-Edit (Zhang et al., 2026), OmniGen2 (Wu et al., 2026a), Qwen-Image-Edit (Wu et al., 2025), Step1X-Edit (Liu et al., 2025), and GPT Image-2.5 (Flare) (OpenAI, 2026b). We evaluate downstream 3D quality with Pixal3D (Li et al., 2026) and TRELLIS.2 (Xiang et al., 2026). 

Evaluation metrics. For degradation-aware routing , we report P URIFY -class recall, overall accuracy, and H, defined as their harmonic mean, all expressed as percentages (Table 1). For 2D image fidelity , we report foreground LPIPS, PSNR, SSIM, Sobel gradient MAE, and foreground mask IoU against the clean reference. For 3D metrics , we report all-surface Mesh Distance (MD) (Xiang et al., 2026) and F all 1 (F1@10 −8), outer-surface Chamfer Distance (CD) (Fan et al., 2017) and F outer 1 (F1@10 −6) after ICP alignment to the ground-truth asset, PSNR-N (Wang et al., 2004) on rendered normal maps, and LPIPS (Zhang et al., 2018) on four-view shaded and albedo renderings.

4.3 2D Purification Results 

Table 2 reports fidelity to clean references. All seven generic editors lower PSNR and SSIM and raise gradient MAE by 

17 .8% –125 .9% relative to unedited inputs. Only FireRed (Qiao et al., 2026) lowers LPIPS, by 0.0004 . Degradation removal thus comes with reduced fidelity to clean references. OmniGen2 (Wu et al., 2026a) also reduces silhouette IoU. Gate3DGen alone improves all five metrics, lowering LPIPS by 39 .1% and gradient MAE by 16 .4% while raising IoU to 0.9758 . The gains over the FireRed backbone support the benefit of our purification training (Section 3.4). 

Inference efficiency. Gate3DGen achieves the best fidelity across all five metrics with an inference time of 2.3 s, compared with 15 .6–100 .8 s for the 6 baselines. This corresponds to a 6.8× speedup over Mage-Flow, the fastest timed baseline, and a 36 .2× speedup over FireRed. Our one-step purifier achieves higher image fidelity and lower inference time than the compared image editing methods.

## 5 Conclusion 

Trained on image pairs synthesized from 3D assets, Gate3DGen combines a degradation-aware router and a 3D-oriented one-step purifier before frozen 3D generators. On 1,082 cases, its purifier outperforms seven image editing methods in image fidelity and surface geometry for TRELLIS.2 and Pixal3D, closing up to 83 .1% of the mesh-distance gap to clean-reference inputs. VLM scores and human preferences on real photographs support identity preservation and degradation correction. Ablations and real-image comparisons motivate evaluating purification through downstream surface geometry, rendered appearance, and object fidelity beyond image similarity or visual quality alone.

## A Method Details 

Under a fixed prompt, the degradation-aware router is trained to output the P URIFY /K EEP decision described in Section 3.3. These decisions correspond to the implementation tokens Purify and Keep , respectively. It updates language-transformer layers 24–27 and the final language normalization. The language-model head and visual backbone are frozen except for rank-16 LoRA adapters in vision blocks 28–31. Training uses token-level supervision with higher weights on the decision tokens and the end-of-sequence token, and with higher sample weights on rare degradation families and on subtle degradations. The discrete routing indicator bγi follows Equation 4, with the threshold τ frozen on the development set. The language-only router variant omits the vision LoRA adapters. For the purifier Cθ, image-transformer blocks 1–28 remain frozen and blocks 29–49 are updated together with the cross-attention projection layers receiving h. Training uses the flow-matching endpoint at t = 1 in Equation 5, estimates 

bz0, and reconstructs bxc = D(bz0) by Equation 6. The subject mask mi is the 8-bit soft alpha predicted by RMBG-2.0 from xci . It is normalized to [0 , 1] and receives the same resize and random crop as the paired images. The degradation map ri averages |xoi − xci |/2 over RGB channels and applies 7 × 7 average pooling with stride 1 and padding 3. In Equation 7, the single-channel weight is broadcast over RGB channels, and each weighted sum is divided by its own broadcast weight sum, clamped below at 1. For the edge term, Du and Dv are one-pixel forward differences, with mui and mvi given by the products of the two subject-mask values adjacent to each horizontal or vertical edge, respectively. The loss weights in Equation 9 are λlat = 0 .1, λ1 = 1 , λlpips = 0 .5, λregion = 1 , and λedge = 1 . We train at 1024 × 1024 resolution for 15,000 updates on 32 GPUs (global batch size 32, BF16 precision, AdamW optimizer, cosine learning-rate schedule, peak learning rate 5 × 10 −7). At inference, image purification uses a single forward pass with guidance scale 1.0, an empty negative prompt, and a fixed random seed.
