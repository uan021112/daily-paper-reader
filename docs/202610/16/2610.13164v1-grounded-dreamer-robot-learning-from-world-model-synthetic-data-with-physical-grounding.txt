# ABSTRACT 

Scaling robot manipulation policies requires large amounts of diverse, action-labeled data, yet collecting such data in the real world remains expensive and difficult to scale. Existing methods either depend on human teleoperation, rely on simulation with limited visual realism, or use human videos that lack embodiment-specific annotations. In this paper, we introduce Grounded-Dreamer, a framework for synthesizing physically grounded robot manipulation data with video world models while avoiding human teleoperation demonstrations. Grounded-Dreamer combines the visual realism of generative video models with simulator physics engines. Starting from a small set of real images depicting robot task initial states, our method automatically reconstructs task-relevant objects and camera geometry, aligns them in simulation, and collects physically valid trajectories. These trajec-tories are used to post-train both a video world model and an inverse dynamics model, enabling the generation of visually realistic robot manipulation videos and corresponding robot actions from real images. To filter physically implausible rollouts, Grounded-Dreamer replays generated trajectories in the reconstructed sim-ulation and retains only those that successfully complete the tasks. The real-world robot policies are trained solely on the grounded synthetic trajectories without any human demonstrations. Empirical results in real-world tasks demonstrate that Grounded-Dreamer can synthesize physically grounded data and substantially facilitate policy learning without requiring human teleoperation effort.

# 1 INTRODUCTION 

Building general-purpose robot policies capable of performing diverse manipulation tasks across varied environments requires large-scale, high-quality data. However, robot data is difficult to scale, and existing sources present a challenging trade-off between quality and quantity. Real-world teleoperation provides high-fidelity demonstrations but is limited by intensive human labor. While UMI data collection (Chi et al., 2024; Xu et al., 2025) improves efficiency, there remains a need for manual operations. Conversely, simulation data offers inexpensive, action-labeled trajectories but is hindered by the sim-to-real visual gap. Human-centric videos are abundant and diverse, yet they lack the robot action annotations essential for direct policy training. Consequently, the field remains trapped between costly manual collection and the limitations of imperfect proxy data. This dilemma motivates the pursuit of scalable robot data synthesis. Prior efforts such as De-moGen (Xue et al., 2025) and MimicGen (Mandlekar et al., 2023) have attempted to augment existing datasets by diversifying robot demonstrations; however, these methods still rely on human-collected trajectories as initial seeds. Other approaches, including RoboGen (Wang et al., 2024b) and Gen-Sim (Wang et al., 2024a; Hua et al., 2025), leverage Large Language Models (LLMs) and simulators to automate task generation and trajectory collection. Generative video world models offer a potential alternative. Trained on internet-scale data, these models capture rich visual and world dynamics priors, enabling the generation of manipulation rollouts. When paired with an Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM), such videos can be converted into action-labeled trajectories. However, pretrained models like Cosmos (NVIDIA et al., 2025) are primarily trained on human-centric videos. Directly applying them to robotic tasks often results in "hallucinated" physics, such as inconsistent object motions or non-physical contacts. In this work, we introduce Grounded-Dreamer , a framework that synthesizes high-fidelity robot manipulation data without a single human teleoperation demonstration. Our core insight is to link the visual realism of generative world models with the grounding of physics simulators. We first build an automatic real-to-sim pipeline that calibrates camera poses, reconstructs task-relevant objects, and aligns them with the scene. Within this reconstructed simulator, we automatically collect diverse trajectories by leveraging the simulator’s ground-truth states. These grounded trajectories serve as the foundation for post-training both the video world model and the IDM. During data synthesis, the post-trained world model generates visually realistic manipulation videos from real-world initial images, while the IDM extracts the corresponding robot actions. To guarantee physical validity, we implement a closed-loop verification step: replaying the inferred trajectories within the reconstructed simulation. This process filters out physically inconsistent generations, ensuring that only successful, grounded trajectories are used for further policy training. We evaluate Grounded-Dreamer across seven diverse real-world manipulation tasks, spanning short-to-long horizon sequences and sophisticated tool use. Our empirical results demonstrate that policies trained exclusively on grounded synthetic data achieve performance competitive with those trained on real-world human demonstrations. Furthermore, owing to the inherent visual diversity and our targeted augmentation strategies, Grounded-Dreamer significantly outperforms real-data policies in out-of-distribution scenarios, showcasing robust zero-shot generalization across novel scenarios. Our contributions are summarized as follows: 2• We propose Grounded-Dreamer , a data synthesis framework that eliminates the need for human demonstrations by linking video world models with simulator physics engines. • We introduce an automated real-to-sim reconstruction and collection pipeline, enabling data synthesis that maintains both high visual fidelity and strict physical grounding. • We demonstrate through extensive real-world experiments that our grounded synthetic data effectively facilitates policy learning, achieving robust performance across diverse scenarios through purely autonomous data generation. 

# 2 RELATED WORK 

2.1 ROBOT DATA SYNTHESIS 

Simulation has been widely used to generate diverse conditions for robot data. Early sim-to-real work relied on visual or dynamics randomization (Tobin et al., 2017; Peng et al., 2018). Later environments extended this idea to broader robot-learning settings: RLBench standardizes manipulation tasks (James et al., 2020), robosuite and SAPIEN provide modular robot-object simulation platforms (Zhu et al., 2020; Xiang et al., 2020), and Isaac Gym and RoboCasa emphasize scalable physics or household manipulation (Makoviychuk et al., 2021; Nasiriany et al., 2024). Recent generative frameworks, such as GenSim, GenSim2, and RoboGen, use language or foundation models to generate tasks, scenes, demonstrations, or supervision (Wang et al., 2024a; Hua et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2024b). Their data quality, however, still depends on generated task programs, planners, solvers, or simulator assumptions. For manipulation, local grounding outweighs global visual realism. Incorrect object pose, axis, or camera frame can make a plausible trajectory physically misleading. 6D pose estimation and reconstruction recover task-relevant structure from real observations (Hodan et al., 2018; Wen et al., 2024), while structure-from-motion, NeRF, and 3D Gaussian Splatting facilitate view-consistent geometry or appearance (Schönberger & Frahm, 2016; Mildenhall et al., 2020; Kerbl et al., 2023). Another route reuses collected trajectories. A series of studies adapt human demonstrations to new object configurations (Mandlekar et al., 2023), corrective interventions (Hoque et al., 2024), long-horizon skills (Garrett et al., 2025), and bimanual dexterous manipulation (Jiang et al., 2025). DemoGen further adapts one demonstration and edits the 3D scene to synthesize new observations (Xue et al., 2025). DROID and Open X-Embodiment instead scale real robot data directly, but require substantial collection effort (Khazatsky et al., 2024; O’Neill et al., 2024). Grounded-Dreamer lies between these directions: it grounds synthetic data in real scene-object inputs, preserves physical structure through simulation, and expands the distribution with world-model rollouts. 2.2 WORLD MODELS FOR ROBOT LEARNING 

World models were originally developed as learned dynamics models for predicting future states before acting (Ha & Schmidhuber, 2018; Hafner et al., 2020; 2025). In robotics, visual foresight leveraged video prediction for model-based control by selecting actions whose predicted futures match a goal (Finn & Levine, 2017). More recently, diffusion-based video models have enabled realistic rollout generation from images or text (Ho et al., 2022), while systems such as UniSim and Genie move toward controllable visual worlds (Yang et al., 2024; Bruce et al., 2024). However, visually plausible videos are not directly usable robot training trajectories, as they often lack actions, embodiment constraints, and physically reliable interactions. DreamGen adapts video world models to robot learning by recovering pseudo actions through latent action or inverse dynamics models (Jang et al., 2025), while GigaBrain-0 further scales world-model-generated data for VLA training (GigaBrain Team, 2025). Prior works have also explored inverse dynamics models to bridge observations and actions (Agrawal et al., 2016; Pathak et al., 2018). Once converted into action-conditioned trajectories, such generated data can train policies including Diffusion Policy, RT-2, and 

π0 (Chi et al., 2025; Zitkovich et al., 2023; Black et al., 2024). Grounded-Dreamer builds on this line of work by coupling world-model rollouts with reconstructed scene inputs and simulation-based grounding to ensure both visual realism and physical consistency before policy learning. 3Policy Training Cosmos-FT

# 3 METHODOLOGY 

We introduce Grounded-Dreamer , an autonomous pipeline designed to generate verifiable, high-fidelity synthetic data for scalable robot policy learning without the need for human-collected real-world trajectories. As illustrated in Figure 2, starting from a small set of reference images captured in the real world, Grounded-Dreamer synthesizes massive, validated datasets through an automated workflow, which consists of four core stages: (i) Task-Oriented Simulation Reconstruction ,where we reconstruct physically consistent simulation scenes and task-relevant objects; (ii) Sim Data Collection and Interaction-Aware Masking , where simulation data is automatically collected to fine-tune Cosmos-Predict 2.5 and the IDM with interaction-aware masking; (iii) Initial Frame Synthesis ,where the Nano Banana-based pipeline aligns real-sim composites and generates augmented initial images to enhance diversity and visual fidelity; (iv) Data Synthesis and Closed-Loop Verification ,where synthetic trajectories are generated from real-world initial frames and validated via simulation verification. 3.1 TASK -O RIENTED SIMULATION RECONSTRUCTION 

Scene Reconstruction. Our framework is built upon the Lehome simulation environment (Li et al., 2026). Given the workspace boundaries and the relative pose of the robot base, we construct a lightweight simulation scene containing the robotic arm and workspace table. To align the simulated and real-world viewpoints, we perform extrinsic calibration using the FEEPE framework (Wu et al., 2025) and directly adopt the intrinsic parameters of the physical camera. This process ensures consis-tent fields of view (FOVs) between simulation and reality, providing a reliable geometric foundation for data grounding. For efficiency, we reuse off-the-shelf Lehome assets for tabletop textures and environmental backgrounds, since pixel-level fidelity in task-irrelevant regions is unnecessary for our pipeline. 

Task-Oriented Object Reconstruction. For rigid objects , we employ SAM3D to reconstruct 3D geometry from the initial observation frame and rescale the resulting meshes to match real-world dimensions (SAM 3D Team et al., 2025). For articulated objects , we first use SAM3D to decompose the object into individual components (SAM 3D Team et al., 2025). Our autonomous agent then 4takes these components, together with predefined connectivity rules and joint specifications, and automatically assembles the articulated kinematic structure within the simulator. 3.2 SIM DATA COLLECTION AND INTERACTION -A WARE MASKING 

With the task-aligned simulation environment established, we develop an automated data collection pipeline for trajectory generation necessary for world model post-training. Instead of manually scripting each task, we employ an LLM-based agent to synthesize and execute data collection scripts from three inputs: (i) Reference Code Templates defining trajectory generation logic, including modular execution stages and inverse kinematics (IK)-based end-effector control; (ii) 

High-level Task Descriptions specifying task objectives and object semantics; and (iii) Object Metadata automatically retrieved from Isaac Sim (NVIDIA, 2023), including object poses and physical properties. Using this framework, we collect 100-150 trajectories for each task, with the volume adjusted according to task complexity and object count. To reduce the sim-to-real domain gap, we propose an Interaction-Aware Masking strategy that preserves only the robotic arm and interacting objects in simulation frames (Figure 3 and 8). This preprocessing encourages Cosmos-Predict 2.5 (NVIDIA et al., 2025) and IDM (Jang et al., 2025; Baker et al., 2022) to focus on modeling interaction dynamics during fine-tuning. As a result, when conditioned on real-world initial frames, the fine-tuned Cosmos model generates realistic robot-object interactions while maintaining stable task-irrelevant backgrounds through its generative priors. Conditioned on the augmented initial frames, the fine-tuned Cosmos-Predict 2.5 model generates rollout videos (Figure 3 & 9). We apply Interaction-Aware Masking using SAM (Kirillov et al., 2023) and employ the IDM to extract action trajectories, forming paired synthetic training data. However, generative world models may produce physical hallucinations , including (1) Morphologi-cal Hallucinations , such as invalid robot kinematics or physically infeasible joint configurations; (2) 

Spatial-Depth Ambiguities , where apparent 2D contacts do not reflect valid 3D interactions. To address these issues, we introduce a Closed-Loop Verification mechanism. Using the recon-structed simulation environment and initialized object poses (Sec. 3.3), we replay synthesized action trajectories in the physics simulator and classify them as Optimal or Sub-optimal according to task success criteria. Figure 4 illustrates this process. This verification stage filters out physically in-consistent or task-failing trajectories, ensuring high-quality data for downstream policy learning. # 4 EXPERIMENTS 

4.1 EXPERIMENTAL SETUP 

Manipulation Tasks. We evaluate Grounded-Dreamer across seven diverse real-world tasks: Pick ,

Close Drawer , Press Button , Pick and Place , Put in Drawer , Sweep Trash , and Slice Sausage . This suite covers a broad spectrum of requirements, ranging from short-horizon object interactions to sophisticated tool usage and handling of deformable objects. Crucially, our evaluation incorporates both in-distribution benchmarks and challenging Out-of-Distribution scenarios, providing a rigorous assessment of the framework’s zero-shot generalization across novel environments and unseen object configurations. 

Hardware Setup. The physical experimental platform centers on a Franka Emika robot arm paired with a Panda gripper. Visual observations are captured by an Intel RealSense D405 depth camera. For real-time inference and control, the trained policies are deployed on a workstation equipped with an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090 GPU. Figure 5 illustrates the comprehensive hardware configuration alongside representative real-world execution trajectories. 

Baselines and Metrics. We employ Diffusion Policy (DP) (Chi et al., 2025) and π0.5 (Physical Intelligence et al., 2025) as representative policy backbones. To provide a comprehensive evaluation of Grounded-Dreamer, we compare our approach against several data regimes and alternative method-ologies: (i) Sim-only : Policies trained exclusively on raw simulation data, representing a performance lower bound; (ii) Real-only : Policies trained on a full set of 50 human-labeled real-world trajectories per task; (iii) Low-data Real : A regime where only 5 real-world samples are available, representing extreme data scarcity; (iv) RIGVid (Patel et al., 2025): A baseline that utilizes a video world model to predict object-centric hand-object interactions, from which end-effector poses are extracted and mapped to robot actions. The primary evaluation metric is the Success Rate . We perform 30 trials per task for scaling law analysis and 20 trials for all other comparative evaluations. 74.2 MAIN RESULTS AND COMPARATIVE ANALYSIS 

We evaluate the performance of DP and π0.5 models trained with different data regimes (50 trajectories per task). As summarized in Table 1, the Sim-only baseline performs poorly on real-world tasks, failing to complete most interactions due to significant visual and physical domain gaps. In contrast, the Real-only baseline maintains a high success rate. Notably, policies trained exclusively on our 

verified GD data achieve competitive performance relative to the Real-only baseline. Specifically, for the Pressing Button task, the π0.5 policy reaches parity with the performance of its real-world counterpart. This demonstrates the immense potential of world model data, particularly for labor-intensive tasks like Sweeping Trash and Slicing Sausage , where synthetic data can be generated autonomously from few frames at near-zero cost. We also compare our framework with RIGVid, which utilizes world models through an alternative paradigm. RIGVid performs significantly below expectations, exhibiting a substantial performance gap compared to both the Real-only and our verified GD-data regimes. This performance degradation is primarily attributed to inaccuracies in hand-pose extraction and susceptibility to viewpoint jitter. Furthermore, we investigate Co-training using a minimal set of real demonstrations combined with 50 GD trajectories. While Low-data Real models fail to generalize across backbones—yielding only 8.6% for DP and 14.3% for π0.5—adding GD data leads to a substantial performance leap. This regime not only outperforms the GD-data-only model but also matches or exceeds the 50-sample 

Real-only baseline. Here, the few real trajectories act as domain anchors , aligning the synthetic distribution with the real domain and further unlocking the world model’s latent potential. 4.3 GENERALIZATION AND ROBUSTNESS TO ENVIRONMENT VARIATION 

To enhance environmental diversity, we utilize the Nano Banana 2 model (Google DeepMind, 2026) to augment real-world initial frames with varying backgrounds, tablecloth textures, and lighting conditions before generating 50 GD trajectories. We then evaluate the DP and π0.5 models in Out-of-Distribution (OOD) scenarios. As shown in Table 2, models trained with GD data exhibit superior generalization capabilities, maintaining robust performance in novel environments. Conversely, the 

Real-only baseline, constrained by the limited diversity of its training set, fails to generalize; its average success rates drop to only 7.1% and 16.4% for DP and π0.5, respectively. Crucially, this environmental variety is achieved autonomously through image-to-image augmentation of initial frames, requiring no additional physical intervention. Attaining a comparable level of diversity through manual real-world collection would necessitate exhaustive and repetitive re-collection cycles, incurring prohibitive overhead in terms of human labor and physical resources.

While Grounded-Dreamer substantially automates data generation, it currently requires a minimal, one-time collection of reference images—typically one scene overview and 1–4 object perspectives— to initialize the environment. Given that image-conditioned editing models are still rapidly evolving, the success rate for our synthesized initial frames is approximately 65%. However, we view this as a robust baseline that will naturally improve as foundational generative models mature. While our experiments establish the framework’s effectiveness with hundreds of trajectories per task, the scale was primarily constrained by available computational resources rather than algorithmic bottlenecks. The inherent design of our pipeline is highly scalable; as hardware constraints are alleviated, we anticipate extending this paradigm to massive-scale data generation to further unlock the scaling potential of generalist robot policies. 

# B BROADER IMPACTS 

Our work introduces a scalable pipeline for generating high-fidelity, verified synthetic data, which has significant implications for the future of Embodied AI. By reducing the reliance on costly and labor-intensive real-world data collection, our approach facilitates the scaling of robotic learning across diverse tasks and environments. While this may accelerate the deployment of autonomous systems, it also underscores the importance of addressing potential biases in world models and ensuring the safety of policies trained on synthetic data. We believe this research promotes more accessible and efficient robot learning, though we encourage practitioners to maintain rigorous safety protocols when deploying such models in real-world human-centric environments.

# E EXPERIMENTAL DETAILS 

In this section, we provide comprehensive details regarding the training and inference pipelines of our framework to ensure reproducibility. E.1 FINE -T UNING DETAILS OF COSMOS 

We fine-tune the 2B-parameter Cosmos Predict2.5 Video2World model (NVIDIA et al., 2025) as the generative backbone. The model is optimized using a learning rate of 4.315 × 10 −5 on a cluster of eight NVIDIA A800 GPUs with context parallelism. Training is conducted for 1,400 to 2,000 iterations with a global batch size of 32. We use a temporal length of 125 frames for the Put in Drawer task and 93 frames for all other tasks. The fine-tuning process requires 74 GB of VRAM per GPU. E.2 TRAINING DETAILS OF THE INVERSE DYNAMICS MODEL (IDM) The IDM (Jang et al., 2025) features a SigLIP2-Large encoder and a flow-matching action head. It is trained across the eight A800 GPUs for 5,000 to 10,000 steps with a global batch size of 512 and a 

10 −4 learning rate. Training is performed in BF16 mixed precision to ensure numerical stability. E.3 SYNTHETIC DATA GENERATION AND ACTION EXTRACTION 

All inference and data generation tasks are also executed on the eight NVIDIA A800 GPU clus-ter. Video Generation via Cosmos. We generate rollout sequences conditioned on five replicated initial frames. For the Put in Drawer task, the model produces 125-frame sequences, while 93-frame sequences are generated for other tasks. The inference memory footprint is approximately 16 GB per GPU. Action Extraction via IDM. Robot actions are extracted from the generated sequences using the IDM, which demonstrates high throughput (0.5 seconds per trajectory). This process is memory-efficient, requiring only 4 GB of VRAM per GPU. The average task success rate during this generation phase is approximately 45%. 17
