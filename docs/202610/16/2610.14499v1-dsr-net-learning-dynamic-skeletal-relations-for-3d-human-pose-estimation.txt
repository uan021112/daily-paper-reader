A B S T R A C T
3D human pose estimation requires accurate modeling of motion-dependent skeletal structures
and inter-joint dependencies. Existing methods typically rely on fixed skeletal topologies in Graph
Convolutional Networks (GCNs) or implicitly capture joint relations through attention mechanisms,
limiting their adaptability to diverse motion patterns.
Toaddress this issue, we propose DSR-Net, a dynamic structural representation learning framework
for 3D human pose estimation. Specifically, a Dynamic Skeletal Relation (DSR) module is introduced
to learn adaptive inter-joint dependencies beyond predefined skeletal connections. Based on the
learned relations, a hierarchical relation-aware fusion strategy is developed to facilitate effective
interaction between structural and temporal representations. In addition, a lightweight Residual
Skeletal Recalibration Module (RSRM) is designed to enhance the stability of structure-aware features
with minimal computational overhead.
Extensive experiments on Human3.6M and MPI-INF-3DHP demonstrate that DSR-Net achieves
MPJPEs of 37.9 mm and 14.9 mm, respectively, consistently improving over strong baselines. The
results validate the effectiveness of dynamic skeletal relation modeling for robust 3D human pose
estimation.
1. Introduction
The goal of 3D human pose estimation is to recover
the spatial positions and motion trajectories of human joints
from temporal observations. Beyond accurate joint coordi-
nate regression, the task also requires effective modeling
of motion-dependent inter-joint relations within the skeletal
structure. Since these relations vary with pose configurations
and dynamic motion states, learning adaptive skeletal
dependencies is critical for accurate 3D pose estimation.
Recent advances in deep representation learning have
significantly improved the capability of learning complex
spatiotemporal representations for human motion analysis
and 3D pose estimation [12]. However, most existing
methods still represent skeletal dependencies using fixed
topologies or implicitly learned relations, limiting their
ability to capture motion-dependent structural variations.
Recently, Transformer-based architectures [25] have
been widely adopted for 3D human pose estimation due to
their strong global modeling capability. By leveraging self-
attention mechanisms, Transformers can effectively capture
long-range temporal dependencies and cross-joint relations.
However, the learned dependencies are often implicitly
encoded within attention weights, making it difficult to
explicitly characterize motion-dependent structural relations
and local skeletal variations. In contrast, graph convolutional
network (GCN)-based methods explicitly model skeletal
topology and are capable of capturing local inter-joint
relations, offering advantages in incorporating structural
priors. Nevertheless, conventional GCNs typically rely on
fixed or weakly adaptive adjacency matrices, making these
methods insufficient for learning dynamic skeletal relations
that evolve with motion.

To combine the global modeling capability of Trans-
formers and the structural modeling advantages of GCNs,
recent studies have explored hybrid Transformer–GCN
architectures that integrate temporal modeling with spatial

GCN methods have achieved improved performance, most
still rely on static or weakly adaptive structural priors,
limiting their ability to model motion-dependent skeletal
relations and adaptive cross-stream interactions under di-

skeletal structures are not explicitly represented, which
limits the ability of existing methods to adapt to diverse
motion patterns. As a consequence, feature interaction across
different modeling branches may become insufficiently
adaptive, while additional structural modeling modules may
also introduce redundant representations and affect training
stability.
In this paper, we propose DSR-Net, a hybrid Graph-
Transformer framework for 3D human pose estimation
centered on Dynamic Skeletal Relation (DSR) model-
ing. The core idea of DSR-Net is to dynamically learn
motion-dependent joint dependencies beyond fixed skeletal
topology, enabling adaptive structural representations that
explicitly characterize skeletal relation variations under
different motion states.
To effectively exploit the dynamically learned skeletal
relations, we further design a hierarchical relation-aware
fusion strategy with adaptive gated interaction and Dynamic
Channel Fusion (DCF) for enhanced cross-stream feature
interaction and spatiotemporal representation learning. In
addition, a lightweight Residual Skeletal Recalibration
Module (RSRM) is introduced to stabilize and refine dynam-
ically learned structural representations while maintaining
computational efficiency.

2. Related Work
2.1. 2D-to-3D Pose Lifting
Monocular 2D-to-3D pose lifting has become a dominant
research direction in 3D human pose estimation due to its
low deployment cost and broad applicability. Unlike direct
regression methods from RGB images, 2D-to-3D lifting
methods first estimate 2D joint coordinates and then map
them into 3D space, effectively decoupling visual perception
from structural representation learning. Martinez et al. [18]
demonstrated the effectiveness of this paradigm, which has
since become a widely adopted baseline framework.
Early lifting methods mainly relied on temporal con-
volution networks to model short-term motion continuity
[22]. More recently, Transformer-based methods have shown
strong capability in capturing long-range temporal depen-
dencies through self-attention mechanisms, while Graph
Convolutional Networks (GCNs) explicitly model skeletal
topology and inter-joint structural relations. Based on
these advantages, recent studies increasingly adopt hybrid
Transformer-GCN frameworks for joint spatiotemporal rep-
resentation learning.
2.2. Spatiotemporal Structural Modeling
Recent 2D-to-3D pose lifting methods mainly improve
pose representation learning through Transformer-based
temporal modeling and GCN-based structural modeling
strategies. Transformer architectures exhibit strong capabil-
ity in capturing long-range temporal dependencies through
self-attention mechanisms, while GCNs explicitly model
skeletal topology and inter-joint structural relations.
Transformer-based methods such as MHFormer [11],
MixSTE [29], MotionBERT [35], DSTFormer [30], Pose-
FormerV2 [33], and STCFormer [24] improve long-range
temporal dependency modeling through attention mech-
anisms, frequency representation learning, or structured
temporal modeling strategies.
Recent studies further explore state-space modeling,
adaptive pose encoding, and motion priors to enhance
temporal representation capability [7, 23, 31].
GCN-based methods explicitly model skeletal structures
using graph representations of human joints and bones
[9]. Representative approaches improve pose estimation
through semantic graph learning, adaptive topology model-
ing, global-local structural interaction, and graph optimiza-
tion strategies [1, 32, 27, 15, 28, 36, 13, 2].
Although Transformer-based methods achieve strong
temporal modeling performance and GCN-based meth-
ods provide explicit structural constraints, existing ap-
proaches still exhibit limitations in jointly modeling motion-
dependent skeletal relations and adaptive spatiotemporal
interactions. Transformer architectures often model joint
relations implicitly, while many GCN-based methods rely
on static or globally shared graph topologies, limiting their
flexibility under complex motion scenarios.
These limitations have motivated the development of hy-
brid Transformer-GCN frameworks for more comprehensive
spatiotemporal representation learning in 3D human pose
estimation.
2.3. Hybrid Spatiotemporal Modeling
Recent hybrid Transformer-GCN frameworks attempt to
jointly model temporal dynamics and skeletal structures
for 3D human pose estimation. Existing studies mainly
improve hybrid representation learning through architectural
integration and cross-branch feature fusion strategies.
Some methods explicitly incorporate graph structures
into Transformer architectures to enhance structural mod-
eling capability [14]. Other approaches perform adaptive
feature fusion between Transformer and GCN branches
to improve spatiotemporal representation learning [19, 16,
34, 3]. In addition, diffusion-based methods have also
demonstrated promising performance in robust 3D pose
estimation [4].
Despite these advances, most existing hybrid frameworks
still rely on static skeletal priors or relatively fixed fusion
strategies, limiting their ability to capture motion-dependent
joint relations and adaptive cross-stream interactions under
complex motion scenarios.
To address these limitations, this work proposes a
dynamic skeletal relation learning framework with adaptive
feature fusion for more flexible spatiotemporal representa-
tion learning in 3D human pose estimation.
3. Method
3.1. Overall Architecture
The proposed DSR-Net is a dynamic skeletal relation
learning framework for 3D human pose estimation. Built
upon the dual-stream architecture of MixTGFormer [3],
DSR-Net introduces Dynamic Skeletal Relations (DSR)
modeling, Residual Skeletal Recalibration (RSRM), and hi-
erarchical relation-aware fusion to enable adaptive structural
representation learning under diverse motion patterns.

Unlike conventional methods that rely on fixed skeletal

As illustrated in Fig. 1, DSR-Net maps a sequence of 2D

a dual-stream DSR architecture, consisting of a Spatial-

capture complementary spatiotemporal dependencies from
different modeling perspectives. Each stage is composed of
stacked DSR Hybrid Blocks, where spatial and temporal
representations are progressively refined through adaptive
relation modeling and cross-stream interaction.
To instantiate the proposed Dynamic Skeletal Relations
(DSR) modeling framework, a graph-based dynamic relation
reasoning module (GCN-DSR) is introduced to explicitly
construct motion-dependent skeletal relations as adaptive
structural representations. Specifically, GCN-DSR dynam-
ically learns motion-aware inter-joint dependencies beyond
predefined skeletal topology, enabling adaptive structural
modeling under diverse motion patterns.
Meanwhile, a Residual Skeletal Recalibration Module
(RSRM) is incorporated to stabilize dynamically learned
structural representations through lightweight structure-
guided feature recalibration.
Furthermore, a hierarchical relation-aware fusion strat-
egy is designed to progressively integrate complemen-
tary spatiotemporal information. Specifically, an intra-block
gated fusion mechanism is employed to adaptively combine
attention-based global representations with graph-based
local structural features, while a Dynamic Channel Fusion
(DCF) mechanism is employed within each DSR Hybrid
Block for dynamic cross-stream interaction. Through the
hierarchical coordination of local structural fusion and cross-
stream feature interaction, the proposed framework achieves
more robust and consistent spatiotemporal representations.
Finally, the regression head outputs the estimated 3D joint
coordinates.
Hybrid spatiotemporal branches. The model consists
of two parallel computational paths: one follows a “spatial-
to-temporal” modeling order, while the other follows a
“temporal-to-spatial” order. This dual-stream design enables
the model to capture complementary spatiotemporal depen-
dencies under different modeling sequences.
In the spatial modeling stage, input features are pro-
cessed in parallel by Spatial Multi-Head Self-Attention (S-
MHSA) and the DSR-guided graph reasoning branch (GCN-
DSR). S-MHSA captures long-range global dependencies
among joints within a single frame, while GCN-DSR
dynamically learns adaptive inter-joint relations based on
motion-dependent feature interactions. This enables flexible
structural modeling beyond predefined skeletal connectivity.

In the temporal modeling stage, Temporal Multi-Head
Self-Attention (T-MHSA) and GCN-DSR are similarly
combined to jointly model cross-frame motion from both
global temporal and dynamic structural perspectives. T-
MHSA focuses on capturing long-range temporal patterns,
whereas GCN-DSR models the dynamic evolution of
motion-dependent relations over time.
Notably, GCN-DSR adopts a unified relation modeling
mechanism across both spatial and temporal dimensions,
enabling consistent learning of adaptive dependencies under
varying motion states. This decouples dynamic relation
construction from feature propagation, allowing adaptive
dependency learning and structural feature aggregation to
be optimized separately.
Residual Skeletal Recalibration (RSRM). To stabilize
dynamically learned structural representations during cross-
stream interaction, a Residual Skeletal Recalibration Mod-
ule (RSRM) is introduced between spatial and temporal
modeling stages. RSRM performs lightweight structure-
guided feature recalibration in a residual manner, adaptively
modulating joint-aware feature responses during feature
propagation. This mechanism helps suppress unstable re-
sponses caused by noisy 2D keypoint observations and
improves the consistency of motion-dependent structural
representations across different modeling streams.
Hierarchical Relation-aware Fusion. To better ex-
ploit dynamically learned skeletal relations, a hierarchical
relation-aware fusion strategy is designed to perform adap-
tive integration at both intra-block and cross-stream levels.
Intra-block Gated Fusion. Within each DSR Hybrid
Block, a learnable scalar gating parameter is introduced to
adaptively weight the outputs of Multi-Head Self-Attention
(MHSA) and the GCN-DSR branch. This establishes a
balance between global semantic modeling and dynamic
structural relation modeling. The mechanism enables the
model to automatically adjust the contribution of each
modeling branch according to motion-dependent feature
characteristics.
Cross-stream Dynamic Channel Fusion (DCF). Within
each dual-stream DSR Hybrid Block, a Dynamic Channel
Fusion (DCF) mechanism is employed to adaptively inte-
grate features from different modeling paths. Specifically,
DCF first applies global average pooling to extract channel-
wise statistics from both branches and concatenates them
to form a cross-stream semantic representation. A shared
multilayer perceptron is then used to model the fused features
and generate channel-wise weights for both branches.
Unlike traditional attention mechanisms that operate on
individual branches, the channel weights in DCF are jointly
determined by features from both branches, enabling cross-
stream collaborative modulation. In addition, a Sigmoid
activation function is applied to produce non-competitive
channel weights, allowing the model to simultaneously pre-
serve complementary information from multiple branches
across different channels.
Finally, channel-wise reweighting is applied to fuse the
features from both branches, achieving fine-grained dynamic
feature integration. This mechanism effectively alleviates
feature distribution discrepancies caused by different mod-
eling orders and improves the consistency of dynamically
learned structural representations across different modeling
streams.
Loss Function and Output Prediction. The model
is trained in an end-to-end manner. The final feature
representation, denoted as 𝐹𝑁, is projected into the 3D pose
space through a fully connected layer followed by a 𝑇𝑎𝑛ℎ
activation function. The loss function consists of a position
loss (𝐿𝑝𝑜𝑠) and a velocity loss (𝐿𝑣𝑒𝑙), which jointly optimize
joint coordinate accuracy and temporal smoothness. This
ensures that the predicted 3D poses are both kinematically
plausible and temporally consistent.
3.2. Dynamic Skeletal Relation Modeling
In 3D human pose estimation, the dependencies among
human joints are highly motion-dependent and exhibit
diverse structural patterns under different action states. How-
ever, traditional Graph Convolutional Network (GCN)-based
methods typically rely on predefined skeletal topologies or
static adjacency matrices, which are insufficient for modeling
dynamically evolving spatial and temporal dependencies.
Such fixed graph structures limit the representation capabil-
ity of the network when handling complex human motions.
To address this issue, we propose a Dynamic Skele-
tal Relation Graph Convolution module (GCN-DSR) for
adaptive modeling of motion-dependent joint relationships.
The proposed module constructs dynamic relation graphs
in a data-driven manner and performs graph-based feature
propagation accordingly, enabling adaptive structural repre-
sentation learning beyond fixed skeletal topology.

3.3. Skeletal Feature Recalibration
In 3D human pose estimation, the human skeleton
follows explicit topological constraints. Although dynamic
skeletal relation modeling enables adaptive structural repre-
sentation learning, feature distributions produced by differ-
ent modeling streams may still exhibit inconsistency during
branch interaction.
In the dual-branch structure (S-T / T-S), the learned fea-
ture distributions from different branches are not identical.
Direct feature fusion may introduce structural distortion.
Therefore, introducing a structural recalibration mechanism
before branch interaction is important to align features and
improve the stability and effectiveness of subsequent fusion.
Based on this analysis, we introduce a Residual Skeletal
Recalibration Module (RSRM) at the branch interaction
stage. This module enhances structural consistency before
feature fusion, thereby improving the geometric plausibility
and semantic representation of the human skeleton. The
overall structure of this module is shown in Fig. 3.
Themoduleisdesignedtobestructure-aware,lightweight,
and stable through residual learning, enabling effective
feature recalibration without introducing significant com-
putational overhead.

3.4. Hierarchical Relation-aware Fusion
Although the proposed DSR framework enables dynamic
skeletal relation learning, the resulting features from dif-
ferent modeling branches exhibit heterogeneous semantic
characteristics. Direct fusion through fixed weighting or
simple summation may therefore lead to feature redundancy
and suboptimal utilization of complementary information.
To address this issue, we propose a hierarchical relation-
aware fusion strategy that performs progressive and adaptive
integration of multi-source features at two levels: intra-
block complementary modeling and cross-stream adaptive
reweighting.
Intra-block Fusion. Within each Spatial/Temporal DSR
Block, a dual-branch parallel structure is adopted, consisting
of a Multi-Head Self-Attention (MHSA) branch and a
graph-based dynamic relation modeling branch (GCN-
DSR). The MHSA branch models long-range dependencies
among joints through a global attention mechanism, enabling
the capture of global contextual information. In contrast,
the GCN-DSR branch explicitly models motion-dependent
skeletal relations through dynamic graph construction,
enabling adaptive structural representation beyond fixed
skeletal connectivity.

and 𝐶denote the batch size, number of frames, number of
joints, and channel dimension, respectively. The input is first
normalized and then fed into the two branches separately:

𝐗𝑔= GCN-DSR(LN(𝐗))
To enable adaptive fusion of the two types of features, a
learnable scalar parameter 𝜆is introduced and constrained
by a Sigmoid function:
𝛼= 𝜎(𝜆),
𝛼∈(0, 1)
The fused feature representation within the block is then
given by:
𝐗𝑖𝑏= 𝛼⋅𝐗𝑎+ (1 −𝛼) ⋅𝐗𝑔
Here, 𝛼controls the relative contribution of the MHSA
branch and the GCN-DSR branch during feature fusion. The
fused features are further passed through a feed-forward
network (MLP) for nonlinear transformation, combined
with residual connections and a LayerScale mechanism to
enhance training stability. Through this gating mechanism,
the model can dynamically adjust the contribution of the
MHSA and GCN-DSR branches according to different
inputs, thereby fully exploiting the complementary strengths
of the two modeling approaches.
Cross-stream Fusion. At a higher level, the model
adopts a parallel dual-stream structure (e.g., ST and TS
pathways) to model spatiotemporal features from different
perspectives. Although collaborative modeling is performed
within each block, discrepancies in semantic representation
and feature distribution still exist across streams. Direct
averaging-based fusion cannot fully exploit the advantages
of the dual-stream design.
To address this, we propose a Dynamic Channel Fu-
sion (DCF) module that performs channel-wise adaptive
reweighting of features from different streams. Let the output
features of the Spatial-First and Temporal-First streams be
denoted as
𝐗𝑆𝑇, 𝐗𝑇𝑆∈ℝ𝐵×𝑇×𝐽×𝐶.
First, global average pooling is applied along the temporal
and joint dimensions to extract channel-wise statistical
representations:
𝑧𝑆𝑇= GAP(𝐗𝑆𝑇),
𝑧𝑇𝑆= GAP(𝐗𝑇𝑆),
where 𝑧𝑆𝑇, 𝑧𝑇𝑆∈ℝ𝐵×𝐶.
The two representations are then concatenated to obtain
a joint channel descriptor:
𝑧= [𝑧𝑆𝑇, 𝑧𝑇𝑆] ∈ℝ𝐵×2𝐶.
This joint descriptor captures complementary channel statis-
tics from the two streams for subsequent adaptive reweight-
ing. This feature vector is passed through a lightweight MLP
to generate channel-wise weights:
𝑤= 𝜙(𝑧) ∈ℝ𝐵×2𝐶,
where 𝜙(⋅) denotes a nonlinear mapping function composed
of two fully-connected layers.
The output is further split into two groups of weights:
𝑤𝑆𝑇, 𝑤𝑇𝑆∈ℝ𝐵×𝐶,
and normalized using a Sigmoid function:̂
𝑤𝑆𝑇= 𝜎(𝑤𝑆𝑇),̂
𝑤𝑇𝑆= 𝜎(𝑤𝑇𝑆).
Finally, the weights are expanded to the original feature
dimensions and applied for channel-wise weighted fusion:
𝐗𝑐𝑠=̂ 𝑤𝑆𝑇⊙𝐗𝑆𝑇+̂ 𝑤𝑇𝑆⊙𝐗𝑇𝑆
where ⊙denotes channel-wise multiplication. This op-
eration enables channel-wise adaptive propagation of com-
plementary semantic information from different streams.

Unlike conventional fusion strategies that employ static
weighting or direct feature aggregation, the proposed DCF
module jointly determines channel-wise importance from
both streams, enabling adaptive cross-stream semantic

This hierarchical fusion strategy enables adaptive inte-
gration of multi-source features at different levels. At the
intra-block level, the gating mechanism facilitates dynamic
collaboration between MHSA and GCN-DSR, allowing

4. Experiments
4.1. Datasets and Metrics
We evaluate the proposed DSR-Net on two widely used
benchmarks, Human3.6M [8] and MPI-INF-3DHP [20], to
assess both in-domain performance and cross-dataset gener-
alization. In addition, qualitative visualization experiments
are conducted on the 3DPW [17] dataset to further evaluate
the robustness and generalization capability of the proposed
method in challenging in-the-wild scenarios.
Human3.6M is a widely used benchmark dataset for
3D human pose estimation, consisting of large-scale indoor
motion capture data with diverse actions and subjects.
Following the standard Protocol #1, subjects S1, S5, S6,
S7, and S8 are used for training, while S9 and S11 are used
for testing. Ground-truth 2D keypoints are used as input, and
the human body is represented by 17 joints.
On Human3.6M, we report Mean Per Joint Position Error

To further evaluate the qualitative performance of DSR-

setting for qualitative analysis. Since the focus of this ex-
periment is qualitative visualization rather than quantitative
benchmarking, no additional evaluation metrics are reported
for 3DPW.
4.2. Implementation Details
DSR-Net is built upon MixTGFormer [3], which serves
as the baseline architecture in this work. The proposed model
retains the original dual-stream Transformer framework
while introducing the Dynamic Skeletal Relation Graph
Convolution module (GCN-DSR), the Residual Skeletal Re-
calibration Module (RSRM), and the hierarchical relation-
aware fusion strategy. Except for the explicitly introduced
modules, all network settings remain identical to the baseline
configuration to ensure fair comparison.
All experiments are implemented using the PyTorch deep
learning framework and conducted on a single NVIDIA
GeForce RTX 4090 GPU. The model is trained using the
AdamW optimizer with an initial learning rate of 5 × 10−4
and a weight decay of 0.01. An exponential decay schedule
with a decay factor of 0.99 is adopted during training. Due
to the long temporal sequence length (243 frames) and the
additional computational overhead introduced by dynamic
graph modeling, the batch size is set to 4 to satisfy GPU
memory constraints. All models are trained for 60 epochs
without early stopping, and the random seed is fixed to 42 to
ensure reproducibility.
During preprocessing, the input 2D keypoint sequences
are normalized with respect to the root joint (pelvis), and
the coordinates are scaled to a unified range for improved
numerical stability. Horizontal flipping is adopted as the
primary data augmentation strategy during both training
and testing. The preprocessing and evaluation protocols
generally follow standard settings commonly adopted in
previous 3D human pose estimation studies [5, 32, 36, 26].
For temporal modeling, input sequences with a length of
243 frames are used. During training, long motion sequences
are segmented using a sliding-window strategy, while the
inference protocol remains consistent with the baseline
model. MPJPE is adopted as the primary evaluation metric
and optimization objective throughout all experiments.
On the Human3.6M dataset, we follow the standard
evaluation protocol by using both the ground-truth 2D
annotations and the 2D keypoints generated by the Stacked
Hourglass detector [21] as input. On the MPI-INF-3DHP
dataset, the ground-truth 2D keypoints provided by the
dataset are directly used without relying on external 2D pose
detectors.
Unless otherwise specified, all ablation studies are
conducted based on the reproduced MixTGFormer baseline
under identical training and evaluation settings. For con-
trolled analysis, only one target component is modified at a
time while all remaining settings are kept unchanged.

4.5. Ablation Studies
To systematically evaluate the effectiveness of each
proposed component, we conduct a series of ablation studies
on the Human3.6M dataset under identical training and
evaluation settings.
4.5.1. Progressive Integration of Modules
We first evaluate the cumulative contribution of each pro-
posed component through progressive integration based on
the reproduced MixTGFormer baseline. Specifically, the Dy-
namic Skeletal Relation Graph Convolution module (GCN-
DSR), the Residual Skeletal Recalibration Module (RSRM),
and the Hierarchical Relation-aware Fusion strategy (HRF)
are sequentially integrated into the baseline framework. The
corresponding results are reported in Table 4, while Fig. 5
provides a visual comparison of the progressive performance
improvements achieved by different components.
As shown in Table 4, integrating GCN-DSR reduces the
MPJPE from 39.16 mm to 38.54 mm, demonstrating the
effectiveness of dynamic relation modeling for capturing
inter-joint dependencies. Further incorporating RSRM de-
creases the MPJPE to 38.24 mm, indicating that structure-
aware feature recalibration improves feature representation
stability. After additionally introducing HRF, the model
achieves the best performance of 37.90 mm, yielding an
overall improvement of 1.26 mm over the baseline.
Overall, the progressive integration results indicate
that the proposed components contribute complementary
improvements to pose estimation performance.
Although the above experiments validate the overall
effectiveness of the proposed modules, the progressive
integration strategy may introduce interdependencies among
different components. To further isolate and analyze the
contribution of GCN-DSR, we conduct a dedicated ablation
study, with the corresponding results presented as follows.
4.5.2. Analysis of GCN-DSR
To evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed GCN-DSR,
we investigate different graph construction configurations,
including static graph modeling, spatial dynamic relation
modeling, and joint spatiotemporal dynamic relation mod-
eling.
The baseline configuration employs a fixed skeletal graph
topology. On this basis, dynamic spatial relations are adap-
tively constructed according to feature similarity between
joints. Temporal constraints are further incorporated to
regularize the dynamic graph construction process, thereby
forming the complete GCN-DSR framework.
The experimental results are reported in Table 5. When
onlyspatial dynamicrelation modelingis introduced,theper-
formance slightly degrades. This suggests that dynamically
constructed spatial relations are susceptible to noise without
temporal constraints, leading to unstable graph structures.
After further incorporating temporal constraints, the
MPJPE decreases significantly from 39.16 mm to 38.54
mm. This indicates that temporal information plays an
important regularization role in dynamic relation modeling
by constraining the variation of adjacency relationships,
thereby improving graph stability over time.
Overall, the results demonstrate that spatial dynamic re-
lation modeling alone is insufficient for stable performance.
By jointly modeling spatial and temporal dynamic relations,
GCN-DSR enables more stable dynamic relation learning
and improves pose estimation accuracy.
4.5.3. Analysis of RSRM
To evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed Residual
Skeletal Recalibration Module (RSRM), we investigate
different recalibration strategies and insertion positions.
Following the original MixTGFormer design, an SE-
based channel attention module is used as the baseline re-
calibration mechanism. Based on this setting, we replace the
SE module with RSRM while keeping all other components
unchanged for fair comparison. The corresponding results
are reported in Table 6.
As shown in Table 6, replacing SE with RSRM under
the same insertion position reduces the MPJPE from 39.16
mm to 38.85 mm, demonstrating the effectiveness of the
proposed recalibration mechanism. Furthermore, inserting
RSRM within each DSR Hybrid Block further improves
the performance to 38.61 mm, suggesting that earlier
feature recalibration is beneficial for feature interaction and
representation learning.
Overall, the results demonstrate that RSRM provides
consistent performance improvements under different inser-
tion configurations.

wrists, which are directly involved in hand-object interaction.
For the sitting action, the model emphasizes relatively
stable dependencies around the torso and hip regions while
suppressing highly dynamic limb interactions.
These observations suggest that DSR adaptively adjusts
skeletal relation patterns according to action semantics, en-
abling motion-dependent structural representation learning.
4.6.3. Comparison of 3D Pose Estimation Results
To provide a more intuitive evaluation of model perfor-
mance, Fig. 9 presents a qualitative comparison between the
proposed method and MixTGFormer on the Human3.6M
dataset. It can be observed that in challenging scenarios
such as limb crossing, rapid motion, and joint occlusion,
MixTGFormer tends to produce joint position deviations or
structurally inconsistent skeletons.
In contrast, the proposed method generates more stable
and structurally coherent 3D poses. For example, in cases
involving arm crossing and large leg movements, the
predicted joint connections are clearer, and bone lengths
remain consistent, helping reduce structural distortions.
These results demonstrate that the proposed dynamic skeletal
relation modeling and feature fusion strategy effectively
enhance structural modeling capability under complex pose
conditions.
4.6.4. Qualitative Analysis in Challenging In-the-Wild
Scenarios
Fig. 10 presents qualitative results on the 3DPW dataset
under challenging in-the-wild conditions. Despite large
viewpoint variations, motion diversity, and partial occlu-
sions, DSR-Net preserves anatomically plausible skeletal
structures and produces stable pose predictions. These
results suggest that the learned dynamic skeletal relations
exhibit favorable generalization capability beyond controlled
indoor benchmark datasets.
For better visualization, the left and right body parts are
highlighted in blue and red, respectively.
5. Conclusion
ThispaperpresentsDSR-Net,ahybridGraph-Transformer
framework for 3D human pose estimation based on dynamic
skeletal relation learning. Unlike conventional methods that
rely on fixed skeletal topology, the proposed framework
dynamically models motion-dependent inter-joint dependen-
cies, enabling more adaptive structural representation under
diverse motion patterns.
To effectively exploit the learned relations, a hierarchical
relation-aware fusion strategy is introduced to enhance
feature interaction across different modeling branches. In
addition, a lightweight Residual Skeletal Recalibration Mod-
ule (RSRM) is designed to improve structural consistency
during feature propagation while maintaining computational
efficiency.
Experimental results on Human3.6M and MPI-INF-
3DHP demonstrate the effectiveness of dynamic skeletal
relation modeling and hierarchical feature fusion for 3D
human pose estimation. The proposed framework achieves
