Abstract
Panoramic 3D reconstruction enables the holistic recovery of complete 360 ◦
scene geometry, offering significant benefits for robotics and AR/VR applications.

However, directly estimating geometry from ERP images is inherently challenged
by spherical distortions. To reduce spherical projection distortions, a common
strategy is to decompose panoramas into distortion-free perspective views, but
independently processing these views often introduces cross-view geometric in-
consistencies. To address these challenges, we introduce GeoPano, a feed-forward
framework for single-view panoramic 3D reconstruction that recovers a geometri-
cally accurate and consistent point cloud from a single panorama. Our approach
is driven by two key innovations: First, we propose a panoramic-aware attention
aggregator, featuring a streamlined attention architecture equipped with a novel
Pano-Attention mechanism, which explicitly exploits the intrinsic spherical ge-
ometry of the panorama to guide cross-view interactions, thereby suppressing
geometric inconsistencies. Furthermore, we formulate a hybrid supervision strat-
egy to jointly enforce fine-grained local details in perspective space and global
structural coherence in spherical space. Experiments demonstrate that our method
significantly outperforms previous SOTA approaches in reconstruction accuracy,
geometric consistency, and visual fidelity.

Introduction
Omnidirectional vision has garnered significant attention due to its capability to capture a comprehen-
sive 360◦field-of-view, acting as a critical enabler for holistic scene understanding [Ai et al., 2025,
Gao et al., 2022]. Consequently, panoramic monocular 3D reconstruction, or depth estimation has
emerged as a cornerstone task for numerous downstream applications, ranging from autonomous driv-
ing [Kumar et al., 2021, Zheng et al., 2023] to immersive virtual reality (VR) [Yang et al., 2022, 2024,
Cao et al., 2023] and visual navigation [Li and Bansal, 2023]. In current pipelines, the equirectangular
projection (ERP) is widely used to represent spherical data on 2D planes. However, this projection is
fundamentally hindered by non-uniform geometric deformations, particularly the extreme stretching
concentrated at the polar regions [Ai et al., 2025]. Such inherent spherical distortions disrupt the
expected spatial distribution of visual features, causing conventional perspective 3D reconstruction to
degrade significantly when applied directly to ERP imagery.
To circumvent these projection artifacts, a prevailing strategy utilizes perspective projection to extract
a set of distortion-free patches from the panorama. Nevertheless, processing these extracted views in
isolation disrupts global contextual dependencies, invariably leading to cross-view inconsistencies
and boundary seams. Existing methods attempt to resolve this fragmentation through two distinct
paradigms: end-to-end supervised fusion and zero-shot training-free inference. End-to-end supervised
architectures [Wang et al., 2020, 2022, Jiang et al., 2021, Li et al., 2022b, Ai et al., 2023] attempt to
bridge this spatial gap by explicitly fusing global ERP context with local extracted features during
training. However, their efficacy is heavily bottlenecked by the prohibitive cost of dense 360◦
depth annotations [Chang et al., 2017, Armeni et al., 2017, Zheng et al., 2020], resulting in limited
cross-domain generalization. Furthermore, the massive computational overhead of multi-branch
networks typically restricts these models to low-resolution inputs. Conversely, zero-shot training-free
approaches [Rey-Area et al., 2022, Peng and Zhang, 2023, Jung et al., 2025] bypass domain-specific
training by leveraging pre-trained perspective foundation models [Ranftl et al., 2020, Yin et al., 2021]
to process the decomposed perspective patches independently. While this effectively avoids the need
for 360◦datasets, these methods face their own critical bottlenecks: reconciling scale ambiguities
and enforcing cross-view alignment heavily relies on either computationally expensive iterative
post-processing [Jung et al., 2025, Rey-Area et al., 2022] or auxiliary panoramic models as references
[Peng and Zhang, 2023].
Currently, the advent of feed-forward 3D reconstruction architectures (e.g., VGGT [Wang et al.,
2025a] and π3 [Wang et al., 2025b]) has established a new frontier in visual geometry, operating as
3D foundation models. These networks excel at reconstructing globally consistent 3D structures from
uncalibrated multi-view images by intrinsically capturing cross-view geometric relations. Motivated
by this, we reformulate panoramic 3D reconstruction as a sparse multi-view problem via view
decomposition, enabling us to directly leverage the robust geometric priors of foundation models for
holistic scene recovery. Furthermore, benefiting from extensive pre-training on millions of real-world
3D scenes, these foundation models inherently possess exceptional sparse-view geometric reasoning
2


capabilities. Such a rich, data-driven prior allows these frameworks to readily adapt to a sparse
decomposed-view setup requiring only minimal fine-tuning data.
Fundamentally, the multi-view reasoning prowess of these foundation models is rooted in their strong
attention aggregators. To adapt them for panoramic scenes, we analyze how their attention aggregator
captures global information across decomposed panoramic views. This exploration exposes two
bottlenecks: (1) early global attention in π3 fails to capture geometric correlations such as spatial
overlap; and (2) while later global attention layers exhibit a clear preference for interacting within
overlapping views, interactions stemming from entirely non-overlapping views remain significant,
which we argue is detrimental to achieving cross-view consistency.
To directly address these two bottlenecks, we propose a novel panoramic-aware Attention Aggre-
gator. Specifically, to prevent the ineffective and diffuse interactions observed in early layers, our
architecture initially employs a sequence of intra-frame self-attention layers. This stage focuses
entirely on extracting reliable local geometry, explicitly bypassing premature cross-view interfer-
ence. Subsequently, to tackle the residual noise in later stages, we introduce alternating attention
layers augmented with our Pano-Attention. Integrated via residual connections, this Pano-Attention
explicitly guides query tokens to interact strictly with geometrically overlapping regions across the
decomposed views. Consequently, it effectively suppresses irrelevant interactions and significantly
enhances globally consistent scene reconstruction. In summary, our contributions are three-fold: (1)
A Panoramic-Aware Attention Aggregator that explicitly incorporates spherical geometry to guide
cross-view feature interactions. This design mitigates irrelevant token interactions and improves
geometric consistency across decomposed perspective views. (2) A hybrid supervision strategy that
jointly optimizes local perspective-space point clouds and global spherical-space structure. This strat-
egy preserves fine-grained local geometry while enhancing globally consistent 3D reconstructions.
(3) Extensive experiments on standard benchmarks demonstrate that our method achieves superior
performance. It consistently outperforms prior single-view panoramic approaches in reconstruction
accuracy, geometric consistency, and overall visual fidelity.

Related Work
2.1
Panoramic Monocular Depth Estimation
Panoramic monocular depth estimation reconstructs holistic 3D scene depth from single equirectangu-
lar projection (ERP) images. Current solutions generally follow two main tracks: direct ERP-domain
estimation and multi-view fusion.
ERP-domain estimation. ERP-based methods preserve global consistency but suffer from spherical
distortions and data scarcity. To mitigate distortions, prior works employ architectural adaptations
like distortion-aware convolution and spherical encoding [Cao and Wang, 2024, Eder and Frahm,
2019, Sun et al., 2021, Tateno et al., 2018, Yun et al., 2023]. To address data limitations, recent studies
focus on data synthesis and knowledge transfer, such as generating pseudo-labels from perspective
estimators [Cao et al., 2025b], expanding datasets via diffusion-based outpainting [Li et al., 2025],
or introducing large-scale panoramic datasets [Lin et al., 2025]. Despite these efforts, inherent
distortions and data scarcity continue to hinder depth accuracy in highly distorted regions.
Multi-view Fusion. Conversely, multi-view fusion paradigms mitigate spherical distortions by
decomposing panoramas into perspective views [Rey-Area et al., 2022, Ai et al., 2023, Jiang et al.,
2021, Li et al., 2022b, Mohadikar et al., 2024, Wang et al., 2018, 2020, 2022]. To address spatial
fragmentation, recent methods explore advanced fusion strategies, such as post-processing optimiza-
tion for view alignment [Jung et al., 2025] or representing static panoramas into sequential video
frames to utilize video depth models [Cao et al., 2025a]. Despite these efforts, the multi-view fusion
strategy inherently disrupts global structural consistency. Consequently, these approaches often
rely on time-consuming post-processing or cross-domain feature fusion, which struggles to enforce
accurate global consistency in complex scenes.
2.2
Feed-forward 3D Reconstruction Models
DUST3R [Wang et al., 2024] introduces a transformative paradigm by directly regressing 3D point
maps and camera poses from uncalibrated images. Subsequent works such as Fast3R [Yang et al.,
2025] and SLAM3R [Liu et al., 2025] extend this to multi-view and video inputs via global fusion

transformers. Scaling this further, VGGT [Wang et al., 2025a] leverages massive 3D datasets
to achieve robust in-the-wild reconstruction. To resolve the performance degradation caused by
reference-frame dependency in these architectures, π3 [Wang et al., 2025b] proposes a permutation-
equivariant design. Despite their generalization strengths, these regression-based methods often lack

Recently, researchers have extended this feed-forward paradigm to the spherical domain for holistic

3D point clouds directly from unordered panoramas by mitigating spherical distortions. VGGT-

3D reconstruction problem utilizing perspective foundation models.

Methodology
3.1
Analysis of Cross-View Interactions
We adopt π3 [Wang et al., 2025b] as our base model, owing to its strong robustness in sparse-view
3D reconstruction. Given N perspective views, π3 utilizes a DINOv2 encoder [Oquab et al., 2023] to
extract feature tokens. These tokens are then processed by an attention aggregator comprising 18
alternating intra-frame and global attention blocks for feature aggregation. Finally, camera and point
map heads decode the aggregated features into camera poses and 3D point maps.
To better adapt the multi-view geometric reasoning capability of π3 to panoramic reconstruction, we
analyze the behavior of global attention across different layers of the attention aggregator, inspired by
the analysis in AVGGT [Sun et al., 2025]. We conduct an exploratory study on a small-scale dataset,
which ultimately motivates the design of our proposed attention aggregator. Specifically, we select
one token from view 4 as the query token. For each global attention layer, we separately extract the
top-100 key/value tokens with the highest attention weights for this query token, and then compute
their view-wise distribution.
As illustrated in Fig. 2 (a), in the early global attention layers (Layers 0 ∼8, blue in the figure),
the attention weights are uniformly distributed across the first eight horizontal views (v0 ∼v7).
The v8 (upward) and v9 (downward) receive minimal attention likely because these low-texture
regions (e.g., skies or grounds) semantically diverge from the horizontal views. Consequently, this
early-stage attention exhibits no preference for views with actual spatial overlap, resulting in a diffuse
distribution.
In contrast, the later global attention layers (Layers 9 ∼17, other colors in Fig. 2 (a)) demonstrate an
explicit interaction bias. The attention becomes highly concentrated on the source view (v4) and its
spatially overlapping neighbors (v5), while interactions with irrelevant views are suppressed. The
Global Decay Slope metric quantitatively confirms this trend. A higher slope means the attention is
more tightly focused. As shown in Fig. 2 (b), the slope increases significantly in later layers, proving
that the model successfully concentrates on geometrically relevant regions.
Consequently, we conclude that under decomposed perspective views, early global attention in π3
fails to capture geometric correlations such as spatial overlap. Furthermore, we find that even in

3.2
Framework Overview
As illustrated in Fig. 3, our framework operates through a three-stage pipeline: view decomposition,
local 3D reconstruction, and panoramic fusion. The input panorama is first decomposed into 10
overlapping perspective views (8 horizontal views, 1 upward, and 1 downward, as detailed in Fig. 4).
During the reconstruction phase, these decomposed views are tokenized into high-dimensional
features by DINOv2 [Oquab et al., 2023]. These tokens are subsequently processed by our proposed
panoramic-aware attention aggregator, which explicitly guides multi-view interactions to enforce
geometric consistency. A local point map head then decodes these aggregated features into per-
view 3D point clouds. Finally, these per-view point clouds are re-projected back into the spherical
coordinate system and seamlessly fused into a globally aligned, holistic 3D point cloud. The entire
framework is optimized via a hybrid loss, which simultaneously supervises the local perspective point
clouds and the global panoramic point cloud.

3.3
Panoramic-aware Attention Aggregator
Base Architecture. Motivated by the findings in Sec.3.1, the panoramic-aware attention ag-
gregator is specifically designed to address two key issues: (1) preventing ineffective and diffuse
interactions in the early global attention layers, and (2) suppressing residual interactions across
non-overlapping regions in the later stages. As shown in Fig. 3, we design the base architecture with
nine layers of intra-frame self-attention followed by nine layers of alternating attention. In layers 10,
12, 14, and 16 of the Attention Aggregator, we compute Pano-Attention and add it to the output of
the alternating attention via a residual connection. The first nine layers focus on extracting reliable
intra-frame geometry, while the subsequent alternating attention layers jointly refine inter-frame con-
sistency and fine-grained intra-frame features. Importantly, the introduced Pano-Attention explicitly
guides the query tokens to interact with tokens in cross-view overlapping regions, thereby enhancing
geometry-aware feature interactions and suppressing interactions with irrelevant tokens.
Pano-Attention. We introduce Pano-Attention, which restricts token interactions to geometrically
coherent regions. Specifically, As shown in Fig. 4 we construct a candidate token mask M, where
the i-th row specifies the key/value tokens (candidate tokens 1∼10 in the figure) that can be queried
by the i-th query token. We apply this mask to the attention logits before the softmax operation, as
illustrate in Fig. 3. We explicitly mask invalid token pairs by setting their pre-softmax scores to −∞,
ensuring their resulting attention weights are strictly zero. In this way, each token only aggregates
information from geometrically valid candidates, reducing unreliable interactions and improving
cross-view consistency.
To construct the candidate mask, we leverage rays in the spherical space to introduce global geometric
information. Specifically, since all decomposed perspective views share an identical camera projection
center, two patches from adjacent views are considered geometrically aligned if they correspond to
the same spherical ray direction. Based on this property, we can establish accurate correspondences
between any two patches, determining whether they are geometrically relevant in the spherical space.
Formally, we quantify this geometric relevance by measuring the cosine similarity between their
associated spherical rays. To construct the candidate mask M, as shown in Fig. 4, we first compute
the cosine similarity sij between the ray of each token i and token j to form a global cosine similarity
matrix S. Subsequently, the candidate mask M is calculated via Eq. 1:

Experiments
4.1
Experiment Setup
Training Datasets. For training, we use Holo360D [Ou et al., 2026], a large-scale real-world
panoramic 3D dataset comprising 75 indoor and outdoor scenes. It provides panoramic RGB images
with corresponding depth maps, each captured at a resolution of 2880 × 1440. We train our model
using 23,000 panoramic frames.
Evaluation Datasets and Evaluation Metrics. For evaluation, following prior work [Lin et al.,
2025], we test our model on three 360◦depth datasets: Matterport3D [Chang et al., 2017] (indoor)
and Stanford2D3D [Armeni et al., 2017] (indoor), and Deep360 [Li et al., 2022a] (outdoor). To
comprehensively assess model performance, we evaluate both point cloud using 3D metrics [Jung
et al., 2025] such as Chamfer Distance, F-Score, and Intersection over Union (IoU), and depth using
standard 2D metrics including Absolute Relative Error (AbsRel), Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE),
and the accuracy under a threshold δi with i=1.25.
Implementation details. For training, each model is trained using four NVIDIA A800 GPUs for 20
epochs, with each epoch consisting of 1,000 iterations. During evaluation, inference is performed on
a single A800 GPU, while monitoring GPU memory usage and runtime performance.

Conclusion
We proposed GeoPano, a feed-forward framework for single-view panoramic 3D reconstruction,
featuring a Panoramic-Aware Attention Aggregator that leverages spherical geometry to improve
cross-view consistency. A hybrid supervision strategy combines local and global point cloud losses,
ensuring fine-grained details and holistic structural coherence. Experiments show that GeoPano
outperforms prior single-view panoramic methods in accuracy, consistency, and visual fidelity while
maintaining practical efficiency.
