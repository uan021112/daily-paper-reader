Physical SLAM: Physics-aware Simultaneous
Localisation and Mapping
Guangming Wang, Zhishuo Liu, Haibing Wu, Wanli Ma, Yixiong Jing, Olaf Wysocki, and Brian Sheil
Abstract—To reconstruct a predictive 4D representation of the
physical world from monocular video captured by a moving
camera, we jointly reconstruct dynamic Gaussian radiance fields
and infer their underlying physical dynamics. This paper proposes
Physical SLAM, a physics-aware method for constructing 4D
Gaussian fields of deformable objects and predicting their future
state when subjected to external actions, given a sequence of RGB-
D images. Our method separates the scene into a static background
Gaussian field for camera localization and a dynamic object
Gaussian field driven by a spring–mass physical model of elastic
deformation, inertia, and damping. To integrate visual observa-
tions with physical dynamics, we introduce virtual observation
forces that gradually pull simulated surface nodes toward observed
depth points, avoiding abrupt replacement of simulated node
positions. Meanwhile, the physical parameters of the spring–mass
model are updated from simulation errors aggregated over the full
reconstruction observation window, enabling the reconstructed
dynamics to align with observations. To couple appearance,
geometry, and physics, we further design a physics-aware Gaussian
transformation and surface alignment strategy, where dynamic
Gaussians are transformed along the deforming physical surface
of the spring–mass model and constrained to remain aligned with
the surface during rendering-based optimization. The resulting
representation supports observed-frame rendering and action-
conditioned simulation, extending SLAM maps to describe how
deformable objects respond to manipulation. On the PhysTwin
benchmark, our method improves reconstruction and prediction
PSNR over PhysTwin by 10.4% and 11.0%, respectively. On the
TUM RGB-D benchmark, it reduces average camera tracking
error by 20.6%, improves SSIM by 3.6%, and reduces LPIPS
by 8.3% compared with 4DGS-SLAM. The source code will be
released upon acceptance.
Index Terms—Simultaneous localisation and mapping, 3D
Gaussian splatting, deformable objects, physical prediction.
I. INTRODUCTION

RACKING, mapping, rendering, and predicting dynamic
3D scenes from streaming RGB-D observations is a
fundamental problem in computer vision and robotics. SLAM
estimates camera motion and scene structure from sequential
observations [1]. Geometric maps support localisation, while
rendering and physical interaction additionally require appear-
ance and dynamics [2], [3]. 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)
has recently emerged as an efficient explicit representation for
high-fidelity scene reconstruction and rendering [4]. Gaussian-
based SLAM systems have leveraged this representation to
incrementally estimate camera poses and optimize Gaussian
primitives from RGB or RGB-D streams, achieving accurate

localization and high-quality dense mapping in unknown
environments [2], [5]–[8]. However, previous methods have
been primarily designed for static scenes. When moving
or deformable objects appear, a static Gaussian map can
introduce inconsistent geometry, blurred regions, and degraded
camera tracking thereby inhibiting its application to dynamic
problems [9], [10].
To handle non-static environments, many dynamic SLAM
and Gaussian SLAM methods detect moving regions and
remove, mask, or down-weight them during tracking and
mapping [9], [11]–[15]. Although this strategy improves camera
localization and produces cleaner static reconstructions, dy-
namic objects are treated as distractors rather than reconstructed
as part of the 4D world. Recent 4D Gaussian Splatting
SLAM methods bridge this gap by explicitly maintaining
dynamic Gaussian primitives and modeling their temporal
transformations [10], [16]. Instead of discarding moving
regions, they reconstruct time-varying Gaussian radiance fields
and enable streaming reconstruction of dynamic appearance
and geometry. Despite this progress, current 4D Gaussian
fields are still primarily visual and kinematic. Their temporal
evolution is usually parameterized by deformation fields [17]–
[19], sparse control points [10], [20], or learned transformations
[21]–[23], and is often further supervised with optical-flow
constraints [10], [24], [25]. Such representations can describe
where dynamic Gaussians move in observed frames, but they
do not explain why the object deforms or how the object will
respond to future actions. This missing physical layer is critical
for robotic manipulation [3].
Recent physics-aware reconstruction methods show that
incorporating physical models into object reconstruction can
improve deformable object simulation and action-conditioned
future prediction [3], [26], [27]. However, these methods are
mostly designed for offline reconstruction of deformable object
geometry, appearance, and dynamics from previously collected
observations. They do not reconstruct a physics-aware 4D
Gaussian field in a streaming setting, where camera poses,
appearance, geometry, and physical dynamics are updated
from incoming RGB-D frames. The remaining challenge is
to combine camera localisation with progressive estimation of
deformable geometry, appearance, and physical dynamics in a
single RGB-D pipeline.

for reconstructing physics-aware 4D Gaussian fields from a
single RGB-D stream, as shown in Fig. 1. Given an RGB-
D sequence, we separate the scene into a static background
Gaussian field and a dynamic object Gaussian field similar
to [10]. The static field supports camera localization and

The novel contributions of our method are summarized as
follows:
• We propose a physics-aware 4D Gaussian reconstruction
method that couples dynamic Gaussian primitives with a
spring–mass physical model, enabling joint reconstruction
of appearance, geometry, and physical dynamics from
streaming RGB-D observations.
• We introduce an observation-force-based physical tracking
formulation that injects visual observations into the
dynamics model through virtual forces, enabling stable
deformable object tracking without direct state replace-
ment.
• We propose a physics-aware Gaussian transformation with
a streaming optimization scheme for dynamic Gaussian
primitives, allowing the reconstructed 4D Gaussian field
to support both high-fidelity rendering and physically
grounded action-conditioned prediction.
II. RELATED WORK
3D Gaussian Splatting and Non-static GS SLAM. 3DGS [4]
provides an explicit and efficient radiance-field representation
for rendering and reconstruction, and has been extended
to geometry-aware reconstruction and dense SLAM from
monocular, stereo, and RGB-D streams [2], [4]–[8]. These
methods achieve strong tracking and mapping performance in
static or mostly rigid scenes, but dynamic objects can cause
pose errors and ghosting artifacts. To improve robustness in non-
static environments, dynamic SLAM and non-static GS-SLAM
methods [9], [11]–[15], [28] detect dynamic or disturbance
regions through various methods, such as motion masks [11],
rendering residuals [13], uncertainty maps [14], or probabilistic
Gaussian labels [15], to suppress dynamic distractors during
pose estimation and reconstruct cleaner static Gaussian fields.
Dynamic Gaussian Splatting. Dynamic neural rendering
and dynamic Gaussian Splatting extend static radiance-field
reconstruction to time-varying scenes through deformation
fields [16]–[18], factorized 4D feature planes [29], 4D neural
voxels and deformation MLPs [21], canonical-space Gaussian
deformation [19], persistent Gaussian trajectories [30], sparse
control points [10], [20], neural transformation caches [23],
and per-Gaussian embeddings [22]. These methods signifi-
cantly improve dynamic novel-view synthesis by learning how
Gaussian primitives or radiance fields move across time. More
closely related to our setting, 4D Gaussian Splatting SLAM
separates static and dynamic Gaussians and learns dynamic
transformations with sparse control points and an MLP [10].
These kinematic transformations do not model the forces and
material dynamics needed for action-conditioned prediction.
Our method addresses this limitation in the dynamic branch.
We bind dynamic Gaussians to a spring–mass model and
update the physical state using our proposed virtual observation
forces. The reconstructed 4D Gaussian field captures time-
varying geometry, appearance, and physical dynamics for
action-conditioned future prediction.
Physics-aware deformable modeling and action-conditioned
prediction. Beyond purely visual 4D reconstruction, physics-


3
aware methods seek to build simulatable object representations
by coupling visual observations with physical dynamics.
Existing approaches instantiate this idea with different scene
representations, including point-cloud/mesh-based deformable
modeling and differentiable simulation [31]–[33], SDF-based
physical reconstruction [34], NeRF or neural-field-based
simulatable objects [35]–[37], and Gaussian-based physical
representations [3], [26], [27], [38]. Some of them prescribe
material parameters or user-defined physical settings [37], [38],
while others perform inverse identification from videos or
observations [3], [26], [27], [34]–[36]. PhysTwin is the closest
work to ours, as it combines spring-mass dynamics, Gaussian
splats, and inverse physics optimization for deformable digital-
twin reconstruction [3]. PhysTwin reconstructs objects from
three fixed-view RGB-D recordings in an offline pipeline. Our
method estimates camera poses and builds a physics-aware 4D
Gaussian field online from a single moving RGB-D stream,
updating the map as frames arrive. Moreover, learning-based
dynamics models [39]–[41] require extensive training samples
and are often limited to specific environments, whereas our
method does not require pre-training and establishes physical
understanding during the SLAM process.

A. Tracking with Observation-Force-Corrected Dynamics
Static camera tracking. Our camera tracking follows the
DROID-SLAM-style dense bundle adjustment (DBA) backend
adopted by WildGS-SLAM [1], [14]. Unlike WildGS-SLAM,
which learns a per-pixel uncertainty map to down-weight
dynamic distractors, we directly use the semantic dynamic mask
M obj
t
. This provides an explicit static/dynamic partition shared
by camera tracking and object reconstruction. The tracking
backend optimizes camera poses from RGB-D and optical-flow
constraints only on background pixels, and passes the estimated
keyframe poses to the mapping module.
Tracking dynamics with observation-force correction. Given
the corrected state ˆXt−1, we advance the node positions
and velocities with the spring–mass simulator defined in
Sec. III. When explicit manipulation is available, we track
points in segmented contact regions with CoTracker3 [47]
and lift them to 3D using depth and camera poses. Their
trajectories define the action at−1 and drive nearby object nodes
through control springs, following PhysTwin [3]. For dynamic
objects with unknown actions, such as human foregrounds
in generic RGB-D SLAM sequences, we set at−1 = 0 and
rely on observation-force correction for tracking. A pure
action-driven rollout accumulates errors due to noisy actions
and imperfect physical parameters, while directly replacing
simulated nodes with observed geometry points may introduce
discontinuities and unstable oscillations. We therefore convert
geometry observations into external observation forces.

A. Reconstruction and Prediction on the PhysTwin Dataset
Setting. We use the same dataset, data processing methods,
train/test split, tasks, and evaluation metrics as PhysTwin [3].
The benchmark contains RGB-D videos of deformable objects
under human interaction, including stuffed toys, ropes, cloth-
like objects, and packages. Following PhysTwin, we evaluate
two tasks: Reconstruction, where the system reconstructs
observed interactions, and Prediction, where the system predicts
unseen future frames under given future control-point actions.
We report the same 3D metrics, including single-direction
Chamfer Distance and tracking error, and 2D metrics, including
IoU, PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS.
Results. The Reconstruction column in Table I shows that our
method achieves strong reconstruction performance. Compared
with PhysTwin, our method matches the best 3D geometry met-
rics, while substantially improving 2D alignment and rendering
quality. This indicates that the proposed physics-aware Gaussian
transformation and surface-alignment regularization preserve
physical consistency while producing sharper and better-aligned
renderings. The Prediction column in Table I further shows that
our method improves over PhysTwin in CD, IoU, PSNR, SSIM,
and LPIPS, while matching the best Track Error. These results
suggest that the progressively updated spring–mass model
captures physical dynamics that generalize to future states,
while the surface-bound Gaussian field transfers the predicted
deformation into coherent appearance and geometry. Fig. 3
also shows that our method reconstructs and predicts more
plausible object deformation by binding dynamic Gaussians to
the deforming physical surface.

D. Ablation Study
We conduct individual ablations on the PhysTwin benchmark
to analyze the contribution of each component. w/o obs-force
removes observation-force correction. w/o surface align loss
removes the surface-alignment regularizer during dynamic
Gaussian optimization.
Table V shows the effectiveness of each component. Re-
moving the observation force weakens both reconstruction and
prediction quality, especially IoU and PSNR. These results
support our observation-force design for coupling visual obser-
vations with physical dynamics: geometry-based forces correct
the surface state that drives dynamic Gaussians. The prediction
gains persist after observation forces are disabled, suggesting
that reconstruction-stage correction also benefits subsequent
action-conditioned rollout. Removing the surface-alignment
loss reduces IoU and rendering quality in both tasks. The
rendering gains support the contribution of surface alignment to
maintaining appearance–geometry consistency during Gaussian
refinement. This is consistent with the regularizer suppressing
normal drift while retaining tangential adjustment, helping the
refined Gaussians stay aligned with the physical surface that
drives their motion.
VI. CONCLUSION
We presented Physical SLAM, a physics-aware 4D Gaussian
reconstruction and prediction method that couples dynamic
Gaussians with a physical model. Unlike existing dynamic
Gaussian SLAM systems that primarily model visual motion,
our method reconstructs the physical state and parameters
of deformable objects from streaming RGB-D observations.
Limitation and Future Work: Our current system still assumes
a fixed spring–mass topology. Future work will relax these
assumptions by supporting topology-changing deformation to
reconstruct tearable objects.
