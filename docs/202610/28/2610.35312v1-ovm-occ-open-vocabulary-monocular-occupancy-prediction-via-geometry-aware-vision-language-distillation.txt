# OVM-Occ: Open-Vocabulary Monocular Occupancy Prediction via Geometry-Aware Vision-Language Distillation 

Shuyang Yu 1,2, Haibo Qin 2, Shaohan Hu 2, Wei Shan 2, Xiaoqing Guan 1, You Wang 1,†, and Guang Li 1

Abstract — Monocular occupancy prediction in indoor scenes aims to recover a scene’s 3D geometry and semantics from a single RGB image. Existing methods are restricted to a closed set of predefined categories, limiting their ability to generalize to unseen categories. This limitation is particularly critical in indoor scenes, where objects exhibit highly diverse and fine-grained semantic categories. We study open-vocabulary monoc-ular occupancy prediction for indoor scenes and present OVM-Occ, a Gaussian-based occupancy framework with an instance-aware vision-language teacher. Specifically, we adopt Language-Embedded Gaussians as the intermediate scene representation, propose a geometry-aware deformable attention module to refine Gaussian semantic features, and construct instance-level vision-language embeddings to provide reliable semantic super-vision. We further introduce geometry-aware decoupled seman-tic distillation to mitigate the interference between geometry learning and semantic learning. To evaluate open-vocabulary occupancy prediction performance, we introduce OpenOcc-ScanNet and OpenOcc-Replica, two extended benchmarks with more diverse and fine-grained semantic categories. Experiments show that our method achieves state-of-the-art performance in open-vocabulary monocular occupancy prediction for indoor scenes, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art approach by 12.2 mIoU and 0.6 IoU on the Occ-ScanNet dataset, with consistent gains under new open-vocabulary evaluation protocols. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

A comprehensive understanding of the surrounding 3D environment is fundamental to embodied perception, robotic navigation, and physical interaction [1], [2]. Occupancy prediction represents the environment as a dense volumetric grid, jointly estimating the occupancy state of each spatial el-ement and assigning semantic labels to occupied regions [3]. This unified representation, which integrates geometry and semantics, supports fine-grained spatial reasoning and down-stream embodied tasks [4]. Vision-based occupancy prediction has rapidly advanced in autonomous driving scenarios [5]–[8], where multi-view images provide strong geometric and semantic cues. By contrast, indoor occupancy prediction is more challenging and comparatively underexplored due to denser layouts and more severe occlusions. These challenges become even more pronounced in the monocular setting, where 3D occupancy must be inferred from a single RGB image without multi-view constraints. Recent methods [9]–[11] have explored Gaussian-based representations for occupancy prediction, achieving promising improvements in indoor scenes. In addition to geometric complexity, indoor scenes contain diverse, fine-grained, and long-tailed semantic categories.

A. Monocular Occupancy Prediction 

Multi-camera occupancy prediction has been extensively studied in autonomous driving and has achieved remarkable performance [5]–[8]. Motivated by embodied perception and advances in visual geometry foundation models [17], [18], recent studies have explored monocular occupancy predic-tion for indoor scene understanding. MonoScene [12] is a pioneering work in monocular 3D occupancy prediction. ISO [13] leverages monocular depth estimation to lift image features into dense 3D volumes for occupancy prediction. Recent works [9]–[11], [19] employ Gaussian primitives as an intermediate scene representation and optimize occupancy prediction via differentiable rendering. However, these meth-ods primarily focus on improving geometric reconstruction, while semantic modeling remains restricted to predefined label sets, limiting generalization to unseen categories. 

B. Open-Vocabulary Occupancy Prediction 

Open-vocabulary occupancy prediction incorporates se-mantic knowledge from VLMs, enabling semantic under-standing beyond predefined categories. POP-3D [20] and AGO [21] establish pixel-to-voxel correspondences through geometric projection and distill the corresponding visual embeddings into voxel representations for 3D occupancy prediction. LOcc [22] employs a 2D open-vocabulary se-mantic segmentation model to generate category-specific masks and distills the corresponding text embeddings into voxel representations. However, these methods are primarily designed for outdoor driving scenarios with multi-view cam-eras, and their direct transfer to indoor monocular settings leads to inferior performance [9]. LegoOcc [23] extends open-vocabulary occupancy prediction to indoor monocular scenes under geometry-only supervision. It introduces 3D LE-Gaussians to jointly model scene geometry and seman-tics, renders Gaussian semantic features into the image plane, and distills them with vision-language embeddings. Overall, research on open-vocabulary occupancy prediction for indoor monocular scenes remains relatively underexplored. III. M ETHODOLOGY

A. Problem Definition 

Given a monocular RGB image I ∈ RH×W ×3, the goal is to predict a voxel-wise semantic occupancy field 

O ∈ RX×Y ×Z×Nc , where X, Y , and Z denote the spatial resolution, and Nc is the number of semantic categories. During training, only binary occupancy labels and depth observations are used, without voxel-level semantic anno-tations. Semantic categories are specified only at inference time through text queries.

C. Instance-Aware Vision-Language Teacher 

Existing open-vocabulary occupancy prediction meth-ods [20], [21], [23] typically rely on dense visual embeddings extracted from CLIP-based models as teacher supervision. However, these embeddings are learned through image-level contrastive pre-training [14] and inevitably encode background context and irrelevant regions, thereby yielding noisy semantic supervision. In contrast, LOcc [22] employs language teacher embeddings together with open-vocabulary semantic masks. While the language embeddings provide rel-atively clean category-level supervision, the semantic masks lack explicit instance awareness, leading to ambiguous super-vision around instance boundaries. Moreover, LOcc ignores rich visual appearance cues, which are crucial for open-vocabulary tasks. Fig. 3 provides a qualitative comparison of the aforementioned teachers and their corresponding open-vocabulary segmentation results. To leverage the complemen-tary strengths of both modalities, we construct an instance-aware vision-language teacher, where language embeddings serve as semantic anchors while visual embeddings provide instance-specific appearance cues, as shown in Fig. 4. We employ a large vision-language model (LVLM) to ex-tract semantic categories from the input image. The predicted categories are subsequently refined through text denoising and temporal completion, yielding the final category set

D. Geometry-Aware Decoupled Semantic Distillation 

Semantic-to-geometry decoupling. LegoOcc [23] renders Gaussian semantic features onto the image plane and applies feature supervision. During optimization, the gradients from the feature rendering loss are propagated to the geometry branch. As a result, the optimization may tend to reduce the rendering loss by adjusting Gaussian geometry rather than improving semantic representations. Meanwhile, the high-dimensional CLIP teacher embeddings inevitably contain noisy semantic responses, introducing unreliable supervision signals into the geometry branch. Evaluation metrics. Following prior work [9], [13], [23], we use overall occupancy IoU and mean IoU (mIoU) over all semantic categories as the evaluation metrics. 

Implementation details. For teacher construction, we use Qwen2.5-VL-3B [28] for semantic category extraction, CLIP text encoder for text embedding extraction, SAM 3 [29] for open-vocabulary instance segmentation, and OpenSeg [30] for visual embedding extraction. For the student model, following [9], [11], [23], we employ a fine-tuned DepthAnything-V2 [17] as the backbone. The network is optimized using AdamW with a weight decay of 0.01. The initial learning rate is set to 2 × 10 −4 and follows a cosine annealing schedule. The model is trained for 10 epochs with a batch size of 8 on 4 NVIDIA RTX 5090 GPUs. For key hyperparameters, we set the number of Gaussians sampled per ray to N = 8 , the visual weight λvis = 0 .3,the depth error threshold τd = 0 .3, and the loss weights 

λ1 = 100 .0, λ 2 = 1 .0, λ 3 = 2 .0, λ 4 = 2 .0, λ 5 = 0 .5. More implementation details will be provided in the released code. 

B. Main Results 

We compare OVM-Occ with representative open-vocabulary methods, including POP-3D [20], LOcc [22], and LegoOcc [23]. Table I reports the quantitative com-parison results on the Occ-ScanNet benchmark. OVM-Occ achieves state-of-the-art performance among all open-vocabulary methods, with 60.10 IoU and 33.25 mIoU, out-performing LegoOcc by 0.60 and 12.20, respectively. We further evaluate the open-vocabulary generalization ability on the OpenOcc-ScanNet benchmark, which contains more diverse and fine-grained semantic categories. As shown in Table II, our method consistently outperforms baselines under all evaluation protocols. Notably, OVM-Occ maintains substantial performance gains even under the challenging 40-class protocol, demonstrating the robustness of the proposed method under a significantly expanded semantic label space. We evaluate few-shot transfer performance on the OpenOcc-Replica benchmark. As shown in Table III, our method consistently outperforms LegoOcc across all training settings. With pre-training on Occ-ScanNet, our method achieves comparable semantic performance using only 100 frames from Replica, reaching 14.44 mIoU versus 14.62 mIoU when trained from scratch on 1.1K frames. With 1.1K frames for fine-tuning, our method further improves to 77.88 IoU and 23.28 mIoU. Fig. 5 presents qualitative comparisons on the Occ-ScanNet benchmark. Compared with LegoOcc, OVM-Occ produces more accurate semantic occupancy predictions with fewer category confusions. Moreover, our method preserves stronger instance-level consistency, assigning coherent se-mantic labels to the same object while reducing fragmented predictions, which demonstrates the effectiveness of the proposed instance-aware vision-language supervision. Fig. 6 presents qualitative comparisons on the OpenOcc-ScanNet and OpenOcc-Replica benchmarks with the ex-panded semantic label space. OVM-Occ exhibits noticeably more reliable and coherent predictions in these challeng-ing settings. Furthermore, OVM-Occ successfully recog-nizes long-tail semantic categories (e.g., guitar and bicycle), demonstrating its open-vocabulary generalization capability beyond frequent categories.

In this work, we present OVM-Occ, a Gaussian-based framework for open-vocabulary monocular occupancy pre-diction in indoor scenes. OVM-Occ introduces an instance-aware vision-language teacher that provides instance-level semantic supervision by integrating visual appearance cues with language semantic anchors. Furthermore, geometry-aware deformable attention and geometry-aware decou-pled semantic distillation enable more effective seman-tic representation learning. We establish OpenOcc-ScanNet and OpenOcc-Replica, two benchmarks with more diverse and fine-grained semantic categories. Extensive experiments demonstrate that our method achieves state-of-the-art perfor-mance for open-vocabulary monocular occupancy prediction. REFERENCES [1] H. Liu, D. Guo, and A.
