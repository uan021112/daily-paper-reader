Generalizable Target-Less Joint Intrinsic–Extrinsic Camera–LiDAR
Calibration using Deep Pixel–Point Correspondences
Simon Bultmann, Daniele Cattaneo, and Abhinav Valada
Abstract— Accurate camera-LiDAR calibration is an essential
prerequisite for robust multi-modal perception in robotics.
Recent target-less methods based on learned pixel-point corre-
spondences achieve high extrinsic calibration accuracy and
generalize across environments. However, they operate on
rectified images with known camera intrinsics, restricting
their applicability to systems whose cameras have already
been individually calibrated. We present a fully automatic,
target-less framework that jointly estimates camera intrinsics,
radial-tangential distortion, and camera-LiDAR extrinsics from
raw images and LiDAR scans. Our method generalizes deep
correspondence-based calibration from rectified images to raw
cameras through intrinsic- and distortion-aware correspondence
learning via synthetic augmentations. During calibration, we
iteratively couple correspondence prediction and nonlinear
optimization to jointly refine the camera parameters and relative
sensor transformation. We evaluate our approach on an unseen
camera-LiDAR configuration from the KITTI dataset and on
three real robotic platforms in a zero-shot manner. The results
demonstrate that joint calibration with our generalized pixel-
point correspondence network improves extrinsic accuracy over
correspondence-based methods using pre-calibrated cameras,
while additionally recovering accurate intrinsics.
I. INTRODUCTION
Accurate camera-LiDAR calibration is a fundamental
requirement for robotic systems that fuse complementary
visual and geometric information for robust perception in
real-world environments [1]–[4]. To this end, measurements
from both modalities must be related, e.g., by projecting
LiDAR points into the camera image, requiring accurate
camera parameters and a rigid six degree-of-freedom (6 DoF)
relative sensor transformation. Calibration errors lead to
spatially inconsistent measurements and can directly degrade
downstream perception performance.
Camera-LiDAR calibration has traditionally been per-
formed using specially designed targets and controlled acqui-
sition procedures. These include checkerboards or fiducial
markers for camera intrinsic calibration [5], hybrid visual-
geometric targets that can be detected by both cameras
and LiDARs [6], [7], and larger structured calibration en-
vironments [8]. Although target-based approaches achieve
high accuracy, they are labor-intensive and require dedicated
equipment and expert supervision. The need to repeat the
procedure after sensor replacement, vibration, mechanical
stress, or thermal effects makes them difficult to scale across
large robotic systems or fleets.
To overcome these limitations, recent research has focused
on target-less calibration using naturally occurring scene

Generalization across sensor configurations is particularly
important for practical learning-based calibration. Common
evaluation on an unseen sequence from the same rigidly
mounted camera-LiDAR pair does not establish generalization
to an unseen sensor configuration, since the camera intrinsics
and sensor transformation remain unchanged and can lead
to overfitting to sensor-specific projection patterns [16]. We
mitigate this problem by training across multiple datasets
and sensor setups, separating learned correspondence estima-
tion from geometric optimization, and augmenting camera
parameters, while focusing our evaluation on camera-LiDAR
configurations not represented during training.
In summary, our main contributions are:
1) A fully automatic, target-less framework for jointly
estimating camera intrinsics, radial-tangential distor-
tion, and camera-LiDAR extrinsics from raw sensor
observations.
2) Intrinsic- and distortion-aware pixel-point correspon-
dence learning with physically plausible camera-
parameter augmentation, enabling a single network to
operate across diverse raw camera configurations.
3) An iterative calibration procedure that couples corre-
spondence prediction with joint nonlinear optimization.
4) Extensive evaluations comprising an unseen camera-
LiDAR configuration from the KITTI dataset and zero-
shot transfer to three real robotic platforms without
sensor-specific retraining.
5) We will release the code and pre-trained models upon
acceptance.
II. RELATED WORK
Camera-LiDAR calibration methods fall into target-based
and target-less approaches. Target-based methods use cali-
bration objects observable in both modalities, e.g., planar
or hybrid visual-geometric targets or larger structured en-
vironments [6]–[8]. They provide accurate constraints and
often support joint intrinsic-extrinsic estimation, but require
dedicated equipment and calibration-specific data collection.
Target-less methods instead exploit information naturally
available during robot operation. Classical approaches use
geometric scene structure, hand-crafted similarity measures,
or sensor motion. Yuan et al. [17] and Yin et al. [18] refine
extrinsics through edge alignment, while Koide et al. [19]
combine automatic initialization with normalized-information-
distance registration. Motion-based approaches align visual
and LiDAR trajectories [18], but require informative motion
and subsequent refinement. These methods avoid fiducial
targets, but estimate only extrinsics and rely on scene-specific
geometric or appearance assumptions.
Learning-based methods replace manually designed similar-
ities with learned cross-modal representations. RegNet [20],
CalibNet [21], and LCCNet [22] directly regress the 6 DoF
transformation from RGB images and projected LiDAR data.
While accurate when trained and evaluated on the same
rigid sensor setup, direct regression depends strongly on
fixed camera intrinsics and sensor-specific projection patterns,
limiting generalizability to unseen sensor configurations [16].
Correspondence-driven approaches use learning for cross-
modal matching while recovering sensor poses through
explicit geometry. I2DLoc [15] and CMRNext [13] formulate
pixel-point association as dense displacement estimation
between RGB images and projected LiDAR measurements,
using the resulting matches for PnP-based camera pose
estimation in pre-built LiDAR maps. CMRNext further
extends this formulation to extrinsic camera-LiDAR calibra-
tion and generalizes to sensor configurations unseen during
training. MDPCalib [11] combines learned correspondences
with motion-based initialization and nonlinear optimization
to obtain a fully automatic target-less extrinsic calibration
pipeline. However, these methods operate on rectified images
and therefore address only extrinsic calibration.
Recent scene-based methods estimate camera-LiDAR ex-
trinsics through differentiable neural rendering rather than
explicit correspondences. MOISST [23] and SOAC [24]
jointly optimize neural radiance fields with sensor parameters,
while subsequent methods use LiDAR-supported Gaussian
representations [25]–[27]. These methods provide a comple-
mentary scene-level formulation, but assume known camera
intrinsics and primarily address extrinsic calibration. We
instead build on correspondence-based calibration, whose
separation of learned cross-modal matching from explicit
geometric optimization facilitates joint intrinsic and extrinsic
calibration while retaining cross-sensor generalization.
Only a few target-less methods estimate both camera
intrinsics and camera-LiDAR extrinsics. Li et al. [10] use
plane-constrained bundle adjustment that couples visual recon-
struction with LiDAR plane registration to jointly estimate
camera intrinsics, including radial distortion, and camera-
LiDAR extrinsics. However, they evaluate joint performance
only on an internal dataset, whereas the KITTI [28] exper-
iments use ground-truth camera intrinsics. Shen et al. [29]
alternate intrinsic and extrinsic refinement using normalized
information distance between camera and LiDAR intensities.
Distortion is estimated during coarse initialization, while the
joint stage refines only the pinhole parameters and extrinsics.
The evaluation is performed on several sensor suites but does
not include a standard benchmark such as KITTI.
Existing target-less joint approaches therefore rely on
geometric primitives or direct intensity alignment, often using
limited camera models or evaluation settings. In contrast,
our framework combines generalizable learned cross-modal
matching with nonlinear optimization. We learn intrinsic-
and distortion-aware pixel-point correspondences from raw
images and alternate correspondence prediction with joint
refinement of the full pinhole model with radial-tangential
distortion and the 6 DoF rigid sensor transformation.

B. Intrinsic- and Distortion-Aware Correspondence Learning
We employ the CMRNext architecture [13] for pixel-point
correspondence prediction. Each LiDAR scan is represented
as a sparse image obtained by projecting it with the current
calibration estimate, and the network predicts a dense dis-
placement field between the resulting LiDAR pseudo-depth
image and the corresponding RGB image. The displacement
at each valid projected LiDAR point defines a pixel-point
correspondence.
While the original CMRNext training procedure assumes
rectified images with pre-calibrated intrinsics and perturbs
only the camera-LiDAR extrinsics, we extend it to raw images
with unknown camera parameters by additionally perturbing
focal lengths, principal point, and distortion parameters when
generating the projected LiDAR input. We sample pinhole
parameters using bounded relative perturbations around their
reference values. We perturb radial distortion parameters
while preserving plausible signs and relative magnitudes, and
sample tangential parameters in a bounded interval around
zero. As in CMRNext, we train multiple network instances
for decreasing perturbation ranges and use them sequentially
during refinement.

but does not expose the network to previously unseen
distorted cameras. We therefore synthesize images under

D. Alternating Correspondence and Calibration Refinement
Existing correspondence-based methods use PnP or a single
nonlinear optimization to update only the extrinsics while
keeping the camera parameters fixed. In our framework,
each correspondence stage is instead followed by the joint
optimization in Eq. (12). Updated intrinsic and extrinsic
parameters change the LiDAR projection used by the sub-
sequent network stage, coupling correspondence prediction
and calibration refinement, as detailed in Alg. 1. We refine
camera parameters for the first Nintr = 5 iterations and
subsequently keep them fixed while continuing extrinsic
refinement until Ntotal = 11. Freezing the camera parameters
early prevents residual correspondence noise from causing
drift in weakly observable components while allowing the
extrinsics to continue improving. We select the iteration counts
through hyperparameter evaluation (cf. Fig. 4).

IV. EXPERIMENTAL EVALUATION
We evaluate the proposed method on the public KITTI
dataset and three real robotic platforms. The experiments
assess three aspects: generalization to an unseen camera-
LiDAR configuration, the accuracy of joint intrinsic-extrinsic
calibration, and the contribution of the proposed training and
optimization components.
A. Experimental Setup
Our training setup follows the protocol of MDPCalib [11]
and CMRNext [13]. We train on the rectified left camera
of KITTI [28] sequences 01–21, together with one rectified
camera from Argoverse [35] and PandaSet [36], respectively.
To calibrate non-rectified cameras, we also include raw images
where available (i.e., the KITTI left camera in sequences 01,
02, and 04-10) and apply the proposed camera-parameter
augmentation during training. We evaluate on the raw right
camera of KITTI sequence 00, which differs from the training
data in both sequence and camera-LiDAR configuration, to
assess generalization to an unseen sensor setup.
At inference time, we use three correspondence networks
trained with decreasing perturbation ranges, denoted coarse,
medium, and fine, as summarized in Tab. I. We use the coarse
model for the first refinement iteration, the medium model for
the next two iterations, and the fine model thereafter. Unless
stated otherwise, camera parameters are refined during the
first Nintr = 5 iterations, after which they are fixed while
extrinsic refinement continues until Ntotal = 11, as selected
by the hyperparameter study in Sec. IV-D. Each calibration
run uses 100 synchronized image-scan pairs, following [11].
For each image-scan pair, the network predicts dense pixel-
point correspondences, from which we retain 10% using
uniform image-space subsampling after the RANSAC inlier
selection stage described in Sec. III-C. We initialize camera
parameters from ground truth (GT), COLMAP SfM (COL), or
perturbed versions (GT+, COL+). For perturbed initialization,
we add independent uniform noise of ±10% to the initial
camera parameters and set the principal point to the image
center. We initialize extrinsics using the motion-alignment
stage described in Sec. III-E.
B. Metrics
We measure extrinsic calibration accuracy by translation
and rotation errors w.r.t. the reference calibration. We report
component-wise translation errors along x, y, and z, their
mean absolute value, component-wise rotation errors in
roll, pitch, and yaw, and the shortest-arc rotation error.
We evaluate intrinsic accuracy both parameter-wise and
geometrically. For focal lengths fx, fy, and principal point cx,
cy, we report relative errors. Since camera parameters have
heterogeneous scales and coupled effects on the projection,
we additionally report reprojection error. Given normalized
camera rays sampled uniformly over the image, we compare
their projections under the estimated and reference camera
parameters and report the median pixel error. We also report
a distortion-only reprojection error, where we fix the pinhole
parameters to the reference values and evaluate only the
estimated distortion model. This separates distortion errors
from errors in focal length and principal point. All results are
averaged over 100 runs and reported as mean and standard
deviation unless stated otherwise.
C. Joint Calibration on an Unseen Camera-LiDAR Setup
Tab. II evaluates calibration on the unseen KITTI right-
camera setup under three increasingly challenging settings:
known ground-truth intrinsics, fixed automatically estimated
intrinsics, and joint intrinsic-extrinsic optimization. We report
translation components in LiDAR coordinate convention,
with x forward, y left, and z up. With fixed ground-truth
intrinsics, our method achieves the lowest overall translation
and rotation errors, reducing the translation magnitude from
2.92 cm for MDPCalib to 2.25 cm and the rotation magnitude
from 0.14 ° to 0.05 °, while also reducing the variance. The
baselines are dominated by lateral translation and yaw rotation
errors, whereas ours is more balanced across translation
components and remains small across all rotation axes. The
importance of robustness to imperfect intrinsics becomes
apparent when replacing the ground-truth camera parameters
with the COLMAP estimate. MDPCalib∗, which processes
images undistorted using the fixed COLMAP intrinsics,
exhibits a large forward (x) translation error of 30.2 cm.
This behavior is consistent with sensitivity to focal-length
mismatch, since an incorrect focal length primarily affects
the inferred depth scale. In contrast, our method retains the
previous order of magnitude, with 2.86 cm translation and
0.13 ° rotation error.

D. Ablation Study
Tab. III shows that the proposed components contribute
to calibration accuracy and robustness. Camera-parameter
augmentation provides the largest improvement, reducing
mean translation error by 2.76 cm, rotation error by 2.32 °,
and reprojection error by 36.6 px. RANSAC-based inlier
selection mainly improves rotation accuracy, lowering the
error from 0.41 ° to 0.24 °. The principal-point prior Jprior
improves both translation and rotation while substantially
stabilizing the intrinsic estimate, reducing reprojection error
from 6.10 px to 3.64 px and also reducing its variance.
This reflects the coupling between principal point and
pose: principal-point errors can be partially compensated
by changes in camera rotation and translation, making the
two parameter sets difficult to separate. Jprior limits this
ambiguity, while inlier selection suppresses geometrically
inconsistent correspondences. Combining both yields the
lowest translation, rotation, and reprojection errors.

translation error is largely insensitive to Nintr (Fig. 4 (a)).

G. Runtime
Tab. V summarizes the runtime of the iterative calibration
procedure, with each iteration processing 100 image-scan
pairs. Since optimizing the camera parameters increases
the nonlinear optimization cost, we report iterations with
active intrinsic refinement, j ≤Nintr, separately from the
subsequent pose-only iterations, j > Nintr. Per-iteration totals
are the sum of correspondence inference and refinement opti-
mization. For the complete calibration, the total corresponds
to the measured wall time and therefore additionally includes
initialization, data handling, and other overhead. All runtimes
were measured on a workstation with an AMD Ryzen 9
PRO 7945 CPU (24 threads), 64 GB RAM, and an NVIDIA
GeForce RTX 4060 Ti (16 GB VRAM, approx. 3.5 GB used
by the correspondence network at the KITTI resolution of
1392×512). With the default Ntotal = 11 and Nintr = 5, the
complete calibration requires approx. 300 s, thereof about 62%
for correspondence inference, 35% for nonlinear optimization,
and 3% for other overhead. In comparison, MDPCalib [11]
reports approx. 3.5 × 103 s for its evaluated setting.


V. CONCLUSION
We presented a target-less framework for joint intrinsic
and extrinsic camera-LiDAR calibration based on learned
pixel-point correspondences and geometric optimization. By
making correspondence learning aware of camera intrinsics
and distortion, the method operates directly on raw images and
generalizes to unseen camera-LiDAR configurations. Alternat-
ing correspondence prediction with joint nonlinear refinement
recovers accurate camera parameters while achieving lower
extrinsic errors than prior extrinsic-only methods that assume
known intrinsics. Experiments on KITTI and multiple robotic
platforms demonstrate practical calibration from COLMAP-
based initialization without privileged calibration information.
Future work will investigate automatic miscalibration detec-
tion and online recalibration.
