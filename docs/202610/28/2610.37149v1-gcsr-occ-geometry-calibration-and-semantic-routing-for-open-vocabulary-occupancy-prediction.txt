# Abstract

One ap-proach reconstructs scenes with 3D Gaussians and con-verts them into voxel occupancy. However, Gaussian scale inflation and excessive overlap can distort voxel occupancy predictions, while semantic mixing can arise within Gaussians spanning multiple categories and during 3D–2D semantic feature alignment. Semantic 3D occupancy represents scene geometry and se-mantics in a unified voxel grid [30, 33, 39], supporting robot navigation and interaction with the environment [8, 10]. Real-world categories are diverse and difficult to enumer-ate, motivating semantic recognition beyond a predefined set of semantic categories [6, 31]. This paper addresses monocular open-vocabulary 3D occupancy prediction in in-door scenes, aiming to predict voxel occupancy states and open-vocabulary semantics from a single RGB image. Direct 3D occupancy prediction from a single RGB im-age faces challenges from depth ambiguity and occlusion of scene regions by foreground objects, while dense voxel rep-resentations entail a trade-off between spatial resolution and computational cost [29, 32]. An alternative is to first recon-struct the scene in 3D and then convert the reconstruction into voxel occupancy, a paradigm we term Reconstruction-then-Occupancy (RTO). RTO uses reconstructed 3D structure to guide occupancy prediction. It can leverage geometric priors from pretrained visual geometry models [3, 36, 38, 44] and incorporate aux-iliary supervision on the reconstructed scene to improve ge-ometric consistency [13, 14, 46, 51]. However, 3D occu-pancy prediction accuracy depends not only on reconstruc-tion quality but also on the reconstruction-to-occupancy conversion mechanism, which is the focus of this work. Direct alignment between 3D representations and text re-mains challenging due to the scarcity of large-scale 3D–text datasets [27, 43]. Therefore, several existing methods use pretrained 2D vision–language models [5, 28] for seman-tic supervision. These methods transfer language-aligned features to 3D through image-space correspondences [15] or rendering supervision [18], without requiring 3D seman-tic annotations [1, 34, 51]. However, 3D-to-2D mapping can associate multiple 3D locations with the same image pixel [41]. Aggregating their features can mix distinct se-mantics and degrade voxel-level semantic predictions. LegoOcc [51] is a representative reconstruction-based approach to monocular open-vocabulary 3D occupancy pre-diction in indoor scenes. Its opacity-aware, Poisson-based Gaussian-to-occupancy operator stabilizes volumetric ag-gregation under weak supervision. However, this formu-lation allows the model to increase occupancy responses through Gaussian scale inflation and greater overlap, with-out necessarily improving 3D geometric accuracy. On the semantic side, it aggregates Gaussian features before text matching, potentially merging different semantics into a single voxel feature. Although temperature scheduling re-duces feature blending during training, text queries still op-erate on this aggregated representation. To address these limitations, we propose GCSR-OCC (Figure 2), which combines geometry calibration and query-conditioned semantic routing for Gaussian-to-voxel aggregation. Our main contributions are as follows: • We introduce an Evidence-Conserving Gaussian-to-Occupancy operator (EC-G2O) , combining kernel mass normalization, p-norm aggregation, and a learn-able gain to suppress scale inflation and excessive local evidence accumulation while calibrating occupancy re-sponses. • We propose Multi-mode Query-First Gaussian Seman-tic Routing (MM-QGSR) , which improves semantic prediction through mode-level text matching and text-conditioned routing. Subclass queries and smoothed ag-gregation further refine voxel-level semantics. • On Occ-ScanNet, GCSR-OCC achieves 63 .31% geomet-ric IoU and 27 .44% open-vocabulary semantic mIoU. Compared with the Poisson baseline, geometric aggrega-tion latency decreases from 19 .76 ms to 12 .88 ms .

# 2. Related Work 

2.1. 3D Semantic Occupancy Prediction 

Vision-based occupancy prediction lifts image information into 3D to recover scene geometry and semantics [40, 48]. MonoScene [2] combines the Features Line of Sight Projec-tion (FLoSP) module with a 3D context prior for monocular scene completion. VoxFormer [20] initializes sparse voxel queries from estimated depth and propagates their informa-tion to dense voxels through attention. TPVFormer [11] uses three orthogonal planes to lift and aggregate image features in 3D, enabling dense semantic occupancy predic-tion under sparse supervision. For indoor scenes, ISO [45] combines a pretrained depth model with dual feature projec-tion and introduces Occ-ScanNet. EmbodiedOcc [42] main-tains a Gaussian scene memory across successive observa-tions for online occupancy prediction. This paper follows a reconstruction-then-occupancy framework and focuses on converting reconstructed 3D representations into voxel oc-cupancy. 

2.2. Reconstruction-Based Occupancy Prediction 

Reconstruction-based methods model scene geometry us-ing implicit fields [21–24, 37] or explicit 3D primitives [7, 9, 17, 25], providing spatial constraints for occupancy pre-diction. SelfOcc [12] represents scenes as signed distance fields and learns 3D occupancy through cross-frame im-age reconstruction. GaussianOcc [4] learns metric-scale 3D occupancy through Gaussian splatting, using cross-camera constraints and efficient voxel rendering. Another line of work directly aggregates Gaussian prim-itives into voxel occupancy. GaussianFormer [13] re-duces memory costs with sparse semantic Gaussians and converts them into dense voxel occupancy through splat-ting. GaussianFormer-2 [14] models geometric occupancy through probabilistic superposition and semantics through Figure 2. Overview of the proposed GCSR-OCC framework. 

a Gaussian mixture model. EmbodiedOcc++ [35] improves Gaussian representations through planar regularization and uncertainty-guided updates. RoboOcc [49] uses opacity-guided interactions and geometry-aware encoding to im-prove semantic and geometric prediction. Our EC-G2O op-erator focuses on Gaussian-to-voxel aggregation, suppress-ing scale inflation and excessive local evidence accumula-tion. 

2.3. Open-Vocabulary Occupancy Prediction 

Language-aligned 2D [19, 28, 50] and 3D representa-tions [26] support open-vocabulary scene understanding. POP-3D [34] distills 2D vision–language features into vox-els for text-based 3D recognition. LangOcc [1] renders 3D language features into image space and aligns them with 2D vision–language features. These methods support open-vocabulary occupancy prediction without 3D semantic an-notations. Gaussian-based methods adopt different paradigms for aggregating semantic information into voxels. Le-goOcc [51] aggregates Gaussian features into voxel features before text matching, using progressive temperature decay to reduce feature blending during rendering supervision. In contrast, GaussTR [16] performs text matching on individ-ual Gaussians and aggregates the resulting semantic scores into voxels. Feature aggregation before text matching can mix distinct semantics. Although GaussTR matches text before aggregation, each Gaussian carries a single seman-tic feature, and the aggregation weights are not explicitly text-conditioned. To address these limitations, our MM-QGSR represents each Gaussian with multiple semantic modes and aggregates query-specific evidence into voxels through learnable text-conditioned routing.

3.2. Evidence-Conserving Gaussian-to-Occupancy 

We aggregate the decoder’s 3D Gaussians into voxel occu-pancy. The same primitives are α-blended in image space, so the 2D and 3D pathways share a single opacity αi. Un-like aggregators that set the peak occupancy of a single Gaussian to 1 (e.g. GaussianFormer-2), this shared-opacity design prevents omitting α in the 3D geometry or introduc-ing a separate 3D opacity. Previous Poisson-based Gaussian-to-Occupancy (G2O) retains the shared α, but uses the unnormalized splatting kernel as an intensity. A single primitive cannot yield high occupancy confidence. Because a linear sum treats each overlapping primitive as an independent hit, merely duplicating a Gaussian increases occupancy just as effec-tively as placing it accurately. Consequently, training ex-ploits a shortcut: compensating for inaccurate placement and weak per-primitive confidence by generating more and larger Gaussians. We propose evidence-conserving G2O (EC-G2O). Evidence is a Gaussian’s contribution to occu-pancy; conservation means that the integrated contribution does not grow with scale. Given the shared α, EC-G2O decouples the integrated mass from the spatial scale and attenuates the extra occupancy contributed by overlapping primitives.

# 4. Experiment 

4.1. Experimental Setup 

Dataset and Metrics. We evaluate monocular 3D occu-pancy prediction on Occ-ScanNet, where each sample pairs a single RGB image with a 60 ×60 ×36 semantic occupancy grid ( 4.8 m ×4.8 m ×2.88 m at 0.08 m resolution). The anno-tation space comprises one empty class and eleven seman-tic categories (ceiling, floor, wall, window, chair, bed, sofa, table, television, furniture, and objects ). The dataset is par-titioned into 45,755 training and 19,764 validation frames, with all metrics evaluated on the full validation set. During inference, our open-vocabulary model processes a single RGB image. Unlike closed-vocabulary baselines trained with semantic voxel labels, our geometry is trained with binary occupancy supervision only. The semantic router is then trained by distilling a frozen Trident teacher, without semantic voxel labels. Category names enter only as text queries, and semantic voxel labels are excluded from both training and evaluation. The implementation details are provided in the supple-mentary material. 

4.2. Main Experiment 

Table 1 presents the monocular occupancy prediction re-sults on Occ-ScanNet. The closed-vocabulary methods are trained with full semantic annotations, whereas the open-vocabulary methods do not use semantic voxel labels. Un-der geometry-only supervision, our model achieves 63.31 IoU and 27.44 mIoU, outperforming the strongest com-pared baseline (59.50 / 21.05) by margins of +3.81 and +6.39, respectively. Notably, significant per-class improve-ments are observed: furniture rises from 5.88 to 26.92 (+21.04), ceiling from 17.81 to 35.27 (+17.46), and bed 

from 33.05 to 38.87 (+5.82). As an extended structural surface, the substantial gain in 

ceiling , along with the overall IoU improvement, is primar-ily attributed to EC-G2O. Specifically, its mass normaliza-tion and p-norm aggregation effectively regulate the con-tributions of overlapping primitives within the same voxel. Conversely, furniture and bed are discrete object categories that the compared baseline struggles to recognize. The cor-responding mIoU gains are driven by the query-conditioned router, which precisely assigns language evidence to indi-vidual primitives via MM-QGSR, smooth aggregation, and subclass queries. Remarkably, our geometric IoU of 63.31 surpasses all closed-vocabulary counterparts, including RoboOcc (56.48), demonstrating that EC-G2O achieves superior occupancy accuracy even without semantic voxel labels. For semantics, our mIoU of 27.44 outperforms closed-vocabulary models like MonoScene (24.62) and TPV-Former (24.94), though it remains slightly below ISO (28.71) and embodied-occupancy models trained with full supervision. This remaining gap reflects the inherent chal-lenge of calibrating fine-grained categories in the absence of dense semantic annotations.

# 5. Conclusion 

This paper presents GCSR-OCC, a novel framework that advances monocular 3D occupancy prediction via geometric calibration and semantic routing. Geomet-rically, our evidence-conserving Gaussian-to-occupancy (EC-G2O) operator effectively suppresses scale inflation and attenuates excessive local evidence accumulation. Se-mantically, the proposed multimodal query-conditioned Gaussian semantic routing (MM-QGSR) mitigates feature mixing both across and within individual primitives. These designs are further complemented by fine-grained sub-class queries and smooth aggregation to enhance open-vocabulary recognition. Geometry is trained with binary occupancy supervision, and the semantic router is dis-tilled from a frozen 2D teacher. Trained solely without dense 3D semantic annotations, GCSR-OCC achieves a ge-ometric IoU of 63.31% and an open-vocabulary mIoU of 27.44% on Occ-ScanNet. Moreover, by substantially re-ducing Gaussian-voxel pairs during splatting, our method simultaneously decreases aggregation latency, demonstrat-ing that rigorous geometric design can unify prediction ac-curacy with computational efficiency. Future work will ex-plore generative models for completing the geometry and semantics of occluded regions from a single RGB image.

Experiment Details. We use Depth Anything V2 (ViT-B) as the image backbone. Its features are converted into 3D primitives by a Gaussian head and projected onto the voxel grid by EC-G2O. Category names are embedded by a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 text encoder. Dense language-aligned fea-tures come from a frozen Trident segmenter (CLIP ViT-B/16, DINO, and SAM). Input images are resized so that the longer side is 518 pixels, preserving the aspect ratio. Geometry is optimized with AdamW (learning rate 2 ×

10 −4, weight decay 0.01 , gradient clipping 1.0), a cosine schedule, and linear warmup. The backbone learning rate is scaled by 0.1. The geometry branch is trained for 15 epochs. The splatting temperature decays exponentially from 1 to 10 −3 over the first 10 epochs and is then held constant. We set p = 2 . The per-GPU batch size is 1, using three NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPUs. The router is trained for two further epochs after occu-pancy parameters are frozen, with learning rate 1 × 10 −4.Each primitive has K = 2 semantic modes. At inference, semantic aggregation uses q = 0 .5 and the subclass queries defined below. Evaluation uses voxels inside the camera frustum. Geometric accuracy is the IoU between predicted and ground-truth occupied voxels. Semantic accuracy is the per-class IoU of the eleven nonempty categories and their mean (mIoU). The empty class is excluded from mIoU.
