Significant advancements in 3D asset creation have enabled high-fidelity 3D generation from images. However, most existing representations remain inherently static, lacking the ability to model physically plausible behaviors under dynamic interactions. We present PhysCreator , a unified framework that directly generates simulation-ready 3D representations with spatially varying physical properties from images. Unlike prior approaches that treat physical attributes as simple post-hoc predictions or rely on expensive per-instance optimization, we reformulate physical property estimation within a unified 3D representation learning framework. We introduce a voxel-aligned latent space by lifting image observations into a spatially structured 3D representation, in which geometry, appearance, and physical properties are jointly modeled and inferred in a feed-forward manner. To enable scalable learning without densely annotated physical ground truth, we leverage vision-language distillation to transfer material-aware semantic priors from powerful vision-language models into spatially grounded voxel-level supervision. Experiments demonstrate that our method achieves superior visual quality, physical plausibility, and generalization compared to prior approaches. 

# 1 Introduction 

Recent advances in 3D generation and reconstruction have enabled the creation of high-fidelity visual assets from images, significantly improving the realism of virtual environments. Methods based on neural rendering (Mildenhall et al., 2021; Poole et al., 2023) and 3D Gaussian representations (Kerbl et al., 2023; Li et al., 2025) can produce photorealistic geometry and appearance, supporting applications in graphics, robotics, and simulation. However, these representations remain inherently static: while they capture how objects look, they do not model how they behave under physical interactions. Enabling simulation-ready 3D assets therefore requires not only geometry and appearance, but also physically meaningful properties such as stiffness and density. Inferring such physical properties from images is challenging, as quantities such as Young’s modulus and density are not directly observable. Existing approaches either rely on per-instance optimization (Zhang et al., 2024; Huang et al., 2025; Lin et al., 2025b) or adopt modular pipelines for separate reconstruction and semantic reasoning (Lin et al., 2025a). A key limitation underlying these approaches is that geometry reconstruction and physical property estimation are typically performed in separate stages, rather than being jointly modeled within a unified representation. Consequently, they often suffer from inefficient inference, limited generalization, and inconsistent geometry–physics coupling. Recent work such as PhysGM (Lv et al., 2026) takes a step toward addressing this issue by jointly predicting 3D geometry and physical properties in a feedforward manner, which improves geometric-physical consistency and enables immediate simulation and 4D rendering. However, it operates primarily in a 2D feature space, mapping pixels to 3D primitives and using global features for physical prediction. This formulation lacks explicit 3D spatial structure, making it difficult to model spatially varying physical properties and leading to oversimplified, globally uniform materials. In this work, we propose PhysCreator , a unified framework that jointly predicts geometry and spatially varying physical properties from images, enabling efficient and generalizable generation of simulation-ready 3D assets, as illustrated in Fig. 1. Our key idea is to lift visual observations into a 3D feature representation, which provides explicit spatial support for both geometry and physics. # 2 Related Work 

2.1 3D Reconstruction and Generation 

3D reconstruction. Reconstructing 3D assets from multi-view images is a fundamental problem in com-puter vision (Hartley & Zisserman, 2003; Oliensis, 2000), traditionally addressed by Structure-from-Motion (SfM) (Agarwal et al., 2011; Frahm et al., 2010; Wu, 2013; Schonberger & Frahm, 2016) and Multi-View Stereo pipelines (Furukawa & Hernández, 2015; Fu et al., 2022; Gu et al., 2020). These approaches estimate camera parameters and recover scene geometry through multi-stage optimization, achieving high-fidelity reconstruction under well-constrained settings. More recently, learning-based methods directly predict 3D structures from images, including point clouds (Lionar et al., 2023; Brachmann et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2024a,b, 2025), depth maps (Gan et al., 2018; Yang et al., 2024; He et al., 2026), meshes (Xu et al., 2020, 2023; Liu et al., 2023), neural fields (Mildenhall et al., 2021; Chen et al., 2022; Liang et al., 2024), and 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023; Li et al., 2025), significantly simplifying the reconstruction pipeline while maintaining strong geometric accuracy. 

3D generation. Complementary to reconstruction, 3D generation has rapidly advanced with the development of modern generative models. Diffusion-based approaches have become the dominant paradigm, enabling 2the synthesis of diverse 3D representations (Zhao et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2023; Luo & Hu, 2021; Hu et al., 2024; Li et al., 2024; He et al., 2024; Wu et al., 2025). In addition, autoregressive models inspired by large language models have been explored for structured 3D generation (Radford et al., 2019; Chen et al., 2025). Recent works further aim to unify different latent representations into a shared framework, improving both generation quality and flexibility (Xiang et al., 2025). Despite these advances, existing methods primarily focus on geometry and appearance, while largely overlooking the physical properties of generated 3D assets. 

2.2 Physics-Based 3D Simulation 

Recent works aim to combine physics simulation with neural 3D representations for generating simulation-ready dynamic assets (Xie et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2024). Most existing approaches follow a multi-stage pipeline, where geometry is first reconstructed and physical properties are then estimated for downstream simulation. Existing approaches either rely on per-instance optimization (Zhang et al., 2024; Huang et al., 2025; Lin et al., 2025b) or adopt modular pipelines for separate reconstruction and semantic reasoning (Lin et al., 2025a; Le et al., 2025). While optimization-based methods are computationally expensive and difficult to generalize, modular approaches often suffer from weak geometry–physics coupling and limited physical consistency. Recent work such as PhysGM (Lv et al., 2026) takes a step toward joint modeling by directly predicting geometry and physical properties in a feed-forward manner. However, it primarily operates in a 2D feature space and predicts physical properties from global features, lacking explicit 3D spatial structure for modeling heterogeneous materials. As a result, these feedforward methods often produce spatially coarse or globally uniform physical properties, limiting their ability to capture fine-grained material variations and realistic physical behaviors. In contrast, we model geometry and physical attributes within a unified voxel-aligned latent representation, enabling spatially varying physical properties and simulation-ready 3D generation. 

# 3 Method 

Given one or more input images, our goal is to reconstruct a simulation-ready 3D representation that jointly models geometry, appearance, and spatially varying physical properties. To this end, we propose PhysCreator, a unified framework that combines a voxel-aligned latent representation (Sec. 3.1) with scalable vision-language physical supervision (Sec. 3.2). As illustrated in Fig. 2, our method consists of two key components. First, we construct a voxel-aligned latent representation from multi-view observations and learn to generate the same representation from a single image using conditional flow matching. This shared representation is then jointly decoded into 3D Gaussian primitives and spatially varying physical properties. Second, we distill material-aware knowledge from pretrained vision-language models into spatially grounded, voxel-level supervision for learning the physical properties. Together, these components enable PhysCreator to generate geometrically aligned and spatially varying physical attributes through efficient feed-forward inference. The physical decoder can further use optional semantic conditioning to guide the predicted physical-property values without manual numerical tuning. 

3.1 Unified Geometry-Physics Learning 

Directly predicting both geometry and physical properties from images is inherently challenging, particularly for spatially varying materials. To the best of our knowledge, PhysGM (Lv et al., 2026) is among the few works that attempt to address this problem. However, it operates primarily in a 2D feature space, where geometry is inferred from pixel-wise predictions while physical properties are predicted from global image features (Lv et al., 2026). This formulation lacks explicit 3D spatial structure, making it difficult to associate physical properties with specific regions in 3D. As a result, it relies on simplified assumptions such as globally uniform materials and struggles to capture fine-grained details. To address this limitation, we introduce a voxel-based representation that lifts visual observations into a 3D feature space. > 3.1.1 Voxel-Based Feature Construction

Given multi-view images {Ii}Ni=1 , we reconstruct a consistent 3D geometry using a pretrained multi-view reconstruction model, VGGT (Wang et al., 2025), which provides both a 3D point cloud and the corresponding camera poses. Based on this geometry, we discretize the 3D space into a regular grid and define the set of active voxels {pi}Li=1 , where each pi ∈ { 0, 1, . . . , n − 1}3 corresponds to the spatial index of a voxel in a discretized grid of resolution n along each axis, and is occupied by the reconstructed point cloud. To bridge 2D visual observations with spatially structured 3D representations, we lift image features into voxel space via multi-view aggregation. For each input view, we extract image features using a pretrained DINOv2 (Oquab et al., 2023) model and lift them into 3D space using the estimated camera parameters. For each active voxel center, we project its 3D coordinate onto every visible input view using the estimated camera parameters and sample the corresponding DINOv2 feature. These features are accumulated at voxel locations across views by average pooling, resulting in a per-voxel feature fi that aggregates multi-view visual evidence. This feature lifting process establishes a direct correspondence between image observations and spatially localized 3D features, enabling appearance-aware modeling over the reconstructed geometry. The resulting set of voxel features {(fi, pi)}Li=1 forms a unified representation of the object, where voxel 4positions provide coarse geometric structure and the associated features encode fine-grained visual details. To further incorporate global context, we map the voxel features into a set of latent embeddings: 

z = E({(fi, pi)}Li=1 ), z = {(zi, pi)}Li=1 , (1) where zi ∈ Rd denotes the embedding associated with voxel pi. The encoder E is implemented as a transformer operating on the voxel tokens, enabling context-aware interaction across the 3D structure. This voxel-based feature representation, together with the feature lifting mechanism, serves as the foundation for jointly predicting geometry and spatially varying physical properties in the following stage.  

> 3.1.2 Joint Geometry and Physics Prediction

Given the voxel-based latent representation z = {(zi, pi)}Li=1 , we jointly decode geometry, appearance, and physical properties. 

1) Geometry and appearance decoding. We first map the latent representation to an explicit 3D representation for rendering. We adopt a 3D Gaussian representation, where each latent embedding zi is decoded into a set of Gaussian primitives: 

G = Dgeo (z), (2) where Dgeo predicts the 3D Gaussian parameters (Kerbl et al., 2023), including position, scale, opacity, and appearance attributes. This formulation enables efficient rendering while preserving fine-grained geometric and visual details. 

2) Physical property prediction. In parallel, we interpret the latent representation z as a spatial field and predict physical attributes at each voxel location. Specifically, the physical properties associated with each voxel are predicted as: 

P = Dphys (z, cphys ), (3) where P = {Pi}Li=1 denotes voxel-wise physical properties. Each element is defined as 

Pi = [ Ei, νi, ρi, Mi], (4) including Young’s modulus Ei, Poisson’s ratio νi, density ρi, and material category Mi. The optional condition cphys encodes a high-level material description, such as “soft” or “rigid” and is incorporated through cross-attention to modulate voxel features. This semantic conditioning provides additional control over the predicted physical properties without requiring users to manually specify individual numerical parameters. Our joint prediction framework defines a spatially varying physical field over the object, where local physical properties are inferred from spatially grounded features. Since both the geometry G and the physical properties 

P are derived from the same latent representation z, the resulting geometry and material attributes are intrinsically aligned, enabling consistent and simulation-ready 3D asset generation.  

> 3.1.3 Single-View Feature Generation

3.2 Vision-Language Distillation 

Learning spatially varying physical properties requires fine-grained supervision, which is difficult to obtain at scale. While simulation-based training with SDS loss (Huang et al., 2025; Lin et al., 2025b; Zhang et al., 2024) can provide physically grounded supervision, it is computationally expensive and often too weak to train a generalizable model from scratch (Li et al., 2024). In this work, we propose a strategy that uses vision-language distillation to extract physical knowledge from pretrained VLMs (Comanici et al., 2025) for large-scale physical training. Given images rendered from 3D objects, we first use a VLM (Comanici et al., 2025) to analyze rendered images and infer a set of semantic parts together with associated physical attributes. This produces a set of semantic descriptions {text c}Cc=1 with the corresponding physical parameters { ˜Pc}Cc=1 consisting of Young’s modulus, Poisson’s ratio, density, and material category. To transfer these predictions to 3D, we leverage CLIP (Radford et al., 2021) as a shared embedding space. We extract dense image features for each view and lift them into voxel space via projection and aggregation, yielding a feature vector vox i for each voxel. In parallel, we encode each semantic description text c into a text embedding using the CLIP text encoder. We then assign a semantic label to each voxel by computing cosine similarity between voxel features and text embeddings:

# 4 Experiments 

4.1 Experimental settings 

Simulation Details. We adopt the MPM (Jiang et al., 2015, 2016) implementation from PhysGaussian (Xie et al., 2024) as the physics simulator. The simulator takes as input a Gaussian point cloud augmented with physical properties, where each particle is associated with a material type and continuous parameters including Young’s modulus, Poisson’s ratio, and density. External forces such as gravity and wind are applied to produce dynamic behaviors. Similar to PhysGM (Lv et al., 2026), we use Objaverse (Deitke et al., 2023) for training and evaluation. For each object, we extract mesh assets and convert them into the input format required by each method. 

Baselines. We compare PhysCreator against the optimization-based methods DreamPhysics (Huang et al., 2025) and OmniPhysGS (Lin et al., 2025b), as well as the feed-forward method PhysGM (Lv et al., 2026). We use the official implementations and recommended configurations for all baseline methods. DreamPhysics and OmniPhysGS require per-instance test-time optimization, meaning their model has to be retrained for each new input. In contrast, PhysGM and PhysCreator requires only one forward pass, without any input-specific optimization or parameter updates at test time. 

4.2 Results 

Following the evaluation protocol of PhysGM (Lv et al., 2026), we use CLIP-based semantic similarity (CLIP sim ) and user-study scores (U score ) as quantitative metrics. CLIP sim measures the semantic alignment between the rendered simulation outcomes and their corresponding textual descriptions of the physical phenomena, while U score reflects human judgments of perceptual quality and physical realism. Our user study involves 26 participants and is conducted under a blind setting using a 5-point Likert scale. We evaluate all baselines using their official implementations and recommended configurations. As shown in Table 1, PhysCreator compares favorably with state-of-the-art approaches (Huang et al., 2025; Lin et al., 2025b; Lv et al., 2026). PhysCreator with multi-view input achieves the highest average scores, benefiting from the additional visual information provided by multiple views. Nevertheless, PhysCreator with a single view closely matches its multi-view counterpart (PhysCreator ∗), demonstrating that the single-view generative model preserves high quality despite receiving substantially less input information. Moreover, PhysCreator consistently outperforms PhysGM, which demonstrates that PhysCreator more effectively recovers semantically appropriate and perceptually plausible physical behaviors from a single image. 7PhysGM

4.3 Analysis and Discussion  

> 4.3.1 Spatially Varying Material Modeling

Modeling spatially varying material properties is crucial for achieving realistic dynamics in composite objects. PhysCreator predicts voxel-level physical attributes, enabling different object parts to respond independently according to their material characteristics. As illustrated in Fig. 4, the flower exhibits natural swaying while the pot remains stable under wind forces, and the snow deforms realistically while the hat preserves its structure. By comparison, methods that assume globally uniform materials, such as PhysGM, often produce unrealistic deformation or structural collapse in similar scenarios. This part-aware material modeling produces flexible yet stable motion across all components, resulting in physically plausible and visually coherent simulations. These results highlight the effectiveness of spatially varying material modeling for generating simulation-ready 3D assets.

> 4.3.3 Controllable Physical Generation

We evaluate the effectiveness of text conditioning for controlling physical properties. As shown in Table 4, PhysCreator exhibits substantially higher sensitivity to textual prompts compared to DreamPhysics (Huang et al., 2025), which also supports text-based control. This result indicates that PhysCreator can effectively adjust physical parameters in accordance with high-level semantic descriptions, while the baseline shows limited responsiveness. Fig. 5 provides qualitative comparisons under different prompts. The figure shows simulated dynamics of a tree under wind forces with different textual prompts. With text conditioning, our model produces distinct and physically consistent behaviors aligned with the prompts, yielding stiffer motion under “hard” and more flexible deformation under “soft”. In contrast, DreamPhysics results in nearly indistinguishable dynamics across different prompts, indicating a lack of effective control. These results demonstrate that the proposed conditioning mechanism enables controllable physical generation from high-level semantic descriptions without manual tuning of individual physical parameters. 

# 5 Conclusion 

We present PhysCreator, a unified framework for generating simulation-ready 3D assets with both geometry and spatially varying physical properties from images. By lifting visual observations into a voxel-aligned latent representation, PhysCreator jointly models geometry, appearance, and physical attributes within a shared 3D space. To alleviate scarce physical annotations, we distill material-aware VLM knowledge into voxel-level supervision and improve pseudo-label reliability through simulation-based verification. The learned representation further supports semantic control over coupled physical attributes. Experiments show that PhysCreator efficiently generates high-quality, physically plausible, and simulation-ready 3D assets across diverse objects. 10 References
