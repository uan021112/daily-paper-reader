Abstract 

Estimating a camera pose from 3D-2D correspondences — Perspective-n-Point — is one of the most fundamental problems in computer vision. Existing methods either solve a minimal problem or replace the objective with an easier one. Surprisingly, no scalable certifiable solver for the actual Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE) exists. We propose PnP*, the first practical solver that guarantees efficient computation and returns a certifiable globally optimal solution. We prove that the underlying convex relaxation is exact under moderate noise, which to our knowledge is the first stability guarantee for any relaxation for the MLE of PnP. Furthermore, we reduce the relaxation by exploiting chordal sparsity pattern and quotient ring reduction such that the relaxation is tractable and scalable. The stability and scalability results are verified numerically, as PnP* returns a provably correct camera pose at over 10 Hz. 

Keywords: Perspective-n-Point, Absolute pose estimation, Certifiable solver, Global solver 

# 1 Introduction 

Perspective-n-Point (PnP) estimates a camera’s pose given N correspondences between 3D points and 2D measurements. It is one of the most funda-mental problems in computer vision, with various applications such as structure-from-motion, visual navigation, and augmented reality. The Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE) of PnP is non-convex because (i) the camera perspective projection is a rational function of the pose, and (ii) the feasible set of the pose is also non-convex. A natural approach is to min-imize it locally through a nonlinear solver, e.g., Levenberg–Marquardt, but local methods require a good initialization. Common methods therefore solve an approximation of the problem in order to obtain an initial point. Minimal solvers fix a small number of correspondences, which yields some equation system that can be solved effi-ciently. However, a minimal set is insufficient to average down the noise, and leaves open which correspondences to select. Non-minimal solvers ,on the other hand, replace the MLE objective by an alternative error 1 that can be solved effi-ciently or even globally, but their solutions are for a wrong objective. Finally, branch-and-bound optimizes the MLE objective directly, but its com-putation time can grow exponentially in the worst case (see Table 1).

We reformu-late PnP as a quadratic program by introducing only the necessary auxiliary variables, and adding redundant constraints such that the problem’s sparsity pattern is preserved. The rest of the paper is organized as follows. Section 2 reviews prior work on PnP and certi-fiable solvers. Section 3 introduces the notation and preliminaries we use throughout. Section 4 derives the relaxation for the MLE of PnP, and Section 5 proves that the relaxation is stable. Section 6 reformulates the relaxation into a scal-able one. Section 7 validates the stability and scal-ability results numerically, and finally, Section 8 concludes. 

# 2 Related work 

## 2.1 Perspective-n-Point 

Perspective-n-Point (PnP) is difficult because the perspective projection is a rational function, hence non-convex. Minimal solvers fix the number of cor-respondences (3 correspondences for PnP), which yields a small equation system that can be solved efficiently. Solvers in this family include Ding, Yang, Larsson, Olsson, and ˚ Astr¨ om (2023); Gao, Hou, Tang, and Cheng (2003); Ke and Roumeliotis (2017); Kneip, Scaramuzza, and Siegwart (2011); Persson and Nordberg (2018). In practice, they are paired with RANSAC (Fischler & Bolles, 1981) and serve as hypothesis generators. Non-minimal solvers replace the objective such that it can be solved efficiently, or even globally through certifiable solvers. In particular, Lep-etit, Moreno-Noguer, and Fua (2009) linearize an alternative weighted reprojection error 2, Terzakis and Lourakis (2020) minimize it by sequential quadratic programs, and Zheng, Kuang, Sugi-moto, ˚ Astr¨ om, and Okutomi (2013) find the crit-ical points of the alternative error using Gr¨ obner basis (Kukelova, Bujnak, & Pajdla, 2008). Hesch and Roumeliotis (2011) and Kneip, Li, and Seo (2014) find the critical points of another alterna-tive point-line error 3. Sun and Deng (2020) and Agostinho, Gomes, and Del Bue (2023) provide certifiable solvers for the weighted reprojection error and point-line error, respectively, and empir-ically return the corresponding global solution. Nevertheless, those optimal solutions are optimal with respect to some alternative error instead of the actual MLE objective. Finally, branch-and-bound methods (Olsson, Kahl, & Oskarsson, 2006) optimize the MLE objective directly, but the computation time can grow exponentially in the worst case. Subsequent work again modifies the objective by adopting the 

L∞ norm (Enqvist & Kahl, 2008; Hartley & Kahl, 2009) to speed up computation. 

> 2The weighted reprojection error multiplies each residual by the denominator of the rational term such that the new (weighted) residual is linear.
> 3The point-line error instead measures the distance from each 3D point to the ray of its measurement, again making the new residual linear.

22.2 Certifiable solvers 

Certifiable solvers offer another approach to solv-ing non-convex problems. Many estimation prob-lems are naturally posed as non-convex quadrat-ically constrained quadratic programs (QCQPs). Such problems admit convex semidefinite program (SDP) relaxations (Luo, Ma, So, Ye, & Zhang, 2010) that can be solved globally in polynomial time. If the relaxation is exact, they also recover the global minimizer of the non-convex QCQP. This framework has been well studied in robotics applications, such as point cloud registration (Bri-ales & Gonzalez-Jimenez, 2017), rotation averag-ing (Eriksson, Olsson, Kahl, & Chin, 2018), pose graph optimization (Rosen, Carlone, Bandeira, & Leonard, 2019), and hand-eye calibration (Wise et al., 2026). Furthermore, scalability has been addressed by exploiting chordal sparsity (Vanden-berghe & Andersen, 2015) or leveraging low-rank SDP solvers (Boumal, 2015; Rosen, 2020). However, a relaxation may not always be exact. In such cases, a common solution is to con-sider higher-order moment relaxation (Lasserre, 2009) or SOS relaxation (Blekherman, Parrilo, & Thomas, 2012) for tighter relaxations, but their computation also increases exponentially. In computer vision, Kahl and Henrion (2007) applied moment relaxations to triangulation, cam-era resectioning, homography and epipolar geom-etry, and such relaxations have since been devel-oped for various problems; we refer to the recent survey by Zhao et al. (2026). To the best of our knowledge, certifiable solvers have reached PnP only through alternative errors (see Section 2.1). In this work, we build a scalable certifiable solver for the MLE of PnP. The closest work to ours (Cifuentes, 2021) proposed a stable relax-ation for camera resectioning, which estimates a general projective camera by minimizing the same objective, but without the rotation constraint. Our relaxation (see Section 4) builds on theirs by adding the rotation constraint, which changes the feasible set and so stability no longer follows. We verify the stability conditions and show that our relaxation is stable (see Section 5). More-over, developing a scalable solver for their relax-ation was left as an open problem in Cifuentes (2021). We exploit our problem’s chordal sparsity and reduce the problem’s dimension by quotient ring reduction (see Section 6), and show that our method is more efficient than existing global methods for PnP.

## 3.3 Higher-order relaxations 

In practice, Shor’s relaxation may not always be tight. In general, a tighter relaxation can be obtained by reformulating the QCQP with respect to a lifted basis that contains higher-order mono-mials, adding redundant constraints 4 that are up to degree 2 with respect to the lifted variable, and applying Shor’s relaxation to the lifted problem. In particular, (Homo-QCQP) is the QCQP with respect to the simplest basis [1 , ˜x], which consists of monomials up to degree 1 of the optimization variable ˜ x.

# 8 Conclusion 

We present PnP*, the first scalable certifiable solver that optimizes the MLE of Perspective-n-Point directly. By introducing only necessary aux-iliary variables and adding redundant constraints that preserve the problem’s sparsity, we derive a convex SDP relaxation that is provably exact under moderate noise. By exploiting the prob-lem’s chordal sparsity structure, and together with quotient ring reduction, we reformulated an equiv-alent scalable SDP that returns a provably correct 12 camera pose over 10 Hz at the minimal configu-ration. More broadly, this work extends scalable certifiable solvers to the perspective projection. Future directions include incorporating outlier robustness for real-world application, and extend-ing the relaxation technique to other large-scale vision problems such as camera calibration and bundle adjustment.
