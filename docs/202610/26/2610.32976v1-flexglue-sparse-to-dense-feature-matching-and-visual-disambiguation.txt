# ABSTRACT 

To estimate correspondences across images, existing approaches match either sparse interest points, which is highly efficient but limited by repeatability, or dense pixel grids, which is robust but expensive. Furthermore, these approaches are trained with only matching pairs and thus often fail to reject unrelated pairs that exhibit visual symmetries, which is equally important in applications like Structure-from-Motion (SfM). We bridge these gaps by introducing FlexGlue, a neural network for asymmetric sparse-to-dense matching and disambiguation. Given two images and coordinates of points in the first image, FlexGlue estimates the coordinates of the corresponding points in the second image while simultaneously rejecting the pair if non-matching. By searching for a limited set of correspondences among dense pixels, our approach is both fast and effective at both matching and disambiguation: it combines the sub-pixel accuracy of dense matching with the speed of sparse matching, while rejecting incorrect pairs with high precision. Experiments on multiple datasets show that FlexGlue pushes the Pareto frontier in relative pose estimation and significantly outperforms existing approaches for SfM and visual localization. FlexGlue is publicly available at github.com/cvg/flexglue .

# 1 INTRODUCTION 

Image matching estimates correspondences across images and is a fundamental building block of 3D vision, from Structure-from-Motion (SfM) to visual localization. Most geometric applications only require correspondences for a sparse set of distinctive interest points (Lowe, 2004; Yi et al., 1arXiv:2610.32976v1 [cs.CV] 23 Oct 2026 Figure 2: Architecture of FlexGlue. Each of the two images is encoded by a CNN backbone. The resulting dense tokens are fused by a Transformer that outputs context-augmented tokens. These tokens are decoded into overlap heatmaps and classify whether the image pair is unmatchable, in which case the inference stops. Otherwise, a query point in one image is turned into a sub-pixel coordinate in the other by interpolation of its feature, dense correlation, and match refinement. 2016; DeTone et al., 2018), which both simplifies the problem and reduces its cost. These points are detected independently in each image and matched by a nearest-neighbor search of their descriptors or by deep networks that learn priors on motion and scene structure (Sarlin et al., 2020; Lindenberger et al., 2023; Nordström et al., 2026). Matching is thus fundamentally bounded by the detector: a 3D point can only be matched if it is detected in both views, and any localization noise of the detections propagates directly into the correspondences. Dense matchers (Edstedt et al., 2023; 2024; Leroy et al., 2024; Edstedt et al., 2026) lift this bound by matching all pixels directly, and are remarkably robust to large viewpoint and appearance changes while achieving subpixel accuracy. This comes at a high cost: they estimate and refine a correspon-dence for every pixel, which restricts them to low resolutions and makes training expensive, even though downstream applications typically use only a sparse subset. Multi-view applications such as SfM further require discrete tracks, forcing pipelines to snap dense correspondences to detected keypoints with additional heuristics (Sun et al., 2021; He et al., 2024). Semi-dense matchers (Sun et al., 2021; Li et al., 2025) reduce the cost by matching on a coarse grid, but anchor correspondences to grid cells and refine all of them, even when only a sparse subset is needed. Existing matchers thus face a clear trade-off between the robustness of dense and the efficiency of sparse matching. Beyond this trade-off, learned sparse and dense matchers share a global blind spot: they are trained exclusively on covisible image pairs. Real-world applications such as SfM and SLAM, however, must reject pairs that depict different places, and matchers that confidently match visually similar structures, e.g ., symmetric facades, cause catastrophic scene collapses, where classical methods like SIFT (Lowe, 2004) often still perform best. Recent works therefore add bespoke models that predict covisibility after matching (Cai et al., 2023; Xiangli et al., 2025), which introduces architectural redundancy and cost, although local matching and global disambiguation are intrinsically the same task: finding overlapping context. To bridge these gaps, we introduce FlexGlue (Fig. 1), which matches sparse queries against dense context: given an arbitrary query point in one image, it predicts its subpixel correspondence in the other. A lightweight Transformer (Vaswani et al., 2017) aggregates context over a dense token grid of both images, and a cheap readout and refinement head extracts correspondences only at the queries (Fig. 2). Correspondences can thus land on any pixel, removing the dependence on repeatable detections while retaining the efficiency of sparse matching. As a result, FlexGlue is substantially cheaper to train and run than dense matchers, while matching or even surpassing them on relative pose estimation, SfM, and visual localization. Crucially, FlexGlue is trained jointly to match covisible pairs and to reject non-covisible ones, exploit-ing the synergy between matching and visual disambiguation. Since its dense context aggregation already builds a global understanding of the image pair, a lightweight covisibility head suffices to outperform bespoke disambiguation models. As training data with symmetries is scarce, we further show that disambiguation can be learned label-free from flip augmentations. Detecting negative pairs in early layers moreover accelerates matching, in contrast to prior efficient matchers (Lindenberger et al., 2023), which stop early on easy covisible pairs but run the full network on unmatchable ones. 2In summary, our main contributions are as follows: (i) Efficient sparse-to-dense matching: a simple architecture that combines the efficiency of sparse matchers with the robustness and accuracy of dense matchers, achieving state-of-the-art accuracy on some two-view estimation and indoor visual localization benchmarks. (ii) Joint matching and disambiguation: a joint formulation that outperforms dedicated disam-biguation models at 4× higher throughput and stops early on unmatchable pairs. It significantly improves the robustness of SfM on ETH3D and the CVPR 2025 Image Matching Challenge. (iii) Indoor doppelganger benchmark: the first indoor and cross-device benchmark for visual dis-ambiguation, which we found is a crucial blindspot of existing methods, built from LaMAR (Sar-lin et al., 2022) with hard-mined negatives. Our method improves by a large margin there. 

# 2 RELATED WORK 

Sparse feature matching detects keypoints independently in each image (Lowe, 2004; Yi et al., 2016; DeTone et al., 2018; Tyszkiewicz et al., 2020; Revaud et al., 2019; Shenoi et al., 2026; Zhao et al., 2023) and matches them by a nearest-neighbor search of their descriptors (DeTone et al., 2018; Mishchuk et al., 2017; Tyszkiewicz et al., 2020; Zhao et al., 2023) or with deep networks that predict a partial assignment (Sarlin et al., 2020; Lindenberger et al., 2023; Nordström et al., 2026). These approaches are efficient and naturally yield the discrete tracks used in multi-view geometry, but since detection is independent per image, their accuracy is upper-bounded by the repeatability and localization noise of the detector. Moreover, their attention aggregates context only at the keypoint locations, even when guided by foundation-model features as in OmniGlue (Jiang et al., 2024), which limits the global understanding of the scene, in particular in textureless or repetitive regions. In contrast, FlexGlue aggregates context over a dense grid independently of the keypoints and uses them only as queries, retaining the efficiency and the discrete tracks of sparse matchers. 

Dense and semi-dense matching circumvents the repeatability bottleneck by estimating corre-spondences directly on image grids (Edstedt et al., 2023; 2024; 2026; Zhang et al., 2025). Dense matchers achieve impressive robustness and accuracy by refining a correspondence for every pixel in a coarse-to-fine scheme, whose cost grows with the image resolution. MASt3R (Leroy et al., 2024) additionally regresses dense 3D pointmaps. Semi-dense matchers (Sun et al., 2021; Wang et al., 2024; Li et al., 2025) reduce this cost with a coarse assignment on a low-resolution grid, but anchor correspondences to its cells, which complicates the extraction of discrete multi-view tracks (He et al., 2024). Our sparse-to-dense formulation aggregates context only on a coarse grid and reads out subpixel correspondences at sparse queries, whose cost scales linearly with their number. Querying keypoints in both images further yields discrete tracks. 

Sparse-to-dense matching bypasses keypoint detection in the target image by matching sparse queries against its dense features. S2DNet (Germain et al., 2020) casts this as a dense classification over CNN hypercolumns, and CAPS (Wang et al., 2020a) uses it to learn descriptors from camera poses only. Both rely on local receptive fields and lack the global context needed to resolve ambiguities, while COTR (Jiang et al., 2021) has global context but refines each query by slow recursive zoom-ins. RCM (Lu & Du, 2024) follows the design of semi-dense matchers but replaces the coarse grid of one view, chosen per pair, by sparse keypoints, which limits its context in that view to the keypoints. Differently, FlexGlue aggregates context over the full dense grids of both images. 

Visual disambiguation is critical to reject non-covisible pairs and visual symmetries, which oth-erwise cause catastrophic failures in 3D reconstruction. Pipelines shortlist pairs with global image retrieval (Arandjelovic et al., 2016; Izquierdo & Civera, 2024; Berton & Masone, 2025) and verify them by matching and view-graph reasoning (Heinly et al., 2014), which fails on subtle symmetries, so-called doppelgangers, since learned matchers are trained on covisible pairs only. VidMap (Pataki et al., 2026) resolves doppelgangers by enforcing sequential constraints, which are absent in unordered collections. Dedicated models (Cai et al., 2023; Xiangli et al., 2025) predict the covisibility of a pair after matching, but add computational overhead, generalize poorly beyond their training data, and are evaluated on outdoor landmarks only. In contrast, FlexGlue predicts point-level matches and pair-level covisibility within a single model, benefiting from joint estimation and, following the adaptive depth of LightGlue (Lindenberger et al., 2023), exits early on unmatchable pairs. 3features matches matches after filtering

# 3 METHOD 

Problem formulation: Given two images Ia, Ib ∈ R3×H×W and their corresponding N and M

keypoints, with coordinates Ka ∈ RN ×2 and Kb ∈ RM ×2, we aim to estimate two sets of sparse-to-dense correspondences. Concretely, for each keypoint in Ia, FlexGlue predicts its corresponding 2D coordinates pa→b ∈ R2 in Ib, and vice versa, pb→a. FlexGlue also predicts whether each point is matchable – a semantic signal indicating whether the point is visible in the other image. From the estimated correspondences, we derive point-to-point assignments between keypoints via geometric matching based on their distances in the images. We further estimate a score whether the two images observe the same scene, in spite of e.g ., visual symmetries. 

Overview: As illustrated in Fig. 2, a frozen pretrained CNN extracts features from each image, which we fuse across layers into a coarse grid of tokens. A stack of L identical layers updates these tokens with self- and cross-attention, while a covisibility head decides at each layer whether the two images overlap and halts inference early on non-overlapping pairs. A lightweight head then reads out, for each query keypoint, a correspondence pa→b with confidence c and matchability m, which a second head refines to subpixel accuracy. 3.1 COARSE MATCHING

Since the coarse readout already places well above 80% of queries within 2 px of their ground truth, the remaining error is local. Instead of a separate fine-scale CNN as in dense matchers (Edstedt et al., 2023; 2024; 2026), we follow EDM (Li et al., 2025) and predict a sub-cell offset ∆i with a small MLP from the tokens of the current layer, interpolated at kia and pia→b and concatenated. As in EDM, the MLP classifies the offset per axis over bins spanning ±1 cell, and we take the expectation, 

pia→b ← pia→b + ∆i. This requires only two interpolations and an MLP per query and thus scales linearly with the number of queries. We stop the gradient through the coarse position pia→b, which is already supervised by Lcoarse , and supervise the refined position with a robust loss (Section 3.4). In practice, it shifts correspondences by only 0.6 px on average, confirming that it corrects residual sub-pixel errors while leaving the coarse matches intact. 3.3 VISUAL DISAMBIGUATION 

Rationale: Maximizing correspondence accuracy is not sufficient in practice: non-overlapping but visually similar pairs (Fig. 3), like opposite facades of a symmetric building, yield spurious corre-spondences that corrupt the view graph and can collapse downstream 3D reconstruction. Prior work addresses this as a separate post-processing step, conditioned on pre-extracted correspondences (Cai et al., 2023) or on a pretrained two-view geometry model (Xiangli et al., 2025). We instead integrate disambiguation directly into the matcher, motivated by the observation of LightGlue (Lindenberger et al., 2023) that deep matchers identify covisibility already in early layers, which we confirm for our model (Fig. 5).

Motivation: Existing doppelganger benchmarks (Cai et al., 2023; Xiangli et al., 2025) consist exclusively of outdoor scenes. Indoor environments, however, exhibit their own, particularly hard failure cases: different floors or hallways of the same building often look nearly identical, while repetitive textures, low visual diversity, challenging lighting, and small scene depth further limit the cues available for disambiguation. To evaluate this so far unexplored setting, we introduce a doppelganger benchmark that includes indoor pairs. 

Labels: We build the benchmark on three large scenes of LaMAR (Sarlin et al., 2022) (CAB, HGE, LIN), which provides accurate ground-truth poses and meshes. We compute the overlap of each image pair by reprojecting depth rendered from the meshes with the ground-truth poses, and label a pair as positive if its overlap is 10–80% in both directions and as negative if it is zero (Section C.1). Randomly sampled non-overlapping pairs are trivially rejected. We therefore mine hard negatives, Fig. 4, with the off-the-shelf image retrieval model MegaLoc (Berton & Masone, 2025), selecting visually similar yet non-overlapping pairs. This mirrors practical SfM pipelines, which shortlist candidate pairs by retrieval before matching. As positives, we include both high-similarity pairs, such that the retrieval score alone does not separate the classes, and pairs with very low overlap, which are critical to retain for loop closure. Each scene contains 1000 pairs, 500 positive and 500 negative. 

Availability of training data: Collecting indoor hard negatives at scale is difficult: unlike internet photo collections, indoor images carry no GPS tags that would reveal visually similar but distinct places. Consequently, all existing training sets for visual disambiguation are outdoor, and we evaluate all methods zero-shot to measure how well they generalize across this domain gap. As shown in Table 3, all methods drop substantially compared to the pure outdoor benchmarks. 4.1 IMPLEMENTATION DETAILS 

We train FlexGlue on eight datasets: MegaDepth (Li & Snavely, 2018), BlendedMVS (Yao et al., 2020), ScanNet++ (Yeshwanth et al., 2023), the TartanAir (Wang et al., 2020b) split of MapA-nything (Keetha et al., 2026), Hypersim (Roberts et al., 2021), Map-free (Arnold et al., 2022), AerialMegaDepth (Vuong et al., 2025) and DL3DV (Ling et al., 2024) with balanced sampling over scenes. For all datasets we sample pairs with dense visual overlap, normalized by area with depth coverage, in [0 .05 , 0.8] . Images are resized to 512 ×512 px. We train for 50 epochs with 150k samples per epoch, a batch size of 16 per device, and mixed precision on 4 H200 GPUs ( ∼4 GPU-days). With gradient checkpointing the same model can be trained on 4 RTX 4090 in about 1.5 days. We use a learning rate of 2 · 10 −4, held constant for 5 epochs, then decayed to 2 · 10 −5 over the remaining 45 epochs. The matching temperature is initialized to 10 and learned independently per layer. We use the ConvNeXt-B backbone of DINOv3, a transformer with 9 layers and an internal dimension of 512, a 64-dimensional matching projection W , and 16 offset bins per axis in the refinement head. We apply light photometric augmentations, and additionally continuous rotation and perspective augmentations in the 10-epoch fine-tuning stage. In the latter, we reset the learning rate to 5 · 10 −5 and mix in Doppelgangers (Cai et al., 2023) pairs (50% positive, 50% negative) and flip augmentations to generate additional hard negatives. At inference, we use a pair threshold t=0 .9 and decay γ=0 .9.4.2 RELATIVE POSE ESTIMATION 

Setup: We evaluate image matching via calibrated relative pose estimation on two established benchmarks: MegaDepth-1500 (Sun et al., 2021), consisting of outdoor phototourism image pairs, and ScanNet-1500 (Dai et al., 2017; Sarlin et al., 2020), an indoor benchmark with challenging self-similarities and textureless regions. We further evaluate all methods on the challenging HardMatch benchmark (Nordström et al., 2026) which contains image pairs under extreme appearance variations. We provide additional details in the supplementary materials. 

Results: As reported in Table 1, in the sparse setting, FlexGlue performs similar to LoMa (Nord-ström et al., 2026). The large improvement on indoor scenes – where sparse matchers traditionally struggle due to repetitive textures and limited visual diversity – demonstrates that grid-based context aggregation is more effective there than between sparse keypoints. In the sparse-to-dense setting, our method performs competitively with RoMav2 while being substantially faster. Notably, dense correspondence estimation consistently improves over the sparse-to-sparse variant on relative pose estimation, but lacks robustness on HardMatch. 4.4 VISUAL DISAMBIGUATION 

Setup: We consider three benchmarks: Doppelgangers (Cai et al., 2023), which contains phototourism image pairs that often require analyzing fine-grained structural details, ViSym (Xiangli et al., 2025), a collection of phone-captured street scene pairs, for which we use our cleaned test set ViSym-F (Section C.2), and our LaMAR-DG benchmark. Matching: Table 7 removes or replaces one component at a time, with SuperPoint (DeTone et al., 2018) queries. Replacing 1.5-entmax by a softmax causes the largest drop in robustness and a clear drop in precision. The windowed soft-argmax mainly improves robustness, while the refinement head improves sub-pixel precision at negligible cost. Fine-tuning for disambiguation slightly improves robustness at a small cost in precision. Stopping after 3 layers nearly halves the matching time but costs accuracy (Fig. 5). Unlike RoMav2, whose coarse matches are imprecise without its dense refinement, our coarse readout is already accurate, and adding the RoMav2 refiner further improves precision. 

Visual disambiguation: Table 8 ablates the covisibility head on ViSym-F and LaMAR-DG. Training for covisibility alone, without the matching losses, drops AUROC by up to 9.3 points, confirming that matching and disambiguation reinforce each other. Without the DG training data, relying only on flip augmentations and negatives from our matching datasets, our model retains most of its performance. Adding additional indoor negatives mined on NaverLabs (Lee et al., 2021) further improves LaMAR. 

# 5 CONCLUSION 

We introduced a deep matching architecture to obtain sparse-to-dense correspondences which bridges the efficiency of sparse matchers and robustness of dense matchers. By combining uniform attention over a grid with well localized query points and visual disambiguation, our method achieves the highest accuracy on two multi-view reconstruction benchmarks (ETH3D, IMC2025). We further improve over literature on all disambiguation benchmarks, especially the proposed indoor LaMAR dataset, on InLoc visual localization, and the challenging two-view HardMatch benchmark, all while maintaining competitive computational efficiency. 10 6 ACKNOWLEDGEMENTS

# F LIMITATIONS 

• Error correlation. Experimentally, we observe that the errors of different queries are correlated because of the forced, learned interpolation between cells on the coarse grid. Despite a high precision, this can reduce the accuracy of relative pose estimation, which assumes the errors of points to be independent. In particular, most errors are also along the epipolar line (61.0 PCK@1px vs. 88.4% epipolar precision @1px on the validation set), suggesting that FlexGlue learns some kind of epipolar geometry, which can be noisy. • Fine accuracy. While our model achieves high accuracy and highest robustness, Ro-Mav2 (Edstedt et al., 2026) yields even more accurate matches on easy pairs because of its dense refiner. We believe that a stronger refinement model or a dedicated sparse-to-dense refiner with finer features could improve here. • Cross-Attention spread is local in later layers. Empirically, we found that the coarse attention is often not improving much in later layers, and that the attention spread in cross-attention is very local deeper in the network. • Accuracy drops on depth discontinuities. The network is forced to interpolate, and around depth discontinuities (where depth jumps) the accuracy drops (from >90% PCK@5px to around 75% PCK@5px). The cause of this is a bimodal distribution (landing on the wrong side of the discontinuity), and our interpolation of coarse features on the query side sometimes causes a correspondence to land on the wrong side. The proposed windowed readout already partially mitigates this issue on the target side, but incorporating finer features could potentially improve even further.
