1 Introduction 

Modern 3D reconstruction models [Kerbl et al. 2023; Leroy et al. 2024; Wang et al. 2025, 2024] have achieved remarkable progress, yet most do not explicitly model compression artifacts that com-monly appear in real-world visual inputs. In real-world deployments, however, visual inputs are frequently stored or transmitted in com-pressed forms, such as JPEG [Wallace 1991], H.265/HEVC [Sullivan et al. 2012], and H.266/VVC [Bross et al. 2021]. Existing compression artifact restoration methods [Dehaghi et al. 2025] mainly formulate compression degradation at the frame level, aiming to recover high-fidelity visual appearance. Recently, latent diffusion-based mod-els [Guo et al. 2025; Yang et al. 2026] have shown strong restoration capability by recovering image details degraded by lossy compres-sion. Despite their effectiveness, existing methods remain largely image or video centric, without explicitly optimizing the multi-view geometric consistency required for 3D reconstruction. As shown in Fig. 1, restoration can substantially affect the reliability of cross-view feature matching. However, this geometric aspect has been largely overlooked in prior restoration methods. Recent diffusion-based restoration methods [Guo et al. 2025; Lin et al. 2024; Wu et al. 2024; Yang et al. 2026; Yu et al. 2024] have demonstrated strong generative priors for recovering perceptually realistic image details. They have been successfully explored for blind image restoration [Lin et al. 2024], real-world image super-resolution [Wu et al. 2024], photo-realistic image restoration [Yu et al. 2024], and compression artifact removal [Guo et al. 2025; Yang et al. 2026]. However, these methods are primarily formulated as image or video restoration problems, where the goal is to improve visual fidelity, perceptual quality, or artifact reduction. When such methods are applied to compressed multi-view observations, restora-tion is typically performed in a view-wise manner. As a result, they

Motivated by these limitations, we newly propose CompMVR ,a compression-aware multi-view restoration diffusion framework designed to improve geometric reliability for downstream 3D re-construction. Our key observation is that compressed multi-view inputs can exhibit compression-induced inconsistency across views: artifacts depend on both the codec and QP, and thus may unsettle geometry-sensitive cues differently across views. To analyze this failure systematically, we construct a compressed multi-view bench-mark by synthesizing compressed versions of existing 3D datasets under multiple codecs and quantization levels, while preserving codec and QP annotations. CompMVR uses codec and QP super-vision during training, while inferring compression cues directly from the input at inference. Our benchmark shows that compression can substantially degrade geometry-sensitive tasks, including novel view synthesis, view matching, and camera pose estimation. To address the challenge of handling view-dependent and spa-tially varying compression artifacts, CompMVR adopts a two-stage restoration framework for compressed multi-view inputs. In the first stage, CompMVR derives coding priors under codec and QP super-vision during training via a compression prior embedder, explicitly characterizing the degradation regime for subsequent diffusion-based restoration. The coding priors provide the global compres-sion context. In the second stage, the model introduces spatially varying artifact cues that capture the local reliability of geometry-critical regions, and jointly refines multiple views guided by these cues. To explicitly enforce geometric consistency, we incorporate correspondence-based supervision that aligns restoration across ge-ometrically corresponding regions, while leveraging multi-view 3D attention to facilitate feature interaction across views. CompMVR combines compression-aware conditioning, multi-view interaction, and correspondence supervision for geometric consistency. The key contributions of our approach are as follows: 

• We newly formulate compression-induced cross-view geomet-ric inconsistency and introduce a codec-synthesized multi-view benchmark with clean/compressed pairs, codec labels, and QP annotations.

2 Related work 2.1 Compression-Aware Restoration 

Recently, diffusion-based restoration methods [Guo et al. 2025; Welker et al. 2024; Yang et al. 2026] have shown notable performance in compression artifact removal. Compared with transformer-based models [Chen et al. 2022; Liang et al. 2021; Zamir et al. 2022], diffu-sion models provide generative priors through iterative denoising. CPGA [Zhu et al. 2024] pioneers the use of explicit coding priors, such as motion vectors, residual frames, and predictive frames. More recent studies further incorporate degradation-related cues, such as quality factors, into diffusion-based restoration [Guo et al. 2025; Yang et al. 2026]. These approaches demonstrate that explicit coding information can provide useful guidance for compression artifact removal. However, compression-aware restoration methods remain primarily focused on visual enhancement. Consequently, they do not explicitly optimize cross-view geometric reliability for down-stream 3D reconstruction. Therefore, existing works remain largely geometry-agnostic, as they do not explicitly adapt restoration to the cross-view geometric requirements of downstream 3D reconstruc-tion. In contrast, our work integrates explicit coding priors with cross-view diffusion restoration to recover geometry-consistent visual evidence from compressed multi-view inputs. 

2.2 Generative priors for 3D Reconstruction 

Diffusion-based models have been widely investigated for 3D re-construction [Gao et al. 2024; Lin et al. 2023; Liu et al. 2023; Long et al. 2024; Poole et al. 2023; Shi et al. 2024; Wang et al. 2023b]. Re-cently, rather than training generative models from scratch, many approaches adapt large-scale pretrained diffusion backbones, such as Stable Diffusion [Rombach et al. 2022], to provide strong visual priors for 3D-related tasks. In image-to-3D reconstruction, diffusion priors are often used to synthesize unseen viewpoints or enrich missing visual details under sparse or ambiguous observations [Lin et al. 2023; Liu et al. 2023; Poole et al. 2023; Wang et al. 2023b]. Several multi-view diffusion methods further extend image diffu-sion architectures by generating or refining multiple views jointly, often introducing 3D-aware attention mechanisms to encourage view consistency [Gao et al. 2024; Long et al. 2024; Shi et al. 2024]. These designs demonstrate that diffusion priors can provide useful structural and appearance cues when observations are incomplete or constrained. However, most existing 3D reconstruction methods do not explicitly consider degradation types. To preserve cross-view consistency, SIR-Diff [Mao et al. 2025] states that independent single-view restoration can compromise geometric constraints and processes multiple views jointly with a 3D self-attention transformer. However, SIR-Diff is not designed for compression-aware restora-tion, as it does not exploit codec- or QP-dependent coding priors that characterize compression artifacts. In contrast, our work studies diffusion priors under practical compression degradation and aims to recover geometry-consistent multi-view evidence for robust 3D reconstruction. 

2.3 3D reconstruction under Degraded Inputs 

Recent 3D reconstruction methods have explored novel-view syn-thesis under degraded observations [Feng et al. 2024; Huang et al. 2023; Pearl et al. 2022; Zhou et al. 2025, 2023a,b]. DIFIX3D+ [Wu et al. 2025] uses diffusion priors to enhance artifact-contaminated NeRF/3DGS renderings, while HQGS [Lin et al. 2025] studies robust 3DGS under degraded observations and CLIPGaussian [Cao et al. 2025] incorporates semantic guidance and 3D spatial consistency for low-quality inputs. Other works address specific degradations such as extreme motion blur to improve camera estimation, geometry recovery, and reconstruction [Jang et al. 2025]. Collectively, these studies show that degradation affects both visual quality and 3D geometry. However, they focus primarily on generic low-quality inputs, rendering artifacts, or specific capture degradations rather than codec-dependent compression in multi-view observations. In contrast, CompMVR explicitly models input-side lossy compres-sion with compression-aware priors and cross-view supervision to improve geometric reliability before downstream 3D reconstruction. 

3 Proposed Method 3.1 Stage 1: Compression-Aware Representation Learning

3.2.1 Inter-View Geometric Consistency. Maintaining consistency across views is crucial for geometrically coherent restoration. Un-like video diffusion models designed for sequential frames with small viewpoint changes [Dehaghi et al. 2025], multi-view images exhibit large viewpoint disparities, where corresponding regions are geometrically displaced. As shown in Fig. 4, applying a video diffu-sion model out of the box leads to view-inconsistent artifacts. This observation clearly demonstrates the necessity of geometry-aware consistency modeling for multi-view restoration. This challenge arises from the difficulty of establishing reliable correspondences under large viewpoint changes, occlusions, and view-dependent arti-facts. Therefore, a robust mechanism for estimating geometry-aware correspondences is essential for enforcing cross-view consistency. 

Cross-View Correspondence Loss. To address this challenge, we introduce a cross-view correspondence loss based on a pre-trained VGGT [Wang et al. 2025], which provides per-pixel world coordi-nates and confidence maps for training-time supervision. VGGT is not required during restoration inference. Implicit Cross-View Consistency via 3D Attention. We further in-corporate Spatial-3D ResNet [Mao et al. 2025; Shi et al. 2024] and 3D self-attention layers [Mao et al. 2025] to model cross-view in-teractions, following prior multi-view diffusion approaches. Unlike SIR-Diff, which initializes the 3D modules from pre-trained Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) weights, we train all 3D components from scratch using zero initialization to avoid introducing temporal conti-nuity priors that are not well aligned with multi-view image settings. These 3D layers enable implicit cross-view interaction by jointly modeling multi-view features. However, they do not explicitly model view-dependent degradation, as the attention mechanism is driven by feature similarity without an explicit notion of per-view qual-ity. Our method complements this implicit modeling with explicit correspondence-based supervision in Eq. 8, resulting in more robust multi-view consistency under view-varying compression artifacts.

4 Experiments 

We train Stage 1 for 200K iterations with a batch size of 8, and Stage 2 for 100K iterations with a batch size of 2 and 4 views, both on a single NVIDIA A6000 GPU. We adopt the Stable Diffusion 2.1 backbone with LoRA [Hu et al. 2022] fine-tuning. 

4.1 Datasets 

We train our model on 146 scenes from DTU [Jensen et al. 2014], LLFF [Mildenhall et al. 2019], Mip-NeRF 360 [Barron et al. 2022], and Tanks and Temples [Knapitsch et al. 2017], using 140 for train-ing and 6 for validation. For evaluation, we use Free [Wang et al. 2023a], CO3Dv2 [Reizenstein et al. 2021], and RealEstate10K [Zhou et al. 2018] to cover diverse 3D reconstruction scenarios. All scenes are encoded using the compression settings provided in the supple-mentary material. Both training and test datasets use four codecs: H.264/AVC [Sullivan et al. 2004], H.265/HEVC [Sullivan et al. 2012], VP9 [Mukherjee et al. 2015], and AV1 [Chen et al. 2020]. For training, we adopt six quantization levels: QP 20, 26, 32, 38, 44, and 50. For testing, we use two quantization levels: QP 27 and 42, both unseen during training. Since QP values are codec-specific, the same nu-merical QP does not necessarily represent equivalent degradation severity across codecs. Detailed encoding parameters, scene lists, and compression configurations are provided in the supplementary material.

4.4 Ablation Studies 

Ablation of the Compression-Aware Prior. As shown in Tab. 8, LQP 

provides stronger guidance than Lcodec alone, while their combi-nation achieves the best or second-best results across all metrics. The t-SNE visualization in Fig. 5 shows that Lcodec separates codec types, whereas LQP organizes representations by compression sever-ity, with both capturing complementary codec- and QP-dependent structures. Replacing the learned Stage-1 prior with a null embed-ding increases Sampson error from 0.078 to 0.081 and MEt3R from 0.1357 to 0.1361 (Tab. 9), while random conditioning shows consis-tent degradation across multiple geometric metrics (Tab. 10). These results indicate that the learned compression prior provides a con-sistent, though moderate, contribution to geometric reliability. 

Ablation on Stage 2 Components. We evaluate the impact of each Stage 2 component, including the compression prior from Stage 1, the compression artifact estimator (CAE) and the cross-view corre-spondence loss Lcorr . As shown in Tab. 11, the compression prior alone provides competitive performance, while combining it with either CAE or Lcorr generally improves perceptual quality. How-ever, the gains from the compression prior are modest and metric-dependent, indicating that it provides a complementary rather than dominant contribution to Stage 2. Nevertheless, using all three com-ponents yields the most balanced performance, achieving the best or second-best results across most metrics. The geometry-focused ablation in Tab. 12 further isolates the effect of correspondence su-pervision. Adding Lcorr improves the inlier ratio from 0.801 to 0.805, reduces the Sampson error from 0.082 to 0.078, improves AUC@5 from 0.516 to 0.531, and reduces MEt3R from 0.138 to 0.132. These results confirm the geometry-side contribution of correspondence supervision beyond image-level restoration. 

5 Discussions 

Limitations. As reported in Tab. 13, our framework requires addi-tional computation and GPU memory, and it is not yet a fully feed-forward pipeline. Developing a faster and more memory-efficient feed-forward version could be a promising future direction. In addi-tion, although we evaluate on several representative downstream tasks, direct applicability to untested 3D tasks requires further vali-dation. Conclusion. In this paper, we introduce CompMVR , a compression-aware multi-view diffusion restoration framework designed to im-prove geometric reliability for downstream 3D reconstruction. Our method leverages coding priors and cross-view supervision to re-store geometrically reliable multi-view inputs. Our results show that com-pression artifacts affect downstream 3D reconstruction, and that the restoration process should consider geometric reliability when targeting 3D vision applications.
