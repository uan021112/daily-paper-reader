# ABSTRACT 

Feed-forward neural renderers synthesize global illumination across novel 3D scenes without per-scene optimization or runtime ray tracing. Existing transformer-based renderers tokenize individual triangles, so self-attention scales quadratically with mesh resolution. Yet macroscopic radiative transport is gov-erned by the underlying surface geometry and object arrangements rather than a particular mesh discretization; finer tessellation can thus inflate token counts without changing the underlying transport physics, motivating a discretization-agnostic and object-centric representation. We introduce RADIANT ( RAD iance-based Inter-Actor Neural Transformer), an object-centric architecture for feed-forward global illumination from point inputs. By representing each object with a compact budget of 32 surface-patch tokens, RADIANT decouples attention se-quence length from polygon counts. In a single network, an inter-object scene transformer models 3D radiative exchange and a view decoder renders images at target camera poses. To capture light transport without materializing O(N 2) bias matrices, invariant form-factor attention embeds geometric distances and surface orientations directly into query-key dot products as SE(3) -invariant inner prod-ucts weighted by learned area and material gains. For primary visibility without mesh connectivity, camera rays cross-attend scene tokens guided by depth-tested point splats in a shared spherical frame. Across a 1.03M-scene benchmark un-der matched model capacity, RADIANT reaches 25 dB tone-mapped PSNR in 

5× fewer training steps than RenderFormer, and achieves 32.2 dB at 600k steps (+3.3 dB over RenderFormer and +7.6 dB over RenderFormer-V2). We further demonstrate the scalability of RADIANT, which completes a 512 2 forward pass of a 500-object scene in 0.83 s, with computational cost scaling with the num-ber of objects rather than polygons. We will release our code and dataset upon publication. 

# 1 INTRODUCTION 

Global illumination effects (such as soft contact shadows, diffuse interreflections, and color bleed-ing) provide visual cues for spatial depth and surface geometry, governed by the rendering equation (Kajiya, 1986). Classical Monte Carlo path tracing (Kajiya, 1986) and radiosity (Goral et al., 1984) compute physically accurate light transport from surface meshes and material definitions, but trac-ing rays at runtime remains computationally expensive. Per-scene methods such as neural radiance fields and 3D Gaussian splatting (Mildenhall et al., 2020; Kerbl et al., 2023; Hadadan et al., 2021) avoid runtime path tracing by fitting scene-specific representations, but cannot generalize across new environments. Feed-forward neural renderers (Zeng et al., 2025; 2026) instead predict radiance across unseen scenes in a single forward pass without test-time optimization. Existing feed-forward renderers such as RenderFormer (Zeng et al., 2025; 2026) tokenize explicit triangle meshes, running self-attention over individual primitives ( T triangles). Because full atten-tion scales as O(T 2), latency and GPU memory grow rapidly with mesh resolution. Subsequent variants introduce windowed attention (Zeng et al., 2026) or object-level pooling (Du et al., 2026), but still require explicit triangle-based geometry and compute primitive-level attention during en-coding. 3D generative models. Prior work has learned light transport on point-based scene representations for static and dynamic indoor scenes (Sanzenbacher et al., 2020), as a direct-to-indirect illumination operator on 3D point clouds (Hermosilla et al., 2019), and as generalizable 3D embeddings for dif-fuse global illumination (Xu et al., 2026). Building on these point-based light-transport methods, we investigate an object-centric formulation that combines compact surface-patch tokens, scene-level transport, and camera-ray HDR decoding guided by mesh-free primary visibility. We introduce RADIANT ( RAD iance-based Inter-Actor Neural Transformer), an object-centric ar-chitecture that learns feed-forward global illumination directly from point inputs. In radiosity and radiative heat transfer (Goral et al., 1984), energy exchange occurs between finite surface regions and objects regardless of surface tessellation. For fixed surface geometry and materials, macroscopic light transport is the same whether a surface is represented by 50 or 50,000 triangles. By defining attention tokens over a compact set of surface patches per object rather than individual polygons, RADIANT decouples transport sequence length from geometric discretization, providing a unified pipeline for meshes and point clouds. RADIANT divides computation into 3D scene transport and 2D view decoding. A point encoder first extracts 32 surface-patch tokens per object via point set abstraction, bounding global transport to O(N 2) attention over 32 N tokens for N scene entities without requiring manifold meshes. An inter-object scene transformer then models radiative transfer among these patch tokens. To incorpo-rate geometric distance and surface orientation without the quadratic memory overhead of explicit pairwise bias matrices, we introduce invariant form-factor attention (Sec. 3.4). Pairwise Euclidean distance and surface-facing angles enter query-key dot products as exact SE(3) -invariant inner prod-ucts, modulated by learned area and material gains. For novel views, a view decoder cross-attends camera rays to the scene tokens (Sec. 3.5). A lightweight point-splat rasterizer supplies primary hit depth, surface normals, and materials, allowing the cross-attention layers to focus on multi-bounce interreflections, soft contact shadows, and color bleeding. Under matched parameter capacity ( ≈206M parameters) and an identical 1.03M-scene dataset (Tab. 1), RADIANT reaches 25 dB tone-mapped PSNR in 5× fewer optimizer steps than Render-Former (16k vs. 80k) and finishes 600k steps at 32.2 dB (+3.3 dB over RenderFormer, +7.6 dB over RenderFormer-V2). Because sequence length scales with object count rather than polygon count (32 N tokens vs. T triangles), a forward pass of RADIANT on a synthetic 500-object scene takes 0.83 s in a runtime-only benchmark, 8.5× faster than RenderFormer with FlashAttention (Sec. 4.4); rendering quality is evaluated on scenes with up to 50 entities. Our main contributions are: • We introduce RADIANT, a mesh-free transformer that renders feed-forward global illumi-nation directly from point inputs, decoupling transport complexity from polygon tessella-tion ( O(N 2) vs. O(T 2) for N scene entities and T triangles, reducing median token counts from 3,205 to 320 ; Tab. 9). 2Preprint • We formulate invariant form-factor attention, exactly factorizing selected geometric in-variants motivated by radiative transfer into query-key dot products without pairwise bias tables, paired with a rasterizer-guided view decoder that resolves primary visibility without mesh connectivity. • On our 1.03M-scene benchmark under matched training budgets, RADIANT reaches 25 dB in 5× fewer optimizer steps than RenderFormer at the same global batch size, improves fi-nal fidelity by +3 .3 dB, and runs a forward pass on 500-object scenes in 0.83 s in a runtime-only benchmark. 

# 2 RELATED WORK 

Per-scene neural rendering. Neural radiance fields (Mildenhall et al., 2020; M¨ uller et al., 2022), 3D Gaussian splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023), and Neural Radiosity (Hadadan et al., 2021) optimize continuous representations for a single scene by minimizing photometric loss or the rendering equa-tion’s residual (Kajiya, 1986). Although these methods avoid path tracing once trained, they require per-scene fitting and cannot generalize to novel geometric arrangements. In contrast, our goal is feed-forward synthesis: predicting global illumination across novel scenes in a single forward pass without test-time optimization. 

Feed-forward rendering of explicit geometry. RenderFormer (Zeng et al., 2025) showed that transformers (Vaswani et al., 2017) can learn light transport over triangle meshes by tokenizing triangles with their material attributes. RenderFormer-V2 (Zeng et al., 2026) adds environment lighting and participating media with windowed attention, but still relies on triangle primitives. Concurrently, RenderFormer++ (Du et al., 2026) pools triangle tokens into object queries, yet still executes full self-attention over each object’s triangles before pooling, which incurs O(P

) cost. RenderFormer++ is concurrent and has not released code; it requires triangle-based geometry and triangle-level attention, whereas RADIANT operates directly on point inputs via set abstraction (Qi et al., 2017b) without computing primitive attention. Other object-centric representations model scattering functions (Guo et al., 2020), object-oriented transfer (Zheng et al., 2023), superposed fields (Zheng et al., 2024), or light probes (Deng et al., 2025). In our framework, surface patches represent the physical scale of macroscopic light exchange directly. 

Point-based and alternative representations for global illumination. Meshless representations have long decoupled illumination from surface tessellation (Lehtinen et al., 2008). Hermosilla et al. (2019) learn a direct-to-indirect illumination operator on 3D point clouds and propagate its features to image pixels, while Xu et al. (2026) map point clouds to continuous 3D shading queries for diffuse transport. Related approaches aggregate virtual point lights via pixel-light cross-attention (Ren et al., 2024) or predict screen-space shading from G-buffers (Nalbach et al., 2017). Here, RADIANT pools point inputs into 32 surface-patch tokens per object, models multi-bounce transfer in 3D self-attention, and decodes HDR radiance along camera rays guided by a point-splat G-buffer (Appendix K). 

Rotary embeddings and attention biases. Spatial geometry and surface orientation determine radiative transfer. Rotary position embeddings (RoPE) (Su et al., 2024) encode relative offsets into query-key dot products, maintaining compatibility with fused attention kernels (Dao et al., 2022). PRoPE (Li et al., 2025) extends rotary embeddings to camera frustums for view synthesis, from which we adopt the CamRay parameterization. Rather than storing explicit O(N 2) pairwise bias matrices, low-rank factorizations (Wu et al., 2025) decompose spatial biases into inner products. Invariant form-factor attention adopts this principle: Euclidean distance and surface-facing angles enter as exact inner products of per-token vectors, weighted by gains predicted from surface area and material attributes. We additionally incorporate feature-wise linear modulation (FiLM) (Perez et al., 2018) and register tokens (Darcet et al., 2024) to condition on shape statistics and prevent attention sinks.

A PointNet-style encoder (Qi et al., 2017a) (10.0M parameters) embeds P point inputs per object in the object’s centered, scale-normalized coordinate frame. Using PointNet++ set abstraction (Qi et al., 2017b), farthest-point sampling (FPS) selects M =32 centroids per object with 128 nearest neighbors, where local max-pooling and linear projection yield 32 patch tokens of width 1,024 per object. Sixteen learnable register tokens (Darcet et al., 2024) absorb extraneous attention weight (−0.13 ± 0.01 dB when removed; Tab. 19). By construction, the scene transformer is equivariant to object permutations, ensuring that rendered imagery is invariant to object input ordering (Prop. 1, Appendix B). 4Preprint To express relative 3D geometry, we inject object pose into attention queries and keys via an object-centric rotary embedding ( OBB-RoPE ). Each token is assigned a 12-dimensional oriented bounding box (OBB) descriptor x(i) ∈ R12 , concatenating the patch centroid with the object’s three principal axes scaled by their standard deviations (computed in world space for the scene transformer and camera space for the predictor; Appendix F). The rotations of queries and keys compose into a function R(x(j) − x(i)) of the relative descriptor (Prop. 2). 3.3 RADIOSITY STATISTICS AND CONDITIONING 

Each object carries eight radiosity statistics shared by its tokens: a log area proxy (1), mean diffuse albedo (3), mean emission (3), and scalar mean specular reflectance (1). Learned receiver and sender encodings δqi and δkj of these statistics are added to the queries and keys of the scene transformer before rotary encoding (Appendix F), and the same statistics determine the gains of invariant form-factor attention (Sec. 3.4). Two FiLM pathways (Perez et al., 2018) (2.1M parameters each) further condition each object’s tokens on rotation-invariant shape statistics of its point inputs and, in the view-dependent part, on its pose relative to the query camera. 3.4 INVARIANT FORM -FACTOR ATTENTION

Ray tokens. We tokenize camera rays by adopting an unnormalized variant of the CamRay parame-terization (Li et al., 2025). Each pixel’s CamRay , obtained from K−1[upix , v pix , 1] ⊤ and converted to the OpenGL camera frame, preserves the linearity of the image plane. Pixels are grouped into 5Preprint 

4 × 4 patches. For each patch, the CamRay map and per-pixel channels described below are linearly projected and layer-normalized into a single ray token positioned at the patch’s CamRay centroid. 

Rasterizer channels. To avoid burdening cross-attention with geometric ray casting, we separate primary surface visibility from global light transport. A depth-tested point-splat rasterization of the point inputs for the query camera ( 7 × 7 dilation) provides a mesh-free primary G-buffer with visible object identity, depth, normal, and material per pixel. The buffer does not explicitly encode incident illumination or shadows; cross-attention to the 3D scene tokens supplies global context for predicting direct and indirect illumination. Besides reflectance, the buffer’s material channels carry only the emission of directly visible emitters. The resulting log-depth, camera-space normal, and hit mask are patchified alongside the CamRay map, ensuring each ray token carries its primary hit geometry. Object identities enter cross-attention as a routing bias : the attention logit between a ray patch and an object’s tokens receives an additive bias proportional to the fraction of patch pixels rasterized to that object, scaled by a learned per-layer signed gate (initialized to zero). For output generation, rasterized normal, materials, and depth (14 channels) are added to full-resolution DPT features via a zero-initialized 1 × 1 convolution. 

Spherical cross-attention RoPE. Cross-attention requires comparing 3D object positions against 2D image-plane coordinates under a unified relative encoding. We project both into a spherical coor-dinate frame consisting of two angles in [−π, π ] and a depth coordinate: an object at camera-space position (X, Y, Z ) maps to (atan2( X, −Z), atan2( Y, −Z), −Z), whereas a ray with normalized image-plane coordinates (u, v ), i.e., OpenGL camera-space direction proportional to (u, v, −1) ,maps to (arctan u, arctan v, ¯d), where ¯d denotes the mean rasterized hit depth of the patch (or 0

for missed rays). Under RoPE subtraction, the difference in the first two coordinates directly mea-sures the angular offset between the ray and the object from the camera viewpoint, while the third coordinate provides explicit depth information for occlusion reasoning. These angular coordinates coincide exactly when a ray points toward the object’s centroid (Prop. 3). 3.6 TRAINING OBJECTIVE

# 5 CONCLUSION 

RADIANT shows that feed-forward global illumination can be learned directly from point inputs without mesh connectivity or per-scene optimization. By grouping point geometry into compact object-centric surface patches, the architecture decouples attention sequence length from polygon counts, while invariant form-factor attention factorizes geometric invariants of radiative transfer into query-key inner products without explicit pairwise bias tables. On a 1.03M-scene benchmark, RADIANT trains to 25 dB in one-fifth the steps of RenderFormer, achieves 32.2 dB at 600k steps, and, in a runtime-only benchmark with 500 objects, runs 8.5× faster than RenderFormer. 

Limitations and future work. Our formulation uses a uniform budget of 32 patch tokens per ob-ject. While effective for typical assets, this choice can over-allocate tokens to simple planar walls and under-sample geometrically complex objects. Allocating tokens adaptively based on surface area or curvature is left to future work. In addition, full self-attention over ray tokens in the view decoder limits scaling beyond 1024 2 resolution, which windowed or hierarchical ray attention could alleviate. Our point-splat rasterizer currently handles only direct surface intersections, leaving vol-umetric media such as smoke or fog to future work. Finally, high-frequency noise in input normals can propagate into rendered planar surfaces (Tab. 24). Pre-filtering normals with learned or bilateral smoothing could improve robustness on noisy real-world scans. 11 Preprint AI USE STATEMENT

Training uses bfloat16 mixed precision with FP32 master weights. # E RUNTIME ANALYSIS 

Complexity. With N objects, M =32 patch tokens per object, and 16 register tokens, the scene transformer attends over S = 32 N + 16 tokens at cost O(S2) per layer; the predictor attends 

Q = HW/ 16 ray tokens over the scene tokens at cost O(QS ) and over themselves at cost O(Q2)

(Q = 16 ,384 at 512 2); the rasterization is one splat pass over the N P point inputs ( P = 2 ,048 ) with a 7 × 7 dilation; the DPT head is linear in HW . None of these terms depends on the triangle count of the input meshes. A triangle-level model attends over T tokens at cost O(T 2) in its first stage.

The rotate-half convention pairs each channel of the first half of the rotary block with its partner in the second half, with a sign flip. The receiver/sender MLPs of the radiosity encodings (Sec. 3.3) are 8 → 256 → 1,024 . The intrinsic-complexity FiLM reads covariance spectra, radial moments, and convexity of the object’s samples. CamRay assumes square images with a centered principal point; the ray-token projection is followed by a LayerNorm. Patches without rasterized hits carry a depth coordinate of zero in the spherical frame. 

Invariant form-factor attention (Sec. 3.4). The receiver and sender gains are two MLPs 8 →

64 → 12 · 8 · 4 over the eight radiosity statistics of Sec. 3.3, one gain per layer, head, and term; the last layer of the sender MLP is initialized at zero, so the invariant terms start at exactly zero. The 16 features per head are appended to the queries and keys after the rotary rotation, the values are padded with zeros, and the attention of these layers is computed in float32 with the softmax scale of the 128-dimensional heads. Centroids are centered at the mean of the scene’s patch centroids and divided by their root-mean-square radius (Prop. 4), which keeps the float32 expansion well conditioned; each mean normal averages, without normalization, the normals of the 128 samples nearest to the patch centroid, the neighborhood of the point encoder, so a patch whose normals cancel carries a short vector rather than an arbitrary direction; register tokens carry a zero normal. The gradients of the gain MLPs are clipped separately from the rest of the model. The extension adds 0.05M parameters (the two gain MLPs, 8 → 64 → 384 ; Tab. 18).

Disentangling 3D transport, explicit visibility, and decoder capacity. To determine whether RADIANT’s gains stem from structured 3D light transport modeling, explicit surface visibility from the point rasterizer, or view decoder capacity (16k versus 4k ray tokens, both decoded by a DPT head), we address three questions: • 3D light transport vs. explicit visibility: While the point rasterizer provides local surface grounding (depth, normals, materials, instance mask), it contains no indirect illumination or shadows. In the control without scene tokens (Tab. 20, on the model without invariant form-factor attention), the view decoder receives all rasterizer channels and ray tokens but no 3D scene tokens, and PSNR drops by 5.66 ± 0.06 dB. Conversely, holding the rasterizer and decoder fixed, restricting the 3D scene transformer to attention within objects lowers PSNR by 2.46 ± 0.66 dB and removing invariant form-factor attention by 0.52 ± 0.08 dB (Tab. 6). Thus, with the rasterizer and decoder fixed, variants of the 3D scene transformer span 2.5 dB ( 27 .50 to 29 .96 dB). • Decoder capacity and ray token density: At 512 2, RADIANT uses 16 ,384 ray tokens (4 × 4 patches) and RenderFormer uses 4,096 tokens ( 8 × 8 patches), both with a DPT decoder, at matched total parameter counts ( ≈206M). The control without the rasterized splat (Tab. 20, same model) removes the per-pixel feature injection into the DPT head and leaves PSNR unchanged, so late convolutional upsampling does not bypass cross-attention transport. # J IMPERFECT GEOMETRY AND ROBUSTNESS 

Raw geometry acquired by physical depth sensors is frequently incomplete due to occlusions. To assess how RADIANT behaves under unobserved surface regions, we evaluate two held-out scenes (five foreground objects each) where each object retains only the point inputs visible from a single simulated depth camera at the first view: points that are camera-facing, within the viewing frustum, and pass a z-buffer test against entity point splats (radius 2 pixels at 512 2). Across objects, 28%– 48% of the point inputs survive this visibility test. Resampling these to 2,048 points per object preserves the standard input budget while covering only the observed portions of each object; room surfaces and emitters remain complete. 

Zero-shot vs. fine-tuning protocol. We evaluate RADIANT at the 600k-step checkpoint in two regimes (Tab. 5), on the four original views of each scene and on eight new views per scene, path traced like the corpus from cameras drawn from the training camera distribution and never used for fitting. (1) Zero-shot inference : without fine-tuning, the partial scans reach 26.33 dB on the new views (26.76 dB on the original views), against 34.24 dB from complete geometry; unscanned surfaces render as voids. (2) Per-scene adaptation : fine-tuning on the four original views for 400 steps (batch size 2, AdamW, learning rate 10 −5, using the loss in Sec. 3.6) brings the partial scans to 37.51 dB on those views, 1.51 dB below the same fine-tuning from complete geometry (39.02 dB), but to 30.30 dB on the new views, and the fine-tuning from complete geometry does not carry over to the new views (33.58 dB against 34.24 dB without fine-tuning). The gains on the fitted views therefore mostly reflect fitting those views; on views that were not fitted, fine-tuning recovers 50% of the 7.91 dB lost to the missing surfaces. Fig. 4 shows three of the new views per scene. 

Zero-shot rendering from corrupted inputs. Tab. 24 evaluates the 600k-step checkpoint without fine-tuning across various geometric corruptions on the first 1,028 held-out images. When corrup-tions are restricted to foreground objects (room and emitters clean), reducing point density to a quarter (512 samples per entity) or adding Gaussian position noise with σ = 0 .01 of the object bounding-box diagonal leaves RADIANT at 27.1 and 28.5 dB, against RenderFormer’s 28.7 dB on clean input on the same images. Corrupting every entity (including high-coverage walls and floor) leads to steeper degradation because rasterized hit channels directly propagate geometric noise into the view decoder.

Neural representations of global illumination. Neural radiance caching (M¨ uller et al., 2021) adapts a network online inside a path tracer instead of pretraining it; active exploration (Diolatzis et al., 2022) trains a neural generator for one variable scene given an explicit parameterization of its configuration; neural vertex features (Su et al., 2025) store learnable features at mesh vertices, with an emphasis on neural radiosity; and object-centric feature grids model multi-frequency reflections in dynamic scenes (Mo et al., 2025). RADIANT is trained once across a million scenes and applied to unseen scenes without further training. 

Point-based rendering and visibility. Surface splatting (Zwicker et al., 2001) renders point clouds without connectivity using a screen-space elliptical weighted average filter. The point splat of RA-DIANT is a simpler depth-tested splat with 7 × 7 dilation that supplies the primary G-buffer. Point-ersect (Chang et al., 2023) infers the intersection of a ray with the surface underlying a point cloud with a set transformer, which enables ray tracing of point clouds, including global illumination. In contrast, RADIANT traces no rays and predicts all lighting in one forward pass. 

# L LIMITATIONS , FUTURE WORK , AND BROADER IMPACT 

Beyond the limitations of Sec. 5, the baselines are trained under our matched capacity and budget rather than their authors’ schedules; the fixed sample and token budget per object bounds the fine geometric detail that can be represented; and small scenes at batch size 1 favor triangle baselines before quadratic scaling dominates (Appendix E). This work uses only synthetic scenes assembled from publicly available Objaverse assets, involves no human subjects or personal data, and its in-tended use is faster physically based rendering; we do not foresee misuse specific to this method beyond that of rendering in general.
