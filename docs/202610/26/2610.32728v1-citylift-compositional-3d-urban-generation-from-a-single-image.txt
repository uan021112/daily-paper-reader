Abstract
Generating editable 3D urban scenes from a single im-
age requires detailed building assets with a coherent spatial
arrangement. Occlusion and perspective ambiguity make it
difficult to infer complete building geometry together with
consistent relative scales and positions. We present CityLift,
a framework that generates individual textured building
meshes from an urban image by coupling scene-level spatial
modeling with building-level synthesis. CityLift first learns
building shape and placement jointly through coupled scene
and instance streams, producing coarse sparse structures in
a common coordinate system. We then introduce generative
spatial inheritance, which maps each building’s coarse oc-
cupancy and scene-conditioned 3D feature field into a local
volume to guide a flow model that generates finer structure.
The same coordinate mapping places the generated meshes
back into the scene, preserving their spatial relationship to
the scaffold. To address the scarcity of urban data pairing
scene images with individual 3D buildings, we construct
CityLift-5K, comprising 4,997 synthetic scenes with instance
masks, building meshes, and placement annotations. Experi-
ments on CityLift-5K and scene blocks from UrbanScene3D
and InstanceBuilding demonstrate improved building geom-
etry and scene composition. The project page is available at
https://ranhaogong.github.io/CityLift/.
1. Introduction
3D urban generation supports embodied intelligence, urban
simulation, and urban world modeling [6, 23, 32]. Existing
approaches employ neural scene representations [47, 48],
asset retrieval and language-guided planning [5, 13, 32], or
satellite-conditioned mesh generation [10, 11]. Neural repre-
sentations do not directly yield complete building meshes,
while unified city meshes require further decomposition to
obtain independently editable assets. Retrieval constrains
geometric diversity to the available library, and iterative
planning and refinement incur additional inference costs.
Scalable data engines for urban world models and em-
bodied intelligence require generators that produce explicit,
textured building assets, support instance-level manipulation,

and generalize to unseen buildings and layouts with efficient
inference [32, 40]. These requirements motivate us to inves-
tigate single-image compositional urban generation, using
an oblique urban image as a joint specification of architec-
tural appearance and spatial composition. Given this image
and building instance masks, the task generates a complete
textured mesh for each building in a shared scene coordinate
system. The resulting assets remain independently editable,
with relative positions, orientations, scales, and heights con-
sistent with the input image.
Compositional image-to-scene methods [12, 28, 53]
demonstrate the potential of generating multiple assets from
one image. In urban blocks, however, three key challenges
arise. (1) Scene consistency. Generated buildings must
maintain coherent relative positions, orientations, scales, and
heights. Occlusion and height variation make these quanti-
ties ambiguous, leaving scene assembly sensitive to errors
in explicit pose prediction [31] or estimated geometric an-
chors [35, 53]. (2) Geometric fidelity across scales. Joint
generation in a shared scene frame models geometry and
placement together [20], but a fixed-resolution grid cover-
ing an entire block restricts the geometric detail available
to individual buildings [54]. Increasing instance resolu-
tion therefore requires preserving spatial correspondence
between local geometry and the scene. (3) Limited urban
supervision. Established scene datasets emphasize indoor
compositions [7], while recent urban resources provide in-
dividual building assets [14, 41] or imagery paired with
regional scene meshes [11]. Learning urban composition
and building completion jointly requires supervision that
combines these levels, pairing oblique block images with
complete building instances and their spatial arrangement.
To address these challenges, we introduce CityLift, a
generative framework that models urban composition in a
shared scene space and synthesizes detailed buildings in
dedicated local spaces. This design reconciles the need for
joint spatial reasoning across buildings with the resolution
required for individual architectural detail. A jointly gen-
erated urban scaffold connects these scales, providing the
spatial reference and contextual conditions for local building
generation.
Specifically, Urban Scaffold Generation jointly generates
coarse building geometry and layout through coupled scene
and instance streams, encoding placement directly in scene-
space geometry without a separate pose-regression stage.
To retain this spatial organization during local generation,
Generative Spatial Inheritance maps scaffold occupancy and
scene-conditioned features into aligned building volumes.
Within these volumes, Conditioned Building Regeneration
synthesizes finer sparse structures for textured mesh gener-
ation. A shared coordinate mapping governs local condi-
tioning, supervision, and assembly, concentrating geometric
resolution on individual buildings while maintaining their
correspondence to the scene scaffold.
Training this framework requires paired supervision for
scene composition and complete building geometry. We
therefore curate building assets for geometric completeness
and texture quality through vision-language-model-assisted
filtering and manual screening, and construct CityLift-5K.
The dataset comprises 4,997 synthetic multi-building scenes
with paired oblique images, instance masks, complete build-
ing meshes, and placement annotations, providing supervi-
sion at both scene and building levels.
Trained on CityLift-5K, CityLift achieves state-of-the-
art geometric performance on its held-out test split and two
external datasets. On the CityLift-5K test split, it reduces
building- and scene-level Chamfer distances by 60.3% and
58.4%, respectively, relative to the strongest baseline at
each level. Further evaluation on synthetic scenes from Ur-
banScene3D and real-world scenes from InstanceBuilding
yields the best scene-level geometric results on both datasets,
demonstrating strong generalization capability.
Our main contributions are as follows.
• We present CityLift, a framework for compositional ur-
ban generation from a single oblique image and building
instance masks. Its Urban Scaffold Generation jointly
models coarse building geometry and layout, establish-
ing shared spatial support for the synthesis and assem-
bly of complete, independently editable textured building
meshes.
• We introduce Generative Spatial Inheritance to align
coarse occupancy and scene-conditioned hidden features
with building-specific local volumes, and Conditioned
Building Regeneration to synthesize finer sparse structures
under these inherited conditions.
• We construct CityLift-5K, a dataset of 4,997 synthetic ur-
ban scenes. CityLift achieves state-of-the-art scene-level
geometric performance on CityLift-5K, UrbanScene3D,
and real-world scenes from InstanceBuilding.
2. Related Work
2.1. 3D urban generation
Urban generation methods differ in how they specify city
structure and construct its constituent buildings. Program-
generating methods [25, 56] construct editable cities through
rules and executable programs, with architectural variation
governed by the available procedural operations. Layout-
conditioned generators [17, 27, 47, 48] synthesize urban
appearance from spatial controls, but their neural represen-
tations do not directly provide complete building meshes
for independent editing and reuse.
Asset retrieval [5]
provides explicit building meshes, with geometric diver-
sity constrained by the asset library.
Generative asset
pipelines [13, 32, 40] support editable scene construc-
tion through map- or language-guided planning, requir-
2


ing scene structure to be established through layouts or
geospatial information. Satellite-conditioned methods in-
troduce image-based scene control. Sat2City and its exten-
sion [10, 11] generate scene-level meshes, which require
additional instance decomposition for building-level manipu-
lation. CitySculpt [52] refines building geometry initialized
from estimated height maps, while Sat2RealCity [41] uses
OSM constraints to spatially organize generated buildings.
These building-level approaches derive scene structure from
overhead geometric estimates or geographic priors.
CityLift introduces single-image compositional urban
generation, jointly generating complete textured building
meshes and their spatial arrangement from an oblique ur-
ban image and building instance masks. This task combines
image-based control over building appearance and block
composition with independently editable assets, enabling
buildings to be replaced, rearranged, and reused in down-
stream environments.
2.2. Single-image 3D object generation
Object generators provide priors for completing geometry be-
yond the visible surface, with different mechanisms for incor-
porating image evidence. Optimization-based methods dis-
till image diffusion priors into a 3D representation [34, 38],
while view-synthesis approaches generate additional obser-
vations to constrain reconstruction [22, 24, 26, 33]. Feed-
forward reconstruction models amortize this inference, map-
ping an input image or synthesized views to meshes, radiance
fields, or Gaussians [9, 36, 39, 42, 49]. Native 3D gener-
ators model shape distributions directly in learned latent
spaces [16, 43, 55, 57]; TRELLIS and TRELLIS.2 orga-
nize geometry and appearance through structured spatial
latents [44, 46]. Beyond isolated assets, SAM 3D generates
masked objects from natural images while also estimating
layout variables [31]. Through large-scale pretraining, these
models learn rich shape and appearance priors that provide a
foundation for building synthesis with limited paired urban
data. CityLift draws on these priors through the SLAT gen-
eration and mesh decoding stages of SAM 3D Objects for
textured building synthesis.
2.3. 3D scene generation
Scene generation requires coupling object geometry with a
shared spatial arrangement. Indoor layout generators pre-
dict object attributes or plan asset placement before assem-
bling a scene [30, 37, 50]. For single-image input, com-
positional generators couple objects or parts during denois-
ing [12, 19], while other architectures represent spatial in-
formation through explicit position prediction [28] or pixel-
aligned 3D feature volumes [15]. An alternative separates
asset synthesis from scene assembly, using geometric corre-
spondences or layout optimization to establish object place-
ment [1, 8, 35, 51]. Mira-Scene aligns canonical object
coordinates with a predicted scene point cloud to recover
object transformations. Structure-conditioned refinement
places greater emphasis on coarse geometric support: HIVE-
3D extracts and hierarchically refines components from a
generated scene [54], whereas 3D-Fixer completes objects
in place using estimated visible geometry, without separate
pose alignment [53]. CityLift shares the coarse-to-fine or-
ganization of component refinement, with a focus on the
coupled placement, height, and geometric detail of urban
buildings. Its link between stages is the joint transport of
generated occupancy and hidden 3D features into building-
specific volumes. Building arrangement is learned in scene
coordinates, while local regeneration allocates resolution to
each instance.

4. CityLift-5K Dataset
To address the scarcity of urban scene data pairing im-
ages with complete building-level geometry, we construct
CityLift-5K, comprising 4,997 synthetic urban blocks and
36,338 building instances. Each scene provides a rendered
image, instance masks, and individually accessible building
meshes in a shared coordinate frame, supporting supervision
of both building geometry and spatial arrangement.
Asset preparation.
We curate CityLift-Asset, a library
of 42,339 building meshes from SAT-SKYLINES [14],
CityCraft [5], UrbanVerse [21], and subsets of TRELLIS-
500K [4, 44]. We apply multiview screening with Qwen3-
VL [2], followed by further manual filtering, to exclude
non-building content, disconnected building groups, and as-
sets with incomplete geometry. The retained assets receive
5


textual descriptions and annotations of function, architec-
tural style, floor count, and estimated dimensions. These
attributes guide asset selection and provide scale priors for
scene construction.
Scene construction and annotations.
We combine
MLLM-assisted layout planning with procedural parcel
placement to assemble urban blocks on a planar ground.
The scenes cover residential, office, commercial, civic, and
industrial settings, with 2–12 buildings per scene. Buildings
are selected according to their annotated function and size,
then scaled and positioned within the planned layout.
We render each scene with Blender’s Eevee engine, pro-
ducing one 1024 × 1024 RGB image and a pixel-aligned in-
stance segmentation map. An orthographic camera captures
an oblique view with an off-nadir angle sampled between
30◦and 40◦. Camera framing accounts for the block extent
and maximum building height, while sun direction and in-
tensity vary across scenes. Each sample retains individual
meshes, placement transforms, camera parameters, and in-
stance identifiers. The dataset contains 4,497 training, 250
validation, and 250 test scenes. Further construction details
and statistics are provided in the appendix.
5. Experiments
We compare CityLift with representative baselines in terms
of building-level geometric fidelity and scene-level spatial
consistency. Experiments cover the held-out CityLift-5K
test split and scene blocks constructed from UrbanScene3D
and InstanceBuilding. We report geometric metrics at both
building and scene levels, complemented by a MLLM-as-a-
judge visual evaluation.
5.1. Experimental Setup
Datasets.
The test split of CityLift-5K contains 250 scenes,
averaging 7.8 buildings per scene. Both the scenes and their
constituent building assets are disjoint from the training split,
ensuring evaluation on unseen scenes and buildings. For ex-
ternal evaluation, we use the synthetic Shenzhen city scene
from UrbanScene3D [18], extracting its scene mesh and
individual building meshes from Unreal Engine. We also
evaluate on Scene3 of InstanceBuilding [3], a real, densely
built town scene reconstructed from UAV imagery. Both
scenes are partitioned into blocks and rendered into RGB im-
ages and instance masks using the CityLift data preparation
pipeline. These tests cover synthetic and photogrammetri-
cally reconstructed building geometry.
Baselines.
We compare with TRELLIS.2 [45], SAM
3D [31], Pixal3D [15], 3D-Fixer [53], and SceneGen [28].
TRELLIS.2 is applied to the entire scene image and produces
a single scene mesh, so we report its scene-level metrics. The
other baselines produce building instances that can be as-
sembled into a scene. We additionally report SceneGen-SFT,
fine-tuned on the CityLift training data, in the CityLift-5K
comparison. This separates the released SceneGen model
from its adaptation to the target domain.
Geometric evaluation.
We evaluate geometry at both the
building and scene levels using Chamfer distance (CD), accu-
racy (Acc), completeness (Comp), and F-scores at thresholds
of one and two grid cells (F@1 and F@2). Lower distance
errors and higher F-scores indicate better performance. De-
tailed evaluation protocols are provided in the appendix.
Implementation details.
Both generation modules are
trained on CityLift-5K using 643 voxel grids. Training is
performed on two NVIDIA A100 GPUs, each with 80 GB of
memory. We use DINOv2 [29] as the image feature extractor.
We first train the scaffold generator using AdamW with a
learning rate of 3.5 × 10−5 and a global batch size of 16.
We then freeze the scaffold generator and train the building
regeneration module, initialized from TRELLIS [44], for
18,000 iterations with a learning rate of 5 × 10−5. At in-
ference, pretrained SAM 3D converts the generated sparse
structures into textured meshes. Further implementation
details are provided in the appendix.
5.2. Comparison on CityLift-5K
CityLift achieves the best geometric results at both building
and scene levels (Table 1). Building-level CD decreases
from 2.547 for Pixal3D to 1.011 cells, a reduction of 60.3%.
Scene-level CD decreases from 2.595 for SAM 3D to 1.079
cells, a reduction of 58.4%, while scene F@2 improves from
0.597 to 0.855. CityLift requires approximately 115 s per
scene, faster than SceneGen, 3D-Fixer, and Pixal3D.
Figure 3 reveals differences in building proportions and
spatial arrangement. TRELLIS.2 produces incorrect build-
ing heights, altering the relative height relationships within a
block. SAM 3D generates recognizable individual buildings
but introduces errors in their relative positions and orienta-
tions. Pixal3D exhibits flattened or fragmented geometry,
while 3D-Fixer shows structural distortions and less coherent
spatial arrangements. SceneGen produces pronounced scale
inconsistencies and building overlaps. CityLift more faith-
fully preserves building proportions, relative orientations,
and spacing, while retaining coherent roof structures and
facade details.
To assess whether dataset-specific fine-tuning can close
the performance gap, we additionally fine-tune SceneGen
on the CityLift-5K training split. SceneGen-SFT still yields
higher building- and scene-level CDs of 3.843 and 3.422,
compared with 1.011 and 1.079 for CityLift. These results
suggest that adapting SceneGen to the same training data is
insufficient to match CityLift’s performance.

5.3. Evaluation on External Scene Sources
UrbanScene3D.
CityLift obtains the lowest building CD
and leads all five scene-level metrics in Table 2. Its scene
CD is 1.577 cells, compared with 2.067 for Pixal3D, and its
scene F@2 is 0.750 compared with 0.693. These correspond
to a 23.7% reduction in scene CD and a 5.7 percentage-
point increase in F@2. The gain persists when the input
arrangement comes from an extracted urban scene rather
than the procedural layouts used to construct CityLift-5K.
At the building level, Pixal3D achieves the lowest ac-
curacy error and the highest F-scores, indicating closer
prediction-to-reference surface agreement on average and
stronger correspondence within the evaluated thresholds.
This advantage is consistent with its pixel-aligned condition-
ing, which back-projects image features into 3D to estab-
lish explicit image–geometry correspondence [15]. CityLift
achieves a lower completeness error of 1.128 cells, compared
with 1.678 for Pixal3D, yielding a lower symmetric CD of
1.421 versus 1.490 cells. Since CD averages the two direc-
tional errors, the reduction in completeness error outweighs
the increase in accuracy error. F-scores instead summarize
surface precision and recall within fixed distance thresholds.

6. Conclusion
We presented CityLift, a framework for compositional 3D
urban generation from a single image and building instance
masks. Urban scaffold generation captures scene layout and
coarse building geometry, while generative spatial inheri-
tance transfers geometric support and hidden features into
local building frames. Conditioned building regeneration re-
fines individual structures, which are converted into explicit,
individually editable meshes and assembled using the inher-
ited spatial transforms. We also introduced CityLift-5K to
support training and evaluation of this task. Experiments on
CityLift-5K, UrbanScene3D, and InstanceBuilding demon-
strate improved scene-level geometric fidelity. These results
support the effectiveness of combining scene-level spatial or-
ganization with local building generation for compositional
urban content creation.
