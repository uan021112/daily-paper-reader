ABSTRACT
Geometric structure provides a direct description of the 3D world and is essential
for robotic manipulation and world understanding. Most VLA models primar-
ily rely on 2D representations of the current observation, while geometry-aware
approaches often require additional 3D geometric information. However, such
geometric information, typically inferred independently from individual observa-
tions, is often discarded rather than accumulated into a persistent task represen-
tation. Our key insight is that action generation needs a compact, task-relevant
geometric abstraction that can be continuously updated over time, rather than a
complete, renderable 3D reconstruction at every step. Based on this insight, we
introduce GeoMem-VLA, a VLA framework that internalizes explicit 3D super-
vision into dedicated geometric tokens, without directly aligning the VLM’s native
visual embeddings to the teacher feature space, and maintains them as temporal
memory for action generation. During training, a frozen geometry foundation
model supervises learnable GeoPlan tokens through a geometric flow-matching
objective over the current scene point map and future end-effector tracks. These
tokens are subsequently integrated with the memory of observation representa-
tions, where historical information is retrieved, adaptively fused with current ob-
servations, and consolidated to keep event frames as the task progresses. At infer-
ence, the policy retrieves historical representations and fuses them with the cur-
rent working representation to predict actions, without invoking the 3D teacher or
explicitly reconstructing the scene. After benchmark-specific downstream adap-
tation, GeoMem-VLA achieves 98.9% average success on LIBERO and 91.10% /
91.24% on the clean/randomized settings of the RoboTwin benchmark. In 5 real-
world tasks, GeoMem-VLA achieves the 78.0% average success. These results
indicate that internalizing and remembering 3D scene understanding is a promis-
ing approach to optimizing robot control strategies.

INTRODUCTION
Robotic manipulation requires combining semantic understanding with precise 3D control. Building
on advances in large language models, vision-language-action (VLA) models have attracted atten-
tion for language-conditioned manipulation (Brohan et al., 2023; Kim et al., 2024; Black et al., 2024;
Shi et al., 2026b). Most use pretrained vision-language models (VLMs) for visual and semantic un-
derstanding and generate actions through tokenized prediction or dedicated action experts. However,
their reliance on current 2D observations offers limited support for precise 3D spatial reasoning and
decision-making that depend on interaction history.

Recent work on 3D-aware VLAs has explored how to leverage the rich geometric priors of the phys-
ical environment (Li et al., 2026a; Qian et al., 2026). Such supervision allows explicit geometric
targets to shape policy representations that require explicit reconstruction during action inference.
Memory-augmented policies, meanwhile, retain perceptual and task context from earlier observa-
tions (Shi et al., 2026a). Connecting these directions raises a central question: How can geometric
supervision shape spatial memory that guides action? This places the spatial content of memory
at the center of policy representation learning.
We propose GeoMem-VLA, a VLA framework that couples geometry-supervised GeoPlan repre-
sentations with causal episode-level memory. During training, learnable GeoPlan tokens summarize
the multimodal context and condition an auxiliary flow-matching head that predicts current-scene
point maps and future Cartesian end-effector tracks. A frozen VGGT teacher (Wang et al., 2025) pro-
vides scene-geometry targets, while demonstration trajectories provide future motion supervision.
These complementary targets train the tokens to encode scene structure and task-related motion. At
deployment, the teacher and reconstruction head are removed from the action-generation path; the
learned tokens provide compact geometric context for the policy.
To preserve this context, we maintain geometric memory alongside perceptual and task-related
memory. Current representations retrieve relevant preceding features, and adaptive fusion integrates
historical context before action generation. Similarity-based consolidation compresses redundant
entries while retaining feature-distinct temporal states. We further use event-aware training that
samples temporally separated observations from the same episode while preserving their chrono-
logical order and causal access. This exposes retrieval to a broader span of interaction history than
contexts formed only from neighboring frames. Together, geometric supervision and temporal re-
trieval connect what the policy learns about the scene with what it can retain for later decisions.
Our evaluation examines spatial understanding, long-horizon decision-making, and applicability
across manipulation tasks. On LIBERO and RoboTwin 2.0, GeoMem-VLA achieves 98.9% average
success across the four LIBERO suites and 91.10% / 91.24% on RoboTwin Clean / Randomized.
Complementary real-world protocols target geometric alignment and long-horizon interaction, con-
necting the evaluation to the spatial and temporal requirements of the architecture.

Our contributions are summarized as follows:
• Geometry-Supervised Spatial Representations. We learn compact GeoPlan tokens from
current-scene point maps and future end-effector tracks, providing geometric conditioning
without explicit reconstruction during action inference.
• Geometry-Aware Event Memory. We couple geometric representations with causally
temporal retrieval and train event-aware memory to retain key information alongside per-

• Evaluation Across Manipulation Settings. Experiments of geometry-supervised memory
across diverse tasks through LIBERO, RoboTwin and real-world manipulation tasks exam-
ine geometric precision and the use of historical context in long-horizon manipulation.

RELATED WORK
Vision-Language-Action Models.
Advances in VLMs (Liu et al., 2024; Karamcheti et al., 2024)
have enabled VLAs through robot-data scaling, vision-language pretraining, and expressive action
decoders. RT-1 (Brohan et al., 2022) scales multitask learning on demonstrations; RT-2 (Brohan
et al., 2023) and OpenVLA (Kim et al., 2024) transfer pretrained knowledge to discrete action pre-
diction. π0 (Black et al., 2024), CogACT (Li et al., 2024a), and GR00T (Bjorck et al., 2025) use
continuous action experts, while π0.5 (Intelligence et al., 2025) adds heterogeneous data and se-
mantic subtask prediction for open-world generalization. FAST (Pertsch et al., 2025) compresses
action sequences via frequency-domain tokenization; OpenVLA-OFT (Kim et al., 2025) uses par-
allel decoding and action chunking for efficient execution. StarVLA (Community, 2026) provides
a modular backbone–expert framework. These advances leave open how task-relevant 3D structure
should be encoded and retained across decisions.
3D Representations and Geometric Supervision.
Spatially informed VLAs use depth (Li et al.,
2026b; Fan et al., 2026), feature-level geometric supervision (Abouzeid et al., 2025), and predictive
representations. Spatial Forcing (Li et al., 2026a) aligns intermediate visual features with frozen
VGGT features; 3DThinkVLA (Shi et al., 2026c) combines feature alignment with spatial reasoning
distillation. Other methods model motion and scene evolution (Bhat et al., 2025; Li et al., 2025b):
GeoPredict (Qian et al., 2026) couples future robot-keypoint trajectories with predictive Gaussian
geometry, while GaussianDream (Zhang et al., 2026e) learns a latent prefix through current and
future Gaussian scene prediction. These approaches enrich spatial representations, but geometric
supervision alone does not specify how the resulting information should be maintained and accessed
throughout an episode.
Temporal and Spatial Memory.
History-aware policies use recurrent states, visual prompts, and
retrievable memory. RoboFlamingo (Li et al., 2024b) aggregates sequential features with a recur-
rent policy head; TraceVLA (Zheng et al., 2025) encodes tracked motion as visual traces. HAM-
LET (Koo et al., 2026) aggregates distinctive moment tokens in a lightweight memory module.
MemoryVLA (Shi et al., 2026a) retrieves, adaptively fuses, and consolidates perceptual and cogni-
tive history; EventVLA (Yang et al., 2026a) predicts keyframes to preserve sparse visual evidence.
BridgeVLA++ (Li et al., 2026c) combines temporal observation history with explicit point-cloud
spatial memory. These designs trade retained detail against temporal coverage and cost, but how
scene and motion targets shape latent history for later decisions remains open. We focus on jointly
learning and using spatial representations across time to guide manipulation.

Deployment.
The VGGT teacher and geometry decoder are omitted from action inference. The
backbone still computes the latent geometric tokens, and memory retrieval and similarity-based
consolidation remain active. No explicit point-map or EEF-track decoding is required for action
generation; latent-token and memory operations remain part of the deployed policy.

4.2
MAIN RESULTS
Table 1 shows that GeoMem-VLA achieves the highest displayed LIBERO average (98.9%), 0.4
points above Spatial Forcing. Spatial, Object, and Goal are near ceiling (99.6%–99.8%); Long is
more discriminative, where our 96.6% ties the highest displayed score and exceeds MemoryVLA
by 3.2 points. The advantage is therefore not confined to the three near-saturated suites.
On RoboTwin, GeoMem-VLA reaches 91.10% on Clean and 91.24% on Randomized, averaging
91.17% (Table 2). It exceeds HyVLA-0.5 by 0.20 and 1.14 points in the two settings, respectively,
and the closest published StarVLA-GR00T reference by 2.92 points on average. The results show
strong performance across both end-effector and joint-action benchmarks, including randomized
evaluation. Since published methods may differ in training data, inputs, and protocols, these com-
parisons provide context rather than a controlled ranking; the matched ablations below assess the
contributions of geometry and memory.
4.3
ABLATION STUDY
Geometry and Memory.
Tables 3 and 4 compare the full model with two matched interventions.
The full model stores GeoPlan tokens in a geometric bank, consolidates similar adjacent entries, and
gates retrieved geometry into the observation context. Removing GeoPlan eliminates its auxiliary
supervision, geometric tokens, geometric memory bank, and gated fusion, while retaining the ob-
servation banks. Removing temporal memory retains current geometric tokens and supervision but
disables historical retrieval from all banks.
On LIBERO, the observation-memory variant lowers average success from 98.9% to 97.9%, with
drops of 1.6 points on Spatial and 1.4 on Long. Without temporal memory, the average falls to
98.2%; Long falls from 96.6% to 94.2%, compared with 0.2-point drops on each other suite. On
RoboTwin, removing GeoPlan lowers the Clean/Randomized mean from 91.17% to 89.71%, while
removing memory yields 90.18%; both interventions reduce success in Clean and Randomized. The
full model exceeds these variants on 35 and 27 of 50 RoboTwin tasks, respectively, after averaging
each task across settings (Table 6). These results show both geometric reconstruction and tem-
poral memory positively impact the model’s task ability. Furthermore, geometric capabilities are
particularly effective in spatial tasks. The full model outperforms the observation-memory variant,
suggesting benefits from incorporating the GeoPlan branch.

frames causally through memory. This is an offline demonstration diagnostic. Additional details are
provided in Section D

auxiliary head reconstructs VGGT point-map targets and demonstration-derived EEF tracks, with

4.5
REAL-WORLD EXPERIMENTS
Experimental setup.
We conduct real-world experiments to evaluate GeoMem-VLA in manipu-
lation scenarios involving geometric reasoning and temporal context. Specifically, we design five
tasks with distinct spatial and sequential requirements as shown in Figure 4: pick and stand sticks,
transfer cup across the rack, separate file pockets on the Astribot S1 platform and pick n times cube,
cover and find the apple under towel on the AgileX platform. We collect 60 demonstrations per
task and GeoMem-VLA is compared to representative VLA baselines, including LingBot-VLA 2.0
(Wu et al., 2026b), π0.5 (Intelligence et al., 2025), and OpenVLA-OFT (Kim et al., 2025), under
the same evaluation protocol. During evaluation, we conduct 10 independent rollouts for each task
under randomized object configurations and report the success rate.
Result analysis.
Figure 4 shows that GeoMem-VLA achieves robust performance across five real-
world manipulation tasks. Specifically, for comprehensive long-horizon manipulation tasks, includ-
ing pick and stand sticks, transfer a cup across the rack, and cover and find the apple, GeoMem-VLA
achieves 90%, 70%, and 80% success rates. These tasks require both precise spatial interaction and
maintaining task-relevant context throughout multi-stage execution, such as in pick and stand sticks
task, the robot needs to repeatedly identify individual sticks, grasp them from a shared workspace,
and place them into a precise upright configuration. The results show that the geometry-supervised
representations provide spatially grounded features for reliable grasping and placement, while the
historical geometric context effectively maintains task progress. Moreover, for the spatially precise
separate file pockets task, GeoMem-VLA achieves 100% success rate by accurately distinguishing
and manipulating the target file pocket among cluttered stacked configurations. For pick n times
cube, the challenge is maintaining progress over repeated interactions. The policy needs to remem-
ber how many successful grasp-and-place cycles have been completed while adapting to the current
scene. The temporal memory mechanism preserves task progress across repeated operations, allow-
ing GeoMem-VLA to complete the required number of interactions.
5
CONCLUSION
Inspired by the thought patterns of human action, we propose GeoMem-VLA to illustrate how hu-
mans conceptualize and store spatial information of the current scene in their minds as memory to
guide action generation during decision-making. This method allows spatial information learned
through reconstruction to remain available as latent context throughout task execution. LIBERO
and RoboTwin results support its applicability across manipulation tasks. Experiments on two phys-
ical robot platforms further cover precise spatial interaction, changing visibility, and multi-stage
manipulation. These results support geometry-supervised latent memory as a practical connection
between 3D representation learning and action generation over time, effectively improving VLA
model’s decision-making ability.

REAL WORLD EXPERIMENTS DETAILS
E.1
HARDWARE AND EXPERIMENTAL SETUP
We conduct real-world experiments on two dual-arm robot platforms, Astribot S1 and AgileX Cobot
Magic 2, as shown in Figure 4. These platforms provide different robot embodiments for evaluating
the applicability of GeoMem-VLA in real-world manipulation scenarios.
The Astribot S1 is a dual-arm humanoid manipulation platform equipped with two 7-DoF arms
and dexterous end-effectors. The robot is equipped with a three-camera visual system, including
one head-mounted RGB camera and two wrist-mounted RGB cameras, which provide global scene
observations and close-range views for manipulation.
The AgileX Cobot Magic 2 is a dual-arm collaborative robot platform equipped with two 6-DoF
robotic arms and parallel grippers. Similar to Astribot S1, it uses a three-camera configuration
consisting of one head-mounted RGB camera and two wrist-mounted RGB cameras.
E.2
TASK DESCRIPTION
We design five real-world manipulation tasks on two robot platforms to evaluate different aspects of
GeoMem-VLA, including spatial understanding, object-level manipulation, and long-horizon task
execution. The tasks cover diverse interaction patterns, ranging from precise object placement to
sequential manipulation with changing task states.

E.3
EVALUATION PROTOCOL
For each real-world task, we conduct 10 independent evaluation rollouts under randomized initial
configurations. Before each rollout, object positions are randomly initialized within the demon-
strated workspace, while task-specific parameters (e.g., the target number in the pick n times cube
task) are randomly assigned.
During evaluation, the robot executes the policy in a closed-loop manner without external interven-
tion. A rollout is considered successful only when the robot completes the entire task procedure and
satisfies the predefined task objective. Partial completion of a task is counted as failure.
The success rate is calculated as:

The reported success rate for each task is computed over the 10 evaluation rollouts.
E.4
BASELINE IMPLEMENTATION
We compare GeoMem-VLA with three representative VLA baselines, including LingBot-VLA 2.0,
π0.5, and OpenVLA-OFT. To ensure a fair comparison, all baselines are fine-tuned and evaluated
following their official configurations and recommended training pipelines.
For each task, the baseline models are fine-tuned using the same task-specific demonstration data
collected in our experiments. The adapted models are then evaluated under the same rollout settings,
including identical initial configurations, evaluation episodes, and success criteria.
