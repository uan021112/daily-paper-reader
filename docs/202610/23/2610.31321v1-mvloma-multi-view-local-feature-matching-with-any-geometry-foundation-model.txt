MVLoMa: Multi-View Local Feature Matching with Any
Geometry Foundation Model
Xinyang Lu1,2, Shengzhe Wei1, Sheng Tang1, Weiqi Zhang3, Juntong Fang3, Zesong Li1
1Kosmo Research
2School of Astronautics, Harbin Institute of Technology
3School of Software, Tsinghua University
Abstract
Local feature matching is typically formulated for image pairs, although structure-from-motion and visual localization commonly
match one reference image against many retrieved or covisible neighbours. Applying a pairwise matcher independently to these
centre–neighbour edges duplicates reference-side computation and prevents the matcher from exploiting complementary multi-view
geometric evidence from the remaining observations. To address these limitations, we introduce MVLoMa, a sparse star-graph
matcher that extends LoMa from image pairs to multiple views. MVLoMa first uses a frozen multi-view geometry model to jointly
describe all images in a star, then injects the resulting cross-view geometric context into local descriptors. It then matches the centre
image to all neighbours in a single forward pass through per-view self-attention and bidirectional centre–neighbour cross-attention.
For supervision, we derive positive correspondences only from depth-valid, mutually visible dense warps and explicitly penalize
high-confidence matches that violate epipolar geometry. On 25-view star graphs from HyperSim, ScanNet++, Mip-NeRF 360, and
Tanks and Temples, MVLoMa obtains the best macro-average relative-pose AUC among the compared methods at all three thresholds
(58.65/85.41/90.99). Its matcher is 2.20× faster than sequential evaluation of the 24 centre–neighbour edges. The same design
supports four tested frozen geometry encoders—DA3, π3, VGGT, and VGGT-Ω—with DA3-Giant performing best on all reported
encoder-ablation metrics. Controlled ablations further isolate the effects of geometry-aware descriptors, visibility filtering, geometric
negative supervision, and joint multi-view inference.
Keywords: local feature matching, multi-view geometry, star graph, geometry foundation models, relative pose estimation

Introduction
Reliable correspondence is the interface through which im-
ages become geometric constraints. It underlies camera pose
estimation, mapping, visual localization, and structure-from-
motion (SfM). Learned sparse matchers (Sarlin et al., 2020;
Lindenberger et al., 2023; Nordström et al., 2026) combine
local descriptors with contextual assignment, while detector-
free and dense methods predict a broader set of correspon-
dences (Sun et al., 2021; Edstedt et al., 2024, 2026). This
progress has made two-view pose estimation substantially
more robust. Yet it also leaves a mismatch between the unit of
computation used by a matcher—an image pair—and the unit
of evidence available to downstream geometry—a group of
mutually overlapping images.
Consider the ubiquitous star graph: a reference image
and its retrieved neighbours. This topology occurs when SfM
builds local subproblems, when localization matches a query
to a covisible map neighbourhood, and when a keyframe asso-
ciates observations over time. A pairwise matcher is invoked
once for every reference–neighbour edge. It consequently re-
computes the reference descriptor and reference-side matcher
branch, and it must decide each correspondence before seeing
supporting or conflicting evidence from the remaining neigh-
bours. The limitation is not merely redundant computation.
Repetitive texture, partial occlusion, and a weak baseline can
make a match ambiguous in one pair while a third image
resolves the ambiguity.
Existing multi-view matchers move contextualization from
isolated pairs to collaborative groups or tracks (Zhang et al.,
2025; Lee et al., 2026). However, their dense or groupwise out-
puts are not designed for the repeated sparse centre–neighbour
assignments of a star graph. Simply batching pairwise match-
ers preserves that sparse interface but prevents communication
between edges. We therefore seek a matcher that reasons
jointly over a star while retaining explicit pairwise outputs for
RANSAC.
There is a second, largely independent source of unused
information. Recent 3D foundation models predict pose, depth,
and geometry from arbitrary view collections by exchanging
information across image tokens (Wang et al., 2025, 2026b;
Lin et al., 2025). Their spatially aligned final-layer tokens
already encode multi-view geometric context, yet local match-
ing commonly starts from a separate frozen 2D descriptor
backbone. Reusing these tokens exposes geometry to the
matcher without introducing another frozen feature stream.
Keeping the foundation encoder fixed also avoids optimizing
its large parameter set and preserves a reusable geometric
representation.
These observations suggest a simple principle: jointly

match a star once, and reuse the frozen geometric represen-
tation that the star has already produced. We instantiate this
principle in MVLoMa, a multi-view extension of LoMa (Nord-
ström et al., 2026). A light trainable descriptor head replaces
LoMa’s frozen DINOv2 stream with last-layer tokens from a
frozen 3D foundation encoder. A star-structured matcher then
shares the centre representation across all edges and alternates
per-view self-attention with bidirectional centre–neighbour
cross-attention. It outputs a conventional score matrix for every
edge, so its matches can be consumed directly by standard
robust pose estimation.
Joint reasoning alone does not guarantee useful super-
vision. Sparse assignment is a classification problem: high-
confidence but geometrically wrong matches can survive
mutual matching and disproportionately harm RANSAC. We
therefore construct positives only from depth-valid, mutually-
visible dense warps, and explicitly penalize predicted matches
whose epipolar error exceeds 3 px. These changes comple-
ment the multi-view architecture rather than replace LoMa’s
matching and matchability objectives.
Our experiments follow a 25-view star-graph protocol and
evaluate the same centre–neighbour edges for every method.
Under this protocol, MVLoMa leads the macro-average at all
three AUC thresholds and improves strict AUC over LoMa-L
on both HyperSim and ScanNet++. A controlled backbone
ablation isolates the effect of geometry-aware features, su-
pervision ablations evaluate visibility filtering and geometric
negatives, and a view-count study shows that additional views
can improve joint multi-view inference. The results also delin-
eate the scope of the method: dense two-view RoMa remains
stronger on Mip-NeRF 360 at the strictest threshold, while
MVLoMa provides a sparse multi-view alternative that oper-
ates on all 25 views in our protocol.
Our contributions are summarized as follows:
• We formulate sparse matching over a star so that
MVLoMa produces all centre–neighbour matches in
one forward pass rather than evaluating each edge inde-
pendently.
• We use frozen features from a multi-view foundation
model—DA3-Giant by default—as geometry-aware de-
scriptor inputs, so matching shares the representation
already used for multi-view geometry.
• We adopt RoMa v2 visibility-filtered ground truth for
star-graph training (Edstedt et al., 2026) and introduce
a geometric negative loss for high-confidence epipolar
violations. Experiments and ablations show consistent
improvements from these components and from multi-
view context.

Related Work
2.1
Sparse and Dense Feature Matching
Local matching has evolved from independently comparing
hand-designed features to learned descriptor and assignment
pipelines. SuperGlue contextualizes detected keypoints with
graph attention (Sarlin et al., 2020); LightGlue improves the
same family with adaptive depth and pruning (Lindenberger
et al., 2023). LoFTR removes the detector by matching coarse
image features (Sun et al., 2021). In parallel, RoMa and RoMa
v2 obtain robust dense correspondence from foundation fea-
tures and coarse-to-fine refinement (Edstedt et al., 2024, 2026).
LoMa shows that a carefully scaled local-feature recipe re-
mains highly competitive (Nordström et al., 2026). These
methods differ in sparsity and representation, but their assign-
ment is fundamentally pairwise. MVLoMa inherits LoMa’s
local descriptor refinement and dual-softmax interface while
moving contextual reasoning from an image pair to a star
graph.
2.2
Multi-View Correspondence and Tracks
Multi-view information has long been used after matching
to merge pairwise correspondences into tracks. More recent
learned methods use it inside the matcher. CoMatcher employs
groupwise interaction to make keypoint matching collabora-
tive (Zhang et al., 2025), whereas MV-RoMa aggregates dense
multi-view correspondence into tracks (Lee et al., 2026). These
approaches establish that complementary observations can
reduce pairwise ambiguity. Our setting is deliberately more
structured: a star has one centre whose features participate
in every output edge. We therefore use a centre–neighbour
communication pattern rather than full neighbour–neighbour
interaction. The resulting model retains a score matrix and
confidence for each individual edge, enabling the usual sparse
matching and RANSAC interface without a post-hoc track
snapping stage.
2.3
Multi-View Geometric Foundation Models
DUSt3R and MASt3R learn feedforward geometric represen-
tations from image pairs (Wang et al., 2024; Leroy et al., 2024).
VGGT, π3, and DA3 extend the input from pairs to view col-
lections and exchange token information across views (Wang
et al., 2025, 2026b; Lin et al., 2025). These models are typ-
ically used to decode camera and scene geometry, while a
separate matching system constructs explicit correspondences.
We instead ask whether their last-layer, cross-view-fused to-
kens can serve as a frozen geometry-aware input to a sparse
matcher. This choice is distinct from directly decoding tracks
with a foundation model: it preserves a specialized assignment
head and leaves the geometry encoder unchanged.
2


2.4
Matching in Reconstruction Systems
Optimization-based SfM obtains global geometry by matching
image pairs, building tracks, and geometrically verifying
them (Schönberger and Frahm, 2016; Pan et al., 2024). Hybrid
systems couple these explicit constraints with feedforward
geometry priors (Duisterhof et al., 2025; Pan et al., 2026).
They motivate star subgraphs as a scalable local unit, but
the correspondence module is still often assembled from
independent pairwise matchers or heterogeneous track sources.
MVLoMa addresses this upstream bottleneck; it is a matching
primitive rather than a replacement for global optimization.

Method
3.1
Problem Formulation
Given a star Sl = {Il, I1, . . . , IN−1} with centre image Il,
our goal is to simultaneously predict the correspondence
sets {M(l, j)}N−1
j=1 . Each M(l, j) contains sparse centre–
neighbour matches. A pairwise matcher instead makes N −1
independent calls, each with a duplicate centre descriptor
and centre-side attention computation. More importantly, it
commits to an edge without observations from the other edges.
MVLoMa is designed around the opposite data flow: frozen
foundation features first encode the complete star, a shared
centre representation then communicates with all neighbours,
and conventional pairwise assignment is recovered only at the
output (Figure 1). The following sections describe these three
stages and their geometric supervision.
3.2
Geometry-Aware Multi-View Descriptors
The first design question is where multi-view context should
enter a local matcher. Recomputing independent 2D descrip-
tors for every edge would preserve LoMa’s pairwise bottleneck.
Conversely, fine-tuning a large geometry encoder solely for
matching is costly and can disturb its general-purpose prior.
We instead reuse the representation already available after a
foundation model has processed the star.
Multi-view geometry models tokenize each image with
a ViT and interleave within-view self-attention with cross-
view attention. Let Fi ∈Rh×w×df be the final alternating-
attention feature map for Ii. Its tokens are spatially aligned and
include information from the other star views. The original
LoMa DeDoDe-G descriptor (Nordström et al., 2026) fuses a
trainable VGG stream and convolutional refiners with frozen
DINOv2-L features (Oquab et al., 2024). MVLoMa keeps
this trainable structure, replacing only the frozen DINOv2-L
stream with frozen foundation features:

3.4
Visibility-Filtered Ground Truth and Geo-
metric Negatives
The attention pattern establishes how evidence is exchanged;
supervision must ensure that this evidence is geometrically
meaningful. We obtain LoMa-style positive correspondences
by reprojecting ground-truth depth with ground-truth pose. A
naive dense warp, however, treats occluded, out-of-image, and
invalid-depth pixels as if they had a valid target. Following
RoMa v2 (Edstedt et al., 2026), we retain a positive only
when its source depth is valid, its reprojection lies inside the
target image, and the prescribed occlusion and target-depth
consistency checks are satisfied. This filtering removes label
noise before it reaches either the descriptor or the assignment
head.
The original dual-softmax matching loss suppresses non-
matches implicitly through row and column competition, and
matchability indicates whether a keypoint has a true correspon-
dence. Neither objective explicitly punishes a high-confidence
false match that survives the final mutual selection. Such
outliers are particularly consequential for sparse geometric
estimation. We therefore mine predictions that violate ground-
truth epipolar geometry and use them as explicit negatives.

Experiments
4.1
Protocol and Implementation
We evaluate multi-view matching on 25-frame sets from
HyperSim, ScanNet++, Mip-NeRF 360, and Tanks and Tem-
ples (Roberts et al., 2021; Yeshwanth et al., 2023; Barron
et al., 2022; Knapitsch et al., 2017). HyperSim and ScanNet++
provide challenging indoor geometry and appearance; Mip-
NeRF 360 and Tanks and Temples add outdoor and large-scale
scenes. The encoder and view-count ablations additionally
use MegaDepth-X and TerraSky3D (Li et al., 2026; D’Urso
et al., 2026) to probe transfer beyond the four primary bench-
marks. The controlled descriptor-backbone ablation also uses
AerialMD (AerialMegaDepth) evaluation pairs (Vuong et al.,
2025). In that ablation, overlap o is computed following the
definition of RoMa v2 (Edstedt et al., 2026), and we report
subsets with o > 0.35 and o > 0.05.
For every 25-frame set, frame 0 is the star centre and
frames 1–24 give 24 centre–neighbour edges. This protocol
4


evaluates matching as it is used in a local multi-view subprob-
lem rather than sampling unrelated pairs. For a multi-view
method that accepts K + 1 < 25 images, we partition the 24
neighbours into groups of size K. The groups cover exactly
the same centre–neighbour edges as a two-view baseline, so
any difference cannot be attributed to easier pair selection.
MV-RoMa exhausts memory at 25 views and is evaluated with
13-view groups under this same coverage rule.
For each edge, we estimate relative pose with PoseLib
RANSAC (Larsson and contributors, 2020) at a 2.5 px thresh-
old. We report AUC@1◦/5◦/10◦, where the pose error com-
bines rotation and translation errors. The strict 1◦threshold
exposes precise correspondence failures that may be con-
cealed by coarse thresholds. Each baseline is evaluated at the
input resolution prescribed by its original paper; MVLoMa is
reported at DA3-G encoder resolutions 504 and 784.
Unless stated otherwise, MVLoMa uses a frozen DA3-G
encoder, LoMa-L initialization, and 4096 DaD keypoints per
image. The matcher architecture and optimization hyperpa-
rameters otherwise follow LoMa-L. Unlike LoMa, we jointly
optimize the trainable descriptor components and matcher
end-to-end, while keeping the geometry foundation encoder
frozen. All reported variants are trained for 100k iterations
with batch size 1. The GT/loss ablation uses fixed four-view
training. The encoder and view-count experiments use dy-
namic 2–8-view training, testing whether a model exposed to
variable local context can generalize to substantially larger
stars.
4.2
Comparison to Matching Baselines
Table 1 compares sparse and dense baselines under identical
centre–neighbour edge coverage. MV-RoMa is evaluated with
13 views because its 25-view configuration exhausts available
memory.
At 784 resolution, MVLoMa improves AUC@1◦over
LoMa-L by 1.91 points on HyperSim and 0.76 points on
ScanNet++. The gains are consistent with the value of jointly
contextualizing centre features and using cross-view founda-
tion features before assignment, although this comparison
does not separate the two factors. It also achieves the best
macro-average at all three thresholds (58.65/85.41/90.99), di-
rectly supporting the aggregate claim in the abstract. The 784
model obtains a higher strict HyperSim score than the 504
model (53.90 versus 53.35); on the remaining benchmarks,
the two resolutions are close.
The comparison also identifies meaningful boundaries.
On Mip-NeRF 360, dense two-view RoMa remains strongest
at AUC@1◦, indicating that fine dense localization can still
dominate for this scene distribution. At the coarser 5- and
10-degree thresholds, MVLoMa is the strongest multi-view
method. On Tanks and Temples, LoMa-L retains the best score
and MVLoMa stays within a small margin. Rather than sug-
gesting that multi-view reasoning universally replaces pairwise
dense matching, these results support it as a complementary
sparse design for star-structured geometric estimation.

5
Reproducibility and Limitations
We describe the architecture, loss, evaluation topology,
RANSAC setting, keypoint budget, frozen/trainable compo-
nents, and reported training schedules in the main paper, with
further detail in Appendix A. The method builds directly
on publicly available LoMa-style matching components and
multi-view 3D foundation encoders. We will release the im-
plementation and evaluation configuration upon publication.
MVLoMa depends on the geometric quality of the frozen
foundation features; textureless or uncertain regions can still
yield sparse or ambiguous matches. The centre–neighbour
topology does not directly exchange information between
neighbours, and the memory requirement still grows with
the number of keypoints and views. Our 2–8-view training
range is also smaller than the 25-view evaluation star. Al-
though the view-count ablation shows useful extrapolation,
it is not uniformly monotonic and does not establish that all
additional observations are beneficial. Learning view selection
or confidence-aware neighbour aggregation is a promising
direction for larger stars. Finally, we evaluate the matcher
through relative pose estimation; its interaction with complete
global SfM systems is outside the scope of this work.
6
Conclusion
We presented MVLoMa to resolve a mismatch in current
geometric pipelines: pairwise assignment discards evidence
and foundation features that are already available across a
view collection. MVLoMa reuses frozen geometry-aware
tokens and shares the centre representation across a star, while
preserving ordinary sparse pairwise outputs for robust pose
estimation. Across four benchmarks, the 784 model attains the
strongest macro-average AUC among the compared methods
at all three thresholds, and its 25-view matcher is 2.20× faster
than sequential pairwise evaluation. The ablations further
show that the gains depend on both the frozen representation
and the proposed supervision, while the non-monotonic view-
count results clarify that additional context is useful but not
uniformly beneficial.

Implementation Details
At inference, we use 4096 DaD keypoints per image. The trainable VGG stream, convolutional refiner, descriptor projection, and
matcher are optimized jointly, while the foundation encoder remains frozen. Geometric negatives use a 3 px epipolar threshold and
weight 1/(N −1). The fixed-view GT/loss ablation uses LoMa-L initialization, four views, batch size one, and 100k iterations at
resolution 784. Encoder and view-count ablations sample 2–8 views; evaluation covers all 24 centre–neighbour edges in each
25-frame set.

Optimization and initialization.
All final experiments use 784 × 784 inputs and the publicly released LoMa-L checkpoint
as initialization. The descriptor and matcher learning rates are 10−4 and 2 × 10−4, respectively. We use AdamW with weight

200k iterations: the first 100k iterations use fixed four-view stars, followed by 100k iterations with the number of views sampled
uniformly from 2–8. MegaDepth-X has sparse depth supervision, so its auxiliary matchability loss is disabled; its geometric
