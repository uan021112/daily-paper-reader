Abstract
This paper studies category-level RGB-D object pose estimation, which recovers
an object’s 3D rotation and translation without instance-specific CAD models
or reference views. This task is highly ambiguous due to intra-class variations,
symmetries, and partial observations. Current solutions struggle to balance re-
liability and efficiency: methods based on intermediate structures are prone to
error propagation, while diffusion models suffer from high inference latency and
complex post-hoc candidate extraction. To address this, we present F2G-Pose, a
real-time direct, single-pass framework that predicts category-level pose, metric
size, and camera-frame completed shape from a single RGB-D observation in one
feed-forward pass. F2G-Pose lifts dense visual foundation features to partial point
clouds, encodes local geometry with DGCNN tokenization, and integrates object-
level structure with a Mixture-of-Experts Transformer. Joint size prediction and
camera-frame shape completion encourage metric consistency through dense geo-
metric supervision. With its task-specific network trained only on pose-annotated
synthetic SOPE data and its frozen RADIO backbone externally pretrained, F2G-
Pose achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on SOPE and ROPE while running at
28 FPS for pose-only inference, and further demonstrates strong camera-frame
geometry on HouseCat6D and real-object transfer on HANDAL. Analyses of la-
tency, robustness, shape completion, and GenPose++ candidate behavior further
demonstrate the efficiency and reliability of F2G-Pose, with real-world manipu-
lation experiments demonstrating deployment potential under segmented RGB-D
inputs. The project page, code, and pretrained models are made available at
https://jrryzh.github.io/F2G-Pose/.

Introduction
Category-level 6D object pose estimation recovers an object’s 3D rotation and translation without
requiring an instance-specific CAD model or reference views at test time. In practical RGB-D
perception, this estimate is often coupled with metric size and usable object geometry for benchmark
evaluation [9, 42, 51], physical interaction [6, 37, 38], and robotic control [14, 15, 24, 25, 44, 45].
This setting is inherently challenging because partial observations from a single crop or segmented
point cloud provide incomplete evidence: symmetries induce rotational ambiguity, occlusions hide
discriminative geometry, and intra-class variations alter shape, size, and appearance. Consequently,
category-level perception must produce reliable metric predictions from ambiguous, incomplete, and
noisy observations.

Previous work has addressed this problem from two complementary directions. Deterministic
category-level methods estimate pose and size through canonical coordinates [42], correspon-
dences [2], shape priors [16], deformation models [39], alignment procedures [23], or direct re-
gression [17, 26, 43]. While these methods enable efficient inference, reliance on intermediate
structures can lead to compounding errors. Under occlusion, missing depth, intra-class variation, and
domain shift, the final metric estimate can become sensitive to the reliability of these intermediate
structures. Direct regression can avoid some post-hoc alignment steps, but reliable direct prediction
still requires representations that preserve local geometric cues and global object structure under
partial observations.
A complementary line of work models pose ambiguity explicitly. GenPose [50] and GenPose++ [51]
formulate category-level pose estimation as conditional pose distribution modeling, sampling multiple
hypotheses and converting them into a prediction through scoring, filtering, clustering, or aggregation.
This is principled for uncertainty representation, but it introduces a candidate-to-decision gap:
deployed accuracy depends not only on the sampled hypotheses, but also on whether post-hoc
extraction can select or aggregate a reliable representative. Moreover, this conversion must operate
under a practical inference budget as in Fig. 3, so sampling quality, candidate extraction, and
computational cost become coupled. Joint-RegDiff [20] and RFMPose [32] improve generative pose
inference through joint regression–diffusion learning with score-scaling sampling and Riemannian
flow-matching trajectories, respectively, but both still rely on iterative generative inference to produce
the prediction, so prediction quality remains coupled with sampling dynamics, candidate extraction,
and inference cost.
We introduce F2G-Pose, a direct RGB-D framework that jointly estimates category-level pose, size,
and camera-frame completed geometry in a single feed-forward pass. By leveraging geometry-aware
foundation feature lifting and dense geometric supervision, it bypasses intermediate canonical shape
alignment and avoids the sampling and candidate extraction used by sampling-based pose estimators.
Specifically, we lift dense visual foundation features [13] to the partial point cloud for semantic cues,
encode local geometry via DGCNN tokenization, and integrate object-level structure using a Mixture-
of-Experts Transformer that provides conditional capacity across diverse shape regimes. Then, the
pose-size head predicts the final metric estimate, while the shape head reconstructs camera-frame
geometry as both auxiliary supervision and a usable output. This joint design encourages the shared
representation to encode visible local evidence, object extent, and visible-to-complete geometric
structure for consistent direct prediction under partial observations.
To evaluate the effectiveness of this design, we train the task-specific network only on pose-annotated
synthetic data from 149 SOPE categories [51] and test on SOPE [51], ROPE [51], HANDAL [10],
and HouseCat6D [17]. These benchmarks cover synthetic benchmark accuracy, synthetic-to-real
transfer, additional real-object evaluation, and camera-frame shape reconstruction. F2G-Pose achieves
state-of-the-art accuracy while running at 28 FPS for pose inference. Beyond benchmark accuracy,
we evaluate latency, robustness to perturbations and occlusion, shape reconstruction, GenPose++
candidate behavior, and real-world robotic manipulation. These results demonstrate the efficiency
and reliability of direct feed-forward prediction, while robotic experiments demonstrate deployment
potential under segmented RGB-D inputs.
Contributions. Our contributions are threefold: (1) We present F2G-Pose, a real-time direct RGB-D
framework that predicts category-level 6D pose, metric size, and camera-frame completed shape
in a single feed-forward pass, sharing its representation across pose, size, and shape learning.
(2) We introduce geometry-aware foundation feature lifting to map dense visual features onto
partial point clouds. DGCNN encodes local geometry, a Mixture-of-Experts Transformer integrates
object-level structure, and camera-frame shape completion provides dense geometric supervision for
metric prediction. (3) Extensive evaluations across synthetic benchmarks and real-world datasets
demonstrate that F2G-Pose achieves state-of-the-art accuracy, real-time inference, and high-quality
shape completion. Furthermore, physical manipulation experiments demonstrate pick-and-place
success, timely decision readiness, and deployment feasibility under segmented RGB-D inputs.

Related work
Category-Level 6D Pose Estimation. Category-level methods aim to generalize pose estimation
across unseen object instances within a category [17, 42, 43]. Early works such as NOCS [42]
introduced the notion of canonical space to enable pose alignment without requiring CAD models.
2


Follow-up approaches [2, 23, 26, 50] leveraged point cloud geometry and symmetry-aware losses to
improve generalization. Some methods further incorporate shape-aware modeling [16, 39] to jointly
predict object size and shape. While these methods remove the need for object-specific models, many
rely on intermediate canonical coordinates, correspondences, deformation fields, or shape priors
whose reliability can degrade under occlusion, missing depth, and strong intra-category variation.
Recent works have attempted to scale category-level 6D learning using larger and more diverse
datasets [18, 53], making representation quality and inference efficiency increasingly important.
Diffusion-based methods such as GenPose [50] and GenPose++ [51] model pose and size as multi-
modal distributions, but often rely on auxiliary scoring networks to filter outlier samples. Joint-
RegDiff [20] eliminates the need for such scoring networks through time-dependent score scaling, and
RFMPose [32] formulates generative category-level pose estimation with Riemannian flow matching;
however, both remain limited to 6D pose estimation and still require iterative generative inference,
which generally increases inference cost and can limit real-time throughput.
SecondPose [3] also combines foundation-model features with geometry for direct category-level
pose and size estimation through an SE(3)-consistent representation. F2G-Pose additionally predicts
complete camera-frame shape to provide dense supervision for the shared pose-size representation,
and uses MoE encoding to provide conditional capacity across diverse categories.
Instance-Level Novel Object Pose Estimation. Another line of work estimates pose for novel object
instances using CAD models [19, 30], single-view references, or multi-view references [11, 21, 27].
They often reconstruct object geometry, either explicitly via 3D modeling [22, 28] or implicitly
through image-based retrieval [12, 29]. FoundationPose [46] supports either CAD or reference
images, combining model-based and model-free paradigms. Recent foundation reconstruction models
such as SAM 3D [35] reconstruct object geometry, texture, and layout from masked single images,
targeting image-to-3D asset reconstruction in natural scenes. Together, these methods broaden object-
centric 3D perception, but generally target instance-specific reconstruction or reference-conditioned
alignment rather than category-level pose-size estimation under a fixed taxonomy. F2G-Pose targets
this complementary category-level setting: it uses category-level canonical supervision during training,
but requires only a segmented RGB-D observation at inference time for the evaluated taxonomy.

Method
Problem Formulation and Overview Given an RGB image crop I ∈RH×W ×3 and a segmented
partial point cloud P ∈RN×3, F2G-Pose predicts the object’s 6D pose T = (R, t) ∈SE(3), metric
size s ∈R3, and completed camera-frame shape Pdense ∈RNd×3 in a single feed-forward pass,
without requiring instance-specific CAD models, reference images, or explicit category labels. Here,
R ∈SO(3) and t ∈R3 denote the object rotation and translation, respectively.
The framework directly predicts the outputs, avoiding the test-time sampling and candidate extraction
of sampling-based estimators, as shown in Fig. 1. Instead, it lifts dense visual foundation features
onto observed 3D points, encodes local geometric structure with DGCNN, aggregates object-level
context with a MoE Transformer, and uses parallel decoder branches for pose-size prediction and
camera-frame shape completion. The pose-size branch directly outputs the metric object state used for
evaluation and downstream applications, while the shape branch provides dense geometric supervision
for the shared RGB-D representation and produces approximate completed geometry in the camera
frame.

3.2
MoE-Based Object Encoding
The DGCNN tokens encode local surface neighborhoods and point-wise visual cues. We process
these tokens with a stack of Transformer blocks with multi-head self-attention [40], following the use
of Transformers for point cloud completion and geometry modeling [48, 49]. This stage integrates
local evidence across the object crop to form an object-level representation for metric prediction.
To increase representation capacity with conditional expert aggregation, we replace the standard
feed-forward layers in each Transformer block with Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) layers [4, 5, 8]. Each
MoE layer contains an independent set of experts and uses a linear gating network to compute softmax
probabilities over Ne = 8 experts. For each token, we select the top-2 experts according to the gating
scores, route the token to these experts, and aggregate their outputs using the selected probabilities
renormalized to sum to one. A soft load-balancing penalty encourages balanced gate probabilities
during training [36]. Finally, the encoded tokens are aggregated by global pooling to produce a
compact object representation for direct 6D pose, size, and shape prediction.
3.3
Direct Pose-Size and Shape Decoding
F2G-Pose uses two lightweight decoding branches on top of the shared object representation. The
pose-size branch predicts the metric state directly, while the shape branch predicts complete geometry
in the camera frame. Both branches are trained jointly, so dense geometric supervision can regularize
the representation used for direct pose and size prediction without adding any test-time optimization
or candidate extraction.
Pose and Size Estimation. The pose-size branch regresses rotation, translation, and metric size
directly from the global object representation. For rotation, we adopt the continuous 6D representa-
tion [56], which uses the first two columns of the rotation matrix R ∈SO(3) and reconstructs a valid
matrix via orthogonalization. This parameterization improves optimization stability while keeping the
prediction deterministic and feed-forward. The branch outputs the final pose-size estimate directly,
without sampling pose candidates or applying post-hoc aggregation.
4


Camera-Frame Shape Completion. From the extracted global feature vector, we first regress a
coarse complete object shape Pcoarse ∈R512×3 using a lightweight MLP. Since the input partial
point cloud P contains observed metric geometry, we concatenate Pcoarse with farthest-point-sampled
observations from P to form a combined point set, which is then processed through another MLP
with a Sigmoid activation to produce a confidence score for each point. This selects reliable points
for fusion, producing the refined fused point cloud Pfuse ∈R512×3. Finally, each point in Pfuse is
augmented with the global feature vector and passed through an MLP to predict four local offsets per
point, yielding Pdense ∈R2048×3. The shape is reconstructed directly in the camera frame, providing
a geometry output and a dense auxiliary signal for learning metric-consistent object representations.

Experiments
Datasets. We use SOPE [51] as the training source for F2G-Pose; it contains 5K synthetic object
instances from 149 categories with category-level canonical pose, metric size, and complete shape
supervision. Pose-size evaluation uses SOPE and ROPE [51]: SOPE measures synthetic benchmark
performance, while ROPE evaluates transfer to scanned real objects under the same category taxon-
omy. For camera-frame geometry evaluation, we use SOPE and HouseCat6D [17], where complete
object geometry is available. For additional real-world transfer, we evaluate selected HANDAL
objects [10], including hammers, mugs, pans, and wrench-like tools. The 92 instances from seven
HANDAL categories map to six SOPE categories; selection follows taxonomy correspondence. Thus,
ROPE and this HANDAL subset evaluate seen-category, unseen-instance transfer (Appendix B.4).
Baselines and metrics. For category-level pose-size estimation, we compare with NOCS, SGPA,
IST-Net, HS-Pose, GenPose, and GenPose++ using the SOPE/ROPE benchmark results reported
by Omni6DPose [51]. Joint-RegDiff [20] and RFMPose [32] are report-only comparisons from
their papers, using public metrics available under the SOPE/ROPE taxonomy. For reference-based
comparisons on real datasets, we additionally report FoundationPose [46] and Any6D [21]. For
runtime comparison, we benchmark executable methods under a unified inference protocol using the
same segmentation masks, RGB-D crops, depth preprocessing, batch size 1, and GPU-synchronized
timing on one RTX 4090; accuracy results follow the evaluation sources specified above. Pose-size
accuracy is evaluated with AUC of 3D bounding-box IoU at thresholds 25/50/75, VUS under joint
rotation-translation thresholds, and mean rotation/translation error. Shape completion is evaluated
using Chamfer-L1 distance.
4.1
Comparison with State-of-the-Art Methods
Pose-size accuracy and latency. Table 1 shows that F2G-Pose improves the accuracy-latency
operating point of category-level RGB-D pose estimation. On SOPE, F2G-Pose improves GenPose++
from 31.9 to 43.7 AUC at IoU50 and reduces mean rotation error from 19.9◦to 15.7◦; on ROPE, it
improves AUC across all IoU thresholds and matches or exceeds the available high-precision VUS
results. This gain is not only a speedup from removing iterative sampling. The larger improvement on
IoU and rotation-sensitive metrics indicates that direct prediction benefits from stronger conditioning:
dense image features preserve category and part cues, local point neighborhoods preserve observed
surface evidence, and global token interactions integrate these cues into a metric pose-size estimate.

5
Conclusion
We present F2G-Pose, a direct RGB-D framework that estimates category-level 6D pose, metric size,
and camera-frame shape from a single observation. By lifting dense visual foundation features to
partial point clouds and encoding local-global geometry with a MoE Transformer, F2G-Pose predicts
the deployed pose-size state in one feed-forward pass. Experiments on SOPE, ROPE, HouseCat6D,
HANDAL, and ARX manipulation show a favorable balance of accuracy, inference latency, and
manipulation success, while the shape branch provides dense geometric supervision and approximate
planning geometry.
Limitations. F2G-Pose assumes a segmented RGB-D crop at inference time, so mask failures and
severe depth corruption can propagate to pose, size, and shape. The method remains sensitive to
thin or asymmetric parts, severe occlusion, and symmetric low-texture objects that admit multiple
plausible rotations. High-precision metric alignment remains weak under cross-dataset domain shift,
which may require downstream refinement for tasks with tight geometric tolerances. Symmetry-aware
supervision does not eliminate ambiguity under severe occlusion or near-symmetry, and the model
provides no calibrated uncertainty or abstention mechanism. Future work will address mask-free
operation, broader real-object coverage, articulated or deformable categories, and active perception
for heavy occlusion.
Broader Impact. F2G-Pose advances category-level object perception for robotics and augmented
reality. While improved pose estimation can benefit assistive manipulation and human–robot col-
laboration, it could also be applied in surveillance or autonomous systems without adequate safety
oversight. We encourage responsible deployment with appropriate human supervision.

Additional Implementation Details
A.1
Model Details
F2G-Pose receives a segmented RGB crop and its corresponding partial point cloud. We sample
1024 points from the segmented depth observation and concatenate each point coordinate with a
lifted visual feature. Frozen RADIOv2.5-L [13] provides feature maps from layers 8, 16, and 23.
Independent 1 × 1 convolutional blocks compress each map from 1024 to 512 channels. Learned
softmax-normalized layer weights combine them by summation, followed by a 3 × 3 convolutional
block producing 1024 channels; both convolutional stages use BatchNorm and ReLU. We gather each
point’s feature by integer-dividing its crop-pixel coordinates by the patch size of 16. This produces
an input tensor of size B × 1024 × 1027.
The fused RGB-D point set is tokenized by a DGCNN encoder with two downsampling stages,
reducing 1024 input points to 128 object tokens. The tokens are projected to 512 dimensions and
processed by 8 Transformer blocks with 8 attention heads. In the full model, each Transformer
feed-forward layer is replaced by a top-2 Mixture-of-Experts layer with 8 experts and the load-
balancing loss described in the main paper. A learned softmax-weighted sum combines the block
outputs; an MLP projects the tokens to 1024 dimensions before max pooling. The pose-size branch
directly regresses rotation, translation, and metric size. The shape branch predicts 512 initial points,
concatenates 256 farthest-point-sampled observed points, scores the 768 candidates and retains 512,
then predicts four local offsets per retained point for a 2048-point completed shape. The selected 512
points and final 2048 points receive reconstruction supervision. Continuous rotational symmetries
use 1◦candidate sampling; objects with at least two continuously symmetric axes are excluded from
the rotation loss.
A.2
Training Details
F2G-Pose is trained only on the SOPE training split used in the main experiments. We train for
100 epochs with batch size 512 using AdamW. The initial learning rate is 10−4, the weight decay is
5 × 10−4, and a LambdaLR schedule decays the learning rate by a factor of 0.9 every 8 epochs with a
lower bound of 2% of the initial value. BatchNorm momentum is decayed from 0.9 by a factor of 0.5
every 3 epochs with a minimum value of 0.01. Training experiments are run with PyTorch 2.4 and
CUDA 12.4 using automatic mixed precision, DistributedDataParallel, and synchronized BatchNorm
on 4 NVIDIA H200 GPUs.
For runtime comparison, we benchmark executable methods under a unified inference protocol

A.3
Controlled Robustness Evaluation
To complement the material robustness study in the main paper, we evaluate controlled perturbations
on ROPE. RGB perturbations simulate common photometric changes, while depth perturbations
simulate sensor noise. These results are intended as stress tests of the current SOPE-trained model
rather than as a separate dataset contribution.
Photometric perturbations.
For RGB inputs, we apply exposure shifts (±0.5/ ± 1.3/ ± 2.2 EV),
white-balance shifts (±800/ ± 2000/ ± 4000 K), and synthetic illumination changes (gradient or
vignetting at 0.10/0.25/0.40 strength). Table 10 shows that performance changes only mildly under
light and moderate perturbations, while heavy perturbations produce a larger but still bounded drop.
Depth perturbations.
For depth inputs, we inject heteroscedastic noise
σ(z) = α + βz2,
where α ∈{0.001, 0.002, 0.003} and β ∈{0.000, 0.003, 0.006}. As summarized in Table 11, depth
noise affects the method more strongly than photometric shifts, consistent with the main paper’s
observation that precise metric alignment is sensitive to corrupted or missing depth.

A.5
Failure Mode Analysis
The qualitative failure cases are drawn from ROPE and are consistent with the limitations stated in
the paper: corrupted depth on thin or reflective structures, heavy occlusion, and ambiguous rotations
for symmetric or weakly textured objects.
Reflective and transparent materials.
Reflective or transparent surfaces often produce missing
or distorted depth. RADIO features provide complementary appearance cues, but they cannot fully
recover metric extent when the observed point cloud loses thin handles, sharp edges, or asymmetric
parts. This leads to high-precision IoU and rotation errors, as shown in Fig. 6.
Occlusion.
Under mild or moderate occlusion, the model can often infer pose from visible geometry
and lifted semantic cues. Severe occlusion removes too much task-relevant evidence, especially when
the hidden region contains the asymmetric part needed to disambiguate orientation.
Category ambiguity.
For symmetric, regular, or small low-texture objects, multiple rotations
can be visually plausible from a single RGB-D observation. The model may therefore predict a
symmetry-related or near-symmetry-related orientation, producing typical 90◦or 180◦errors as
illustrated in Fig. 7.

B.3
MoE Expert Preferences
Beyond load balance, we analyze instance-level routing over 7,466 object samples. Routing exhibits
an association with category (Cramér’s V = 0.306, p < 0.001). Different experts show preferences
for broad geometry-related groups, including bag-like, flat, and bowl-like objects, with substantial
overlap. This suggests distributed specialization rather than a rigid one-expert-per-category partition.
Associations with material and visibility are weaker, and no significant independent association with
symmetry was found. These observations complement the capacity and balance analysis in Table 6.

B.5
Reference-Free and Single-Reference Evaluation Protocols
Any6D (zero reference).
“Zero reference” means that no additional reference view is supplied
beyond the test RGB-D observation. We use the official repository’s reference-free image-to-3D
pathway, which reconstructs a coarse shape from the test observation and subsequently estimates
pose using that reconstructed shape.
Any6D (one reference).
We randomly sample one additional view of the same object from its
ROPE multi-view sequence as the reference image and apply the standard reference-query inference
protocol.
FoundationPose (one reference).
We evaluate the model-free pathway under the same one-
reference constraint. One additional real view of the same object is randomly sampled from its
ROPE sequence; an object mesh is reconstructed from that view and used for pose estimation.
