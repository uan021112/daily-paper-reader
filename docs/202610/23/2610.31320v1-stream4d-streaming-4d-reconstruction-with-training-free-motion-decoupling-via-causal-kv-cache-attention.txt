Abstract
4D scene reconstruction requires robust handling of dynamic ob-
jects, yet existing streaming methods commonly assume static
scenes, leading to pose drift and ghosting artifacts over long se-
quences. We introduce Stream4D, a training-free, motion-decoupled
streaming reconstruction framework that can be directly integrated
into causal Transformer backbones (e.g., StreamVGGT, Stream3R).
Our key observation is that causal attention implicitly encodes rich
temporal motion cues within the KV cache. We adaptively aggregate

Introduction
4D scene reconstruction is a core capability for embodied intel-
ligence systems [16, 17, 20, 49], with broad applications in au-
tonomous [6, 8, 57], AR/VR [54, 56, 58], and robotic navigation [26,
27, 29, 30]. Traditional SfM/MVS pipelines rely on explicit geomet-
ric constraints and iterative optimization [2, 4], making it difficult
to balance speed and scalability [47, 48, 51, 52]. Recent end-to-end
learning methods have advanced multi-view 3D prediction, yet
pairwise approaches typically require costly global alignment and
post-processing, while memory-augmented schemes are prone to
error accumulation [50]. Fast3R [40] and VGGT [23] enable dense
global interaction via Transformer feed-forward structures, but
depend on global self-attention, forcing reprocessing of the en-
tire sequence for each incoming frame. To achieve online and effi-
cient inference, causal architectures such as StreamVGGT [59] and
Stream3R [9] leverage temporally causal attention and KV-Cache
historical memory, substantially reducing computation and storage
on long sequences—marking a key step toward scalable 4D vision.
However, existing streaming reconstruction methods commonly
assume static scenes and overlook interference from dynamic ob-
jects in the real world. Moving objects break photometric consis-
tency and epipolar geometry, mislead camera pose estimation, and
accumulate into ghost artifacts over long sequences. Although 3D
foundation models like VGGT [23, 25] can quickly provide high-
quality initial estimates, their training and inference typically de-
fault to static assumptions and lack motion-decoupling mechanisms.
Prior attempts such as VGGT4D [5] and Easi3R [1] perform training-
free dynamic segmentation via attention statistics, but respectively
rely on global bidirectional or pairwise local interactions: the former
is incompatible with causal streaming, and shallow-layer attention
becomes ineffective under causality constraints; the latter only cap-
tures near-frame relations, yielding limited temporal consistency
and boundary quality, and fails to prevent dynamic information
from accumulating over time. Consequently, stabilizing dynamic-
scene reconstruction under causal streaming remains a bottleneck
for practical long-sequence 4D reconstruction.
We present Stream4D: a training-free, pluggable, motion de-
coupled streaming 4D reconstruction framework compatible with
causal backbones (e.g., StreamVGGT [59], Stream3R [9]) for dy-
namic scenes. Our main contributions are as follows:
• We reveal that causal attention implicitly encodes rich tem-
poral motion cues within the KV cache, and propose an
attention-based saliency aggregation method that adap-
tively aggregates these cues from multiple attention layers
and across temporal windows.
• We introduce a robust dynamic mask generation pipeline
that fuses attention-derived saliency with depth priors, ef-
fectively handling challenging cases such as occlusions and
textureless regions.
• We design a soft key masking scheme that directly oper-
ates on the KV cache to suppress dynamic regions during
historical aggregation, improving camera pose estimation
accuracy without retraining or architectural modifications.
• Extensive experiments on dynamic-scene benchmarks demon-
strate that Stream4D, without any additional training or fine-
tuning, consistently improves pose accuracy, produces reli-
able dynamic masks, and significantly mitigates long-term
ghosting artifacts.

Related Work
Streaming Reconstruction. Streaming approaches offer a more
scalable alternative for 3D reconstruction problems, such as monoc-
ular SLAM pipelines [28]. Inspired by learned online reconstruc-
tion, Spann3R [22] extends DUSt3R [25] with memory-based up-
dates to achieve incremental reconstruction via recursive memory
writes. Fast3R [40] and VGGT [23] replace asymmetric decoders
with Transformer-based attention stacks, enabling direct multi-view
fusion and delivering strong reconstruction quality. However, these
methods rely on global full attention, requiring reprocessing of the
entire sequence as length grows, which limits real-time scalability.
CUT3R [24] adopts an RNN-style architecture to incrementally
process unstructured inputs, but suffers from limited memory ca-
pacity and poor compatibility with modern hardware acceleration.
Recently, StreamVGGT [59] and Stream3R [9] re-conceptualize
point-graph prediction as a decoder-only Transformer [21, 34] task,
achieving efficient causal inference via techniques like KV-Cache
and windowed attention, scaling well to long sequences while re-
maining compatible with modern LLM-style training infrastruc-
ture. Nonetheless, these streaming methods generally assume static
scenes and lack mechanisms to handle dynamic objects, constrain-
ing applicability in real-world scenarios [12, 13, 33, 35, 36].
4D Scene Reconstruction. Early 4D reconstruction methods typi-
cally rely on RGB-D sensors [11, 15], limiting deployment in real-
world settings [7, 31, 32, 38, 55]. Many approaches jointly estimate
depth, pose, and residual motion, often using motion masks on static

regions, including self-supervised variants and test-time optimiza-
tion schemes such as CasualSAM [53] and Robust-CVD. More recent
pipelines, e.g., MegaSaM [14], leverage strong monocular depth pri-
ors [41] to achieve robust pose estimation and reconstruction on
dynamic videos, while Uni4D [43] combines multiple vision foun-
dation models with multi-stage bundle adjustment for high-quality
4D results. Although effective, these pipelines depend on powerful
priors and extensive test-time optimization, which hinders scalabil-
ity. Recent work reduces or replaces test-time optimization using
learned priors or attention mechanisms: MonST3R [45] fine-tunes
on dynamic data and exploits optical flow; DAS3R [39] augments
the DPT head to produce feed-forward motion masks; CUT3R [24]
fine-tunes MASt3R [10] on mixed static/dynamic data for fast recon-

perform training-free 4D reconstruction on DUSt3R [25]. Despite
these advances, most pipelines still require fine-tuning or second-
stage post-processing, and training-free variants are often tightly
coupled to specific backbones. More critically, these methods are
designed around global bidirectional Transformer architectures,
making them incompatible with causal streaming and leaving the
long-sequence accumulation of dynamic information unresolved. In
contrast, our Stream4D is the first training-free 4D reconstruction
framework tailored for causal streaming architectures. It achieves
motion decoupling via selective KV-Cache updates, supports online
reconstruction over long sequences, and avoids ghost accumulation.

We evaluate Stream4D across three core tasks: dynamic object seg-
mentation (Sec. 4.1), 4D reconstruction (Sec. 4.2), and long-sequence
streaming efficiency (Sec. 4.3), followed by ablations. Our protocol
emphasizes real-time settings, causal inference, and training-free
integration atop pretrained streaming backbones.
Baselines. We compare against state-of-the-art streaming recon-
struction methods and training-free 4D approaches. StreamVGGT [59]
and Stream3R [9] are representative causal streaming architectures
but assume static scenes; VGGT4D [5] and Easi3R [1] perform
training-free dynamic segmentation under global bidirectional de-
signs. Stream4D applies training-free motion decoupling directly
on pretrained causal streaming models, requiring no fine-tuning or
additional data.

the official protocols. For 4D reconstruction and camera pose esti-

4.1
Dynamic Object Segmentation
We evaluate dynamic object segmentation on DAVIS-2016 [18],
DAVIS-2017 [19], and DAVIS-all following the official DAVIS pro-
tocol and splits. Metrics are the mean IoU J𝑀and the IoU re-
call J𝑅, which jointly measure segmentation accuracy and robust-
ness. All online methods are strictly single-pass and forbid future-
frame access to comply with causal streaming constraints. Our
method and the causal baselines use identical resolution and pre-
processing to ensure a controlled comparison. Since the original
StreamVGGT [59] lacks a segmentation head, we integrate the
temporal attention aggregation to produce saliency maps, then
apply temporal consistency filtering and thresholding to generate
binary masks. Easi3R_online keeps the original implementation
and parameters, with the only change of disabling historical-frame
access to match causal streaming. Offline methods (DUSt3R [25],
MonST3R [45], DAS3R [39], VGGT4D [5], and Easi3R [1] variants)
use author-recommended configurations and serve as performance
upper bounds.

dynamic segmentation that robustly separates motion from the
static background without requiring external supervision, and (2)
soft key masking that suppresses dynamic interference during his-
torical aggregation. More visualized results are provided in the

Ghosting suppression. A core failure mode of causal streaming
reconstruction in dynamic scenes is temporal over-integration: as
each new frame attends to and fuses information from the past, mov-
ing objects are repeatedly written into the scene, creating stacked
geometric replicas along their motion paths (ghosting artifacts).
This artifact intensifies with longer sequences and may eventu-
ally bleed into nearby static surfaces, degrading ground, building
facades, and road textures. As illustrated in Fig. 6, baseline stream-
ing methods exhibit clear motion trails—pedestrians and vehicles
appear multiple times at different locations, forming overlapping
silhouettes and blurred structures.
Stream4D suppresses this accumulation by decoupling motion
from the causal fusion process. Specifically, we aggregate temporal
saliency from causal attention to derive dynamic masks on-the-fly,
and apply soft key masking to suppress dynamic regions in the
cached keys. This prevents dynamic features from dominating his-
torical aggregation, so that the static background remains stable and
sharp, and dynamic objects are retained only in the current frame
without overlapping replicas, even over long streaming sequences.
4.3
Efficiency and Scalability
We evaluate the inference time and scalability of our proposed
method across varying image sequence lengths (𝑁∈{10, 20, 50,
100, 200}) with an input resolution of 512 × 384 on a single NVIDIA
A100 GPU. Figure 8 illustrates the comparison between Easi3R [1],
StreamVGGT [59], and our Stream4D. As shown, Easi3R [1] suffers
from severe computational bottlenecks due to its reliance on global
pairwise attention. Its inference time grows quadratically, reaching
260.0s at 𝑁= 100. Crucially, due to its heavy memory footprint,
Easi3R [1] fails to process longer sequences entirely, triggering an
Out-of-Memory (OOM) error before reaching 𝑁= 200.
In contrast, Stream4D demonstrates robust scalability with near-
linear growth in computation. By leveraging a causal architecture
similar to StreamVGGT, Stream4D maintains efficient inference
even for long sequences. At 𝑁= 200, Stream4D achieves an in-
ference time of 93.1s, which is comparable to the highly efficient
StreamVGGT baseline (84.1s). The marginal increase in latency
(≈10%) is attributed to the lightweight overhead introduced by our
dynamic segmentation and soft key masking mechanisms. This
result confirms that Stream4D successfully avoids the OOM issues
of Easi3R while maintaining high computational efficiency for long-
term 4D reconstruction, making it highly practical for continuous
real-world video applications.
5
Conclusion
We present the Stream4D, a training-free framework for motion-
decoupled 4D scene reconstruction tailored to causal streaming
architectures. Our key insight is that causal attention implicitly en-
codes rich temporal motion cues within the KV cache. Leveraging
multi-level attention aggregation with depth priors, we derive reli-
able dynamic masks that decouple static and dynamic regions. We
then apply soft key masking to directly suppress dynamic regions
in the cached keys, preventing dynamic features from interfering
with historical aggregation. This design improves camera pose es-
timation accuracy and helps mitigate ghosting artifacts over long
sequences, all under strict single-pass, causal inference constraints.
Experiments on DAVIS and DyCheck demonstrate that Stream4D
achieves competitive or superior performance compared to exist-
ing streaming baselines in both dynamic segmentation and pose
estimation, while maintaining efficient inference for long-sequence
4D reconstruction.
Limitations and future work. While Stream4D demonstrates
the potential of mining motion cues from causal attention, cur-
rent aggregation relies primarily on attention statistics and depth
priors. Future work will explore richer cue extraction from the
KV cache, such as incorporating cross-scale temporal patterns and
semantic-aware saliency. Beyond static-scene reconstruction, we
aim to extend the framework toward dynamic object reconstruction
by leveraging the decoupled motion representations.
