Abstract. Online semantic 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) enables open-
vocabulary scene understanding from streaming observations. However,
existing methods recover geometric context missing from image-derived
semantic features using an additionally learned 3D network, incurring
extra training and runtime overhead. We observe that such geometric-
semantic evidence may already exist in the agent’s observation history
and can be retrieved rather than learned. Based on this insight, we
introduce RecallGS (Geometry-Grounded Recall for Online Semantic
Gaussian Splatting), a lightweight, training-free mechanism that treats
past observations as a non-parametric geometric-semantic prior. RecallGS
follows a recall-ground-fuse pipeline: CLIP retrieves relevant observations,
alpha-compositing weights identify geometrically supported views, and
aggregate their semantic features over each Gaussian’s rendered footprint.
On ScanNet and ScanNet++, RecallGS achieves strong semantic segmen-
tation performance at 2.48 FPS, demonstrating that geometric-semantic
context can be effectively recovered from observation history without an
additional learned 3D branch.
Keywords: 3D Gaussian Splatting, 3D Segmentation, Scene Understand-

Introduction
3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) provides an efficient explicit representation
for real-time novel-view synthesis[11]. Recent works extend 3DGS with open-
vocabulary semantics by distilling vision-language features into Gaussians, either
through rendering-based supervision[19,22,30] or direct 3D feature lifting and
referring[26,12]. This paradigm has further expanded to online embodied settings,
using either language-aware 3DGS-SLAM[9,31] or generalizable feed-forward
reconstruction[14], alongside online 3D instance segmentation methods that fuse
2D foundation-model predictions into persistent 3D representations[27,24].

Despite this progress, existing online semantic 3DGS pipelines recover the
geometric context missing from image-derived language features through an
additional learned 3D branch, rather than directly reusing geometric-semantic
evidence from past observations. EmbodiedSplat[14], for instance, complements
its 2D CLIP features [21] with geometry-aware 3D features produced by passing
the local Gaussian point cloud through a sparse 3D U-Net[6] equipped with a
temporal memory adapter[28]. This 3D branch, however, must be trained on
large-scale posed 3D indoor scene data, which is costly to collect and introduces
additional GPU-memory consumption and latency that directly reduce the
online reconstruction speed. EmbodiedSplat-fast removes the 3D U-Net and
memory adapter, increasing throughput from 0.75 FPS to 5.18 FPS at the cost of
geometric-semantic context. This highlights the training and inference overhead
of recovering explicit 3D context through an additional learned branch. We ask
whether such context must be learned at all, or can instead be recovered from
the agent’s observation history.
We observe that this trade-off is a consequence of treating each observation in-
dependently. An embodied agent operates in a persistent environment: a Gaussian
that is geometrically or semantically ambiguous from the current viewpoint may
correspond to a physical surface already observed earlier under a more favorable
viewpoint, illumination, or occlusion pattern. This suggests that part of the
geometric prior EmbodiedSplat’s 3D branch must learn from parameters could
instead be recovered from the agent’s own trajectory, treating its accumulated
observation history as a non-parametric geometric-semantic prior queried on
demand rather than compressed once and discarded. However, effectively reusing
past observations introduces several challenges. First, semantic similarity does
not imply physical correspondence: a CLIP-based retrieval system[21] may return
frames containing a visually similar object-another chair, table, or door-at an
entirely different location. Second, a Gaussian may project inside a historical im-
age but still be occluded by closer geometry. Sampling the projected pixel would
then transfer features from the occluder rather than from the queried surface.
Finally, exhaustively verifying every historical camera against every incoming
frame would itself undermine the efficiency this history is meant to provide, as
a long-running agent may accumulate hundreds to thousands of observations.
The problem is therefore not retrieval of similar-looking memories, but memory
routing: determining, without prior semantic knowledge of the current Gaussians,
which past observations should be allowed to influence each one.
To this end, we propose RecallGS (Geometry-Grounded Recall), a lightweight
mechanism requiring no additional training that treats an agent’s observation
history as a queryable source of geometric-semantic evidence, routed to the
current Gaussians through a recall–ground–replay pipeline. Our contributions are
threefold: (1) semantic recall, a cheap CLIP-based retrieval stage that narrows
an unbounded observation history to a small candidate pool of appearance-
plausible frames; (2) geometric grounding, which reranks these candidates using
alpha-compositing weights to identify views that geometrically support the
current Gaussians; and (3) compositing fusion, which reuses the same alpha-

Our contributions are as follows:
– We show that an agent’s observation history can serve as a non-parametric
geometric-semantic prior, providing complementary semantic evidence with-
out additional 3D training or per-scene optimization.
– We propose RecallGS, a training-free recall-ground-fuse framework that effi-
ciently retrieves relevant past observations, grounds them against the current
geometry, and fuses their semantic evidence into the current Gaussians.
– We validate the contribution of each component and show that exploit-
ing observation history consistently improves online semantic 3DGS while
maintaining efficient inference.

Related Work
2.1
Language-Embedded Gaussian Splatting
Building on 3D Gaussian Splatting[11], one line of work embeds open-vocabulary
semantics onto Gaussians via per-scene optimization, either by distilling 2D
vision-language features through differentiable rendering supervision[19,22,30] or
by directly associating semantic features with 3D structure through clustering,
feature lifting, or compact Gaussian-level representations [26,12,16,5,1,20,23].
These methods establish 3DGS as an effective carrier of language features and
improve their spatial consistency, instance awareness, or representation efficiency.
However, most construct semantic fields specifically for each scene, which limits
their applicability when observations arrive continuously.
To remove scene-specific optimization, a class of feed-forward methods predicts
3D Gaussian representations directly from input images without per-scene training.
This paradigm was established by geometric backbones such as pixelSplat[2] and
MVSplat[4], which regress RGB Gaussians from sparse image pairs. Building on
this feed-forward formulation, subsequent work adds an open-vocabulary semantic
branch: LSM [8] jointly regresses geometry, appearance, and semantics from un-
posed image pairs by lifting a pre-trained 2D language-based segmentation model
into a 3D-consistent feature field decoded into semantic Gaussians, SLGaussian
[3] targets the sparse-view regime with a multi-view language memory bank that
maps high-dimensional CLIP features to compact per-object IDs for fast open-
vocabulary querying, and SemanticSplat [17] further improves language-aware
feed-forward reconstruction by fusing SAM [13] and CLIP-LSeg [15] features
into a cost-volume representation for cross-view semantic aggregation. These
approaches avoid per-scene semantic optimization and generalize across scenes,
but still operate on a fixed, small set of input views rather than maintaining a
persistent semantic representation under a streaming observation sequence.

2.2
Online Gaussian Splatting Reconstruction and Segmentation
A separate line of work extends 3DGS to the online setting required by em-
bodied perception. Within visual SLAM, SplaTAM [10] and Gaussian Splatting
SLAM [18] use explicit Gaussians for incremental tracking, mapping, and ren-
dering, demonstrating that 3DGS can serve as a persistent scene representation
under streaming observations. Building on this formulation, Online-LangSplat [9]
and EA3D [31] inject language or foundation-model features into the online re-
construction loop. Online-LangSplat’s language module itself runs in milliseconds,
but the overall SLAM-GS pipeline remains bottlenecked by per-frame camera-
pose optimization, yielding well under 2 FPS end-to-end; EA3D similarly relies on
a recurrent joint optimization module to align geometric and semantic features,
at a comparable throughput cost. In parallel, online 3D instance segmentation
methods incrementally fuse 2D foundation-model predictions into temporally
consistent 3D instances [27,24], building on adapter-based caching mechanisms
that aggregate RGB-D features frame-by-frame to endow otherwise-offline back-
bones with temporal learning ability [28]; these methods, however, operate on
point-based representations rather than a differentiable and renderable Gaussian
semantic field.
Closest to our setting are online, generalizable feed-forward pipelines that avoid
per-scene optimization entirely. FreeSplat++ [25] targets efficient whole-scene
indoor reconstruction from long image sequences, using a low-cost cross-view
aggregation module and a pixel-wise triplet fusion strategy to incrementally
merge overlapping Gaussian primitives across views, together with a weighted
floater-removal step that exploits the accumulated blending weights from this
fusion. EmbodiedSplat [14] extends this backbone with open-vocabulary semantics
through a sparse CLIP codebook and a learned 3D U-Net branch that injects
geometric context into image-derived semantic features. This improves geometric-
semantic awareness but requires additional 3D training and incurs non-negligible
inference overhead. In contrast, we recover complementary geometric-semantic
evidence directly from the agent’s own observation history and route it to the
current Gaussians through a lightweight recall-ground-fuse mechanism without
an additional learned 3D semantic branch.

with episodic memory. For each incoming observation, we retrieve relevant past
views, verify their geometric compatibility with the current scene, and reuse their
semantic evidence to refine the current representation. This enables the model
to exploit previously observed information while preserving an efficient online
pipeline.

geometrically grounded historical replay provides complementary evidence that
stabilizes semantic predictions across viewpoints and improves the consistency of
the reconstructed semantic field.

Table 3 studies how Gaussian geometry is used during historical replay. Projected

indicating the expected benefit of exploiting the complete Gaussian footprint
for both historical observability and semantic transfer. These variants do not
isolate the transmittance factor T from opacity α; therefore, the occlusion-specific
contribution of T is not separately measured by this ablation.

Recall and Grounding Budget Sensitivity
Table 4 varies the recall budget Brec and the grounding budget Bgeo. Performance

A smaller Brec may exclude informative views from the candidate pool, whereas
a larger one admits more appearance-similar but geometrically unsupported
candidates. Likewise, a smaller Bgeo provides less complementary evidence, while a
larger one adds redundant or weakly supported views. We therefore use Brec = 16
and Bgeo = 3 as the default; note that this setting was chosen on the same

Conclusion
We propose RecallGS, a lightweight mechanism requiring no additional train-
ing that recasts an embodied agent’s observation history as a non-parametric
geometric-semantic prior via a recall-ground-fuse pipeline: retrieving plausible
historical frames, verifying their geometric support through alpha-compositing
responsibility, and fusing only this verified evidence into the current Gaussians.
On ScanNet and ScanNet++, RecallGS narrows the gap to EmbodiedSplat’s full
3D branch with higher speed than the full model.
We observed that RecallGS’s quality is bounded by the past observations
it relies on, offering limited benefit when historical views are sparse or low-
quality and additionally, grounding rasterizes only the current local Gaussians, so
occluders in the global map are not modeled and visibility may be overestimated.
Future work may extend retrieval with a certainty or assessment gate that
evaluates the reliability of a recalled observation, for instance based on viewpoint
quality, illumination, or depth confidence, before allowing it to influence the
current Gaussians.
