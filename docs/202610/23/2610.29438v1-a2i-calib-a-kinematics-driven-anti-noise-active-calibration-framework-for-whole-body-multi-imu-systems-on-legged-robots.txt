A2I-Calib++: A Kinematics-Driven Anti-noise Active Calibration
Framework for Whole-Body Multi-IMU Systems on Legged Robots
Fangyu Jiang†, Chaoran Xiong†, Graduate Student Member, IEEE, Kehui Ma,
Zhen Sun, Zeyu Zhang, and Ling Pei∗, Senior Member, IEEE
Abstract—Recently,
multi-node
inertial
measurement
unit
(multi-IMU) systems have shown promise for robust odometry in
legged robots. Multi-IMU fusion can improve legged robot odom-
etry but requires accurate spatiotemporal calibration among the
sensors. Existing multi-IMU calibration methods, designed for
aerial or handheld platforms, face challenges on legged robots
because routine locomotion may provide insufficient excitation,
while foot slips, impacts, and vibrations introduce measurement
disturbances. To address these challenges, we propose A2I-
Calib++, a kinematics-driven anti-noise active calibration frame-
work for body-mounted IMUs covered by the robot kinematic
model. It estimates temporal offsets, extrinsic rotations and
translations, and inertial biases through temporal correlation
search followed by analytic spatial and bias estimation, without
artificial calibration targets or complex parameter tuning for
the target IMU. A2I-Calib++ comprises three core modules: 1) a
Noise-Isotropy-Inspired Trajectory supported by a finite-horizon
conditioning proposition that generates sufficient excitation to
limit noise sensitivity; 2) a constraint-aware Adversarial Motion
Priors controller that combines the anti-noise excitation with
a rolling-contact reward for contact-aware execution; and 3) a
contact-constrained virtual IMU that fuses pre-calibrated calf
IMUs and joint kinematics under a rolling-contact model to
construct the calibration reference. Furthermore, evaluations on
both simulation and real-world quadruped platforms demon-
strate notably reduced noise sensitivity and simulation calibra-
tion errors compared to conventional methods, together with
repeatable real-world calibration and improved proprioceptive
odometry accuracy for legged robots.
Index Terms—Legged robots, calibration and identification,
multi-node inertial measurement units, reinforcement learning,
proprioceptive odometry.

II. RELATED WORK
Multi-IMU estimators for legged robots commonly use a
central IMU for state propagation and kinematically coupled
peripheral IMUs for corrective updates [6]–[8]. Their fusion
therefore requires consistent temporal offsets, extrinsic trans-
formations, and sensor biases. Otherwise, spatiotemporal mis-
alignment introduces inconsistent kinematic constraints and
systematic odometry drift.
Existing multi-IMU calibration methods can be grouped into
exteroceptive-aided and pure-inertial approaches. Rehder et
al. [15] extended Kalibr with camera observations for multi-
IMU extrinsic and intrinsic calibration. Light Detection and
Ranging (LiDAR) measurements have similarly been used to
assist multi-IMU calibration [10], while Hartzer et al. [16]
proposed online calibration using visual-inertial odometry.
Pure-inertial methods instead exploit trajectory correlation,
but their accuracy remains sensitive to measurement noise
and excitation quality [17], [18]. Although active calibration
can deliberately improve excitation, existing trajectory-design
methods mainly address industrial manipulators rather than
agile quadrupeds [19], [20].
Deep reinforcement learning (RL) has substantially ex-
panded quadruped motor skills [21]–[23], yet its use for sensor
calibration remains underexplored. A2I-Calib [14] employs
an RL controller but is limited to calf-mounted IMUs and
estimates only their temporal offsets, rotations and gyro-
scope biases, leaving body-mounted IMUs, translation and ac-
celerometer biases unaddressed. A2I-Calib++ therefore relies
on A2I-Calib as a preliminary calf-IMU calibration step to
recover the spatiotemporal parameters of body-mounted IMUs.
The resulting capability extension and comparison with iKalibr
are illustrated in Tab. I.

IV. METHODOLOGY
A. System Overview
The A2I-Calib++ pipeline is shown in Fig. 2. First, the
Noise-Isotropy Proposition (NIP) characterizes the finite-
horizon conditioning of orthogonal harmonic excitation and
guides the construction of an anti-noise calibration trajectory.
A preliminary A2I-Calib step estimates the calf-IMU tem-
poral offsets and extrinsic rotations required for kinematic
propagation. The constraint-aware AMP policy is trained with
a style reward derived from the reference motion’s state-
transition distribution and a rolling-contact task reward to
generate a contact-aware excitation. Under the rolling-contact
model, measurements from the pre-calibrated calf IMUs and
joint encoders are propagated to leg VIMUs and fused into
a reference VIMU, reducing sensitivity to disturbances from
any single leg. The target and reference sequences are then
processed by the search-and-solve calibration procedure to
estimate X. Finally, the estimated parameters are used to
correct the IMU measurements, and their downstream effect is
evaluated through proprioceptive odometry against a LiDAR-
IMU odometry (LIO) reference.

E. Constraint-Aware Adversarial Motion Prior Controller

between the ideal reference and feasible robot motion, causing
foot slip, contact-induced noise, and instability. We therefore
employ a constraint-aware AMP controller [21] to learn the
reference state-transition distribution and retain its anti-noise
excitation, while encouraging pure-rolling contact to reduce
disturbances in the kinematic reference.

ground truth, whereas the real-world Slip and VIMU Error
entries are proxy estimates because ground truth is unavailable.

velocity errors for both reference motions. On hardware, the

large remaining error when the single-leg error is high. The
fused error must therefore be considered together with the
reduction percentage.

VI. CONCLUSION
This paper presents A2I-Calib++, a kinematics-driven active
calibration framework for multi-IMU systems covered by a
legged robot’s kinematic model. After the prescribed calf-IMU
preliminary calibration and data collection, the framework
performs largely automated estimation of temporal offsets,
extrinsic rotations and translations, and IMU biases through
an efficient Search-and-Solve procedure. NIT provides an
anti-noise reference excitation, AMP realizes it as contact-
aware robot motion, and the contact-constrained VIMU fuses
multi-leg measurements into the calibration reference. Fur-
ther evaluations show lower calibration errors in simulation,
repeatable real-world calibration, and reduced downstream
odometry errors. Future work will improve adaptability to
varying friction conditions and ground properties, mitigate
sim-to-real gaps, and extend the framework beyond multi-IMU
calibration to exteroceptive modalities.
