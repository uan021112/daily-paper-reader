RaSFNet: Ray-aligned and Scan-aware Fusion Network for LiDAR
Semantic Segmentation
Chuang Chen
Abstract—LiDAR point cloud semantic segmentation is a
fundamental component of 3D perception for autonomous driving
and robotics. Projection-based methods map 3D point clouds into
2D representations for feature extraction using 2D networks.
However, aggregating multiple points during projection can
obscure geometric differences within each frustum, while local
feature extraction limits the modeling of spatial relationships
across scanning regions. Moreover, proximity in the projected
representation does not necessarily imply proximity in 3D space,
and interactions between point and projected features may mix
features from different ranges, reducing semantic discriminabil-
ity. To address these challenges, we propose the Ray-aligned
and Scan-aware Fusion Network (RaSFNet), which incorporates
point cloud geometry and LiDAR scanning characteristics into
projection-based semantic segmentation. Specifically, the Ray-
Aligned Geometric Distribution Encoding (RAGDE) module
characterizes point distributions within each frustum using a
ray-aligned coordinate system. To model spatial context, a Scan-
Topology-Aware Mamba (STAM) block organizes frustum fea-
tures according to the LiDAR azimuth sampling pattern and
captures long-range dependencies across scanning regions. In
addition, a Range-Constrained Point-Frustum Fusion (RCPFF)
mechanism uses shared range information to guide feature
exchange between point and frustum representations while main-
taining spatial correspondence. RaSFNet achieves mIoU scores
of 70.2% and 77.7% on the SemanticKITTI and nuScenes
validation sets, respectively, indicating competitive segmentation
performance compared with the evaluated methods.
Index Terms—LiDAR semantic segmentation; 2D representa-
tion; geometric encoding; Mamba; point-frustum fusion.
I. INTRODUCTION
L
IDAR point cloud semantic segmentation is a funda-
mental task for 3D scene understanding, providing fine-
grained environmental perception for autonomous driving,
robotic navigation, and intelligent transportation systems, and
serving as an essential foundation for outdoor autonomous
decision-making [1], [2]. However, LiDAR point clouds ac-
quired in real-world outdoor environments are inherently char-
acterized by irregular spatial distributions, sparsity, and highly
non-uniform point densities, which make it challenging for
existing models to effectively capture fine-grained geometric
structures [3], [4], [5]. Consequently, spatially adjacent ground
classes with similar geometric characteristics, such as parking
areas, sidewalks, and terrain, can be easily confused, limiting
segmentation accuracy and the capability of existing models
to meet the demands of complex outdoor perception [6], [7],
[8], [9], [10]. Therefore, developing feature modeling methods

which can jointly capture fine-grained geometric details and

remains a key challenge in 3D LiDAR perception [11], [12].
Considering that LiDAR acquires three-dimensional struc-
tural information by continuously scanning the surrounding
environment with multiple laser beams along the horizontal
direction, the resulting point clouds preserve regular spatial
indexing relationships in the angular domain [13], [14]. Build-
ing on this property, range-image methods [15], [16], [17],
[7] employ spherical projection to determine the row and
column positions of each 3D point on a 2D grid according to
its elevation and azimuth angles, transforming irregular point
clouds into structured 2D representations. Such representations
preserve the intrinsic scanning geometry and support the use
of mature 2D backbone networks, providing structured inputs
for LiDAR perception and making range-view methods a
mainstream paradigm for point cloud semantic segmentation.
However, projecting 3D point clouds onto a fixed-resolution
2D grid is inherently a lossy spatial compression process that
cannot fully preserve the original three-dimensional structural
information [18], [19]. On the one hand, multiple points at
different ranges may be mapped to the same pixel, causing
projection conflicts and the loss of local geometric details. On
the other hand, sparse LiDAR sampling can lead to insufficient
feature information in critical regions. These issues introduce
discrepancies between 2D pixel adjacency and the 3D spatial
topology, making it difficult to accurately capture geometric
relationships in complex scenes and limiting the model's
ability to effectively represent complete three-dimensional
structures [7], [8], [9], [18], [19], [20].
Notably, the aforementioned projection issues are not lim-
ited to the representation of raw point clouds and may be
further amplified by data augmentation strategies [9], [17].
A common strategy is to copy local point sets from other
scenes into the current scene [21], [22], enriching scene
compositions and object distributions in the training data while
alleviating overfitting to specific point cloud distributions.
Although such methods can generate richer scene layouts in
3D space, they typically focus on the geometric plausibility
of insertion positions while overlooking LiDAR ray visibility
and occlusion constraints [9], [20]. In a LiDAR system, only
the first valid return along a given laser direction is generally
retained [16], [17]. After external point sets are introduced,
newly inserted points may share the same scanning direction
as foreground points in the original scene. As illustrated in
Fig. 1, when the augmented point cloud is projected onto
the range image, points with different ranges and semantic

labels may fall into the same image cell, disturbing the local

existing studies organize multiple points mapped to the same

2D frustum features, allowing the original point information
in overlapping projection regions to continuously participate

this improvement, the intrinsic characteristics of LiDAR point

structures with similar geometry, as illustrated in Fig. 1.
Motivated by the above limitations, we propose a new
method for collaborative learning between 3D structural fea-
tures and 2D frustum features, termed the Ray-aligned and
Scan-aware Fusion Network (RaSFNet), which enhances fine-
grained 3D structure discrimination and improves the represen-
tation of geometrically similar objects in complex scenes. To
distinguish different range distributions and geometric patterns
within each frustum, we design a Ray-Aligned Geometric
Distribution Encoding (RAGDE) module. Points are explicitly
represented by their radial range and tangential offsets in a
ray-aligned coordinate system, while multi-frequency sine and
cosine functions are used for Fourier feature [23] mapping to
enhance the representation differences among distinct spatial
organization patterns. To enhance the global modeling capa-
bility of frustum features, we design a Scan-Topology-Aware
Mamba (STAM) block. The module sequentially organizes
features according to the LiDAR azimuth sampling pattern
and leverages Mamba [24] to capture long-range dependencies
across scanning regions, improving the representation of struc-
tural variations among different scan regions and global spatial
relationships. To address the decoupling between 3D structural
features and 2D frustum features during deep interaction,
we introduce a a Range-Constrained Point-Frustum Fusion
(RCPFF) mechanism that uses distance information as a shared
cue linking 2D projections and 3D spatial structures, guiding
the collaborative update of both feature types while enhancing
spatial consistency and semantic complementarity.
Overall, our main contributions are summarized as follows:
1) We propose RaSFNet, a network for collaborative learn-
ing between 3D structural features and 2D frustum fea-
tures. Unlike existing point-frustum interaction methods
that lack persistent spatial constraints, RaSFNet explic-
itly models the geometric structures within frustums
and LiDAR scanning relationships, while using range
information to constrain the interaction between point
and frustum features.
2) We propose RAGDE, which uses each frustum ray as
a reference and encodes radial range, tangential offsets,
and local variations with multi-frequency Fourier fea-
tures to distinguish geometric distributions.
3) We propose a STAM Block, which reorganizes frustum
features into sequences according to the LiDAR azimuth
scanning pattern, extending feature extraction from fixed
local neighborhoods to global scan-wise dependencies.
4) We propose RCPFF, which uses distance information to
constrain the interaction between 3D structural and 2D
frustum features, guiding their complementary updates
while maintaining spatial correspondence.
5) Comprehensive experiments on the SemanticKITTI [13]
and nuScenes [14] validation sets show that RaSFNet
achieves mIoU scores of 70.2% and 77.7%, respectively.
II. RELATED WORK
A. Point Cloud Feature Encoding
Point cloud feature encoding aims to extract discriminative
3D geometric information from the spatial distribution of
raw point clouds. PointNet [3] uses shared multilayer per-
ceptrons (MLPs) for point-wise encoding, while PointNet++
[25] adds hierarchical local feature aggregation. RandLA-
Net [4] strengthens neighborhood features through attentive

3
local encoding. Cylinder3D [26] instead organizes points with
cylindrical voxelization. Recent methods further improve point
cloud representation from different perspectives. SFPNet [5]
employs sparse focal point modulation to aggregate multi-
level contextual information, PointGeo [27] jointly captures
local and global geometric features, and MDSLCA [28] com-
bines multi-scale local attention with dilated spatial attention
to enhance structural representations over different spatial
ranges. Unlike methods that directly process unordered point
sets, RangeNet++ [15] projects range, 3D coordinates, and
remission values onto a regular 2D grid, while SphereFormer
[11] introduces radial windows to accommodate the distance-
dependent sparsity of LiDAR point clouds. Although these
methods enhance geometric feature encoding, their representa-
tions remain largely focused on local neighborhood structures,
with insufficient explicit modeling of differences in range
distributions, geometric patterns, and spatial organizations.
Consequently, point sets with distinct structural characteristics
may still produce similar encoded representations. In this
work, we introduce RAGDE, which characterizes radial and
tangential structures along the LiDAR sensing ray, while
multi-frequency Fourier mapping improves the separability of
local point distributions.
B. Projected Image Feature Extraction
Projection-image feature extraction transforms point clouds
into structured image representations and leverages 2D net-
works to learn semantic features at different scales. RangeViT
[17] divides the projected image into feature patches and
employs Transformers [29] to integrate local details with
global semantics, while RangeFormer [7] adopts a hierarchical
Transformer architecture to strengthen feature interactions
across different regions. Fast FMVNet [8] improves range-
image representation through an efficient multi-scale architec-
ture, whereas MINet [30] employs multiple feature paths at
different scales with dense cross-scale interactions. RangeRet
[31] introduces circular retention to exploit the 360◦continuity
of range images and model spatial dependencies through
distance-aware decay. Nevertheless, the projected features in
these methods are still organized primarily on regular 2D
grids, with limited consideration of range and point-density
variations across different azimuthal regions. In this work,
we propose STAM, which reorganizes feature sequences ac-
cording to the LiDAR azimuth sampling pattern and performs
differentiated modeling across scanning regions.
C. Point Cloud-Projected Image Feature Fusion
To compensate for the information loss caused by a sin-
gle projected representation, point-projection fusion methods
retain the original point features and exploit the spatial cor-
respondence between points and image representations for
cross-representation interaction. PRNet [18] introduces range-
view projection and point-range convolution to dynamically
map range-image features back to the original points. FRNet
[9] employs point-frustum mappings to alternately propagate
frustum features and aggregate point features across multiple
network stages. HARP-NeXt [32] further develops a multi-
scale point–range fusion backbone to enable interaction be-
tween 3D geometric information and 2D semantic information
at different feature levels. MFINet [12] constructs 3D point-
view, bird's-eye-view, and range-view branches, enabling bidi-
rectional 3D-2D interaction through point-feature projection
and 2D feature enhancement while integrating complementary
information across different views. However, these methods
mainly rely on fixed point–projection correspondences for fea-
ture fusion, without maintaining a shared geometric constraint
during feature updates. In this work, we propose a RCPFF
mechanism that treats the range information shared by both
representations as a common geometric anchor and explicitly
incorporates it into feature interaction and updating, preserving
spatial correspondence while enhancing the complementary
representation of 3D geometric and 2D semantic information.
III. METHODOLOGY
RaSFNet enables collaborative learning between 3D struc-
tural representations and 2D frustum representations through
multi-stage feature interaction, as illustrated in Fig. 2. Given
an input point cloud, spatial correspondences between points
and frustums are first established according to the projection
relationship, producing the corresponding 3D and 2D repre-
sentations. RAGDE, STAM, and RCPFF are then integrated
into successive feature stages to encode, update, and fuse the
two types of features. Finally, the fused features are mapped
back to the point space through the prediction head to generate
point-wise semantic labels.

E. Fusion Head Module
Multi-stage point-frustum feature interaction progressively
updates the point and frustum features, yielding hierarchical
representations with varying spatial resolutions and receptive
fields. These hierarchical representations encode spatial infor-
mation at different granularities, with high-resolution features
preserving finer spatial details and features from deeper stages
capturing responses over broader receptive fields. The fusion
head aggregates the multi-stage point and frustum features
to integrate these complementary representations into unified
point and frustum representations. Specifically, the point fea-
tures are concatenated and fused according to point-wise cor-
respondences, whereas the frustum features are first upsampled
to a common spatial resolution and then concatenated and
fused. The fused frustum features are subsequently mapped
back to the 3D point domain and further integrated with the
fused point features for final point-wise semantic prediction.

In this section, we conduct systematic experiments to eval-
uate the effectiveness of RaSFNet for outdoor LiDAR point
cloud semantic segmentation. Comparisons with state-of-the-
art methods and ablation studies are conducted to assess its
overall segmentation performance and the contributions of
individual components, while qualitative results are provided
to further demonstrate its segmentation performance.

A. Datasets
We conduct comprehensive evaluations on two widely
used outdoor LiDAR semantic segmentation datasets, Se-
manticKITTI [13] and nuScenes [14], covering diverse real-
world autonomous driving scenarios. SemanticKITTI [13] was
collected in Karlsruhe, Germany, using a Velodyne HDL-64E
LiDAR sensor, with a vertical field of view (FoV) ranging
from −25◦to 3◦. Following the official split, 19,130 LiDAR
scans are used for training and 4,071 for validation. Each
scan contains approximately 120,000 points, with 19 semantic
categories considered for evaluation. nuScenes was collected
using a 32-beam LiDAR sensor with a vertical FoV ranging
from −30◦to 10◦. It contains 28,130 training samples and
6,019 validation samples from 1,000 driving scenes. The
dataset provides annotations across 32 semantic categories, of
which 16 categories are evaluated for semantic segmentation.

C. Implementation Details
Although SemanticKITTI [13] and nuScenes [14] differ
in LiDAR beam count (64 vs. 32), both utilize a full 360◦
horizontal FoV. The range image resolutions were set to
64×1024 and 32×1024 for SemanticKITTI [13] and nuScenes
[14], respectively. Each projected point is represented by its
3D coordinates and reflectance intensity. The feature encoder
uses channel dimensions of (64,128,256,256) and compresses
the output to 20 channels, while the four backbone stages
each output 128 channels. The model is trained for 250,000
iterations using AdamW with an initial learning rate of 0.001,
a batch size of 4, and the OneCycle learning-rate policy.
All experiments were conducted on two NVIDIA RTX 4090
GPUs.
D. Quantitative Results
We compare RaSFNet with LiDAR semantic segmentation
methods based on different representations, including point-
based, sparse-voxel, bird's-eye-view, and range-view represen-
tations, on the SemanticKITTI [13] and nuScenes [14] valida-
tion sets. The results are presented in Tables I and II, respec-
tively. RaSFNet achieves mIoU scores of 70.2% and 77.7%
on the SemanticKITTI [13] and nuScenes [14] validation sets,
respectively. On SemanticKITTI [13], RaSFNet performs well
in the motorcycle, parking, sidewalk, and fence classes. On
nuScenes [14], RaSFNet also obtains favorable results in the
bicycle, motorcycle, bus, and pedestrian classes. The results
suggest that RaSFNet maintains competitive overall accuracy
and performs well across several semantic classes.
As the sensing distance increases, the spatial sampling
density of LiDAR point clouds gradually decreases, accom-
panied by an overall decline in semantic segmentation perfor-
mance across different methods. Near-range regions generally
yield higher mIoU than far-range regions, indicating the per-
formance variation under different point-density conditions.

F. Ablation Study
Component Ablation: Table IV evaluates the contribu-
tions of RAGDE, STAM, and RCPFF through leave-one-
component-out experiments under identical training settings.
Removing RCPFF reduces mIoU by 2.7 percentage points
on SemanticKITTI and 2.2 percentage points on nuScenes,
highlighting the importance of effectively integrating 3D struc-
tural information with 2D frustum features. Removing STAM
also degrades performance on both datasets, suggesting that
axis-aware aggregation and serpentine sequence modeling help
capture contextual information from the encoded geometric
features. Removing RAGDE reduces both mIoU and mAcc,
supporting the contribution of ray-aligned encoding to local
geometric representation. These consistent trends across the
two datasets support the contributions of RAGDE, STAM,
and RCPFF within the overall framework. The complete
configuration outperforms all ablated variants in both mIoU
and mAcc on both datasets.

V. CONCLUSION
This paper presented RaSFNet for projection-based LiDAR
semantic segmentation. Guided by the physical acquisition
characteristics of LiDAR, RaSFNet amplifies subtle differ-
ences among similar local point distributions through ray-
aligned encoding, serializes frustum features in a serpentine
row-wise order along the azimuthal dimension, and uses range
information shared by point and frustum representations to
constrain their bidirectional updates. Across the evaluated
datasets, RaSFNet produces more coherent semantic regions
and clearer class boundaries, with less confusion between spa-
tially adjacent classes that share similar geometric structures.
Class-wise evaluations and ablation studies provide further
analyses of the segmentation performance and the roles of
the main components. Future work will explore extending
RaSFNet to other 3D perception tasks, including 3D object
detection and semantic occupancy prediction.
