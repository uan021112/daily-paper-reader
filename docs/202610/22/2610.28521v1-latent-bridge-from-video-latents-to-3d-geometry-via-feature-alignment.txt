ABSTRACT 

Video generation and feed-forward 3D reconstruction typically use separate rep-resentations, requiring RGB decoding and visual re-encoding to recover geometry from generated latents. We propose Latent Bridge, which connects video gen-erative latent spaces with geometric feature spaces to directly predict 3D geom-etry from video latents. We develop representation-specific bridges for Wan2.1 and V-RAE, enabling feed-forward prediction of cameras, depth, and 3D point clouds without the intermediate RGB pathway. Each bridge is trained on RGB videos through feature distillation, while the video encoder and geometry model remain frozen. Training requires neither ground-truth geometry nor execution of the downstream geometric aggregator or prediction heads. At inference, the same video latents support both RGB decoding and geometry prediction. The same trained bridge can be reused for image-to-video and text-to-video genera-tion within the same video latent space, without bridge retraining. Experiments on Co3Dv2 and WildRGB-D demonstrate competitive geometric reconstruction quality, efficient end-to-end generation and reconstruction, and faster latent-to-geometry decoding, highlighting latent alignment as a practical and reusable in-terface between video generation and 3D reconstruction. Our code is available at: https://github.com/hustvl/Latent-Bridge. 

1 INTRODUCTION 

Recent advances in video generation and feed-forward 3D reconstruction have enabled com-pelling visual synthesis and efficient geometric inference. Video generation models, such as CogVideoX (Yang et al., 2025) and Wan2.1 (Wan et al., 2025), combine spatiotemporally com-pressed latent representations with diffusion transformers to synthesize high-quality, temporally coherent videos. Meanwhile, feed-forward reconstruction models, such as VGGT (Wang et al., 2025), jointly predict camera parameters, depth maps, and 3D point maps from multi-view images in a single forward pass. Recent work has begun to explore the joint execution of these two tasks: generating videos while simultaneously reconstructing the generated content. Despite their complementary goals, video generation and feed-forward 3D reconstruction typically rely on different representations. Video generators synthesize content in spatiotemporally com-pressed latent spaces, whereas reconstruction models often aggregate features from visual founda-tion models such as DINOv2 (Oquab et al., 2024) to infer scene geometry. This representational gap means that combining the two models typically involves decoding generated latents into RGB frames and re-encoding them into geometric features, introducing additional computation. This raises the question: Can scene geometry be generated directly without RGB decoding? 

Recent work has begun to integrate video generation with explicit geometry prediction. Gen3R (Huang et al., 2026) compresses intermediate VGGT features into geometric latents, aligns their distribution with video appearance latents, and fine-tunes a video diffusion model to jointly generate both. As shown in Fig. 1(a), joint generation in a shared latent space requires additional training and can reduce efficiency due to the increased number of tokens. VGGRPO (An et al., 2026) uses latent-space geometry predictions to construct rewards for video model post-training, while Beyond Pix-els (Liu et al., 2026) combines latent alignment, LoRA adaptation, and geometry-supervised fine-tuning of prediction heads for direct latent-to-4D prediction. As shown in Fig. 1(b), these methods all aim to train a 3D/4D decoder module, requiring substantial data and retraining of the decoder, while also raising concerns about generalization. Unlike the aforementioned methods, we argue that the key lies in aligning the generative and re-construction latent spaces. We propose Latent Bridge, which directly maps video latents into the representation space of a frozen feed-forward geometry model (Fig. 1(c)). The frozen downstream VGGT aggregator and depth head can then be used to reconstruct the scene. This provides an elegant solution that eliminates the need for joint feature training or training downstream decoder heads. It also generalizes well, enabling the reuse of existing generative models and downstream tasks built on the same representation. Specifically, we adapt our framework to two representative video en-coders, the Wan2.1 VAE and V-RAE (Guo et al., 2026), by designing a dedicated Bridge network for each. This enables models built upon either encoder to directly generate scene geometry. Our contributions can be summarized as follows: • We propose Latent Bridge, which aligns the generative latent space with the representation space of a reconstruction model, enabling geometry to be recovered directly from generated latents. The Bridge module is trained through feature distillation and requires only video data for training. This substantially reduces the cost of recovering geometry from the latent spaces of generative models. • We adapt our framework to two video encoders, Wan2.1 and V-RAE, by designing a dedi-cated Latent Bridge network for each. Both variants achieve strong performance, demon-strating that the Latent Bridge framework can effectively accommodate different generative latent spaces. • Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed Latent Bridge enables high-quality and efficient reconstruction. It also exhibits strong generalization: within the same latent space, it can support generative models designed for different generation tasks, as well as other downstream tasks, without retraining. 

2 RELATED WORK 

Generative latent space. Modern video generation models commonly extend latent diffusion to the spatiotemporal domain, where a video VAE compresses RGB frames into compact latent representations and a diffusion transformer models their temporal dynamics. Stable Video Diffu-sion (Blattmann et al., 2023) establishes a scalable latent video diffusion framework, while Hun-yuanVideo (Kong et al., 2024) demonstrates the effectiveness of large diffusion transformers and progressive training for high-fidelity video generation. Wan2.1 (Wan et al., 2025) follows this 2Preprint paradigm and combines a causal spatiotemporal VAE with a diffusion transformer to support both text-to-video and image-to-video generation. REPA (Yu et al., 2025) and VA-VAE (Yao et al., 2025) align diffusion features and VAE latents, respectively, with pretrained visual representations to im-prove generative modeling. RAE (Zheng et al., 2026) directly adopts frozen visual encoders, while V-RAE (Guo et al., 2026) extends this paradigm to video through temporal pooling. 

Feed-forward reconstruction. DUSt3R (Wang et al., 2024) formulates 3D reconstruction as pointmap regression, jointly predicting dense 3D points for an image pair in a shared coordi-nate frame without requiring known camera parameters. Moving beyond pairwise prediction, VGGT (Wang et al., 2025), built upon a DINOv2-pretrained visual encoder (Oquab et al., 2024), jointly predicts camera parameters, depth maps, 3D point maps, and point tracks from multiple RGB images in a single forward pass, without per-scene optimization. VGGT-Ω (Wang et al., 2026) further initializes its trainable visual backbone from DINOv3 (Sim´ eoni et al., 2026) and introduces scene registers, register attention, and a shared dense prediction head. Together with large-scale self-supervised video training, these designs improve the scalability and efficiency of feed-forward geometry reconstruction for both static and dynamic scenes. 

Joint generative-reconstructive method Recent work has explored video and 3D co-generation through complementary approaches. FantasyWorld (Dai et al., 2026) couples video and geometry latents through a trainable geometry branch on a frozen Wan2.1 backbone, while LYRA (Bahmani et al., 2026) introduces a self-distilled 3D Gaussian decoder. Gen3R (Huang et al., 2026) jointly generates video and geometry in a unified latent space, whereas PixWorld (Gao et al., 2026a) op-erates in pixel space to avoid VAE-induced detail loss. VGGT-World (Sun et al., 2026) instead forecasts geometry-centric VGGT tokens and decodes future geometry using frozen heads, with-out synthesizing RGB frames. VGGRPO (An et al., 2026) uses latent-space geometry predictions to construct rewards for video DiT post-training, while Beyond Pixels (Liu et al., 2026) directly decodes geometry from video latents. VIST3A (Go et al., 2026) connects video latents to a se-lected intermediate reconstruction layer and jointly trains the stitching layer and downstream LoRA adapters using teacher-predicted geometry. Pano2World (Li et al., 2026) aligns diffusion hidden features with a pretrained reconstruction model’s token space through feature distillation, followed by joint fine-tuning of the adapter and Gaussian decoder under rendering supervision. In contrast, we map video latents to a predefined geometric feature interface through feature alignment, keeping the downstream geometric aggregator and prediction heads entirely frozen. 

3 PRELIMINARIES 

3.1 VIDEO LATENT REPRESENTATIONS 

Video generation models typically use an encoder to compress videos into a latent space. We define an RGB video with T frames, height H, and width W as X ∈ RT ×3×H×W . In this paper, we focus on two types of VAEs: the convolutional VAE used in Wan2.1 (Wan et al., 2025) and the representation-based autoencoder V-RAE (Guo et al., 2026). 

Wan2.1 VAE. Wan2.1 (Wan et al., 2025) employs a causal spatiotemporal VAE with a spatial compression factor of 8 and 16 latent channels. The first frame is compressed only spatially, while subsequent frames undergo an additional temporal downsampling factor of 4. For T − 1 divisible by 4, its encoder produces 

ZW = EW(X) ∈ RTW ×16 ×(H/ 8) ×(W/ 8) , TW = 1 + T − 14 , (1) where EW denotes the encoding operation and TW is the latent temporal length. The corresponding decoder reconstructs RGB frames from ZW.

V-RAE. V-RAE (Guo et al., 2026) instead constructs its latent space using a frozen visual repre-sentation encoder. We consider its DINOv3-based variant, which extracts frame-wise features with 

1024 channels and a spatial compression factor of 16 . Throughout this paper, V-RAE refers specif-ically to the pretrained weights of this DINOv3-based variant. A learned temporal attention pooler 3Preprint aggregates adjacent feature maps while preserving their channel dimension: 

ZR = PR(ER(X)) ∈ RTR×1024 ×(H/ 16) ×(W/ 16) , (2) where ER is the frozen representation encoder, PR is the temporal pooler, and TR denotes the pooled temporal length. With four-frame pooling, each latent step summarizes a four-frame feature window, yielding TR = T / 4. A spatiotemporal Transformer decoder maps these representations back to RGB videos. Unlike the low-channel VAE bottleneck, V-RAE retains high-dimensional visual features as its generative representation. 3.2 FEED -F ORWARD GEOMETRY RECONSTRUCTION 

VGGT (Wang et al., 2025) extracts DINO-based image tokens and aggregates them through alternat-ing frame-wise and global attention. Its prediction heads estimate camera parameters, depth maps, and point maps. VGGT-Ω (Wang et al., 2026) introduces register attention for efficient cross-frame information exchange and simplifies dense prediction to a single depth head, alongside a camera head. For the camera–depth pathway: 

{Dt, Kt, Rt, tt}Tt=1 = Hgeo (Ageo (Eimg (X))) , (3) where Eimg is the image feature encoder, Ageo is the multi-frame aggregator, and Hgeo denotes the prediction heads and camera-parameter conversion. Here, Dt ∈ RH×W is the depth map, 

Kt ∈ R3×3 is the intrinsic matrix, and Rt ∈ R3×3 and tt ∈ R3 define the world-to-camera rotation and translation. The predicted depth maps and camera parameters can be used to recover 3D point clouds through standard depth unprojection. 

4 METHOD 

We propose an alignment framework between generative latents and the representations of a feed-forward geometry model, which we term the Latent Bridge . The key idea is to jointly support video generation and 3D reconstruction by aligning generative and reconstruction representations, without retraining the existing vision autoencoder, generative model, or 3D reconstruction heads. For the Wan2.1 VAE and V-RAE, we design distinct Latent Bridge networks and develop a corresponding training procedure, as illustrated in Fig. 2. V-RAE Semantic-Latent Bridge. The V-RAE branch operates on a semantically richer latent representation. A frozen DINOv3 K7 encoder extracts frame-level feature grids, and a frozen temporal pooler compresses each group of four frames into one latent chunk. For a 49-frame video at 560 × 560 resolution, the V-RAE latent has shape ZV ∈ R13 ×1024 ×35 ×35 , matching the spatial grid and channel dimension of the VGGT-Ω tokens. The target token tensor has shape 

bYV ∈ R49 ×1225 ×1024 . Thus, temporal expansion is sufficient to match the target dimensions. To align the conditioning frame with the following 48 frames, we prepend three copies of the first frame before temporal pooling, producing a 52-frame input and 13 latent chunks. For each spatial token, LayerNorm and a linear projection produce four residuals. Adding each residual and its learned phase embedding to the original token yields four temporally ordered tokens, expanding 13 latent chunks into 52 frame features. Because the V-RAE latent already matches the spatial grid size and channel dimension of the VGGT-Ω tokens, no spatial resampling is required. The bridge applies a residual spatial MLP to align the expanded features with VGGT-Ω tokens, followed by per-patch temporal attention to model relationships across frames. 6 ABLATION STUDY AND GENERALIZATION EVALUATION 

Parameter settings. In this ablation study, we investigate how bridge capacity affects latent-space alignment. As shown in Fig. 4, we vary only the internal hidden width of the bridge network to control its capacity, keeping all other settings fixed within each model family. For the Wan Bridge, widths of 256, 512, and 768 yield 9.11M, 35.65M, and 79.62M trainable parameters, respectively. 7 CONCLUSION 

We presented Latent Bridge, a framework that connects video generation and feed-forward 3D re-construction through latent-space feature alignment. By mapping Wan2.1 and V-RAE video rep-resentations to a predefined geometric feature interface, our method enables direct prediction of cameras, depth, and point clouds while keeping the downstream geometric model frozen. This de-sign bypasses RGB decoding and visual re-encoding in the geometry branch, allowing a shared video latent representation to support both video synthesis and geometry prediction. Experiments on Co3Dv2 and WildRGB-D demonstrate competitive reconstruction quality with improved infer-ence efficiency. More broadly, our results suggest that feature alignment offers a practical route to reusing pretrained geometric models with generative video representations. AI USE STATEMENT

As shown in Tab. 5, we compare our method with existing approaches from two perspectives: com-patibility with existing models and training overhead. Cross-DiT checkpoint reuse refers to whether the method can produce 3D outputs using different generative DiT checkpoints without retraining. FantasyWorld trains a geometry branch and cross-branch adapters while keeping the video back-bone frozen, whereas Gen3R fine-tunes the video DiT to jointly generate appearance and geometry latents. Beyond Pixels and our method instead learn geometry pathways from video latents without updating the DiT, enabling reuse across compatible generators that share the same VAE checkpoint and latent conventions. We demonstrate this reuse by applying the same Wan Bridge checkpoint to camera-conditioned generation and the official Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B model without additional Bridge training. Pretrained task-head reuse refers to the ability to reuse existing downstream heads be-cause our method outputs features in VGGT’s DINO feature space. Consequently, the resulting features can be used not only for reconstruction with VGGT but also for cross-view segmentation with VGGT-S, as shown in Section A.2. Gen3R reconstructs intermediate VGGT geometry tokens, which are decoded by pretrained geometry heads. In contrast, FantasyWorld and Beyond Pixels incorporate their decoder heads into training and therefore do not offer this advantage. Therefore, our method offers substantially greater versatility than existing approaches. In addition, we compare the additional training costs required to support both generation and re-construction. FantasyWorld trains a geometry branch and cross-branch adapters, while Gen3R fine-tunes the video DiT for joint video and geometry generation. Beyond Pixels trains an alignment module, LoRA adapters, and geometry prediction heads. In contrast, we train only the Bridge through feature alignment, keeping the video encoder, geometric aggregator, and prediction heads frozen. Keeping the decoder heads frozen further reduces the training cost. Furthermore, Fanta-syWorld and Gen3R introduce additional geometry tokens. Our bridge is trained using feature su-pervision alone, without executing the downstream geometric aggregator or prediction heads during optimization. A.4 LIMITATION .Our Bridge network has the following limitations: First, our model depends on a pretrained geom-etry model. The bridge learns to match teacher features while keeping the downstream geometry model frozen, and therefore cannot be expected to correct the teacher’s geometric errors. Second, inconsistencies in generated videos are not automatically corrected. Our method extracts geometry from generated latents but does not explicitly constrain the video generation process. Therefore, if the generated content exhibits structural changes or object inconsistencies across frames, the Bridge cannot guarantee the reconstruction of a coherent 3D scene. 15
