## Abstract 

In generalized few-shot 3D point cloud segmentation, support annotations are often compromised by sparsity and occlusion. Blindly adapting to such low-quality sam-ples risks learning spurious geometric patterns rather than robust novel concepts. Although 3D Vision-Language Models have been introduced to supplement supervi-sion, the potential of uncertainty to actively govern adaptation dynamics remains un-derexplored. Existing signal-level methods like loss reweighting fail to prevent un-reliable episodes from steering the optimization trajectory. To address this, we pro-pose Uncertainty-Gated Adaptive (UGA) , to the best of our knowledge the first framework that elevates uncertainty to update-level gating for generalized few-shot 3D point cloud segmentation. The core module, Uncertainty-Gated Update (UG-Update) , estimates episodic reliability via intra-class dispersion over merged sup-port and pseudo-labeled novel supervision, and selectively gates residual gradient updates. This encourages the model to learn novel semantics from reliable episodes while attenuating updates during unreliable ones. Complementing this, we intro-duce an Uncertainty-Based Pseudo-Label Filter (UB-Filter) to enforce learning from high-confidence dense signals. On ScanNet200 and ScanNet++ (1-/5-shot), UGA sets new state of the art with an average +3 .08 HM IoU over the strongest prior baseline. Code: https://anonymous.4open.science/r/UGA/ .

## 1 Introduction 

Semantic segmentation of 3D point clouds serves as a fundamental engine for embodied Artificial Intelligence (AI) and multimedia retrieval. To reduce the prohibitive cost of dense 3D annotation, Generalized Few-Shot 3D Segmentation (GFS-3DS) requires a model to acquire novel classes from only a few support examples while preserving its recognition ability on base classes [2]. Given the inherent sparsity, occlusion, and viewpoint limitations of 3D scanning, a support shot is frequently a fragmented partial view or a noisy outlier rather than a canonical class prototype [14]. Blindly forcing the model to adapt to such compromised support samples inevitably results in the learning of spurious geometric features, leading to severe semantic drift and poor generalization [37, 32]. The core limitation of existing adaptation pipelines is that they treat every episode as an equally trustworthy optimization event. Although uncertainty has been widely used for signal-level calibration, such as loss reweighting [17], filtering pseudo-labels [34], and reliability benchmarking in 3D scenes [18], these methods still allow every episode to induce an update, implicitly assuming that the resulting optimization direction is safe. We argue that when the global geometry of episode-level novel supervision is highly dispersed or non-representative, signal-level adjustments are insufficient. In such cases, the optimization trajectory is fundamentally misguided. Robust novel-class adaptation therefore requires selective plasticity: the model should release novel-directed updates only when the current episode provides coherent and reliable supervision.

As illustrated in Figure 1, prior pipelines typi-cally update the model after every support episode, even when the observed geometry is fragmented or non-representative. To address this limitation, we propose Uncertainty-Gated Adaptive (UGA) ,a framework that elevates uncertainty from signal-level calibration to update-level control. UGA es-timates episodic reliability by measuring the intra-class dispersion of novel features after merging sparse support annotations with pseudo-labeled su-pervision. It then decomposes the adaptation gra-dient into a base-aligned component, supported by the Phase-1 optimization geometry, and a residual component that captures novel-directed adapta-tion. The proposed Uncertainty-Gated Update (UG-Update) module uses episodic uncertainty to regulate this residual component: compact novel supervision preserves residual adaptation, whereas dispersed supervision attenuates unstable residual updates and pulls the update toward the base-aligned direction, reducing the risk of semantic drift. This allows adaptation to remain plastic when supervision is reliable while becoming conservative when the episode is likely to induce misleading gradients. While UGA prevents the corruption of learned representations by rejecting low-quality updates, this conservatism may reduce the total volume of supervision. To compensate, we leverage 3D Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to generate dense pseudo-supervision for novel regions, a direction that has shown promise in recent open-vocabulary tasks [3, 13]. However, consistent with our rigorous quality standards, we recognize that VLMs can also produce hallucinations in cluttered 3D scenes. Therefore, we integrate a complementary Uncertainty-Based Pseudo-Label Filter (UB-Filter) . UB-Filter retains only high-confidence VLM predictions, forming a two-level uncertainty design, UB-Filter purifies the supervision entering the loss, whereas UG-Update regulates the residual gradient induced by that supervision. Our contributions are summarized as follows: • Update-level uncertainty gating: We propose UGA with UG-Update, which uses episodic reliability to regulate residual gradients and prevent noisy adaptation episodes from causing semantic drift. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work to leverage uncertainty for update-level gating in GFS-3DS. • Uncertainty-filtered VLM supervision: We introduce UB-Filter to retain only reliable VLM pseudo labels, providing high-purity dense signals to mitigate support sparsity. • State-of-the-art(SOTA) results: UGA achieves new SOTA performance on ScanNet200 and ScanNet++ under generalized 1-/5-shot settings [28, 39], improving the harmonic mean by an average of +3.08 HM (up to +4.96 HM) over the strongest baseline. 

## 2 Related Work 

2.1 Generalized Few-Shot 3D Segmentation 

Few-shot segmentation was first extensively studied in 2D images [29, 31], but 3D point clouds present unique challenges due to data sparsity. Early 3D works adopted episodic meta-learning [40] or transductive inference [26]. However, real-world deployment requires GFS-3DS, recognizing both base and novel classes [25, 2]. Various strategies have been proposed to tackle this, including prototype adaptation [12], decoupling localization from segmentation [20], and learning “what not to segment” to handle backgrounds [19]. Others have explored incremental settings [4], prediction calibration [24], and class-contrastive learning [22] to mitigate bias. Recent benchmarks have further revisited evaluation protocols [1] and proposed non-parametric networks to reduce training time [42]. While strong fine-tuning baselines [11], geometry-aware methods [37, 32], and dynamic routing strategies [14] have improved GFS-3DS performance, they largely assume that each adaptation episode provides a trustworthy optimization signal. As a result, episode reliability remains under-modeled, especially when sparse supports or noisy pseudo supervision induce misleading updates. 22.2 Uncertainty and Reliability in 3D Understanding 

Uncertainty estimation has long been used to improve reliability, from Bayesian approximations such as dropout [9, 16] to signal-level calibration in segmentation, including uncertainty-weighted losses [17]. In semi-supervised and domain adaptation settings, uncertainty-aware pseudo-label filtering and rectification are standard practices to mitigate noise [34, 41, 43, 35]. In 3D, Calib3D recently established benchmarks for model calibration [18]. However, these approaches predominantly operate at the signal level, modulating input data or loss weights. They do not explicitly control optimization dynamics (e.g., gradients). The proposed UGA framework bridges this gap by elevating uncertainty to the update level, protecting the model from noisy gradient flows. 

2.3 VLM-Assisted 3D Segmentation 

Vision-language models have substantially expanded open-vocabulary 3D understanding by aligning 3D representations with language-supervised visual features [27, 15, 28, 6]. By projecting 3D point clouds to CLIP-aligned feature spaces, these methods enable zero-shot recognition capabilities. In the context of few-shot segmentation, SOTA methods, most notably GFS-VL [2] which serves as our primary baseline, leverage VLMs to generate dense pseudo-supervision to compensate for support sparsity [3]. More recently, LLM-driven reasoning has been introduced to refine 3D segmentation results [13]. While promising, VLM-derived supervision can introduce erroneous novel pseudo labels under domain gaps, projection errors, and cluttered 3D observations. Complementing these works, this work introduces UB-Filter to verify VLM signals before using them for adaptation. 

2.4 Safe Adaptation and Gradient Control 

Preventing catastrophic forgetting is a core concern in continual learning. Approaches like Gradient Episodic Memory (GEM) [23] and its efficient variant A-GEM [5] constrain updates using memory. Orthogonal Gradient Descent (OGD) [8] projects gradients to avoid interference in continual learning. More recently, gradient/subspace-based update control has been further explored, such as continual gradient low-rank projection fine-tuning (GORP) [33]. In 2D segmentation, orthogonality-constrained prototype learning is introduced to reduce base–novel interference [21]. In contrast, UGA uses episodic uncertainty to regulate the residual update outside the base-aligned subspace. This provides an episode-adaptive form of gradient control, whereas prior gradient-control methods are typically episode-invariant and signal-level uncertainty methods do not regulate the update direction. 

## 3 Methodology 

3.1 Overview 

We propose UGA, a unified framework for GFS-3DS that stabilizes novel-class adaptation while pre-serving base-class semantics under limited supervision. UGA follows a standard two-phase protocol with base pretraining (Phase 1) and novel adaptation (Phase 2), and introduces two complementary uncertainty-aware mechanisms that are activated only during Phase 2. The proposed framework is illustrated in Figure 2. The first component, UG-Update, operates at the optimization level by decomposing the adaptation gradient into a base-aligned component and a residual component, and adaptively suppressing the residual update under high episodic uncertainty. The second component, UB-Filter, operates at the supervision level by retaining only low-uncertainty pseudo-labeled points from a frozen VLM. Together, these two components decouple supervision reliability from update dynamics and effectively mitigate base–novel interference in GFS-3DS. In each Phase 2 batch, UB-Filter first removes high-uncertainty pseudo labels. UG-Update then computes dispersion on the merged support/retained-pseudo supervision and gates the resulting update. The two modules act on orthogonal axes of the same proximal step (see details in Appendix A). UG-Update is a trust region on the update direction via λ(ue), while UB-Filter is a quality filter on the supervision content feeding the gradient g itself. 3.2 Uncertainty-Gated Update (UG-Update) Motivation. Generalized few-shot adaptation is sensitive to unreliable supervision, since even a small fraction of noisy novel signals can steer gradients toward directions that interfere with base semantics. UG-Update addresses this issue by explicitly separating a base-aligned update component (consistent with Phase 1 optimization geometry) from a residual component (plastic but potentially interfering), and then using episodic uncertainty to regulate how much residual update is allowed.

Because GFS-3DS Phase 2 supervision still contains base labels, g∥ is sourced from supervision the backbone has already been trained on, while g⊥ aggregates updates introduced by the few-shot novel supports and VLM pseudo labels, precisely the noisy signals we want to gate when the episode is unreliable. ## 4 Experiments 

4.1 Experimental Setup 

We evaluate UGA on ScanNet200 [28] and ScanNet++ [39] under GFS-3DS [2]. We use disjoint base and novel class splits, as detailed in Appendix C. Following the GFS-VL protocol, models are pretrained on base classes, adapted with episodic 1-/5-shot novel supports, and evaluated on query scenes spanning all classes. We report comparisons against the strongest GFS-VL baseline. We report mIoU (%) on base ( B), novel ( N), and all ( A) classes, plus harmonic mean ( HM ) between base and novel mIoU as the stability–plasticity summary. Unless stated otherwise we follow GFS-VL’s protocol using PTv3 [36] and frozen RegionPLC [38], with experiments on 4 NVIDIA A100 GPUs averaged over 5 runs; Appendix D reports mean ±std over the five runs. ## 5 Conclusion 

We propose UGA, the first GFS-3DS framework to actively regulate adaptation dynamics using update-level uncertainty for stable few-shot 3D adaptation under noisy, sparse supervision. UGA combines UG-Update, which gates residual gradients by episodic reliability, and UB-Filter, which retains reliable VLM pseudo-labels via class-wise uncertainty filtering. Achieving SOTA performance on ScanNet200 and ScanNet++ (1-/5-shot), UGA demonstrates a superior stability–plasticity trade-off. Beyond GFS-3DS, using uncertainty to reshape optimization geometry, rather than simply reweighting signals, holds broad relevance for continual learning and other sparse-supervision regimes. Limitations. Validated on indoor RGB-D; outdoor LiDAR requires re-estimating the base-gradient subspace, and UB-Filter cannot correct confidently wrong pseudo-labels (affecting both loss and dispersion estimate). Reproducibility / broader impact. ## D Implementation Details and Reproducibility 

Overall protocol. We follow the standard two-phase generalized few-shot training protocol. In Phase 1, the segmentation network is trained on base classes only. In Phase 2, the model is adapted to 16 novel classes with few-shot support annotations while being evaluated on all classes. Our proposed components, UG-Update and UB-Filter, are activated only in Phase 2; Phase 1 follows GFS-VL [2]. 

Backbone and pseudo supervision. We adopt Point Transformer v3 (PTv3) as the 3D backbone and use a frozen 3D vision–language model to provide dense pseudo supervision during Phase 2. The vision–language model is used solely to produce per-point pseudo labels and the associated predictive scores needed for uncertainty estimation; it is not fine-tuned. 

UG-Update: uncertainty-gated residual gradient update. UG-Update is applied after backprop-agation and before each optimizer step in Phase 2. We compute episodic uncertainty ue from the intra-class dispersion of novel-class features under the merged support/pseudo-label supervision within the current batch (Eq. (2)), normalized by an exponential moving average ( β=0 .99 ) of running mean/variance (Eq. (5)); during the first 10 steps the EMA statistics are still accumulating and we return ue=0 , so the gate is essentially open ( s ≈ 1) and UG-Update reduces to a no-op until normal-ization is well-defined. We then decompose the Phase-2 adaptation gradient g into a base-aligned component g∥ and a residual g⊥ via projection onto a fixed base-gradient subspace (Eq. (9)). The basis B is constructed once at the end of Phase 1 by sampling T =500 per-batch base-task loss gradients ∇ϕLbase (x; ϕ∗

> base

) on the base validation stream, then applying per-parameter QR factor-ization and retaining the first r orthonormal columns to form a rank-r basis (cf. Eq. (8)); B is then frozen throughout Phase 2. Finally, UG-Update gates the residual update via the sigmoid coefficient 

s = σ(−k(ue − τ )) to produce the controlled gradient gUG = g∥ + sg ⊥ (Eq. (10)). PUG comprises the PTv3 backbone parameters and the base segmentation head; the novel-class head is excluded so its parameters remain unconstrained, preserving novel-class adaptation capacity. Implementation-wise, an empty apply_to list in our config is interpreted as “apply to all parameters that have a stored basis,” which after pretraining covers exactly PUG .

UB-Filter: uncertainty-based pseudo-label filtering. UB-Filter improves the reliability of dense pseudo supervision in Phase 2 by discarding uncertain pseudo-labeled points. Given per-point predictive probabilities from the frozen vision–language model (Eq. (11)), we compute a point-wise uncertainty score using one of three alternatives: entropy (Eq. (12)), margin (Eq. (13)), or max-probability (Eq. (14)). We perform class-wise quantile filtering over novel pseudo labels: for each novel class c, we retain the lowest-uncertainty ρ fraction of points and map all others to the ignore label. Importantly, the class-specific filtering threshold qc is not treated as a tunable hyperparameter; it is computed on-the-fly from the uncertainty distribution within class c via Eq. (15). This keeps the training objective unchanged while ensuring that only high-confidence dense supervision contributes to the loss. 

Optimization and hyperparameters. All experiments follow the GFS-VL [2] optimization setup. The only difference across UGA variants is the point-wise uncertainty definition used by UB-Filter (Entropy/Margin/Max). All key training and UGA settings are summarized in Table 6. 

Reproducibility notes. We ensure that episodic uncertainty used for UG-Update is consistent across distributed workers by synchronizing it before computing the gate coefficient. For each independent run, the optimizer state is reset at the beginning of Phase 2 adaptation to avoid cross-run coupling via momentum statistics.

## F Generality Across Backbones and VLMs 

Backbone generality. Table 9 shows that UGA consistently improves over GFS-VL under SCN [10], with gains of +1.83 / +2.42 / +2.30 / +2.51 in B/N/A/HM. The transfer is mechanistic rather than tuned: both components of UGA operate on backbone outputs generically—UG-Update builds its base-gradient subspace B from whatever Phase 1 gradients the backbone produces and decomposes every Phase 2 gradient through the same projector, while UB-Filter only consumes per-point VLM probabilities and is agnostic to how the backbone encodes geometry. The larger gain on Novel (+2.42) than Base (+1.83) is consistent with UG-Update’s intended role: attenuating residual updates during unreliable episodes mostly helps novel-class adaptation, where sparse supports are the dominant noise source. 

VLM generality. Table 10 shows that UGA improves performance under both RegionPLC and OpenScene [27]. OpenScene is the considerably weaker VLM on this split (baseline HM 30 .87 

vs. RegionPLC’s 43 .12 ), yet UGA still delivers +2 .78 HM on top of it, compared with +2 .53 on the stronger RegionPLC. This is consistent with the design logic of UB-Filter: as VLM reliability drops, the heavy tail of high-uncertainty pseudo-labels enlarges, so class-wise quantile pruning has more noise to remove and its marginal contribution does not shrink with VLM quality.
