## Abstract 

This paper studies LiDAR-assisted RGB semantic seg-mentation, where LiDAR point-cloud features improve dense pixel-wise predictions in the RGB image plane rather than 3D point labels or detections. Existing 2D fusion methods often project point clouds into shallow depth/range maps and apply spatially uniform fusion, discarding rich 3D structure and ignoring the range-dependent reliabil-ity of outdoor LiDAR. We propose RAHF-Net, a Range-Aware Heterogeneous Fusion Network. Its Cylindrical-to-Perspective (C2P) module projects pretrained cylindrical voxel features into the image plane, preserving structured 3D semantic priors. A Range-Aware Heterogeneous Fusion (RAHF) module then uses a soft depth-derived range mask to perform LiDAR-guided geometric enhancement in reli-able near-field regions and visual-context enhancement in sparse far-field regions. On KITTI-360, RAHF-Net achieves 65.76 mIoU, outperforming representative RGB-only and RGB-LiDAR baselines, including CMX by 1.45 points. Ex-periments on DELIVER further demonstrate improved ro-bustness under adverse weather, illumination degradation, and sensor corruption. 

## 1. Introduction 

Semantic segmentation is a core perception task for au-tonomous driving, where dense pixel-level scene under-standing supports safe planning, obstacle avoidance, and decision making [7, 24]. Modern RGB-based segmen-tation models have achieved strong performance through fully convolutional networks, encoder-decoder designs, and transformer architectures [5, 16, 17, 20, 23]. Nevertheless, camera-only perception remains physically tied to the imag-ing condition. tion blur, or shadowed regions, visual texture and color can become unreliable, causing severe degradation for safety-critical categories such as pedestrians, traffic signs, and ve-hicles [4, 24, 28]. LiDAR provides complementary depth and geometric structure that is less sensitive to illumination, making RGB-LiDAR fusion an important direction for ro-bust driving-scene segmentation [6, 11, 13, 25]. In this paper, we focus on a specific and relatively under-explored setting: using LiDAR point-cloud features to im-prove RGB image-plane semantic segmentation. The out-put of the model is a dense 2D semantic label map aligned with the camera image, rather than point-wise 3D segmen-tation, 3D object detection, or semantic scene completion. This task setting is different from many recent LiDAR-camera perception studies that use RGB to enhance 3D point-cloud prediction. Here, LiDAR acts as an auxiliary geometric source for the RGB segmentation branch, so the central challenge is how to transfer 3D point-cloud knowl-edge into the 2D image plane without introducing unreliable projected noise. 1arXiv:2610.25400v2 [cs.CV] 20 Oct 2026 Despite this complementarity, effective RGB-LiDAR fu-sion for 2D semantic segmentation remains challenging. A common pipeline first projects LiDAR observations into the image plane as depth maps, range maps, or sparse geo-metric channels, and then fuses them with image features using concatenation, convolution, attention, or token ex-change [3, 9, 21, 22, 25]. Such a pipeline is convenient be-cause it converts multi-sensor alignment into a 2D feature-fusion problem. However, it also introduces two limitations that are especially important in autonomous driving. First, projection compresses a structured 3D signal into a shal-low 2D map. The voxel topology, radial density variation, and high-level semantic geometry learned by modern Li-DAR encoders are largely discarded before fusion. Sec-ond, most fusion modules share parameters spatially over the whole image. This assumes that LiDAR is equally in-formative everywhere, which is inconsistent with outdoor sensing physics. The reliability of LiDAR is strongly range-dependent. As illustrated in Figure 1, near-field points are dense and can provide accurate object boundaries, making them valu-able for correcting RGB ambiguity under shadows, reflec-tions, or strong illumination. In contrast, far-field points are sparse, incomplete, and more vulnerable to calibration error or quantization noise. Forcing the same RGB-LiDAR inter-action in both regions may therefore underuse reliable near-field geometry while injecting noisy far-field features into visually coherent regions. This observation suggests that RGB-LiDAR segmentation should be both representation-aware and range-aware: it should transfer richer 3D seman-tic features into the image branch, and it should adapt the fusion behavior to the physical reliability of LiDAR across distance. Motivated by this analysis, we propose RAHF-Net, a Range-Aware Heterogeneous Fusion Network for LiDAR-assisted RGB image-plane semantic segmentation. In-stead of treating LiDAR as only a projected depth channel, RAHF-Net reuses a pretrained cylindrical 3D encoder [27, 29] to extract high-level voxel features that preserve the radial structure and density pattern of driving-scene point clouds. We then design a Cylindrical-to-Perspective (C2P) projection module that maps non-empty cylindrical voxel features into the perspective image plane, enabling pixel-level 2D segmentation to benefit from structured 3D priors. On top of this aligned representation, we introduce a Range-Aware Heterogeneous Fusion (RAHF) module. RAHF gen-erates a soft range mask from projected depth and decom-poses feature interaction into two complementary branches: a near-field branch that uses LiDAR geometry to refine boundaries, and a far-field branch that suppresses sparse Li-DAR cues while strengthening global visual context. This design differs from uniform multimodal fusion in both what is fused and how it is fused. C2P transfers high-level cylindrical features rather than shallow depth values, reducing the information loss caused by 3D-to-2D compres-sion. RAHF then performs distance-adaptive fusion rather than global feature mixing, allowing the model to call on the most reliable modality according to spatial range. As a result, the network can exploit dense geometric structure for nearby objects and maintain semantic consistency for distant regions where LiDAR evidence is weak. This 2D setting complements rather than replaces 3D perception: it targets stacks that already contain both sensors and require dense camera-aligned labels, rather than advocating that Li-DAR be added solely for 2D segmentation. Our main con-tributions are summarized as follows: • We study LiDAR-assisted RGB image-plane semantic segmentation and propose a range-aware heterogeneous fusion framework for this task. • We introduce a C2P projection module that transfers pre-trained cylindrical 3D semantic features into 2D RGB segmentation, preserving richer geometric structure than conventional depth-map-based fusion. • We develop a RAHF module that decomposes near-field and far-field interaction strategies, improving segmenta-tion accuracy and robustness under challenging weather, illumination, and sensor conditions. 

## 2. Related Work 

Semantic segmentation for autonomous driving. Se-mantic segmentation has developed from fully convolu-tional networks and encoder-decoder architectures [17, 20] to transformer-based designs that model longer-range con-text and stronger mask representations [5, 16, 23]. These advances have substantially improved dense prediction in natural images and driving scenes. However, autonomous driving presents additional difficulties that are not fully cap-tured by generic segmentation benchmarks. Outdoor scenes contain large depth variation, severe class imbalance, and safety-critical small objects. Their appearance also changes dramatically under night-time illumination, over-exposure, rain, fog, shadows, and motion blur [4, 24, 28]. As a result, a model that performs well with clean RGB appearance may still fail when the visual signal is degraded or ambiguous. 

Task scope: LiDAR-assisted RGB segmentation. 

LiDAR-camera fusion has been studied under several dif-ferent output spaces. A large portion of recent work focuses on 3D perception, such as using images to enrich point clouds for 3D semantic segmentation, 3D scene understand-ing, or object-level prediction [11, 13, 21]. These tasks are important, but they differ from the setting of this paper. We study image-plane semantic segmentation: the input in-cludes RGB and LiDAR, but the target is a dense 2D label map in the camera view. RGB-LiDAR multimodal fusion. RGB-LiDAR fusion addresses this limitation by combining dense image appear-ance with explicit geometric measurements. Existing meth-ods differ mainly in the stage and manner of cross-modal interaction. Early fusion strategies commonly project depth or point measurements into the image plane and concate-nate them with RGB features [6, 9]. Sequential or painting-style methods enrich points or pixels with features from the other modality before downstream prediction [21]. More recent approaches use token exchange, cross-modal atten-tion, or multi-resolution fusion to improve feature interac-tion [3, 22, 25]. These designs demonstrate that LiDAR can improve perception when the two modalities are aligned and complementary. However, most of them still treat the projected LiDAR representation as a spatially homogeneous auxiliary signal. The same convolution, attention, or token-mixing rule is usually applied to nearby and distant regions, even though the LiDAR point density and reliability are very different across range. 

3D LiDAR representation learning. In parallel with multimodal fusion, LiDAR representation learning has rapidly advanced [15]. Point-based methods operate di-rectly on unordered point sets [8, 19], while pillar-, voxel-, cylindrical-, and range-view-based methods organize point clouds into structured forms for efficient learning [1, 10, 12, 29]. Cylindrical voxelization is particularly suitable for driving scenes because it follows the radial distribution of LiDAR scans and better balances near- and far-range spatial resolution [27, 29]. Compared with raw depth maps, high-level 3D features extracted by such encoders contain richer semantic shape, local geometry, and density information. Yet many 2D RGB-LiDAR segmentation pipelines only use shallow projected depth or range cues, leaving the represen-tation power of pretrained 3D encoders underexploited. 

Cross-view projection and feature alignment. Bridging 3D LiDAR features and 2D image features requires cross-view alignment. A straightforward strategy is to project raw points or range-view measurements into the image plane, which is computationally simple but discards much of the original 3D feature structure [6, 21, 25]. Cross-view feature projection provides a stronger alternative: learned 3D fea-tures can be associated with their physical coordinates and then injected into corresponding image locations through camera calibration. This process must handle sparsity, oc-clusion, many-to-one projection conflicts, and resolution mismatch between 3D voxel grids and 2D feature maps. Our C2P module follows this direction by projecting non-empty cylindrical voxel features into the perspective image plane, using pooling to resolve collisions and channel align-ment to make the projected representation compatible with the RGB branch. This allows 2D segmentation to benefit from structured 3D priors without replacing the efficient im-age decoder. 

## 3. Methods 

3.1. Overview 

Figure 2 presents the overall pipeline of RAHF-Net. Given an RGB image and the corresponding LiDAR point cloud, the network predicts a dense semantic map in the RGB image plane. The LiDAR stream is used as an auxiliary source of 3D geometric and semantic priors, not as a sep-arate point-wise prediction target. The network follows four stages. First, an asymmetric dual-stream encoder ex-tracts dense 2D visual features and sparse 3D cylindrical LiDAR features. The RGB branch captures appearance, texture, and scene context, while the LiDAR branch pre-serves geometric structure in the original 3D space. Sec-ond, the proposed Cylindrical-to-Perspective (C2P) module projects non-empty cylindrical voxel features into the per-spective image plane and aligns them with the image fea-ture map. Third, a soft range mask is generated from the projected LiDAR depth, providing a continuous estimate of whether each feature location belongs to the near field or far field. Finally, the Range-Aware Heterogeneous Fusion (RAHF) module performs different interactions in different regions: LiDAR-guided geometric enhancement for reliable near-field points and visual-context enhancement for sparse far-field regions. 3.4. Range Mask Generation 

After C2P, the network obtains image-aligned 3D semantic features. The remaining question is how much the model should trust these features at each spatial location. A uni-form fusion rule is suboptimal because LiDAR density de-creases with distance: near-field points are dense and ge-ometrically accurate, while far-field projections are sparse and may act as noise. We explicitly encode this physical prior with a soft range mask. We project the raw LiDAR points into the image plane and construct a sparse depth map D. When multiple points fall into the same pixel, we use min pooling to keep the near-est visible surface, which follows the occlusion principle of perspective imaging and avoids using hidden background points as supervision for the foreground pixel. Interpretation and cost. The depth-derived mask is a transparent routing prior, not a learned uncertainty estima-tor; both branches remain learned end to end. This isolates the range prior without adding a confidence network. Al-though RAHF itself is lightweight, the full system inherits the LiDAR encoder’s cost, so we make no end-to-end real-time claim. 

## 4. Experiments 

4.1. Datasets and Metrics 

We use KITTI-360 [14] as the main benchmark for RGB-LiDAR semantic segmentation, and report additional ro-bustness analysis on DELIVER [26] in Section 4.4. The main task studied in this paper is 2D pixel-wise seman-tic segmentation with RGB-LiDAR fusion, where LiDAR point-cloud features assist RGB image-plane prediction. Therefore, we choose benchmarks that provide synchro-nized camera images, LiDAR scans, reliable calibration, and dense 2D semantic annotations. We do not compare against single-modal 3D point-cloud segmentation, 3D de-tection, or scene-completion methods, because their predic-tion spaces and annotation protocols are different from the 2D image-plane task considered here. Following common practice in semantic segmentation, we report mean Intersection-over-Union (mIoU) as the pri-mary metric. In addition to the overall mIoU, we report representative per-class IoUs for road, building, traffic sign, and pedestrian. Road and building are large background categories that dominate driving scenes, while traffic sign and pedestrian are small foreground categories with strong long-tail characteristics and high safety relevance. Report-ing these classes allows us to analyze whether a method im-proves only dominant scene regions or also benefits small objects that are easily missed in image-plane segmentation. Our baseline set is therefore task-matched rather than sim-ply year-matched: it includes RGB-only 2D segmentation models and representative RGB-X/RGB-LiDAR 2D fusion methods that produce image-plane semantic predictions un-der the same metric. 

4.2. Implementation Details 

For the visual branch, we adopt SegFormer-B2 [23] as the 2D backbone because it provides a strong balance between local detail and global context. For the LiDAR branch, we use the pretrained cylindrical encoder proposed in Cylin-der3D [29]. The cylindrical grid configuration follows the pretrained model, with a maximum radial distance of 50 m, full 360 ◦ azimuth coverage, vertical range of (−4, 2) , and voxel resolution of (240 , 180 , 20) . Both projected LiDAR features and image features are aligned to 256 channels be-fore fusion. The segmentation decoder upsamples the fused feature map to the original image resolution and produces per-pixel class probabilities. The model is trained with AdamW [18], using an initial learning rate of 6 × 10 −5, weight decay of 0.01 , and a poly-nomial decay schedule with power 0.9. All experiments are conducted on four NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPUs with a batch size of 4 per GPU. For the range mask, we set α = 10 .0

4.3. Main Results on KITTI-360 

Table 2 compares RAHF-Net with task-matched RGB-only and RGB-LiDAR methods that output 2D semantic maps. RAHF-Net achieves the best result with 65.76 mIoU, ex-ceeding the strongest RGB-only baseline, Mask2Former, by 9.39 points and the strongest fusion baseline, CMX, by 1.45 points. The improvement over CMX suggests that the gain is not explained by generic cross-modal interaction alone. It extends to both dominant background classes and small safety-critical classes, supporting the use of complementary geometry rather than RGB appearance alone. Simply adding LiDAR is not sufficient: HRFuser and TokenFusion remain below the strongest RGB-only base-line in this setting, consistent with the difficulty of handling projected sparsity and feature conflict. By contrast, RAHF-Net reaches 94.92 and 88.78 IoU on road and building, and 52.22 and 59.96 on traffic sign and pedestrian. Thus, the improvement is not confined to easy background pixels; it also covers small foreground regions that often occur at long range or near cluttered boundaries. Figure 3 provides complementary qualitative evidence. In the left example, the baseline confuses part of a distant, strongly illuminated building with the sky, whereas RAHF-Net preserves a more complete building region. In the right example, RAHF-Net produces a cleaner boundary for the nearby vehicle. These examples are consistent with the in-tended behavior of RAHF: visual context stabilizes ambigu-ous far-field semantics, while denser near-field geometry helps refine object boundaries. We treat them as illustrative rather than as a substitute for the quantitative evaluation.

Effect of soft mask hyperparameters. To further ana-lyze the impact of hyperparameters α and β on segmen-tation performance during RAHF, we conduct a grid search ablation study with α ∈ { 5, 10 , 20 } and β ∈ { 2, 5, 8}. The overall mIoU results on the KITTI-360 validation set are presented in Table 4. The results indicate that the model achieves an optimal mIoU of 65.76% when α = 10 and 

β = 5 . This implies that the best performance is obtained when the near-far transition threshold is set at approxi-mately 50% of the maximum detection range with moder-ate smoothness. As shown in Equation 6, α controls the smoothness of the mask. Meanwhile, β controls the physi-cal critical threshold separating the near and far regions. An excessively large or small β results in either the introduction of far-field noise or the under-utilization of near-field geo-metric features, which inevitably limits the performance. 

Effect of fusion strategy. We then compare different fu-sion strategies. Using only near-field 3D enhancement im-proves segmentation over the 2D-only baseline, confirming the value of LiDAR geometry for nearby objects. Using only far-field context yields a smaller improvement, while applying 3D fusion uniformly across near and far regions does not perform as well. The best result is obtained by the proposed gated heterogeneous fusion, showing that explic-itly separating near-field and far-field interaction is more ef-fective than applying a shared fusion rule across the whole scene.

## 5. Conclusion 

We presented RAHF-Net, a range-aware framework for LiDAR-assisted RGB image-plane segmentation. C2P transfers structured cylindrical features into the camera plane, while RAHF balances near-field geometric refine-ment and far-field visual context through a soft range prior. KITTI-360 results show gains over task-matched RGB-only and RGB-LiDAR baselines, and DELIVER provides com-plementary evidence under adverse conditions. These re-sults support range-dependent fusion when LiDAR is al-ready available to assist dense camera-view prediction, while broader datasets and explicit system-level efficiency measurements remain important future work. 8References
