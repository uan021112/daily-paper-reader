ABSTRACT
Recovering metric world-space hand motion from egocentric video supports un-
derstanding human interactions and transferring them to embodied agents. Three
challenges complicate this task: multi-stage pipelines propagate errors from inde-
pendently estimated hand and camera motion; monocular observations leave phys-
ical scale ambiguous; and occlusion and out-of-view hands admit multiple plausi-
ble motion trajectories. We present DewHand, a unified model for direct joint re-
construction of world-space hand and camera motion. To avoid compounding er-
rors from multi-stage estimation, DewHand jointly predicts hand and camera mo-
tion in a shared world coordinate system within a single-stage pipeline. To resolve
metric-scale ambiguity, it combines scene-level geometric features from VGGT-Ω
with metric-space supervision, while hand-specific features preserve fine-grained
articulation. To handle occlusion and out-of-view hands, we formulate reconstruc-
tion as conditional motion generation with flow matching. Under the same archi-
tecture, this generative formulation outperforms direct regression in both accuracy
and temporal smoothness with only two sampling steps. Experiments on HOT3D
and ARCTIC demonstrate improved world-space accuracy and temporal consis-
tency over state-of-the-art methods. DewHand reduces rigid-aligned world-space
error relative to StableHand from 32.9 to 28.4 mm on HOT3D and from 76.2 to
36.2 mm on ARCTIC. It also recovers camera scale within 10% on two thirds of
HOT3D clips, versus about a quarter for SLAM. Moreover, in zero-shot evalua-
tions on HoloAssist, H2O, and TACO, captured with different devices, DewHand
achieves the lowest rigid-aligned world-space error (WA-MPJPE) on each dataset.

Hands are our primary means of interact-
ing with the physical world. Recovering
their 3D motion from wearable cameras
can support the analysis of human activ-
ity, the capture of manipulation skills, and
the transfer of human demonstrations to
embodied agents. These applications re-
quire more than an accurate hand pose in
each video frame: they require a metri-
cally scaled trajectory in a stable world co-
ordinate system, so that a hand remains
correctly placed as both the wearer and the
camera move.
This work addresses world-space 3D hand
motion reconstruction from monocular
egocentric video. Given a video, the goal
is to recover the time-varying pose of both
hands in a common world frame.
Con-
sider a wearer reaching for an object while
turning their head. In camera coordinates,
even a stationary hand appears to move
with the camera; conversely, camera mo-
tion can hide or amplify the hand’s motion in the image. A useful reconstruction must separate these
effects and preserve the hand’s position relative to the surrounding scene throughout the interaction.
Three challenges motivate our formulation. First, accurate local hand reconstruction does not by it-
self ensure a consistent world-space trajectory. Despite strong per-frame estimators (Pavlakos et al.,
2024; Potamias et al., 2025), existing world-space pipelines typically estimate hands and camera
motion separately, compose their outputs, and optionally apply temporal optimization or motion-
prior refinement (Zhang et al., 2025a; Yu et al., 2025; Xu et al., 2026; Zeng et al., 2026). Camera
drift and mismatches between these estimates can therefore propagate into the reconstructed hand
motion. Second, monocular video provides projective observations rather than direct metric mea-
surements, leaving physical scale ambiguous even when local articulation is accurately recovered.
Third, self-occlusion, hand-object interactions, motion blur, and frequent exits from the field of view
make hand observations incomplete. The visible evidence may be consistent with multiple motion
trajectories, motivating a conditional distribution over hand and camera motion.
We introduce DewHand, a unified model that directly generates metric world-space motion of both
hands and the camera from egocentric video. To avoid error propagation, we jointly generate wrist
poses, local articulation, and camera motion in a shared world frame instead of composing inde-
pendent estimates. To support metric-scale recovery, we condition on geometric features from the
frozen VGGT-Ωbackbone (Wang et al., 2026) and supervise in metric space; hand-specific features
add cues for local articulation. To handle ambiguous and missing observations, we learn a condi-
tional distribution over joint hand-camera motion using flow matching. Compared with regressing
a single trajectory, this generative formulation produces more accurate and temporally smoother
motion, while exhibiting greater sample diversity where the hand is unobserved.
Our contributions are threefold:
• We formulate egocentric world-space hand reconstruction as direct joint generation of met-
ric dual-hand and camera motion instead of composing separately estimated hands and
camera; supervising the generated camera reduces rigid-aligned hand error by 6.4 mm.
• We develop a flow-matching architecture that combines geometry-aware and hand-specific
video features to jointly model wrist placement, articulation, and camera motion in a shared
world frame; replacing the geometric features with DINOv2 increases hand error by 13 mm
and camera error by 57 mm.

• We establish state-of-the-art results on HOT3D and ARCTIC, reducing rigid-aligned world
error against StableHand from 32.9 to 28.4 mm and from 76.2 to 36.2 mm, and recovering
camera scale within 10% on two thirds of HOT3D clips, versus about a quarter for SLAM.

RELATED WORK
3D Hand Pose and Mesh Reconstruction: Large-scale models such as HaMeR (Pavlakos et al.,
2024) recover detailed articulated pose in the wild, while WiLoR (Potamias et al., 2025) additionally
targets robust 3D hand localization. Interaction and physical priors (Zuo et al., 2023; Suzuki et al.,
2026; Zhang et al., 2025b) and spatio-temporal context (Zhao et al., 2024) further address occlusion
and motion ambiguity. Recent work has explored complementary strategies for monocular hand
reconstruction, including contrastive pre-training in SiMHand (Lin et al., 2025), controllable syn-
thetic data generation in SesaHand (Zhao et al., 2026), exemplar-based in-context reconstruction in
EgoHandICL (Xie et al., 2026), and kinematics-aware architectures in Rodrigues Network (Zhang
et al., 2026). Egocentric datasets and benchmarks capture hand-object interactions (Ohkawa et al.,
2023; Fan et al., 2024). HOPformer (Bansal et al., 2026) models egocentric hand-object pose, and
EgoForce (Millerdurai et al., 2026) estimates camera-space hand pose. These advances improve
hand pose and shape, but a shared metric world trajectory also requires camera motion.
World-Space Hand Motion Reconstruction: For full-body motion, WHAC (Yin et al., 2024)
jointly recovers metric human and camera trajectories. For articulated hands, HaWoR (Zhang et al.,
2025a) and Dyn-HaMR (Yu et al., 2025) construct global motion from camera-space hand estimates
and separately recovered camera trajectories. HandFlow (Xu et al., 2026) learns camera-space hand
motion distributions and transforms its predictions into world coordinates using SLAM. Stable-
Hand (Zeng et al., 2026) instead learns world-space refinement of hand observations constructed
from separate hand and camera estimates, using quality-aware initialization to preserve reliable ob-
servations. UniHand (Sun et al., 2026) generates hand motion through latent diffusion in a fixed
coordinate system defined by the first camera frame, but does not generate the camera trajectory.
ReViV (Lyu et al., 2026) jointly models camera trajectories and camera-space hand kinematics,
then aligns the reconstructed viewer and scene in a metric frame. DewHand jointly generates metric
hand and camera trajectories in a single stage, conditioned on geometric and hand-specific features.

3.2
VISUAL CONDITIONING AND MISSING OBSERVATIONS
Metric world-space reconstruction requires scene-level geometric cues to constrain scale and global
placement, alongside fine-grained hand cues for local articulation. We therefore encode the video
with two complementary frozen backbones: VGGT-Ω(Wang et al., 2026) provides clip-level scene
geometry and camera cues, while HaMeR (Pavlakos et al., 2024) provides detailed per-hand ap-

JOINT HAND-CAMERA FLOW MODEL
Flow matching (Lipman et al., 2023; Hu et al., 2023) models the trajectories compatible with in-
complete observations by learning transport from noise to data. We apply it to the joint hand-camera
state, conditioned on visual features. The model uses two integration steps at inference.
At flow time τ, xglobal

subscript 1 below denotes the clean endpoint of the flow. We use one token per entity and frame.
The global motion stream contains three 9D tokens for the left wrist, right wrist, and camera. Each
token represents 3D translation and 6D rotation in world coordinates. The local hand stream contains
two 100D tokens, one per hand, containing the 6D rotations of the 15 articulated MANO joints and
the 10D MANO shape. The condition stream contains the three tokens defined above. Each input
is projected to width D, and entity- and frame-specific embeddings retain its identity and temporal
position. The three streams contain eight tokens in total at every frame. In Fig. 3, Cam denotes the
camera token, Wh the global wrist token, and Ah the local hand token for side h ∈{L, R}.
Each layer performs attention in two successive stages: within-stream attention, then joint attention
across streams. Within-stream attention first relates the entities at each frame and then aggregates
temporal context per entity. They run independently on the global motion, local hand, and condition
streams, with separate parameters, to build per-stream spatio-temporal representations.
Joint attention across streams then lets global motion, local hand, and condition tokens attend to one
another at each frame in a Multimodal Diffusion Transformer (MMDiT) block (Esser et al., 2024).
As in HandFlow (Xu et al., 2026), condition tokens are updated rather than kept fixed.

EXPERIMENTS
4.1
WORLD-SPACE HAND MOTION
Datasets: We evaluate on HOT3D (Banerjee et al., 2025) and ARCTIC (Fan et al., 2023), following
prior work on world-space hand motion reconstruction, including HaWoR (Zhang et al., 2025a)
and HandFlow (Xu et al., 2026) on HOT3D and StableHand (Zeng et al., 2026) on both datasets.
We train on HOT3D (for the StableHand split, a separate model that excludes its test windows;
Appendix B.3) and fine-tune the resulting checkpoint on ARCTIC. For both training and inference,
we use sequences of T = 150 frames at 30 fps, corresponding to 5-second clips.
Evaluation Metrics: We report PA-MPJPE, W-MPJPE, WA-MPJPE, MRRPE-A, and acceleration
error (AccEr) on the 21-joint MANO skeleton. MRRPE-A measures inter-wrist displacement after a
shared rigid alignment. We report WA-MPJPE with similarity (Sim(3)) and rigid (SE(3)) alignment.
Rigid alignment removes differences in world-frame origin and orientation while preserving scale,
so scale errors remain visible; similarity alignment additionally corrects scale. We also report wrist-
level WA-MPJPE (wWA), which scores only the wrist trajectory and thus separates world placement
from finger articulation. Appendix B details the scoring protocol. Unless stated otherwise, inference
uses two Euler steps and five samples.
Baselines: We compare per-frame hand estimators (WiLoR, EgoForce (Millerdurai et al., 2026),
HOPformer (Bansal et al., 2026)) lifted with ViPE, and the video-based methods HaWoR (Zhang
et al., 2025a), HandFlow (Xu et al., 2026), and StableHand (Zeng et al., 2026); all but HandFlow
are trained on ARCTIC. Baseline implementation details are in Appendix B.

4.3
CROSS-DEVICE ZERO-SHOT GENERALIZATION
We evaluate the HOT3D-trained model without fine-tuning on HoloAssist (Wang et al., 2023), test-
ing transfer from Aria fisheye video to HoloLens 2 perspective video. HoloAssist provides cam-
era poses and articulated hand tracks, enabling world-space evaluation across capture devices. On
randomly sampled validation windows, DewHand achieves the lowest errors across all reported
metrics, including PA-MPJPE, world-space placement, acceleration, and inter-hand placement (Ta-
ble 3). Camera-space baselines are lifted with per-window ViPE (Huang et al., 2025) rather than
ground-truth cameras; target-domain-trained references are marked in the table.
We further evaluate zero-shot transfer on the held-out subject of the H2O (Kwon et al., 2021) pose
benchmark and on TACO (Liu et al., 2024), lifting the camera-space methods with per-window
ViPE trajectories. On both datasets, DewHand achieves the lowest SE(3)-aligned joint and wrist-
level WA-MPJPE, as well as AccEr (Table 3). Other metrics, particularly local pose and inter-hand
placement, show mixed results.

5
CONCLUSION
We addressed metric world-space reconstruction of two hands and the camera from monocular ego-
centric video. DewHand jointly generates global hand and camera motion, local articulation, and
hand shape in a shared world frame, avoiding the composition of separately estimated trajecto-
ries. Geometric and hand-specific features provide complementary cues for global placement and
articulation, while flow matching models motion under incomplete observations. On HOT3D and
ARCTIC, DewHand achieves the lowest rigid-aligned world-space hand error. The small gap be-
tween rigid- and similarity-aligned hand errors further supports metric-scale recovery without scale
correction at evaluation. On HOT3D, it also improves metric camera scale recovery over the com-
pared SLAM systems. Camera supervision primarily improves global hand placement, and flow
matching yields more accurate and temporally consistent motion than direct regression. Zero-shot
results show the lowest rigid-aligned WA-MPJPE on HoloAssist, H2O, and TACO, although other
metrics do not always lead. Future work could extend the model beyond fixed offline windows and
incorporate manipulated objects and hand-object contact.

B.2
CAMERA METRICS
All camera trajectories are expressed relative to frame 0. ATE is the root-mean-square distance
between predicted and ground-truth camera centers, measured directly (raw) or after one clip-level
rigid (SE(3)) or similarity (Sim(3)) alignment. Scale accuracy δ < κ is the percentage of clips
satisfying max(s, 1/s) < κ, where s is the predicted-to-ground-truth trajectory scale ratio. The
rotation error is the mean geodesic distance between corresponding camera rotations; RPEt@1 is
the root-mean-square error of one-frame relative camera translations.
