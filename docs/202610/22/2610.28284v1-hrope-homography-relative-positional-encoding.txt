## ABSTRACT 

Geometric Relative Positional Encoding has become the primary mechanism for injecting spatial inductive biases into multi-view transformers. Existing designs condition attention on camera-level frustums or ray-marched depth, associating cross-view tokens through the sensor geometry. Such sensor-centric formula-tions, however, describe only where a token is observed from, leaving the rich surface geometry of the scene region depicted by the token. In this paper, we advocate a shift toward surface-aware positional encoding that explicitly grounds patch tokens in the geometry of physical surfaces. We introduce HRoPE , which represents the local geometry of each patch as a slanted 3D plane, following the slanted support-window assumption of classical multi-view stereo. We in-ject token-level, plane-induced homographies into attention as relative transfor-mations. This equips the transformer with a geometric prior for rectifying per-spective distortions and aligning patch features across viewpoints. Our encoding requires no ground-truth geometry: the plane parameters can be predicted end-to-end under a view-synthesis objective alone, or derived from foundation mod-els. Extensive experiments on RealEstate10K, CO3Dv2, and DL3DV show that our best variant of HRoPE consistently outperforms the evaluated geometric posi-tional encodings for novel view synthesis. Extensive ablations and analysis show the effectiveness of the proposed encoding. Please refer to our project page at: https://gaohchen.github.io/HRoPE. 

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

The Transformer has become the de facto architecture for recent feed-forward 3D vision (Wang et al., 2024; 2025; Jin et al., 2025). By tokenizing images from multiple viewpoints and exchanging information via attention, these models exhibit strong capabilities in jointly resolving appearance, visibility, and scene geometry. Attention, however, is permutation-equivariant: it operates on un-ordered sets and is inherently agnostic to the camera poses, metric scales, and epipolar constraints that couple multi-view observations. Consequently, geometric priors must be explicitly injected, either by conditioning token representations directly (e.g., via camera tokens (Wang et al., 2025) or ray maps (Jin et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2024a)) or by imposing epipolar constraints on attention (He et al., 2020; Charatan et al., 2024; Ye et al., 2024). Positional encoding (PE) introduces geometric structure directly into attention by modulating pairwise token comparisons. While existing PE designs exhibit increasing geometric fidelity by using priors from images, cam-eras, and rays, they invariably anchor positional information to the sensor. RoPE extensions (Su et al., 2024) encode 2D intra-view offsets, leaving inter-view transformations implicit (Weinzaepfel et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2024). GTA (Miyato et al., 2024) and CaPE (Kong et al., 2024) inject rel-ative camera extrinsics to achieve invariance to global rigid motions, while PRoPE (Li et al., 2025) generalizes this to the full projection matrix. Ray-based formulations like RayRoPE (Wu et al., 2026) and URoPE (Xie et al., 2026) integrate depth by localizing patches along viewing rays and

Across this progression, geometry is parameterized via sensor-centric abstractions: an image coordinate, a camera frustum, or a point of ray termination, leaving the rich local surface structure of the underlying scene unmodeled (see Fig. 1, left and middle). This limitation is fundamental because multi-view matching does not compare isolated points; it compares local neighborhoods whose appearances are related through surface-induced deforma-tions. A patch in one view and its corresponding patch in another are connected by a warp de-termined by the underlying surface geometry, and reliable correspondence requires compensating for this warp before comparing visual features. Given the pixel location and camera geometry, depth locates the patch center, while surface orientation additionally determines how its surround-ing neighborhood deforms across views. By providing only view or positional information, existing PEs implicitly impose a fronto-parallel assumption on the attention computation mechanism. Al-though large neural models can learn to compensate for this bias given sufficient training data, doing so compromises efficiency and generalization. We argue that surface orientation is the missing de-gree of freedom: current PEs capture the zeroth-order position of a patch but neglect the first-order local orientation that dictates its cross-view appearance transformation. To this end, we present HRoPE , a Homography Relative Positional Encoding that embeds orientation-aware rectification directly into the attention mechanism (Fig. 1, right). Inspired by the slanted support windows of classical multi-view stereo (Bleyer et al., 2011; Wang et al., 2021a), HRoPE anchors a local tangent plane to each patch token. We use the plane normal and distance, together with the camera poses, to define the homography governing cross-view neighborhood de-formations. Concretely, we apply a per-patch 3 × 3 transformation block-diagonally to the queries, keys, and values, and incorporate a closed-form inverse into the key projection so that the result-ing attention logits depend exclusively on the relative homography between tokens. As we show in Sec. 3.2 and Appendix A.2, this mechanism acts as an in-attention analogue of computing a matching score only after both neighborhoods have been rectified onto a shared tangent plane. Consequently, whereas prior geometric PEs merely encode where a token is located, HRoPE explicitly models how its underlying surface transforms across views. This formulation offers two compelling advantages. First, HRoPE strictly generalizes point- and ray-based PEs: when a predicted normal aligns with the viewing direction, the induced transformation naturally reduces to the fronto-parallel case. Second, the per-patch transformation parameters can be regressed from patch features by a lightweight head trained jointly with the view-synthesis model, using the view-synthesis objective alone. The head can optionally use pretrained features from DINOv2 (Oquab et al., 2024) or VGGT (Wang et al., 2025), without changing the encoding formulation or training objective. 2Preprint We validate HRoPE on feed-forward novel view synthesis (NVS) across the RealEstate10K (Zhou et al., 2018), CO3Dv2 (Reizenstein et al., 2021), and DL3DV (Ling et al., 2024) datasets, where it consistently outperforms view-, projection-, and ray-based encodings. Notably, HRoPE surpasses PRoPE on RealEstate10K without ground-truth camera poses, demonstrating that per-patch homog-raphy is a stronger prior than view-level projection. Furthermore, our ablations show that this ge-ometry must condition token interactions multiplicatively, injecting the same information as ad-ditive features sharply degrades performance. Finally, HRoPE improves generalization to unseen zooms and wider baselines, supporting its role as a geometric inductive bias. Beyond NVS, HRoPE transfers unchanged to a multi-view diffusion model and to the visual tokens of a spatial reasoning MLLM, improving performance on both tasks (Appendix A.6). We summarize our main contributions as follows: 1. We formulate the principle of surface-aware positional encoding , positioning local 3D surface geometry as the natural anchor for comparing patch neighborhoods across views. 2. We propose HRoPE , a per-patch relative encoding that uses plane-induced transforms with closed-form inverses to condition attention logits on relative cross-view homographies. 3. We demonstrate consistent improvements over the evaluated geometric positional encodings on multiple NVS benchmarks and under out-of-distribution view settings. Controlled ablations an-alyze the roles of transformation structure, local geometry, and feature source, supporting the benefit of the homography inductive bias. 

## 2 RELATED WORK 

Geometric Positional Encoding. Geometric PEs encode camera or scene geometry in tokens and attention. Absolute variants encode Pl¨ ucker rays (Sitzmann et al., 2021; Sajjadi et al., 2022; Jin et al., 2025) or 3D positions (Liu et al., 2022), but depend on the chosen world frame. Relative vari-ants extend rotary encodings (Su et al., 2024; Shaw et al., 2018) to camera geometry: GTA (Miyato et al., 2024) and CaPE (Kong et al., 2024) use relative extrinsics, while PRoPE (Li et al., 2025) also includes intrinsics through projection matrices. Recent methods move from view- to token-level ge-ometry: RayRoPE (Wu et al., 2026) predicts per-token depth, RoRE (Griffiths & Dansereau, 2026) rotates features along rays, and URoPE (Xie et al., 2026) projects per-head depth anchors into the query frame, following epipolar attention (He et al., 2020; Suhail et al., 2022). These encodings describe camera relationships and point- or ray-level geometry. HRoPE adds an explicit local-plane parameterization whose orientation, together with distance and camera geometry, determines a pla-nar neighborhood’s cross-view deformation. This distinguishes surface-conditioned transformations from point localization without requiring the latter to assume fronto-parallel surfaces. 

Multi-view Transformers. Multi-view transformers use camera geometry to associate image to-kens across views. Their applications include stereo depth estimation (He et al., 2020; Yao et al., 2018; Gu et al., 2020), camera and structure recovery (Wang et al., 2024; 2025), cross-view com-pletion and matching (Weinzaepfel et al., 2023), spatial reasoning (Yang et al., 2025; Wu et al., 2025), and camera-controlled video and world modeling (Li et al., 2026; Zhang et al., 2026; Ma et al., 2025), as well as novel view synthesis (NVS). For feed-forward NVS, methods use feature-conditioned radiance fields (Yu et al., 2021; Wang et al., 2021b; Chen et al., 2021; Trevithick & Yang, 2021; Hong et al., 2024), predict 3D Gaussians (Charatan et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2024a; Zhang et al., 2024a), or directly regress pixels (Jin et al., 2025; Jiang et al., 2025). We evaluate HRoPE primarily on novel view synthesis using the LVSM backbone with plane-induced positional encoding. 

Slanted Support Windows. Traditional local stereo methods assume constant disparity within a support window, introducing a fronto-parallel bias on slanted surfaces (Bleyer et al., 2011). Building on randomized correspondence search (Barnes et al., 2009), PatchMatch Stereo (Bleyer et al., 2011) addresses this limitation by estimating a local disparity plane at each pixel and evaluating matching costs over slanted support windows. More generally, local planar geometry induces a homography that relates corresponding image neighborhoods across views (Hartley & Zisserman, 2003). Learned multi-view stereo methods also use differentiable geometric warping to compare features under depth hypotheses (Yao et al., 2018; Gu et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2021a). ## 3 METHOD 

HRoPE is a geometric positional encoding that incorporates a per-token surface plane transform. In Sec.3.1, we first review how prior work incorporates camera geometry into multi-view attention. Then, in Sec. 3.2, we introduce the plane-induced transformation and show how it modulates multi-view attention. With consistent camera geometry, the relative transformation exactly maps points on a shared physical plane across views. For independently predicted per-patch parameters, the same algebraic form modulates attention without requiring an exact physical correspondence for every token pair. Finally, Sec. 3.3 details how these per-patch parameters are predicted from image features and presents a calibrated variant that leverages known camera parameters. The overall pipeline of HRoPE is illustrated in Fig. 2. 3.1 PRELIMINARIES : G EOMETRIC POSITIONAL ENCODINGS 

Self-attention alone does not encode token positions or camera geometries. RoPE (Su et al., 2024) applies position-dependent rotations to queries and keys so that their dot product depends on relative positions. Multi-view relative PEs extend this principle to cameras: GTA (Miyato et al., 2024) and CaPE (Kong et al., 2024) encode relative extrinsics, while PRoPE (Li et al., 2025) adds intrinsics.

4.1 NOVEL VIEW SYNTHESIS 

Experimental Setup. We evaluate HRoPE on novel view synthesis by applying it to the decoder-only LVSM (Jin et al., 2025) architecture. Each model takes two reference views as input and synthesizes a target view. Following PRoPE (Li et al., 2025), we train downsized LVSM backbone variants ( ∼25M parameters) with different positional encoding methods. We train and evaluate the models separately on CO3Dv2 (Reizenstein et al., 2021), RealEstate10K (Zhou et al., 2018), and Objaverse (Deitke et al., 2023), and report PSNR, SSIM (Wang et al., 2004), and LPIPS (Zhang et al., 2018). To evaluate scaling with the number of reference views, we additionally follow the RayZer (Jiang et al., 2025) protocol and train a larger LVSM backbone variant ( ∼53M parameters) on DL3DV (Ling et al., 2024). We evaluate the model with 2, 4, and 8 reference views on the held-out DL3DV benchmark. 

Results. Table 1 shows that the VGGT-based HRoPE outperforms baseline PEs on CO3Dv2, RealEstate10K, and Objaverse. Relative to PRoPE, it gains 0.53 , 1.27 , and 1.03 dB in PSNR, re-spectively, with higher SSIM and lower LPIPS. On DL3DV, HRoPE also outperforms PRoPE with 

2, 4, and 8 reference views (Table 2). PSNR and SSIM gains are largest with two views, suggesting greater benefits under sparse observations. Qualitative comparisons on RealEstate10K show that HRoPE better preserves object boundaries and fine details (Fig. 3). 4.2 ABLATION STUDIES AND ANALYSIS

Training configuration. We train every model on four NVIDIA RTX 4090 ( 48 GB) GPUs with a per-GPU batch size of 16 , using a cosine learning-rate schedule with peak rate 4 × 10 −4 and a warmup of 2500 iterations. All scene-level models are trained for 80 k iterations at a fixed resolution of 336 × 336 obtained by center cropping. Camera poses are normalized following the convention that matches the input regime. With few input views (e.g. 2 views) we follow PRoPE (Li et al., 2025): the first reference camera is set to the identity (its frame becomes the world origin) and all translations are scaled by the distance between the first and second reference cameras, so that this distance is unit length. With many input views we follow the original LVSM (Jin et al., 2025) convention: the scene is translated and rotated to align with the mean camera center and mean viewing direction, then rescaled by the maximum absolute camera-center coordinate times a fixed factor of 1.35 to a common scale. Our objective combines a photometric term with a perceptual loss; we weight the perceptual term at 0.5 for scene datasets and lower it to 0.1 for the object dataset. 

Numerical stability of the predicted transformation. To stabilize training, we clamp the pre-dicted translation t and inverse plane distance κ to [−0.5, 0.5] , normalize n, and construct R to be orthonormal. This bounds the rank-one update in A = R + κtn ⊤ and guarantees det A ≥

1 − √3/4 ≈ 0.57 . We additionally clamp the Sherman–Morrison denominator to 0.1 and the entries of A and A−1 to [−5, 5] . Both matrices are computed in float32 and then cast to the training precision.

Spatial Reasoning in Multimodal LLMs. We further evaluate how HRoPE enhances the spatial reasoning ability of multimodal LLMs. Visual tokens in these models are indexed only by their 2D raster location within a frame and their ordinal position in the frame sequence, so self-attention has no explicit representation of the 3D geometric relationship between observations of the same surface. Our proposed HRoPE enriches the visual tokens of a pretrained MLLM with surface-aware positional information and cross-view correspondence cues, while modifying only their positional encoding. Concretely, we build on LLaVA-Video-7B (Zhang et al., 2024c), sampling 32 frames per video and processing them with a frozen VGGT (Wang et al., 2025) aggregator in parallel with the model’s native vision tower. From VGGT’s per-patch features, the prediction head described in Sec. 3.3 regresses (Ri,u , ti,u , ni,u , κ i,u ), which parameterize the per-patch matrix Ai,u in Eq. 5. We then insert the block-diagonal operator in Eq. 17 into the language model’s self-attention, applying it only to visual tokens in layers L − 10 through L − 2 of the 28 -layer backbone ( L = 28 ): the geometric halves of each head’s query and key for token (i, u ) are transformed by H⊤ 

> i,u

and H−1 

> i,u

,respectively, where Hi,u = KiAi,u , while the remaining halves retain the native rotary encoding. In this way, the visual stream of the MLLM is explicitly conditioned on per-patch surface geometry, while text tokens and the temporal ordering of frames are left unchanged, making HRoPE a strictly additive augmentation to the pretrained VLM. Following VLM-3R (Fan et al., 2026), we fine-tune the model for 3 epochs on its released VSI-Bench training split, which combines spatial question– answer pairs over ScanNet (Dai et al., 2017) scenes with embodied route-planning instances, while keeping VGGT frozen throughout. We report the evaluation results on VSI-Bench (Yang et al., 2025) in Table 6. Our model and the fine-tuned control take 32 frames per video as input. For Spatial-MLLM (Wu et al., 2025) and the Qwen2.5VL series (Bai et al., 2025), we use 16 frames as input. For the other open-source methods and GPT-4o (Hurst et al., 2024), we follow the VSI-Bench evaluation protocol to select the number of input frames (ranging from 8 to 32 frames). For Gemini-1.5 Pro (Team et al., 2024), it samples video frames at 1 FPS. HRoPE reaches an average of 59 .5, ahead of every model in the table and 1.8

points above the same backbone fine-tuned on the identical data without it. The gains concentrate in the categories that require relating a surface across frames, including absolute distance ( +8 .0), rel-ative direction ( +7 .1), and route planning ( +5 .7). Categories answerable from a single frame show 22 Preprint smaller changes. This pattern is consistent with the encoding’s emphasis on local surface geometry. The exception is appearance order, which falls 6.9 points: it is the one purely temporal task in the suite, and HRoPE spends half of each visual token’s head dimension on the plane-induced map, weakening the ordinal cue it depends on. Since the language backbone, the fine-tuning data, and the frame budget are held fixed across the two rows, both the improvements and this regression are attributable solely to the positional encoding. This controlled comparison substantiates our central claim: the plane-induced homography benefits self-attention whenever it must establish correspon-dence between two observations of the same surface, irrespective of whether those observations are rendered, denoised, or sampled from video.
