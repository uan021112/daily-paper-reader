# OmniVLoc: Topo-metric Visual Localization for Omnidirectional Cameras 

Zhuo Yu 1, Shuyang Zhang 1, †, Huihua Zhu 1, Wenjia Ouyang 1,2 , Zuo Zuo 3, Jianhao Jiao 4,Yilong Zhu 5, Xiaopin Zhong 1, and Zongze Wu 1

Abstract —Omnidirectional cameras provide surround-view in-formation, significantly overcoming the field-of-view constraints for traditional visual localization systems. However, existing localization systems fail to fully exploit the characteristics of omnidirectional cameras, struggling to deliver accurate and robust localization performance amidst scene content variations and illumination-induced appearance changes. In this work, we propose OmniVLoc, a surround-view localization system based on a topo-metric framework. By integrating state-of-the-art global place recognition and monocular depth estimation methods, we introduce a multi-constrained visual localization paradigm. By solving a scale-aware pose graph and performing precise spherical bundle adjustment, we accurately recover the metric-level pose for each query frame. Furthermore, a hierar-chical verification mechanism is designed to filter out erroneous outputs across different stages, effectively bolstering the overall system robustness. Extensive experiments on both public and our custom datasets validate the superior accuracy and reliability of OmniVLoc. To benefit the community, our source code and dataset will be made publicly available upon publication. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Accurate metric visual localization is a fundamental prereq-uisite for autonomous navigation in mobile robot systems [1], [2]. While traditional structure-based systems achieve high precision by establishing 2D-3D correspondences against pre-built 3D maps, they face significant scalability bottlenecks. Constructing and storing dense 3D points, local descriptors, and co-visibility graphs incur prohibitive overhead in large-scale environments [3]. Furthermore, maintaining these 3D maps over extended periods is exceedingly difficult; feature matching and triangulation are highly sensitive to viewpoint shifts and illumination-induced appearance variations. Conse-quently, dynamic environmental changes and map obsoles-cence drastically degrade localization performance [4], [5], rendering traditional 3D map-based paradigms inadequate for large-scale deployments and lifelong autonomy [6] (as con-ceptually compared in Fig. 1).

In contrast, recent topo-metric localization paradigms [7]– [9] replace dense point clouds with sparse topological image nodes, significantly reducing storage and maintenance costs while performing local metric recovery at these nodes. How-ever, existing topo-metric methods face two major limitations. First, they typically rely on conventional cameras with a narrow field of view (FOV). This necessitates redundant multi-view storage to capture the full scene during mapping, and the limited visual context severely exacerbates perceptual aliasing during localization [10]. Second, local metric recovery in these systems is fragile in complex environments: stereo-vision-based methods are bounded by physical baselines, while traditional monocular approaches suffer from severe scale ambiguity and drift, compromising absolute pose accuracy. To address these challenges, we propose OmniVLoc, a surround-view localization system based on a topo-metric framework. By introducing this ultra-wide FOV camera, we effectively overcome the issues of perceptual aliasing and map redundancy. To achieve precise metric recovery without relying on pre-built 3D geometry or stereo baselines, we introduce a multi-constrained visual localization paradigm. Specifically, we leverage a visual foundation model (VFM) arXiv:2610.27522v1 [cs.CV] 20 Oct 2026 for monocular depth estimation and formulate the query-to-map registration as geometric hypotheses regarding the unknown depth scale. By solving a scale-aware pose graph and performing precise spherical bundle adjustment, OmniVLoc accurately recovers the metric-level pose for each query frame. Additionally, a hierarchical verification mechanism is designed to filter out erroneous outputs, effectively bolstering system robustness against illumination and scene variations. Our main contributions are summarized as follows:  

> •

A continuous metric localization framework for surround-view topo-metric maps. This framework obviates the need for pre-built 3D map points or depth maps, providing a unified formulation for precise metric localization using omnidirectional cameras.  

> •

A scale-aware factor graph optimization scheme. It lever-ages VFM-derived monocular depth with scale uncer-tainty to construct multi-hypothesis 3D-2D visual con-straints, tightly coupled with temporal motion priors to recover accurate absolute scales and poses.  

> •

A hierarchical verification mechanism based on multiple observation and motion priors, ensuring system stability under severe perceptual aliasing and feature degradation.  

> •

Extensive experiments on both public and our custom datasets, which validate the superior accuracy and robust-ness of OmniVLoc compared to state-of-the-art baselines. II. R ELATED WORKS 

Visual localization is a fundamental task in robotics, aiming to estimate the camera pose within a known map via visual ob-servations. Existing methods can be categorized into structure-based , learning-based , and topological visual localization. 

A. Structure-based Visual Localization 

Structure-based visual localization estimates absolute cam-era poses by establishing 2D-3D correspondences against a pre-built 3D map. Traditional systems like ORB-SLAM [11] rely on Bag-of-Words for coarse localization and hand-crafted features for pose optimization. To improve robustness in complex environments, recent pipelines like Hierarchical Lo-calization (HLoc) [12] and PixLoc [13] integrate learned descriptors and feature-metric optimization. Some work [14] even fuse offline LiDAR point clouds as geometric priors to push the absolute accuracy limits. Nevertheless, these methods heavily rely on global 3D structural maps, incurring prohibitive storage costs and rendering them brittle in dynamic environ-ments [15], where scene changes necessitate time-consuming re-triangulation and global optimization. 

B. Learning-based Visual Localization 

Learning-based approaches encode scene representations directly into neural network parameters. Absolute pose re-gression (APR) networks like PoseNet [16] directly regress 6 degrees of freedom (DoF) poses, while scene coordinate regression (SCR) methods like ACE Zero [17] map images to 3D coordinates without ground-truth poses. Recent advance-ments like Reloc3r [18] improve zero-shot generalization via relative pose regression. While Novel View Synthesis tech-niques [19], [20] are frequently adopted for post-optimization pose refinement, their computational overhead hinders real-time large-scale deployment. Furthermore, compressing vast scene details into limited network weights inevitably leads to catastrophic forgetting [21]. Updating the map to reflect physical environmental changes necessitates computationally expensive network retraining [22], severely limiting their scal-ability for lifelong robot autonomy. 

C. Topological and Topo-metric Visual Localization 

Topological visual localization represents environments as lightweight graphs, avoiding the massive geometric maps required by structure-based methods. While visual place recog-nition (VPR) [23], [24] enables efficient scene redirection, it yields only coarse localization. To achieve precise 6-DoF estimation, recent works have shifted towards topo-metric paradigms. Methods like LiteVLoc [8], OpenNavMap [9], and FreeReg [7] achieve metric registration directly between query and reference images without global 3D maps. However, existing topo-metric methods predominantly rely on perspective cameras. Their limited field of view (FOV) re-quires dense orientation sampling during mapping and suffers from severe perceptual aliasing during localization. Moreover, their metric recovery heavily relies on stereo baselines or monocular depth accuracy, which are baseline-bounded and scale-ambiguous in complex environments. In this work, we introduce omnidirectional cameras into the topo-metric framework to formulate OmniVLoc. The holistic surround-view effectively overcomes FOV limitations and mitigates perceptual aliasing [25]. By integrating a VFM for omnidirectional depth recovery and formulating a scale-aware factor graph with multi-frame constraints, our system suc-cessfully recovers precise, absolute-scale metric poses without relying on pre-built 3D geometry or stereo setups. C. Panoramic Visual Front-end 

The front-end retrieves plausible map nodes, establishes query–map feature correspondences, and combines them with query depth to form the 3D-to-bearing constraints used by spherical PnP. 

1) Global Topological Retrieval: Given a query panorama 

Iqk, we employ MegaLoc [24] to extract its global descriptor 

gqk and rank all map nodes by descriptor cosine similarity. Since a panorama may overlap multiple nearby nodes, we retain the top Nret candidates rather than only the highest-ranked image [8]; all candidates are passed to local matching (Nret = 5 in our setup). 

2) CubeMap-based Feature Association: We adopt Super-Point [27] and LightGlue [28] for feature extraction and association. Since these models target perspective images, we decompose each panorama into six CubeMap faces to avoid ERP distortion. SuperPoint features extracted per face are back-projected and concatenated in the ERP domain, after which LightGlue matches the query against each candidate.

A. Datasets 1) 360Loc Dataset: We primarily evaluate OmniVLoc on the 360Loc dataset [31], which comprises four campus environments with 14 sequences, covering campus scenes with repetitive structures and perceptual aliasing. Each scene provides a daytime mapping sequence together with several daytime and nighttime localization sequences, recorded at approximately 3 Hz. Since 360Loc does not provide IMU measurements, we generate simulated IMU readings from its ground-truth camera trajectory, with white noise and random walks for bias, following the EuRoC-ADIS16448 noise model. 

2) Custom Dataset: We also collect a custom campus dataset, supporting a higher query-image rate of 24 Hz. Our ac-quisition rig (illustrated in Fig. 5) rigidly combines an Insta360 X5 panoramic camera with a Manifold Q9000 commercial mapping device, which comprises a Mid-360 LiDAR and three fisheye cameras. Its multi-sensor mapping system provides sub-centimeter localization accuracy. We use its estimated trajectory as the localization ground truth and calibrate the extrinsic transformation between the Insta360 X5 and the Q9000 LiDAR rig, allowing the reference poses to be ex-pressed in the panoramic-camera frame. Similar to the 360Loc protocol, each scene contains a daytime mapping sequence and separate daytime and nighttime localization sequences. We collect 3 scenes with 9 sequences with complementary localization challenges, such as open-space geometric sparsity, illumination changes, and severe appearance repetition. For each scene in both datasets, we construct the panoramic image-node map from daytime mapping sequences using our rule defined in Sect. III-A. We set the distance threshold to 

τd = 5 .0 m and the descriptor similarity threshold to τs = 0 .7.

C. Evaluation: Localization Performance 

We evaluated the localization performance on both datasets, and the results are shown in Table II. Since the baseline meth-ods (HLoc and ACE0) perform localization using independent images, we disabled our query-query process and excluded IMU measurements for a fair comparison. This degrades our approach to a single-frame localization setup (denoted as 

Ours-NoSeq in Table II). Under this setting, our method out-performs all baseline methods. While HLoc is operational, its metric-level localization accuracy is compromised due to the visual pre-processing relying on single limited-view images (Opt) or the ambiguities in multi-view fusion (Fus). ACE0 exhibits severe overfitting to the map scene representation. It breaks down entirely under large appearance gaps between mapping and localization (day vs. night) or in environments with low discriminability (the Avenue scene). Furthermore, by incorporating inter-frame information, our full pipeline ( Ours-Full ) significantly reduces the mean ATE, yielding stabler localization and higher accuracy compared to the surround-view-based Stella SLAM, even with a higher input image frequency. Qualitative analysis (Fig. 6a) reveals that the campus sce-nario exhibits severe perceptual aliasing, posing significant challenges for global localization. To address this, our system employs top Nret map image candidates for matching and per-forms multiple consistency verifications to filter out erroneous observation pairs, thereby significantly improving robustness. D. Evaluation: Ablation Study 

We conducted an ablation study to evaluate our system. By disabling the temporal processing and consistency verification modules, we observed that while the median ATE remains largely unaffected in most sequences, the mean ATE increases significantly. This demonstrates that incorporating temporal odometry and temporal cross-validation effectively enhances the stability and reliability of our system. We also evaluated the effect of the number of map candi-dates ( Nret ) on accuracy, latency, and the number of candidate usage, detailed in Table III. Meanwhile, using only the best VPR result ( Nret = 1 ), as in traditional visual localization, allows almost all frames to pass verification but yields poor localization precision due to insufficient data checks from a single observation. Increasing the observations (e.g., Nret = 3 )rapidly improves accuracy, proving the effectiveness of our multi-observation constrained factor graph. We find Nret = 5 

to be optimal by balancing the accuracy and map usage. Ad-ditional observations neither increase the verified candidates nor improve accuracy. Finally, we evaluate the computational efficiency of our system. Accelerated via C++ and ONNX, our system operates stably at 1.30 Hz, satisfying a real-time requirement of 1 Hz for online deployment as defined in our system setup.

V. D ISCUSSION 

We also identified several key findings during our work. First, although foundation models like DINOv2 [32] endow current VPR methods with high precision, their recall remains relatively low, which inherently constrains the success rate of downstream metric-level localization. While OmniVLoc lever-ages this high-precision low-recall nature, integrating a more robust VPR paradigm, such as sequential VPR, could further boost global localization performance in our framework. Second, local feature matching failures remain the primary cause of localization degradation in extreme cross-illumination scenarios, particularly when coupled with severe viewpoint shifts (as illustrated in Fig. 6b). Although recent work [8] has introduced feed-forward neural networks (e.g., Mast3r [33]) for wide-baseline and cross-illumination matching, these mod-els are typically trained on perspective camera models and struggle to generalize to equirectangular projections. There-fore, designing feed-forward networks specifically tailored for omnidirectional cameras represents a promising direction to further bolster system robustness in challenging environments. VI. C ONCLUSION 

In this work, we proposed OmniVLoc, a topo-metric om-nidirectional localization system. We constructed a scale-optimized visual localization paradigm driven by multi-view map constraints and monocular depth estimation. By perform-ing pose graph estimation and precise BA optimization, our approach yields accurate metric-level localization. A hierar-chical verification mechanism rejected unreliable intermediate outputs, ensuring high system stability. Experimental results confirmed that our system maintains robust localization despite significant illumination and scene variations. Future work will focus on the incremental construction and lifelong mainte-nance of topo-metric omnidirectional maps, aiming for seam-less compatibility with large-scale street-view applications.
