Abstract
Reconstructing dynamic 3D scenes from monocular video requires inferring motion from noisy and intermittently
visible observations. Long-range tracks provide valuable geometric constraints, but integrating their fragmented temporal
coverage into shared, continuous motion remains challenging. We introduce RayCo4D, a ray-conditioned continuous
motion model for monocular 4D reconstruction that explicitly separates visual measurements from latent 3D motion states.
Each track is associated with a reference-time 3D query constrained to its camera ray, and its image and depth observations
constrain shared latent motion nodes through local many-to-many associations. Each node maintains an SE(3) pose and

on diverse monocular dynamic scenes show that RayCo4D achieves competitive novel-view synthesis quality, improves
structural and temporal consistency under rapid motion and incomplete tracks, and supports continuous-time rendering.
1 Introduction
Reconstructing 3D scenes from images is an important step toward persistent and renderable representations of the
physical world [9, 24, 42]. Such representations can support applications, such as 3D content creation [10, 20, 34, 37],
robot perception [11, 12, 25], and dynamic scene understanding [18, 28, 43]. In monocular dynamic reconstruction,
only one viewpoint is available at each instant, leaving scene geometry, camera motion, and non-rigid deformation
jointly under-constrained. Occlusions, motion blur, and tracking failures further fragment the cross-frame evidence
available for motion estimation.
Recent methods build on the explicit representation and efficient rendering of 3D Gaussian Splatting [15], using
long-range tracks [13] and monocular depth to constrain dynamic scene reconstruction. An important modeling choice
is how these geometric cues relate to the underlying motion states. MoSca [18] initializes motion-node trajectories from
lifted tracks, while SE(3) B-splineGS [41] initializes continuous spline control poses from lifted tracklets. Shape of
Motion [28] uses tracks to supervise shared SE(3) motion bases, illustrating the value of pooling observations through
a common motion representation. For incomplete tracks, the central challenge is to combine measurements across
visibility intervals into locally shared motion states with coherent continuous-time evolution.
Our central perspective is that image tracks are measurements of latent 3D motion, rather than the motion states
themselves. Based on this perspective, we introduce RayCo4D, a ray-conditioned continuous motion model for
monocular 4D reconstruction. Fig. 1 summarizes its two central design choices: ray-conditioned measurement–state
modeling and continuous pose–velocity state estimation. Using camera and depth estimates from Depth Anything
3 [19], we constrain each track with a reference-time 3D query and use its visible observations to estimate the shared
motion. Track geometry helps initialize a compact motion support, but subsequent optimization does not maintain a
dedicated motion state for each track. Instead, each query is associated with a local neighborhood of motion nodes. A
track can constrain multiple nodes, while each node aggregates observations from multiple tracks and timestamps. This
many-to-many association allows track observations with different visibility intervals to contribute to a shared motion
representation.
To estimate this shared motion continuously, each node maintains an SE(3) pose and body-frame velocity at
sparse support times. We connect consecutive states using a white-noise-on-acceleration (WNOA) process prior from
continuous-time state estimation [3]. The process prior couples neighboring pose–velocity states and defines consistent
interpolation between support times. Track and depth measurements constrain node transformations at their observation
times. As a result, observations from different frames and visibility intervals contribute to the same latent state sequence.
Intervals without direct observations remain connected through the process model. Temporal continuity enters both the
motion representation and its estimation.
To obtain renderable dynamic geometry, we propagate the node transformations to Gaussian primitives through
dual-quaternion blending. This shared scaffold provides a common motion structure, but cannot capture every fine-scale
non-rigid deformation. We therefore augment each dynamic Gaussian with a local SE(3) residual expressed in its
reference configuration. The residual transform is exactly the identity at the Gaussian’s reference time, ensuring that it
introduces no local correction at that anchor. Together with regularization on residual magnitude and variation, this
design encourages local corrections to complement, rather than replace, the shared motion. RayCo4D thus combines
continuous shared motion with reference-anchored local refinement in a hierarchical dynamic representation.
Our main contributions are summarized as follows: (1) We introduce a ray-conditioned measurement–state
formulation in which 2D tracks and camera–depth geometry constrain shared latent motion nodes through many-
to-many associations, decoupling track identity from motion-state identity. (2) We formulate shared motion using
sparse continuous-time SE(3) pose-velocity states, with WNOA process factors coupling neighboring supports and
defining interpolation for measurement evaluation and rendering. (3) We develop a hierarchical Gaussian motion
representation that combines shared node transport with reference-anchored local SE(3) residuals, capturing coherent
structural motion and fine-scale non-rigid deformation. (4) Experiments demonstrate competitive novel-view synthesis

and improved structural and temporal consistency under fast motion and incomplete tracks, together with continuous-time
rendering within the reconstructed interval.
2 Related Work
Structured Motion for Dynamic Gaussian Splatting.
Time-conditioned Gaussian deformation is used to model
dynamic scenes [32, 35, 38]. Structured representations introduce shared motion across primitives: Shape of Motion [28]
fits shared SE(3) motion bases using track and depth supervision, while MoSca [18] initializes sparse motion nodes from
lifted tracks and propagates their transformations to Gaussians. OriGS [33], RiGS [31], and USPLAT4D [8] further
introduce orientation fields, motion-type decomposition, and uncertainty-guided motion graphs, respectively. Building
on shared motion representations, RayCo4D connects ray-conditioned track measurements to latent nodes through local
many-to-many associations and augments their motion with reference-anchored Gaussian-level corrections.
Continuous Motion and State-Space Modeling.
SplineGS [21] represents Gaussian positions with cubic Hermite
splines, while SE(3) B-splineGS [41] models position and orientation using cumulative SE(3) B-splines initialized from
lifted tracklets. Spline-based motion models are also used for dynamic scene reconstruction in autonomous driving [36].
GP-4DGS [16] uses variational Gaussian processes to model correlated deformations and their uncertainty. Related
dynamical formulations include prediction–observation fusion over Gaussian states in Gaussians on their Way [5],
learned velocity fields in EvoGS [2], and latent ODE dynamics in ODE-GS [27]. RayCo4D adopts a WNOA prior from
continuous-time state estimation [3, 4] to jointly estimate shared node poses and body velocities at sparse support times.
The same pose-velocity model defines temporal process factors and continuous motion queries, allowing ray-based
observations to constrain a common latent state sequence. Additional discussion is provided in Appendix A.

3.1 Ray-Conditioned Motion Measurements
Long-range tracks connect observations across time, but their visibility, confidence, and depth support vary across
frames. The goal is to convert these fragmented observations into geometric constraints on a compact, shared motion
field. RayCo4D anchors each track to a camera ray in a reliable reference frame and associates its 3D query with nearby
latent motion nodes. The resulting node-induced motion is then evaluated against image and depth measurements.

3.2 Continuous-Time Latent Motion with WNOA

must ensure temporal coherence and provide node transformations at arbitrary times. RayCo4D therefore represents
each node with sparse SE(3) pose–velocity states and connects adjacent supports using a white-noise-on-acceleration
(WNOA) process. The same continuous model is used for measurement evaluation, Gaussian motion, and subframe
rendering.

for each primitive. Shared motion captures coherent structure-level dynamics, while local corrections model fine-scale
non-rigid deformation. Both components are jointly optimized with Gaussian geometry and appearance under visual
and geometric supervision.

5 Conclusion
We presented RayCo4D, a ray-conditioned continuous motion model for monocular 4D reconstruction. RayCo4D
utilizes 2D tracks together with camera-depth geometry to constrain shared latent motion nodes through local many-to-
many associations. Sparse SE(3) pose–velocity states, coupled by a white-noise-on-acceleration process prior, form
continuous node trajectories that integrate evidence across different visibility intervals. The inferred motion is propagated
to dynamic Gaussians through dual-quaternion blending, while reference-anchored local SE(3) corrections capture
fine-grained non-rigid deformation. Experiments on DyCheck and NVIDIA scenes demonstrate competitive novel-view
synthesis quality and improved novel-view synthesis, motion correspondence, and intermediate-time reconstruction.
Our results validate that separating visual measurements from latent motion states provides a coherent and robust
foundation for monocular 4D reconstruction.

A Additional Related Work
Spatial structure and motion sharing.
Recent structured representations address different aspects of dynamic scene
complexity. OriGS [33] uses a Global Orientation Field to organize region-specific deformations. RiGS [31] separates
static, rigid, and transient Gaussians to accommodate motion at different temporal scales. USPLAT4D [8] constructs a
spatio-temporal motion graph using per-Gaussian uncertainty, propagating reliable motion cues toward weakly observed
regions. RayCo4D organizes spatial sharing through local track–node associations: reference queries retain their
geometric parameters, while shared node states encode time-varying motion.
Spline trajectories and local refinement.
Beyond SplineGS [21] and SE(3) B-splineGS [41], WebSpline [22]
initializes a Structural Proxy Graph from 2D tracks to regularize per-Gaussian Hermite trajectories. RetimeGS [29]
defines continuous-time Gaussian behavior for temporal retiming and intermediate-time rendering. RayCo4D also uses
cubic Hermite expressions, at two distinct levels: the WNOA conditional mean interpolates shared pose–velocity states
in a local SE(3) chart, and per-Gaussian Hermite curves parameterize reference-anchored local corrections. The shared
process factors and local residual constraints determine how these temporal representations are estimated and combined.
Probabilistic and dynamical formulations.
GP-4DGS [16] models spatio-temporal deformation correlations through
variational Gaussian processes, supporting uncertainty estimation and temporal extrapolation. Gaussians on their
Way [5] treats Gaussian means and covariances as dynamic states and fuses motion predictions with deformation
observations. EvoGS [2] integrates a learned velocity field, while ODE-GS [27] evolves latent states derived from
reconstructed Gaussian trajectories through a Neural ODE. RayCo4D places its WNOA prior on sparse shared SE(3)
pose–velocity states. Ray-conditioned measurements constrain these states through local associations, and their
continuous transformations drive the dense Gaussian representation.

D.7 Additional Implementation Details
Initialization and representation.
Lifted tracks initialize motion nodes through spatial coverage sampling; the node
count depends on the scene. Missing positions are interpolated linearly between observations and extended from the
nearest endpoint outside the observed span. These filled positions are used only for initialization and are not marked as
measured support. Pose–velocity supports are placed every two frames, including both endpoints; initial rotations are
identities and body velocities are estimated from neighboring support poses. We use 𝐾= 16 candidate nodes, scaffold
scale ℓ= 0.02 in normalized scene units, 𝑞𝑝= 𝑞𝑅= 1, and 𝐿= 8 local Hermite controls. Initialization uses at most
80,000 static depth samples, 80,000 dynamic track seeds, and 40,000 additional dynamic depth samples.
Sampling and updates.
Each motion update samples up to 16,384 visible observations with replacement. Motion
regularization uses a contiguous window of up to 16 support states and up to 1,024 sampled nodes; this is a minibatch
size, not the total node count. Track candidates are refreshed every 100 motion-warm-up steps. During scene fitting,
binding refreshes occur at initialization, step 3,000, and as required by node-structure changes. Layer supervision is
evaluated every two steps and appearance transport every four steps, with their losses scaled by the respective intervals.
Optimization.
We use Adam with the learning rates in Tab. 6. During motion warm-up, node and query-depth rates
follow a cosine decay to 5% of their initial values; scene fitting starts these groups at 10% of their initial warm-up rates.
From scene-fitting step 2,000, Gaussian position rates decay exponentially to 1.6 × 10−6, and camera and depth-scale
rates to one tenth of their initial values. The other rates remain constant.
D.8 Baseline Details
We compare against methods covering the main motion representations used in recent monocular dynamic reconstruction.
MoSca [18] constructs a sparse motion scaffold from lifted tracks and propagates node transformations to dynamic
Gaussians. Shape of Motion [28] represents scene dynamics using shared SE(3) motion bases constrained by long-range
tracks and monocular geometry. SE(3) B-splineGS [41] parameterizes continuous motion using SE(3) B-spline control
trajectories, providing the most direct comparison to our WNOA pose–velocity formulation. OriGS [33] and RiGS [31]
are included for their treatment of complex local deformation and multi-timescale motion, respectively. USPLAT4D [8]
evaluates reconstruction under incomplete and unreliable observations, including occlusion.
D.9 Detail Datasets and Evaluation Protocols
DyCheck Dataset.
The DyCheck Dataset [7] contains casually captured monocular videos of dynamic scenes together
with spatially separated evaluation views. It is particularly challenging because the training and evaluation cameras
often observe only partially overlapping scene regions. We follow the standard seven-scene protocol at 2× resolution.
Novel-view synthesis metrics are computed within the provided co-visibility masks, yielding mPSNR, mSSIM, and
mLPIPS.

E.2 Challenging Cases and Limitations
Long intervals without reliable observations leave the intervening motion ambiguous. If motion changes abruptly within
such an interval, the locally smooth WNOA prior may yield inaccurate intermediate motion.
The measurement–state formulation separates motion-node identity from individual tracks, but the latent states still
rely on geometric observations. Large errors in tracks, estimated camera poses, or monocular depths can therefore
distort the recovered motion and geometry. Future work could model measurement uncertainty, adapt temporal support
to motion changes, and use stronger scene-level priors for poorly observed intervals. Fig. 6 shows examples of remaining
reconstruction errors.
