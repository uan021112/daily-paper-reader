Abstract
Semi-dense local feature matching commonly aggregates contextual informa-
tion before coarse-to-fine correspondence estimation. However, intra-image and
inter-image aggregation serve different roles: the former should propagate spa-
tial evidence within each image while retaining structured two-dimensional de-
pendencies, whereas the latter should gather information from plausible corre-
sponding regions while limiting noise from unrelated locations.
We propose
WovenAnchor Matcher (WAM), a semi-dense matching framework based on
role-specialized context aggregation. WAM introduces two complementary op-
erators.
WovenMamba performs intra-image aggregation through horizontal-
then-vertical state-space propagation, allowing vertical updates to operate on
horizontally contextualized features and thereby encouraging a structured two-
dimensional receptive-field bias. AnchorCrossAttention performs inter-image
aggregation by using cross-attention to gather context from plausible correspond-
ing regions across the image pair.
It performs this exchange over local rep-
resentative anchors that aggregate feature evidence within each window.
On
MegaDepth, WAM achieves 66.1 pose AUC@5◦, improving on the JamMa base-
line by 2.0 AUC points under the same evaluation setup. Ablations show that
removing or replacing either specialized component reduces accuracy, provid-
ing evidence that intra-image propagation and inter-image retrieval benefit from
role-specific aggregation designs. Code and pretrained weights are available at
https://github.com/mmaiLab/WovenAnchorMatcher.

Introduction
Local feature matching is a core component of visual localization, structure-from-motion, SLAM,
and image alignment [1–5]. Modern semi-dense matchers estimate many correspondences from
learned feature maps and commonly follow a coarse-to-fine pipeline [6–8]. Before coarse matching,
a context aggregation block updates descriptors using information from both the same image and
the paired image.
These two sources of context are not interchangeable. Intra-image aggregation should propagate spa-
tial evidence while respecting the image’s two-dimensional layout. Inter-image aggregation should
instead gather evidence from plausible corresponding regions and avoid contaminating descriptors
with visually similar but unrelated content. This matters because dense affinities can be unreliable
under repetition, viewpoint changes, and local ambiguity.
Existing matchers only partially encode this asymmetry. LoFTR-style methods explicitly separate
self-attention and cross-attention, but both rely on attention-based token interaction as a generic
aggregation primitive [6, 9–12]. JamMa improves efficiency by replacing attention-heavy aggre-
gation with Mamba-based sequence modeling, but its JointMamba block applies a closely related
sequence-modeling bias to within-image propagation and cross-image interaction [7]. These de-

We propose WovenAnchor Matcher (WAM), a
semi-dense matcher that makes this operator-
level asymmetry explicit. WAM keeps the stan-
dard coarse-to-fine matching framework but re-
places the generic context block with a role-
specialized WovenAnchorModule.
For intra-
image aggregation, WovenMamba applies hori-
zontal state-space propagation followed by ver-
tical propagation.
Since the vertical stage
operates on horizontally contextualized fea-
tures, the operator encourages composed two-

ditive combination of independent directional
scans. We analyze this bias with a simplified

context should preserve where supporting ev-

dence exists somewhere in the image.
For inter-image aggregation, AnchorCrossAttention uses cross-attention to gather context from plau-
sible corresponding regions, but performs this exchange over local representative anchors instead of
dense point-wise tokens [13–15]. Multiple anchors are formed within each local neighborhood, al-
lowing different structures in the same area to receive different cross-image updates. This design
aims to reduce reliance on ambiguous token-level affinities by aggregating local evidence before
cross-image retrieval.
Experiments support the value of this role-specialized design. On MegaDepth, WAM improves
over JamMa under our setup, increasing pose AUC@5◦from 64.1 to 66.1. Component-removal
and operator-replacement ablations show that removing or substituting either WovenMamba or An-
chorCrossAttention lowers accuracy. Shared/reversed-role experiments further support the proposed
assignment of WovenMamba to intra-image propagation and AnchorCrossAttention to inter-image
retrieval.
Our contributions are threefold. First, we formulate semi-dense context aggregation as two roles
with different inductive biases: spatially structured intra-image propagation and correspondence-
aware inter-image retrieval. Second, we introduce WovenMamba, a horizontal-then-vertical SSM
composition whose simplified receptive field exhibits direction- and distance-dependent two-
dimensional support. Third, we introduce AnchorCrossAttention, a cross-attention operator that
retrieves cross-image context through locally grouped anchors.

Related work
Local feature matching.
Feature matching methods can be grouped into sparse, dense, and semi-
dense approaches. Sparse pipelines detect keypoints and match descriptors [16, 17], with represen-
tative learned systems including SuperPoint with SuperGlue and LightGlue [18–20]. Dense match-
ers such as DKM and RoMa estimate correspondences over denser grids and can achieve strong
accuracy at higher computational cost [21, 22]. Semi-dense matchers seek a balance by learn-
ing dense coarse features and refining selected correspondences; representative examples include
LoFTR, JamMa, and EDM [6–8]. WAM belongs to this semi-dense family and is most directly
related to JamMa in its overall coarse-to-fine framework.
Context aggregation for semi-dense matching.
LoFTR introduced detector-free matching with
self-attention and cross-attention over coarse feature maps [6, 9]. Later methods improve atten-
tion structure, feature interaction, or efficiency while retaining attention-based aggregation [10–12].
JamMa replaces attention-heavy aggregation with a Mamba-based module for efficient long-range
modeling [7]. WAM follows this JamMa-like coarse-to-fine setting, but differs mainly in context ag-

State-space models and compact cross-image interaction.
State-space sequence models such
as Mamba provide an efficient alternative to attention for long-range modeling [23, 24], and recent
vision variants adapt scan-based SSMs to image grids [25, 26]. For image features, the scan order
determines the spatial inductive bias; WovenMamba differs from scan-and-sum designs by compos-
ing the two scan orders rather than summing independent directional responses. For inter-image
aggregation, dense cross-attention can mix information from non-corresponding regions, especially
when noise or visual ambiguity increases their apparent similarity. This motivates compact local
representations for more correspondence-aware cross-image aggregation.

followed by overlap fusion on the dense feature grid.

Experiments
4.1
Experimental setup
Implementation.
WAM follows the overall framework family of JamMa [7] with a lightweight
ConvNeXtV2-style encoder [27]. Coarse features at 1/8 resolution are updated by two WovenAn-
chorModule stages, while fine features at 1/2 resolution are used for local refinement. Anchor-
CrossAttention uses 8 × 8 windows with a stride of 4 and K = 4 anchors per window. We train
on MegaDepth images resized and padded to 832 × 832 [28] for 20 epochs with batch size 8, using
AdamW with a base learning rate of 4 × 10−4, linear warm-up for 14,550 optimization steps (ap-
proximately 3.16 epochs), and cosine annealing. Training takes about 26 hours on a single NVIDIA
H100 GPU. Appendix A provides the remaining hyperparameters, matching thresholds, RANSAC
settings, and runtime protocol.
Evaluation.
We evaluate relative pose estimation on MegaDepth-1500, an outdoor benchmark
based on MegaDepth [28], and zero-shot relative pose estimation on ScanNet-1500, an indoor
benchmark based on ScanNet [29], following the LoFTR/JamMa protocol [6, 7]. MegaDepth im-
ages are resized and padded to 832 × 832; ScanNet images are resized to 640 × 480. We use
LO-RANSAC [30] and report pose AUC at 5◦, 10◦, and 20◦. For zero-shot homography evaluation
on HPatches, a planar homography benchmark with viewpoint and illumination changes [31], we
follow LoFTR/JamMa: resize to a 480-pixel short edge, retain the top 1000 matches by confidence,
and use vanilla RANSAC [32]. We report corner-error AUC at 3, 5, and 10 pixels. Runtimes are
measured with batch size 1 on the same H100 GPU, including feature extraction and matching but
excluding image I/O and RANSAC.

4.3
Controlled ablations
The ablations are conducted under the same setup. The component ablation first shows that both
intra-image and inter-image context aggregation are useful. The alternative-module comparisons
and the shared/reversed-role experiment then evaluate which operators are better suited to each role.
Component ablation.
Table 3 removes the two context aggregation components of WAM. Re-
moving WovenMamba reduces AUC@5◦from 66.1 to 64.6, while removing AnchorCrossAttention
reduces it to 64.0. Removing both gives 62.0. These results show that both intra-image and inter-
image context aggregation are important.
Intra-image alternatives.
Table 4 compares intra-image context modules while keeping Anchor-
CrossAttention fixed. UniMamba, BiMamba, OrthMamba, and FourMamba are directional-scan
baselines with one, two opposite, two orthogonal, and four independent scan directions, respectively.
All scan variants are below WovenMamba, and FourMamba increases runtime without matching
WovenMamba’s accuracy. Self-Attention serves as an attention-based intra-image alternative, but it
is slower and less accurate. These results suggest that the proposed woven horizontal-then-vertical
propagation gives a better accuracy–efficiency trade-off than either independent directional scans or
self-attention.

5
Discussion and limitations
The additional visual localization evaluation in Appendix G provides a diagnostic view of how WAM
behaves in a larger localization pipeline. The experiments support the value of specializing intra-
image and inter-image aggregation: the simplified receptive-field analysis, trained-model ERFs, and
intra-image ablations support horizontal-then-vertical propagation, while the inter-image ablations
support local anchor grouping for correspondence-aware retrieval. A limitation is computational
cost and scalability. WovenAnchorModule has a higher measured runtime than JointMamba in our
evaluation (Table 8). Although AnchorCrossAttention reduces the number of interactions relative
to dense token-wise cross-attention, its interaction complexity remains quadratic in the number of
local windows, limiting scalability at high input resolutions. Another limitation is the JamMa-
style nearest-neighbor matching strategy, which may produce many-to-one correspondences and
may contribute to the localization gap between WAM and LoFTR in the Aachen pipeline. WAM
generally improves over JamMa under the same matching strategy, but these results do not isolate
the effect of match filtering.
6
Conclusion
We presented WAM, a semi-dense matcher that specializes context aggregation for intra-image and
inter-image modeling. WovenMamba organizes spatial context with horizontal-then-vertical SSM
propagation, while AnchorCrossAttention retrieves cross-image context through locally grouped
anchors. A simplified receptive-field analysis suggests that WovenMamba provides a structural
bias toward composed two-dimensional dependencies. Controlled ablations show that both forms
of context are useful and that the proposed specialized designs are better suited to their respective
roles. Benchmark results further show that WAM improves over JamMa under our setup. Overall,
these results support designing semi-dense matchers with context modules matched to the different
requirements of intra-image and inter-image aggregation.

Implementation and evaluation details
Architecture details.
Unless otherwise stated, WAM uses a coarse feature dimension of Cc = 256
and a fine feature dimension of 64. The WovenMamba propagation branch has dimension 512, cor-
responding to an expansion ratio of 2. WovenMamba uses state dimension 8 and 3 × 3 depthwise
convolutions before the horizontal and vertical SSM scans. RMSNorm is used in all WovenMamba
and AnchorCrossAttention blocks. AnchorCrossAttention uses overlapping windows of size 8 × 8
with a stride of 4, K = 4 anchors per window, anchor dimension D = 256, geometry feature di-
mension Cg = 16, 4 attention heads, and zero attention dropout. We use two WovenAnchorModule
stages unless otherwise stated.
WovenMamba separator tokens.
For the reported WovenMamba models, we insert fixed sepa-
rator tokens into the flattened scan sequences at scan-boundary positions: row boundaries for the
horizontal-order scan and column boundaries for the vertical-order scan. The SSM is applied to the
augmented sequence; after unscanning the augmented output, we remove the separator positions and
keep only the original image-grid tokens. At separator positions, we add a fixed offset of 6.0 to the
pre-softplus SSM step-size parameter. This is a boundary-handling implementation detail and is not
treated as a separate contribution.

Matching details.
For coarse matching, we apply softmax independently along the rows and
columns of the similarity matrix with a temperature of 0.1. We retain directional nearest-neighbor
matches with probability greater than 0.2 and take the union of the two sets, assigning the larger
directional probability to duplicate pairs. For fine matching, we use a fine matching window of size
5 × 5, dual-softmax temperature 0.1, and confidence threshold 0.1. Unless otherwise stated, the
same matching thresholds are used for the main benchmark results and ablations.
Training details.
The main model is trained on MegaDepth for 20 epochs with batch size 8 using
the training protocol described in Section 4.1. Training uses a single NVIDIA H100 GPU; the peak
allocated GPU memory for the main model is about 85GB. Each WAM training run takes about 26
hours on one H100 GPU. The sub-pixel refinement loss uses a squared symmetric epipolar distance
threshold of 10−4 on MegaDepth. The main benchmark tables use a representative WAM checkpoint
whose MegaDepth AUC@5◦equals the median over seven runs, as reported in Appendix E. Results
labeled as zero-shot transfer evaluate this representative MegaDepth-trained checkpoint on the target
dataset without additional fine-tuning.
Ablation protocol.
All ablation variants are trained on MegaDepth using the same training pro-
tocol as the main model, unless otherwise stated. We change only the component specified in
each ablation table and keep the backbone, feature resolutions, number of WovenAnchorModule
stages, optimizer, learning-rate schedule, batch size, matching thresholds, and evaluation protocol
fixed. This ensures that the ablations isolate the contribution of the modified component rather than
changes in the training or evaluation setup. Ablation variants are single-run controlled trainings
unless repeated-run statistics are explicitly reported.

Spatial resolutions in WAM
Table 9 summarizes the representations used by WAM. The backbone produces dense coarse features
at 1/8 of the input resolution. Both WovenAnchorModule stages preserve this grid: WovenMamba
propagates context over its positions, and ACA constructs anchors from overlapping windows, then
uses anchor-to-feature projection and overlap fusion to return their updates to the same grid. Coarse
matching uses the resulting dense 1/8 features.
With the default 8 × 8 windows and stride 4, adjacent ACA window centers are separated by four
coarse cells, or 32 input pixels. Each window supplies K = 4 anchors. Thus, the 1/32 window-
center spacing describes the sampling of anchor windows, rather than a dense 1/32 feature map. For
a padded 832×832 input, the coarse grid is 104×104 and ACA constructs 25×25 windows, giving
2500 anchors per image. Cross-attention is applied separately to the 625 anchors in each anchor-
ID group. Padded image regions are masked; ACA’s window-boundary padding and masking are
described in Appendix D.

Discussion.
WAM generally improves over JamMa under this controlled HLoc-based evaluation,
but it does not outperform LoFTR on Aachen Day-Night v1.1. One possible reason is the match
selection strategy. Like JamMa, WAM uses nearest-neighbor matching, which can produce many-to-
one assignments. Aggregating or suppressing such matches may introduce additional sensitivity in
the localization pipeline. In contrast, LoFTR uses mutual nearest-neighbor filtering in our evaluated
pipeline, which more directly suppresses many-to-one correspondences. Compared with JamMa,
which uses a similar nearest-neighbor selection strategy, WAM generally performs better, suggesting
that the proposed WovenAnchorModule yields more effective matching features than JointMamba
in this setting.
H
Limitations and broader impact
Responsible use.
Improved local feature matching can benefit visual localization, SfM, SLAM,
mapping, image alignment, augmented reality, and robotic perception [1–5]. Potential negative uses
include privacy-invasive localization, surveillance, and reconstruction from imagery without ade-
quate consent. Incorrect matches may also propagate errors to downstream robotic or mapping sys-
tems. Responsible deployment should consider privacy constraints, consent for imagery, benchmark
limitations, and downstream failure handling.
Limitations.
The nearest-neighbor-based matching strategy can produce many-to-one correspon-
dences, which may contribute to the localization gap observed on Aachen in Appendix G. The
comparison does not isolate the effect of match filtering; localization performance may depend on
both the learned features and how correspondences are filtered and aggregated. Another limita-
tion is computational scaling. WovenAnchorModule has a higher measured context-module runtime
than JointMamba at the default resolution (Table 8). Although local anchors reduce the number
of interactions relative to dense token-wise cross-attention, AnchorCrossAttention retains quadratic
interaction complexity in the number of local windows, limiting scalability at high input resolu-
tions. Future work could combine WAM with stronger mutual filtering, one-to-one assignment,
localization-specific match pruning, or more scalable anchor retrieval.
Datasets.
The work uses existing public computer-vision benchmarks and does not involve new
human-subject data collection, crowdsourcing, or participant intervention. The method is not a
generative model and does not introduce a new scraped dataset.
