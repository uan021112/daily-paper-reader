# Abstract 

Pose-free 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) aims to bypass cumbersome Structure-from-Motion (SfM) by directly re-constructing scenes from unposed image sequences. In this work, we present CrossFit-GS, a deferred-commitment progressive framework that mitigates cumulative trajectory drift and breaks the self-reinforcing loop of distorted-map error propagation. Specifically, we partition the input se-quence into interleaved subsets and maintain two comple-mentary submaps. Each candidate pose is cross-refined against a frozen opposite submap that has never been op-timized using the target frame, thereby providing an inde-pendent geometric reference for pose estimation. Checker-board validation and spatial coverage safeguards further reject unreliable updates, and only verified poses are com-mitted to their corresponding owner submap. Once the tra-jectory has been refined, the intermediate submaps are dis-carded and the final global representation is trained from scratch using the fixed refined poses. Extensive evaluations across multiple benchmarks demonstrate that CrossFit-GS substantially reduces cumulative trajectory drift and con-sistently improves both camera pose estimation and novel-view synthesis over existing methods. 

# 1. Introduction 

3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) [1, 16] has demonstrated remarkable success across a wide range of applications [23, 28, 29, 36]. However, standard 3DGS heavily relies on pre-computed camera extrinsics from classical Structure-from-Motion (SfM) pipelines like COLMAP [32]. How-ever, SfM preprocessing is computationally expensive, sen-sitive to rapid camera motion, and prone to failure in texture-less or low-overlap regions, hindering the practical deployment of 3DGS in real-time or streaming scenarios [11, 13, 22]. Recent efforts explore pose-free reconstruction paradigms that estimate camera trajectories and scene geometry directly from unposed image sequences [5, 19, 21, 40]. Initial implicit methods [14, 43, 46], such as BARF [20] and Nope-NeRF [2], pioneered this direction by fitting coordinate-based Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) [25] alongside camera frame parameters. Leveraging the explicit representation and fast rasterization of Gaussians, frameworks like CF-3DGS [9] adapted this 1arXiv:2610.28190v1 [cs.CV] 20 Oct 2026 concept into a sequential regime by estimating relative poses with local Gaussian models while dynamically expanding a global scene representation. Follow-up studies improve complementary components of this pipeline by enhancing Gaussian coverage through hierarchical merging [15], refining local pose estimation via feature and frequency regularization [44], and introducing global trajectory constraints using long-range feature tracks [33]. Despite these empirical advances, existing progressive paradigms fundamentally struggle to reconcile local track-ing tractability with global geometric consistency. Standard sequential frameworks estimate pairwise relative transfor-mations from local Gaussian representations and compose them into a global trajectory [9, 15, 44]. Although this lo-calized formulation makes pose tracking tractable, residual pairwise errors can accumulate along the sequence and are absorbed by the global scene representation [12, 24]. Spa-tially flexible Gaussians can deform to accommodate these registration offsets, yielding deceptively low training-view reconstruction errors despite inaccurate camera trajectories [13, 18]. As additional frames are incorporated, the evolv-ing representation is repeatedly optimized to explain new observations under accumulated pose errors, causing ge-ometric distortion to propagate throughout the reconstruc-tion. While scene-level pose refinement offers a theoretical remedy for such cumulative drift, optimizing a camera pose against a scene representation trained on that very same ob-servation creates a self-explanation shortcut [27, 31, 37]. Unresolved, this underlying dilemma often leads existing methodologies to focus heavily on refining initial pose es-timates, rather than addressing systemic trajectory drift at its root. We highlight this self-explanation loop as a critical bottleneck in progressive camera-free reconstruction. To overcome this fundamental limitation, we propose CrossFit-GS, a progressive pose-free reconstruction frame-work built on a deferred-commitment protocol. Centered on dual-map information-flow isolation, CrossFit-GS strictly decouples camera pose refinement from scene map up-dates for unposed image streams. Specifically, the input sequence is partitioned into two interleaved temporal sub-sets ( A/B folds), each maintaining an independent Gaus-sian submap. For an incoming frame, a candidate pose proposal is generated from the preceding motion estimate and cross-refined exclusively against the frozen opposite submap. To improve optimization stability, this cross-map pose refinement is strictly regulated by checkerboard val-idation masks and spatial coverage gates. Following our deferred-commitment principle, candidate pose updates are committed to the global trajectory and integrated into owner submap updates only after passing these validation checks. Once all camera extrinsics across the sequence are verified and frozen, the intermediate submaps are discarded, and a single global Gaussian representation is initialized and trained from scratch under the fixed trajectory. Through these coordinated strategies, CrossFit-GS effectively breaks the self-reinforcing pose–map error propagation loop, sub-stantially mitigating cumulative trajectory drift while yield-ing highly accurate camera estimates and superior novel-view rendering quality. Our contributions are summarized as follows: • We diagnose a fundamental bottleneck in COLMAP-free 3DGS, highlighting how accumulated pairwise er-rors distort the evolving scene representation and how reusing this pose-contaminated map for scene-level re-finement creates a self-explanation shortcut that closes a self-reinforcing pose–map fitting loop. • We design a deferred-commitment progressive frame-work, CrossFit-GS, that prevents error propagation and suppresses trajectory drift by explicitly decoupling pose refinement from scene reconstruction through dual-map information isolation and cross-validation safeguards. • Extensive experiments validate that CrossFit-GS signifi-cantly suppresses trajectory drift, achieving state-of-the-art camera tracking and view synthesis. 

# 2. Related Work 

2.1. Generalizable Feed-Forward Methods 

One line of research addresses unposed sparse-view re-construction using generalizable models [7, 39] learned from large-scale multi-view data [3, 8, 12, 35, 38, 48]. FreeSplatter [45] predicts pixel-aligned Gaussian primitives in a shared reference frame and recovers camera parame-ters from the predicted geometry. SPFSplat [12] jointly pre-dicts camera poses and 3D Gaussians from unposed sparse views in a single feed-forward pass without pose supervi-sion. Related approaches combine learned multi-view geo-metric priors with per-scene optimization. InstantSplat [6], for example, initializes camera poses and dense geometry using a pretrained multi-view model and subsequently re-fines the scene through Gaussian bundle adjustment. These methods are primarily designed for rapid recon-struction from a small set of sparse, potentially unordered views. Their learned priors provide strong geometric ini-tialization but introduce dependence on the training distri-bution and are not specifically formulated for incremen-tally processing long, temporally ordered sequences. Our work instead considers per-scene progressive reconstruc-tion, where camera poses must remain globally consistent as observations are incorporated throughout an extended se-quence.

# 4. Experiments 

We evaluate CrossFit-GS on challenging unposed bench-marks to assess both novel view synthesis fidelity and cam-era pose recovery accuracy. 

4.1. Experimental Setup 

Datasets. Following previous benchmark evaluation proto-cols [9, 15, 33, 44], we evaluate on Tanks and Temples [17] using a frame-subsampling stride of 5 to simulate challeng-ing wide-baseline camera motion. Additionally, we bench-mark on CO3D-V2 [30] across representative scenes fea-turing diverse geometric and textural complexities. In line with standard evaluation setups, every 8th frame is held out as test views for novel view synthesis. 

Metrics. Novel view synthesis is evaluated using PSNR, SSIM [42], and LPIPS [50]. Camera trajectory estimation is aligned via Sim(3) Umeyama alignment and evaluated using Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) as well as Relative Pose Errors for translation (RPE t) and rotation (RPE r ) [2, 20]. 

Implementation Details. Training sequences are split into alternating A/B folds ( i mod 2 ). Local relative regis-tration uses 1,000 iterations for reference view fitting and 

300 for target tracking. Cross-map pose optimization uses Adam ( 1.0 × 10 −3) for up to 100 iterations, gated by 20% 

initial alpha coverage and checkerboard-masked validation. A pose update is committed if validation loss improves by 

> 1.0 × 10 −5 while retaining ≥ 50% visible coverage. Frozen poses are then used to train a unified global Gaus-sian map for 500 × N steps from scratch.

Despite the additional cross-map refinement, CrossFit-GS remains close to CF-3DGS in runtime because it avoids replay during progressive estimation and trains the final global representation only once. 4.4.3. Diagnosing Target-to-Map Self-Explanation 

To directly test our core hypothesis, we evaluate four vari-ants across two orthogonal factors—whether the reference map incorporates the target view prior to refinement, and whether it remains frozen during optimization: Target-conditioned trainable (TCTM), Target-conditioned frozen (TCFM), Target-excluded trainable (TETM), and Target-excluded frozen (TEFM, ours). Table 6 reveals a clear mismatch between validation loss and downstream accuracy. Target-conditioned maps ob-tain extremely low losses, but freezing them barely changes ATE or PSNR because the target has already been ab-sorbed. Target exclusion alone is likewise insufficient when the map can still adapt during pose optimization. Our target-excluded frozen variant instead improves PSNR by 2.457 dB and reduces ATE by 63.0% over the target-conditioned frozen variant, despite having a substantially higher validation loss. This inversion provides direct evi-dence of target-to-map self-explanation. 

# 5. Conclusion 

We presented CrossFit-GS, a deferred-commitment frame-work designed for COLMAP-free 3DGS. By enforcing strict observation isolation and refining each candidate pose against a frozen out-of-fold submap, our approach effectively eliminates the target-to-map self-explanation loop and prevents pose errors from being absorbed into Gaussian primitives. Experiments on benchmarks demon-strate that CrossFit-GS achieves substantial improvements in both global trajectory accuracy and novel-view ren-dering.
