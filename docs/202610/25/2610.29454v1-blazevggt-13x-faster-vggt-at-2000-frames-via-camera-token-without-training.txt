Abstract —Feed-forward visual geometry models, exemplified by VGGT, have demonstrated strong multi-view reconstruction capabilities through dense global attention. However, the result-ing all-to-all cross-view computation scales quadratically with the number of input views, making long-sequence inference par-ticularly expensive due to substantial frame-level redundancy among highly overlapping observations. We uncover a topology-first, geometry-later evolution in VGGT camera tokens: shallow-layer tokens reliably capture local view neighborhoods, whereas deeper-layer tokens progressively encode globally organized inter-view geometry. Motivated by this observation, we intro-duce BlazeVGGT, a training-free framework for structure-aware frame-level key–value sparsification. At each global-attention block, BlazeVGGT constructs a view-level similarity kernel from camera-token relations and employs greedy de-terminantal point process inference to select a compact set of mutually complementary views. The selected views constitute the key–value context, while all input frames are retained as queries. This asymmetric attention design preserves dense per-frame predictions while eliminating redundant cross-view computation, allowing our method to be directly integrated into pretrained VGGT as a plug-and-play module without param-eter updates. Its advantages become increasingly pronounced with sequence length: at 2,000 frames, BlazeVGGT achieves approximately 13 × faster inference than VGGT while reducing the Chamfer Distance from 0.41 to 0.32. I. I NTRODUCTION 

Feed-forward visual geometry models have recently emerged as powerful alternatives to conventional multi-stage reconstruction pipelines. In particular, VGGT [1] jointly esti-mates camera parameters, depth maps, point maps, and point tracks from an arbitrary collection of input views within a single feed-forward network. Its strong multi-view reasoning capability is largely enabled by global attention, which facil-itates information exchange across all input views. However, the resulting all-to-all cross-view computation scales unfa-vorably with the number of input frames. Given S views and 

P tokens per view, each global-attention block operates over 

SP tokens and incurs a dominant computational complexity of O(S2P 2C), where C denotes the feature dimension. This quadratic dependence on S becomes prohibitively expensive for long image sequences, which commonly contain substan-tial redundancy among highly overlapping observations. Existing VGGT acceleration methods [4]–[10] primarily reduce the global-attention workload by compressing to-ken or KV representations, or sparsifying global attention. Although effective, these approaches retain several limi-tations. Some methods [5]–[7] require additional training, fine-tuning, or input-specific optimization, restricting their arXiv:2610.29454v1 [cs.CV] 21 Oct 2026 UNDER REVIEW 2

Training-free alter-natives [4], [8]–[10] generally operate at the granularity of in-dividual tokens, token descriptors, attention heads, or sparse attention blocks, without explicitly modeling the structured redundancy among complete views. Consequently, multiple near-duplicate frames may continue to serve as cross-view information sources even after their spatial representations have been compressed. Moreover, aggressive token-level reduction may fragment the coherent intra-frame evidence required for geometric reasoning. These limitations motivate a complementary perspective: rather than determining which individual tokens should be retained, we ask which views are necessary to provide a representative and geometrically complementary global context. This view-level perspective is particularly relevant to multi-view geometry, where redundancy exhibits a strong frame-wise structure. Adjacent views frequently capture nearly identical scene content, whereas a small number of geometrically distinct viewpoints may provide substantially more complementary evidence. Naive frame sampling, how-ever, can overlook geometrically important observations and fail to represent the global scene structure. An effective view-selection mechanism must therefore preserve each selected frame as a complete unit while identifying a compact subset that jointly covers the input view distribution. As illustrated in Fig. 2(a), the input views densely populate the camera-token space with considerable redundancy, whereas a compact sub-set can span its underlying view structure. The key challenge is to identify such representative views during inference, particularly in shallow layers where reliable camera geometry has not yet been recovered. To determine whether the intermediate representations of VGGT provide an effective signal for view selection, we sys-tematically analyze the relational structure encoded by cam-era tokens at every global-attention block and compare it with ground-truth camera geometry. As shown in Fig. 2(b), camera tokens exhibit a characteristic topology-first, geometry-later evolution. Even in shallow layers, they achieve high Near AUC and Recall@10, indicating that local view neighbor-hoods are already reliably preserved. At the same time, their rank correlation with global camera geometry remains limited: shallow camera tokens can distinguish nearby views from non-neighboring ones but do not yet accurately encode their global arrangement. Around the middle of the network, this behavior changes markedly. The camera-token similar-ity structure becomes increasingly consistent with global view geometry, particularly with respect to camera rotation, whereas metric translation remains less precisely calibrated. These observations suggest that camera tokens progressively evolve from neighborhood-aware frame descriptors into task-relevant geometric representations, rather than explicitly re-covering complete metric 6-DoF camera poses. This progressive representation is particularly well aligned with the requirements of frame-level attention sparsification. In shallow layers, accurate global geometry is unnecessary for redundancy suppression: identifying that multiple views belong to the same local neighborhood is already sufficient to avoid repeatedly attending to nearly duplicate observations. In deeper layers, where cross-view reasoning benefits from a more globally representative context, camera tokens have developed a substantially more organized inter-view geome-try. We therefore arrive at a simple principle: local topology is sufficient for redundancy suppression, whereas global geometry promotes scene-wide view coverage. Importantly, both forms of structure emerge directly from the camera tokens of the pretrained network, eliminating the need for an external pose estimator or an additional feature encoder. Motivated by these observations, we introduce BlazeVGGT, a training-free framework for structure-aware frame-level key–value (KV) sparsification. At each global-attention block, BlazeVGGT constructs a view-level similarity kernel from the evolving relations among camera tokens and employs greedy determinantal point process (DPP) selection [2] to identify a compact set of mutually complementary views. Unlike independent view ranking, the DPP criterion jointly accounts for redundancy and diversity within the selected set. As camera-token representations evolve across layers, the same selection mechanism naturally transitions from suppressing local view redundancy in shallow layers to promoting broader scene coverage in deeper layers. The selected views are used exclusively to construct the KV context, while all input frames are retained in the query path. Once a view is selected, its complete token block is preserved, thereby maintaining the coherent intra-frame spatial evidence required for geometric reasoning. This query–KV asymmetry preserves predictions for every input UNDER REVIEW 3

frame while substantially reducing redundant cross-view computation. Specifically, if Kℓ ≪ S views are selected at the ℓ-th global-attention block, the dominant attention complexity is reduced from O(S2P 2C) to O(SK ℓP 2C).Because BlazeVGGT modifies only the routing of the KV context and introduces no learnable parameters, it can be directly integrated into pretrained VGGT models as a plug-and-play module without retraining. Extensive experiments demonstrate that BlazeVGGT achieves a favorable accuracy–efficiency trade-off, with in-creasingly pronounced gains as the sequence length grows. A. 3D Reconstruction 

Classical 3D reconstruction estimates scene structure and camera motion through feature correspondence, geometric estimation, and global optimization. Structure-from-motion (SfM) pipelines recover sparse geometry using matching, triangulation, and bundle adjustment [13]–[19]. Their cor-respondence front ends have evolved from handcrafted de-scriptors and dense matching [20]–[22] to learned descrip-tors [23]–[26], learned matching [27]–[32], and featuremet-ric refinement [33], while classical solvers and registration remain fundamental [34]–[39]. Dense multi-view stereo com-plements SfM through traditional patch-based methods [40]– [44] and learned cost-volume formulations [45]–[49], al-though sparse wide-baseline and large-scale settings remain challenging [50], [51]. Neural rendering methods such as NeRF [52] and 3D Gaussian Splatting [53] improve novel-view synthesis but typically require per-scene optimization. Direct and learned SfM systems [54]–[60], together with non-rigid formulations [61], further extend this paradigm, but generally retain iterative, pairwise, or sequential processing. 

B. Feed-Forward Visual Geometry Models 

Feed-forward visual geometry models replace explicit per-scene optimization with learned geometric priors. DUSt3R [62] predicts aligned point maps from image pairs, while MASt3R [63] incorporates metric-aware match-ing. Fast3R [64], Spann3R [65], and CUT3R [66] extend this paradigm to multi-view and persistent reconstruction. VGGT [1] instead jointly infers cameras, depth, point maps, and tracks by alternating frame-wise and global attention, enabling direct interaction across the entire image collec-tion. StreamVGGT [11] adapts this architecture to contin-uous video, while VGGT-Long [67], IncVGGT [68], and MERG3R [69] address long-range reconstruction through chunking, incremental processing, or divide-and-conquer. Nevertheless, benchmarks such as KITTI-360 [70] highlight the continuing need for scalable global reasoning over large image collections. 

C. Efficient VGGT 

Efficient VGGT methods can be broadly divided accord-ing to whether they require adaptation of the pretrained model. Optimization-dependent approaches include VGG-T3 [7], which compresses variable-length KV representa-tions through test-time training; LiteVGGT [5], which fine-tunes the model for geometry-aware token merging; and Co-Me [6], which trains an auxiliary merging controller. Although differing in where adaptation occurs, these methods require either offline training or input-specific optimiza-tion. Training-free methods instead modify inference while preserving the pretrained parameters. FastVGGT [4] and HTTM [8] reduce redundancy through global or head-wise token merging, whereas SparseVGGT [9] adopts adaptive block-sparse attention. Related approaches use descriptor compression, sensitivity-aware sparsity allocation, or re-designed global attention [10], [71], [72]. These methods primarily operate on spatial tokens, attention heads, or con-nectivity patterns. BlazeVGGT is likewise training-free but targets redundancy among complete views, restricting the KV context to geometrically complementary frames while retain-ing all input queries and the full token block of each selected view. Its view-level operation is therefore complementary to token merging, sparse attention, and model adaptation. III. M ETHODOLOGY

B. Our Method 

BlazeVGGT exploits the progressively emerging inter-view structure of VGGT camera tokens to reduce redun-dant cross-view computation. As illustrated in Fig. 3, our framework consists of two simple steps. First, we construct a view-level similarity kernel from camera tokens and select a compact set of mutually complementary views using a determinantal objective. Second, we retain all input frames as Queries while restricting the KV context of global attention to the selected views. The entire procedure is training-free and operates directly on the intermediate representations of a pretrained VGGT. 

Camera-Token Guided View Selection. Our analysis in Sec. I shows that pairwise relations among camera tokens provide a progressively richer representation of inter-view structure. Rather than decoding explicit camera poses, BlazeVGGT directly uses this relational structure to determine which views provide redundant or complementary evidence. IV. E XPERIMENTS 

A. Experimental Setup 

Experimental Protocol. Unless otherwise specified, VGGT in this section denotes the VRAM-efficient implementation of VGGT [1] used in our experiments, enabling larger inputs. For the 2,000-frame comparison, following the long-sequence implementation of VGG-T 3 [7], we adapt the A800 evaluation implementations of VGGT, FastVGGT [4], SparseVGGT [9], LiteVGGT [5], VGG-T 3, and BlazeVGGT to support inference on two NVIDIA A800-SXM4-80GB GPUs connected by NVLink, while preserving their respec-tive algorithmic configurations. All remaining experiments are conducted on a single NVIDIA A800 GPU, except that LiteVGGT-related experiments outside the 2,000-frame comparison and high-frame-count PAGE-4D experiments are conducted on an NVIDIA H20 GPU. 

Datasets and Evaluation Scope. Our evaluation consists of main experiments and two complementary generalization studies. The main experiments compare BlazeVGGT with state-of-the-art methods for point cloud reconstruction on ScanNet-50 [3], 7 Scenes [75], and NRGBD [76], and for camera-pose estimation on ScanNet-50 under multiple input lengths. They further include ablations on ScanNet-50 to isolate the effects of the selection threshold, the Top-K safety constraint, and the activated layer range. Complementarily, we examine generalization in two directions: (1) seamless UNDER REVIEW 7

composition with existing VGGT accelerators on ScanNet-50 and Tanks & Temples [77], and (2) direct transfer to other VGGT-based models—StreamVGGT [11] for online recon-struction on 7 Scenes and PAGE-4D [12] for video-depth estimation on Sintel [78], Bonn [79], and DyCheck [80]. 

B. Comparison with State-of-the-Art 

Quantitative Comparison. We compare BlazeVGGT with VGGT [1] and four recent variants, including the training-dependent LiteVGGT [5] and VGG-T 3 [7] and the training-free FastVGGT [4] and SparseVGGT [9]. Evaluation covers point-cloud reconstruction and camera-pose estimation, with an emphasis on long-sequence inference. 

(i) Point Cloud Reconstruction. TABLES I and II demonstrate that inference efficiency is the most prominent advantage of BlazeVGGT. On ScanNet-50, BlazeVGGT achieves the shortest inference time among all compared methods at 100, 500, and 1,000 views, and remains the fastest training-free method at 2,000 views. Moreover, its speedup over VGGT grows rapidly as the input sequence becomes longer, reaching 14.0 × at 1,000 views and remaining above 13 × under two-GPU inference at 2,000 views. Although the trained VGG-T 3 is marginally faster in the 2,000-view setting, it produces substantially less accurate reconstructions, high-lighting the favorable quality–efficiency trade-off achieved by BlazeVGGT without training or input-specific optimization. This efficiency advantage is also accompanied by competi-tive reconstruction quality: compared with the trained vari-ants, BlazeVGGT performs particularly well in the longest-sequence setting, whereas LiteVGGT retains an advantage on several shorter-sequence and surface-normal metrics. More directly, among the training-free methods, BlazeVGGT achieves the best or near-best distance-based reconstruction accuracy across the evaluated sequence lengths while con-sistently requiring less inference time than FastVGGT and SparseVGGT, with the efficiency gap becoming especially pronounced for long sequences. Furthermore, the same be-havior generalizes beyond ScanNet-50: on both 7 Scenes and NRGBD, BlazeVGGT records the lowest reported inference time while maintaining strong geometric accuracy and com-pleteness. SparseVGGT obtains higher normal consistency in some settings; nevertheless, the overall results show that BlazeVGGT substantially reduces the dominant cost of cross-view attention without sacrificing competitive reconstruction quality. Taken together, these results confirm that view-level KV sparsification is particularly effective for long and highly redundant image sequences, where it provides increasingly substantial computational savings while preserving the geo-metric evidence required for dense reconstruction. 

(ii) Camera Pose Estimation. Having established the re-construction efficiency of BlazeVGGT, we further examine whether its compact KV context preserves the global geo-metric reasoning required for camera pose estimation. TA-BLE III shows that BlazeVGGT remains highly competitive across different sequence lengths. Most notably, at 1,000 views, it achieves the lowest ATE, ARE, RPE-t, and RPE-r among all compared methods, indicating that a compact set of complementary KV views can retain sufficient global context for accurate trajectory estimation. Compared with the trained variants, BlazeVGGT outperforms both LiteVGGT and VGG-T 3 across all four pose metrics at 500 and 1,000 views, despite requiring neither additional training nor input-specific optimization. Within the more directly comparable training-free group, BlazeVGGT achieves the best overall pose accuracy at both 1,000 and 2,000 views and remains close to FastVGGT at 500 views. Taken together with the substantial runtime reductions reported above, these results show that BlazeVGGT can preserve competitive global pose reasoning while greatly improving long-sequence inference efficiency.

C. Ablation Studies 

TABLE IV analyzes three design choices of BlazeVGGT: the diversity threshold ϵ, the Top-K constraint, and the range of global-attention blocks to which view selection is applied. 

Selection Threshold. The diversity threshold ϵ controls the aggressiveness of determinantal view selection and therefore the trade-off between reconstruction and pose metrics. Rel-ative to the baseline, ϵ = 0 .20 reduces CD from 0.4375 to 0.4109, while increasing ATE from 0.0920 to 0.1032. The lowest CD in this ablation is obtained at ϵ = 0 .35 (0.4011), but its ATE, ARE, RPE-t, and RPE-r are all higher than at 

ϵ = 0 .20 . Increasing ϵ beyond 0.20 does not monotonically improve CD: it worsens at ϵ = 0 .25 and again at ϵ = 0 .40 

relative to their preceding settings, while the pose errors increase overall. We therefore use ϵ = 0 .20 as a balanced default rather than as the optimum of any individual metric. 

Top-K Constraint. We next study whether the explicit upper bound on the number of selected views affects predic-tion quality. Enabling or removing Top-K results in nearly identical CD and camera-pose metrics, showing that the performance gains of BlazeVGGT primarily arise from the diversity-driven selection criterion rather than from a fixed cardinality constraint. The role of Top-K is therefore mainly practical: it acts as a safety ceiling for blocks in which the adaptive criterion would otherwise retain an unusually large KV set, while the effective number of selected views remains determined by the camera-token similarity structure. We retain this constraint in the default configuration to make the computational budget more predictable without materially altering reconstruction or pose accuracy. 

Layer Range. Finally, we vary the global-attention blocks in which BlazeVGGT is activated. Applying view selection only to the deepest blocks leaves the reconstruction result close to the unsparsified baseline, whereas progressively extending sparsification toward earlier blocks consistently improves CD. This observation is closely aligned with the layer-wise analysis in Fig. 2. D. Compatibility with Existing VGGT Accelerators 

By targeting view-level redundancy without modifying pretrained weights, BlazeVGGT is complementary to other VGGT acceleration mechanisms. We evaluate this prop-erty by directly combining it with FastVGGT [4] and LiteVGGT [5], without retraining. 

Point Cloud Reconstruction. TABLES V and VI demon-strate consistent compositional behavior on both ScanNet-50 and Tanks & Temples [77]. On ScanNet-50, adding BlazeVGGT to either FastVGGT or LiteVGGT provides sub-stantial additional acceleration in the long-sequence regime while generally improving reconstruction quality. The gains are particularly clear at 1,000 frames, where both composed variants achieve lower CD and considerably shorter runtime than their corresponding backbones. The same overall be-havior transfers to Tanks & Temples. BlazeVGGT provides further speedups when composed with both FastVGGT and LiteVGGT while leaving their average F1 scores nearly unchanged across the two evaluation thresholds. The consis-tency across ScanNet-50 and Tanks & Temples indicates that frame-level sparsification can serve as a plug-and-play com-plement to existing VGGT acceleration mechanisms rather than being tied to a particular accelerated backbone. 

Camera Pose Estimation. TABLE VII shows a similar long-context trend for camera pose estimation on ScanNet-50. At 1,000 frames, integrating BlazeVGGT improves the absolute pose errors of FastVGGT and improves all reported pose metrics for LiteVGGT, while substantially reducing their reconstruction-time cost as shown above. For shorter sequences, the additional sparsification can introduce modest degradation on some pose metrics, particularly when the underlying accelerator has already compressed the represen-tation. These results suggest that different acceleration mech-anisms provide largely complementary benefits in the target regime of BlazeVGGT, namely long image sequences with substantial redundancy. When the input context is already small, however, combining multiple aggressive acceleration strategies does not necessarily produce consistent improve-ments across all metrics. 

E. Generalization to VGGT-based Models 

Beyond its compatibility with VGGT accelerators, we evaluate the transferability of view-level sparsification to StreamVGGT for streaming reconstruction and PAGE-4D for video geometry. In both settings, BlazeVGGT is integrated without retraining the underlying model. 

Online Reconstruction with StreamVGGT. The kernel scale in the original BlazeVGGT depends on the median of pair-wise descriptor distances over the entire sequence. Since single-pass streaming inference cannot access future obser-vations at the current time step, this full-sequence statistic cannot be reproduced exactly. To establish a streaming base-line that does not rely on scene priors, we fix the kernel scale to its default value; all StreamVGGT + BlazeVGGT experiments and visualizations in this paper use this setting. Building on this baseline, we recommend adopting a dual-memory design: a short-term FIFO memory retains recent observations and local temporal context, while a determi-nantal criterion maintains the long-term KV history. When a frame leaves the FIFO memory and is considered for insertion into long-term memory, the kernel scale is computed online using only the descriptors of the current long-term memory and the candidate frame. This allows the kernel to adapt to the structure and redundancy of the scene observed so far, without access to future frames or priors about the entire scene. V. C ONCLUSION 

In this paper, we identify frame-level redundancy as a key bottleneck in VGGT and introduce BlazeVGGT, a training-free method that uses evolving camera-token relations to select complementary views for KV sparsification while retaining all queries. Experiments show substantial efficiency gains on long sequences with competitive geometric accu-racy, alongside compatibility with existing accelerators and VGGT-based models.
