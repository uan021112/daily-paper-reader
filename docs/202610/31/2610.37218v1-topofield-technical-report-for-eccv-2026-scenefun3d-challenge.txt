Abstract. Functional segmentation requires locating small interactive
parts, such as knobs, buttons, and sockets, in indoor point clouds and re-
turning confidence-ranked instance masks. A point-wise semantic predic-
tor produces a continuous probability field, whereas the benchmark eval-
uates a discrete set of scored instances. This creates a substantial field-to-
instance gap. We present TopoField, a deterministic decoder built on
top of VOLT. TopoField first converts 13 test-time forward passes into a
semantic consensus field, then decodes instance candidates from the con-
sensus geometry, and finally ranks the candidates in a class-aware man-
ner. The augmentation stage retains the processed-point identity of every
fragment prediction, accumulates probabilities in a common point array,
normalizes by coverage count, and fuses the resulting caches. The decod-
ing stage separates high-confidence cores from broader support regions,
constructs a symmetric radius-pruned point graph, and uses a multi-
source graph-geodesic partition to split support regions that contain mul-
tiple seeds. On 561 ground-truth instances across 30 validation scenes, an
IoU-0.5-valid candidate exists for 67.2% of instances, and top-1 coverage
is 20.0%. These complementary measurements motivate joint attention
to candidate construction and ranking. TopoField achieves 28.286% AP,
41.383% AP50, and 47.558% AP25. TopoField achieved second place
on the ECCV 2026 SceneFun3D Challenge.

Introduction
SceneFun3D [1] asks a model to locate nine categories of functional interaction
elements, such as knobs, buttons, handles, and sockets, in complete indoor laser
scans. Each prediction requires a category, a point mask, and a confidence score.

These elements occupy only a small portion of their host objects, and adjacent
elements can share material, color, and local geometry. The task therefore re-
quires not only recognizing an interactable object, but also recovering the spatial
extent of the part that affords an action. Related efforts emphasize fine-grained
video understanding [2], region-focused visual representations [3], and entity-
level alignment in composed retrieval [4–6].
Two coupled difficulties arise after a point-wise semantic predictor such as
VOLT [7]. Small and partly occluded parts make per-point probabilities noisy:
a high threshold truncates weak boundaries, whereas a low threshold can merge
the target with its host or a neighbor. In addition, the predictor returns a contin-
uous probability field, while the benchmark evaluates discrete masks sorted by
confidence. Even with reasonable semantics, proposal construction and ordering
can therefore cause a large field-to-instance error.
We quantify this gap on 30 validation scenes containing 561 evaluable in-
stances. The examined pool has an IoU-0.5-valid candidate for 67.2% of the
instances, and top-1 coverage is 20.0%. These complementary statistics charac-
terize candidate-pool coverage and the overlap achieved by top-ranked candi-
dates.
We present TopoField, whose method follows the two stages in Fig. 1.
Stage 1, TTA-based Semantic Consensus, applies the shared predictor to
13 test-time forward passes over 12 distinct rotation–scale outcomes. Each frag-
ment retains a processed-point index that scatters its softmax output into a
common point-by-class array; accumulated probabilities are divided by cover-
age count and the independent caches are renormalized and fused. Stage 2,
Geometry-aware Instance Decoding, selects a class-specific operator from
argmax connectivity, threshold-support connectivity, or a seeded graph basin.
The basin branch separates high-confidence cores from broader supports, builds a
symmetric radius-pruned 32-nearest-neighbor graph, and assigns support points
to seed components by accumulated graph-path length. Candidates are then
scored and suppressed class by class in the original scan point order.
Our contributions are as follows.
– We formulate the gap between point-wise semantics and the benchmark’s
ranked instance-set output, and quantify it through complementary any-
candidate and top-k coverage statistics.
– We organize the system as two explicit stages that mirror its implementation:
index-aligned test-time semantic consensus and geometry-aware instance de-
coding. The first stage makes point identity and cache fusion explicit; the
second exposes core selection, graph construction, geodesic seed assignment,
and candidate ranking.
– The parameter-free decoder achieves 28.286% AP, 41.383% AP50, and 47.558%
AP25.

Related Work
3D instance segmentation. PointGroup [8] groups point-wise semantics with
center offsets in two coordinate spaces, while SoftGroup [9] reduces hard-label
error propagation with soft semantic grouping. Query-based methods such as
Mask3D [10] and SPFormer [11] learn instance or superpoint queries and directly
predict masks. These methods jointly learn representation, grouping, and refine-
ment [12, 13]. In visual tracking, complementary approaches explore window-
based attention [14], correlative masked modeling [15], and query-gated feature
selection [16]. TopoField converts a fixed semantic predictor’s output into a
ranked instance set through deterministic grouping and confidence-based rank-
ing.
Functional interaction element segmentation. SceneFun3D [1] extends in-
door scene understanding from complete objects to functional parts such as
handles, knobs, and buttons. These parts are small, often partly occluded, and
geometrically similar to their neighbors [17,18]. Related work on composed video
retrieval models shared and differential semantics [19] and calibrates directional
anchors in composed representations [20]. VOLT [7] supplies the point-wise se-
mantics in our system; TopoField constructs and ranks instance proposals from
these semantic predictions. In video tracking, semantic consistency strengthens
point correspondence [21], while temporally coherent object flow supports multi-
frame object association [22].
Graph-based region partitioning. Watershed-like methods propagate labels
from seeds under a local adjacency structure [23]. A sparse point graph con-
strains propagation to 3D neighborhoods and prevents unreachable regions from
borrowing labels across empty space [24–26]. Graph-structured relations also
support heterogeneous nearest-neighbor retrieval [27] and collaborative motion
reasoning in multi-object tracking [28]. TopoField applies this idea to a class-
conditioned support: cores supply seeds, a radius-pruned symmetric kNN graph
defines paths, and multi-source shortest paths assign support points to seeds.
Connected components first isolate disconnected supports; geodesic propagation
then handles connected supports with several cores.
Test-time augmentation. Test-time augmentation reduces variance by trans-
forming the input and aggregating the corresponding outputs [29–32]. For per-
point prediction, point identity must be preserved across augmentation and test-
time voxelization. Stage 1 therefore scatters probabilities through retained point
indices, normalizes by coverage, and fuses the caches before Stage 2 consumes the
consensus field. Complementary evidence is also integrated through coupled mul-
timodal state-space models [33]. Egocentric video understanding systems further
explore category-conditioned inference and test-time consistency [34], as well as
capability-based reasoning routes [35].
Noise-aware visual matching. Reliable visual matching depends on identi-
fying task-relevant evidence and estimating the confidence of candidate associ-
ations. Research on noisy correspondence studies these issues in image–text re-
trieval, where training pairs can contain incorrect semantic associations. Huang
et al. [36] introduce the Noisy Correspondence Rectifier, which partitions training

3
Method
TopoField is a deterministic decoder after a point-wise predictor. Stage 1 sta-
bilizes the probability field and resolves index misalignment between augmented
inputs; Stage 2 converts the field into instance masks and scores without updat-
ing predictor parameters.

3.4
Implementation details
VOLT predicts at a 1 cm voxel resolution from XYZ, RGB, and normals. It has
12 transformer blocks, 768-dimensional embeddings, and 12 heads, initialized
from ScanNet200 weights. Training uses AdamW (3 × 10−4, weight decay 0.05),
label-smoothed cross-entropy, multiclass Lovász loss, and EMA (0.999). Test
inference uses frozen probability caches without gradient updates. Unless stated
otherwise, Stage 1 is fixed while Stage 2 is varied.

4.1
Setup
SceneFun3D [1] contains 160 training, 30 validation, and 30 test scenes over
nine categories. We train VOLT with XYZ, RGB, and normals at 1 cm. Stage 1
is used only at inference. The official metric is AP over IoU 0.50–0.95, with
AP50 and AP25 also reported. Rany is the fraction of ground-truth instances
whose candidate pool contains an IoU-0.5-valid mask; Rk restricts this test to
the class-wise top-k candidates.

4.4
Discussion
Decoder components. The successive configurations in Table 2 increase development-
split AP by 1.68 points with support/core selection, 0.71 points with graph
geodesic partitioning, and 0.85 points with class-conditioned decoding. Together,
these components raise full-validation AP from 36.58 to 39.67.
Method behavior. The two stages perform complementary operations.
Stage 1 aggregates index-aligned point predictions into a semantic consensus
field. Stage 2 constructs and ranks instance masks using class-conditioned con-
nectivity and graph-geodesic partitioning. Support/core selection retains bound-
ary evidence and supplies confident seeds, while geodesic assignment partitions
multi-core support regions according to their 3D connectivity.
5
Conclusion
We presented TopoField, a two-stage deterministic decoder for functional inter-
action element segmentation. Stage 1 turns multiple test-time forward passes

into a stable semantic field by retaining processed-point indices, accumulating
softmax probabilities in a common point array, normalizing repeated fragment
contributions by coverage, and fusing independent caches. Stage 2 uses class-
conditioned instance decoding; its seeded graph-basin branch separates a high-
confidence core from a broader support region, constrains propagation with a
symmetric radius-pruned point graph, and assigns support points to seed com-
ponents by graph-geodesic distance. The validation analysis characterizes candi-
date coverage and ranking, and the combined decoder achieves 39.67% validation
AP and 28.286% official-test AP. The decoder adds no learnable parameters to
the point-wise predictor.
