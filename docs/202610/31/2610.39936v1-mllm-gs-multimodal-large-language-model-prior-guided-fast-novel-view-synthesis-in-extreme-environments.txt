Abstract
Novel view synthesis (NVS) in degraded scenes aims to reconstruct 3D scenes from multi-view obser-
vations affected by various degradations, and to generate clear and photorealistic images at unseen
viewpoints. Despite recent advances, existing methods often rely on degradation-specific models,
limiting their generalization across diverse extreme environments. Generative models provide rich
visual priors, but their outputs often suffer from pixel misalignment and color deviations, which can
propagate into the reconstructed 3D scene and compromise reconstruction quality. Furthermore, the
high computational and memory costs of 3D reconstruction hinder the practical application of NVS
in extreme environments. To this end, we propose MLLM-GS, a multimodal large language model
(MLLM) prior-guided framework for fast NVS in extreme environments. MLLM-GS consists of two
branches: MLLM prior-guided view enhancement and fast 3D scene reconstruction. The former lever-
ages the rich visual priors of a frozen MLLM to enhance views under different severe degradations
and introduces a lightweight correction network to reduce pixel shifts and color distortions in MLLM
outputs. With supervision from only a few paired clean views, this branch produces reliable enhanced
views for all degraded observations, providing pseudo-GT views for subsequent 3D reconstruction. The
latter improves 3DGS efficiency by optimizing rasterization, Gaussian memory layout, and parameter
updates, reducing redundant computation, memory-access overhead, and optimization costs. Exten-
sive experiments on multiple degraded scenes demonstrate that MLLM-GS effectively handles various
severe degradations and achieves high-quality NVS with significantly lower reconstruction time and
memory consumption. Project Page: https://MLLM-GS.github.io.
Keywords: Novel view synthesis, Degraded scenes, 3D Gaussian Splatting, Multimodal large language model
1 Introduction
Novel view synthesis (NVS) (Yu et al. 2026b; Liu
et al. 2026b; Li et al. 2025b) aims to model a 3D
scene from existing multi-view observations and
generate photorealistic images at unseen camera
viewpoints. It requires not only accurate scene
geometry and appearance, but also spatial con-
sistency and high visual quality across different
views (Bao et al. 2026; Guo et al. 2025). By
enabling 3D scene modeling and free-viewpoint

rendering, NVS supports a wide range of appli-
cations, including virtual reality (Yu et al. 2024),
digital twins (Plizzari et al. 2024), robotic percep-
tion (Lei et al. 2025), autonomous driving (Zhou
et al. 2026a), and digital content creation (Dai
et al. 2025). With the rapid advancement of
immersive visual applications and 3D intelligence,
NVS has become an important topic in computer
vision and computer graphics (Gao et al. 2026b;
Yu et al. 2026b).
Early NVS methods synthesize novel views by
image interpolation (Chen and Williams 1993),
view reprojection (Seitz and Dyer 1996), and
explicit geometric reconstruction (Debevec et al.
1996). Such pipelines often rely on accurate cam-
era poses, cross-view correspondences, and scene
geometry. As a result, they can produce noticeable
rendering errors under large viewpoint variations,
complex occlusions, or inaccurate geometry esti-
mation. With the development of deep learning,
researchers began to learn continuous scene rep-
resentations with neural networks, reducing the
dependence on explicit geometric reconstruction.
Among these approaches, Neural Radiance Fields
(NeRF) (Mildenhall et al. 2021) implicitly models
the mapping from spatial positions and viewing
directions to color and density, and uses volume
rendering to synthesize high-quality novel views.
NeRF significantly improves the representation
capacity and rendering fidelity in complex scenes,
and many follow-up methods (Chen et al. 2023;
Liu et al. 2026c; Levy et al. 2023; Cui et al.
2024b) have been developed based on NeRF. How-
ever, NeRF requires dense sampling along camera
rays and a large number of network evaluations,
resulting in long optimization times and making
real-time rendering difficult.

In extreme environments involving smoke, low
light, motion blur, and exposure variations, image
structure, texture, and color information can be
severely degraded. Such degradations weaken the
visual features required for accurate geometry
estimation and appearance modeling, leading to
structural errors, missing textures, and color dis-
tortions in reconstructed scenes. As illustrated in
Fig. 1, existing NVS methods (Lin et al. 2025; Liu
et al. 2025a; Lee et al. 2024; Liu et al. 2025b) for
degraded scenes mainly address these problems by
introducing physical priors into the reconstruction
process. However, these methods typically rely on
degradation-specific imaging models and assump-
tions. As different degradations follow distinct
2


imaging mechanisms, such assumptions may not
hold under unknown or mixed degradation con-
ditions, limiting their generalization in extreme
environments. Consequently, existing approaches
struggle to achieve robust NVS across diverse
extreme environments within a unified framework.
An intuitive solution is to restore degraded
images
before
performing
3D
reconstruction.
However, conventional image restoration net-
works (Yue et al. 2025; Wang et al. 2026a; Wu
et al. 2026b; Long et al. 2026) are typically trained
on large-scale paired datasets and often specialize
in particular degradation types. Therefore, their
performance can be limited by variations in degra-
dation distributions or the lack of sufficient paired
clean images. This problem is particularly chal-
lenging in real-world extreme environments, where
acquiring clean observations corresponding to all
degraded viewpoints is often difficult. Recent gen-
erative models (Wei et al. 2026; Li et al. 2026;
Zhu et al. 2025; Jiang et al. 2026) offer a promis-
ing alternative by leveraging rich visual priors
learned from large-scale data to recover plausible
scene structures and textures under diverse degra-
dation conditions. Nevertheless, their generation
processes generally lack strict pixel-level con-
straints and may introduce spatial misalignment,
altered textures, and color deviations. Although
the restored images may appear visually realistic,
pixel misalignment and color deviations can make
them inconsistent with the underlying scene geom-
etry and appearance (Zhao et al. 2026; Yang et al.
2025b). When these images are used to supervise
3D reconstruction, such errors can propagate into
the learned 3D representation, leading to struc-
tural distortions and appearance inconsistencies in
novel-view renderings. Therefore, effective NVS in
extreme environments requires not only recovering
visually plausible content but also improving the
pixel-level fidelity of restored images under limited
clean-image supervision.
Beyond reconstruction quality, computational
efficiency remains another important challenge for
NVS in severely degraded scenes. Although view
enhancement improves the quality of degraded
observations, residual artifacts and cross-view
inconsistencies can still disrupt Gaussian opti-
mization and induce redundant densification (Lyu
et al. 2026; Zhou et al. 2026b). The resulting
increase in Gaussian primitives can further exac-
erbate the computational and memory overhead of
standard 3DGS (Kerbl et al. 2023). First, its con-
servative screen-space bounding regions include
many pixels receiving negligible Gaussian con-
tributions, resulting in unnecessary rasterization
operations. Second, its adaptive densification and
pruning can scatter spatially neighboring Gaus-
sians across memory, reducing memory-access
efficiency. Third, updating different Gaussian
attributes through separate optimization opera-
tions introduces additional kernel launches and
global memory accesses. Although recent accel-
eration methods (Ren et al. 2026; Hahlbohm
et al. 2026; Hanson et al. 2025a) have improved
3DGS efficiency, these strategies have mainly been
developed and evaluated using high-quality obser-
vations. Their effectiveness in degraded scenes
remains insufficiently explored. Therefore, achiev-
ing both high-quality reconstruction and low com-
putational cost under diverse severe degradations
remains challenging.
In this paper, we propose MLLM-GS, a multi-
modal large language model (MLLM) prior-guided
framework for fast NVS in extreme environments.
MLLM-GS consists of two branches: MLLM prior-
guided view enhancement and fast 3D scene
reconstruction. The former leverages the visual
priors of MLLMs to generate reliable pseudo-GT
views from degraded observations, thereby allevi-
ating the scarcity of reliable supervision for 3D
reconstruction. Specifically, we employ a frozen
MLLM to generate corresponding enhanced views
from degraded observations under diverse degra-
dation conditions. In this way, the framework
exploits the rich visual priors of the MLLM to
handle multiple degradations in a unified man-
ner, avoiding the need for degradation-specific
restoration models. Indeed, a similar prior-based
enhancement strategy was also validated in the
NTIRE 2026 3D Restoration and Reconstruction
Challenge (Liu et al. 2026a), where our Smoke-
GS (Zheng et al. 2026) solution achieved second
place in Track 2. However, the MLLM gener-
ation process lacks strict pixel-level constraints
and may introduce pixel shifts and color distor-
tions. To address this issue, we further design
a lightweight correction network. The network
refines the MLLM-enhanced results in a color
space that decouples luminance and chrominance,
enabling more effective correction of pixel mis-
alignment and color distortions. By leveraging the
pretrained visual priors of a frozen MLLM and
3


training only the lightweight correction network,
the view enhancement branch can be effectively
optimized using a few paired clean views. In this
way, the view enhancement branch reduces struc-
tural and color distortions in the restored images,
providing more reliable pseudo-GT supervision for
subsequent 3DGS optimization.
After obtaining reliable pseudo-GT views, we
further focus on reducing the computational and
memory overhead of 3DGS during scene recon-
struction. To this end, we design a fast 3D scene
reconstruction branch and accelerate the opti-
mization process from three aspects: rasterization,
memory layout, and parameter updates. Specifi-
cally, we adopt opacity-aware axis-aligned rectan-
gular bounding boxes to reduce unnecessary ras-
terization computations. We reorder active Gaus-
sians using Morton codes to improve memory-
access efficiency for spatially adjacent Gaussians,
and fuse the Adam updates of different parame-
ter groups into a single CUDA kernel to reduce
kernel launches and global memory access. With
these designs, MLLM-GS significantly improves
the optimization efficiency of 3DGS while main-
taining high reconstruction quality. Extensive
experiments demonstrate that MLLM-GS consis-
tently achieves high-quality NVS under diverse
degradations. Compared with baselines trained
for 30K iterations, MLLM-GS achieves superior
reconstruction quality with only 5K iterations,
reducing training time by approximately 9× while
consuming substantially less GPU memory.

combines MLLM prior-guided view enhance-

high-quality NVS across diverse severe degra-
dations using only a few paired clean views.
• We develop an MLLM prior-guided view
enhancement branch that leverages the visual
priors of a frozen MLLM to restore degraded
views.

2 Related Work
2.1 NVS in Degraded Scenes
3DGS (Fei et al. 2025; Ren et al. 2026; Zhang et al.
2024; Sabour et al. 2025; Wang et al. 2025; Lee
et al. 2026; Chen et al. 2025; Song et al. 2025;
Wang et al. 2026c) has become an effective repre-
sentation for 3D reconstruction and NVS, achiev-
ing high-quality rendering with relatively low com-
putational cost. However, standard 3DGS (Kerbl
et al. 2023) relies on reliable multi-view obser-
vations, and its performance can degrade sub-
stantially under severe imaging conditions, such
as smoke, low light, and motion blur (Liu et al.
2026a; Guo et al. 2026). To address this limitation,
recent studies (Yoon and Yoon 2026; Lin et al.
2025; Liu et al. 2025a; Lee et al. 2024; Yu et al.
2026b; Liu et al. 2025b; Yan et al. 2026) have
introduced physical priors into 3DGS to improve
reconstruction from degraded observations. For
example, DehazeGS (Yu et al. 2026a) integrated
an atmospheric scattering model into Gaussian
primitives, and jointly estimated transmission and
atmospheric light to recover clear scene appear-
ances from hazy images. RestorGS (Qiao et al.
2025) decoupled scene appearance from degra-
dation effects and introduced depth-guided self-
supervision to improve reconstruction quality.
WaterSplatting (Li et al. 2025a) extended Gaus-
sian representations with a volumetric medium
field to model underwater backscattering effects.
Although these methods improve reconstruc-
tion under specific degradation conditions, they
typically rely on degradation-specific imaging
models and assumptions. Since different degra-
dations arise from distinct imaging mechanisms,
these assumptions may not hold under unknown
or mixed degradations, limiting their general-
ization across diverse extreme environments. In
4


contrast, our method leverages the general visual
priors of a pretrained MLLM to enhance observa-
tions under different degradation conditions with-
out requiring degradation-specific imaging mod-
els, thereby providing more reliable supervision for
subsequent 3D reconstruction.
2.2 Generative Priors for
Restoration and Reconstruction
Recent advances in generative modeling (Conde
et al. 2024; Lin et al. 2024; Yılmaz et al. 2026; Fei
et al. 2026; Gu et al. 2026; Wu et al. 2026a; Tang
et al. 2026; Wang et al. 2026b) have opened new
opportunities for image restoration. Unlike con-
ventional restoration networks (Zhang et al. 2025;
Gao et al. 2026a; Jiang et al. 2025; Zhang et al.
2026; Jin et al. 2025; Cui et al. 2026; Li et al.
2025c) that often specialize in specific degrada-
tion types, generative models leverage rich visual
priors to handle diverse degradations. Instruc-
tIR (Conde et al. 2024) introduced instruction-
conditioned restoration for multiple degradation
tasks. DiffBIR (Lin et al. 2024) employed diffu-
sion priors to recover realistic image details, while
Edit2Restore (Yılmaz et al. 2026) explored adapt-
ing image editing models to restoration tasks.
Recent studies (Wei et al. 2026; Li et al.
2026; Zhu et al. 2025; Jiang et al. 2026) further
demonstrated the potential of general genera-
tive models for image restoration across diverse
degradations. Such restoration strategies have also
been explored for 3D reconstruction. For example,
Smoke-GS (Zheng et al. 2026) leveraged genera-
tive visual priors to enhance degraded observa-
tions for subsequent reconstruction. GenSmoke-
GS (Cao et al. 2026) and Dehaze-then-Splat (Chen
and Wang 2026a) investigated restoration-assisted
reconstruction in smoky scenes, while NAKA-
GS (Zhu et al. 2026) addressed reconstruction
under low-light conditions. However, generative
restoration models may introduce pixel misalign-
ment and color deviations (Sun et al. 2026; Zhao
et al. 2026), which can propagate into 3D recon-
struction and compromise geometry and appear-
ance consistency (Liu et al. 2026a; Wang et al.
2026d). To address this limitation, our method
combines the visual priors of a frozen MLLM with
a lightweight correction network trained using
only a few paired clean views, providing more reli-
able pseudo-GT supervision for 3D reconstruction.
2.3 Efficient 3DGS
Although 3DGS enables efficient rendering, its
adaptive density control can introduce a large
number of Gaussian primitives, resulting in sub-
stantial computational and memory overhead. To
improve efficiency, existing studies (Chen and
Wang 2026b; Jiao et al. 2026; Ren et al. 2026;
Hahlbohm et al. 2026) have explored reducing
redundant Gaussian growth, accelerating rasteri-
zation, and optimizing the training process. For
instance, Mini-Splatting (Fang and Wang 2024)
introduced spatially aware densification and Gaus-
sian simplification to concentrate primitives in
informative regions. Taming 3DGS (Mallick et al.
2024) combined gradient information with auxil-
iary signals to guide Gaussian growth and reduce
redundant densification. LightGaussian (Fan et al.
2024) reduced the number of primitives through
opacity- and volume-based pruning, while PUP-
3DGS (Hanson et al. 2025b) employed sensitivity
scores to identify and remove redundant com-
ponents from pretrained representations. Beyond
Gaussian reduction, Speedy-Splat (Hanson et al.
2025a) improved rasterization efficiency by tight-
ening Gaussian tile coverage to avoid unneces-
sary computations. Faster-GS (Hahlbohm et al.
2026)
further
accelerated
3DGS
optimization
through efficient backward propagation, locality-
preserving Gaussian reordering, and fused param-
eter updates. Other studies (Lan et al. 2025; Pehli-
van et al. 2026; Ye et al. 2025) further explored
efficient optimization and GPU implementations
to reduce the computational cost of 3DGS.
Despite these advances, existing acceleration
methods have primarily been developed and evalu-
ated using high-quality observations. In degraded
scenes, residual artifacts and cross-view inconsis-
tencies may persist even after view enhancement,
potentially disrupting Gaussian optimization and
inducing redundant densification. This can fur-
ther increase rasterization costs and memory-
access overhead during reconstruction. However,
the effectiveness of existing acceleration strate-
gies under such conditions remains insufficiently
explored. To address this challenge, we inves-
tigate efficient 3DGS optimization in degraded
scenes by optimizing rasterization, memory lay-
out, and parameter updates to reduce redundant
computation and memory-access overhead.

3 Methodology
3.1 Problem Formulation
Let X
=
{x 1, x 2, . . . , x n} denote degraded
images (e.g., smoke, low light, defocus blur,
motion blur, exposure variation) of a scene cap-
tured from n different views, where x i ∈Rh×w×3.
The corresponding camera poses are denoted by
Π
=
{π1, π2, . . . , πn}. We assume that only
a small subset of degraded views has corre-
sponding clean images. Without loss of gen-
erality, we denote these paired clean images
as C
= {c1, c2, . . . , cm}. They correspond to
{x 1, x 2, . . . , x m}, where m ≪n. Our goal is to
learn a reliable 3D scene representation Gθ from
the degraded observations X using only a few
paired clean images for supervision, and to synthe-
size a clean novel view at an unseen camera pose
πt /∈Π:
byt = Gθ(πt) ∈Rh×w×3.
(1)
The rendered view byt should be consistent
with the corresponding clean ground truth image
yt ∈Rh×w×3. It should faithfully reflect the scene
geometry and view-dependent appearance, and
preserve realistic textures and colors.
To achieve this goal, we propose MLLM-GS, a
framework for fast NVS in extreme environments.
As illustrated in Fig. 2, MLLM-GS consists of two
branches: MLLM prior-guided view enhancement
(Section 3.2) and fast 3D scene reconstruction
(Section 3.3). The former leverages the rich visual
priors of a frozen MLLM to enhance degraded
observations and employs a lightweight correc-
tion network to reduce pixel misalignment and
color distortions under few-shot supervision. This
branch generates reliable pseudo-GT views for all
degraded observations, providing supervision for
subsequent 3D reconstruction. The latter recon-
structs the 3D scene using these pseudo-GT views
and improves 3DGS optimization efficiency in
terms of rasterization, memory layout, and param-
eter updates. By combining reliable view enhance-
ment with efficient 3D reconstruction, MLLM-GS
enables high-quality and fast NVS across diverse
severe degradations.
3.2 MLLM Prior-Guided View
Enhancement
Images captured in extreme environments often
suffer from severe degradations, including smoke,
low light, defocus blur, motion blur, and exposure
6


variation. These degradations can damage both
structural and color information. Directly using
degraded views to supervise 3D reconstruction
can introduce errors in both scene geometry and
appearance, leading to suboptimal NVS results.
An intuitive solution is to restore the views before
reconstruction. However, existing restoration net-
works (Zhang et al. 2025; Jiang et al. 2025) typ-
ically require extensive paired training data and
are often specialized for specific degradation types,
limiting their applicability under diverse degra-
dations. To address these limitations, we develop
an MLLM prior-guided view enhancement branch
that combines a frozen MLLM with a lightweight
correction network. The MLLM leverages gen-
eral visual priors to enhance observations under
diverse degradations, while the correction network
reduces residual structural and color distortions
using only a few paired clean views. In this way,
the branch extends limited clean supervision to all
input views, providing reliable pseudo-GT images
for subsequent 3D reconstruction.
Specifically, we first feed each degraded view
x i and a text instruction p into a frozen MLLM
M(·) to obtain an enhanced image ei:
ei = M(x i, p).
(2)
Although ei recovers plausible scene structures
and textures, it may still contain residual struc-
tural and color distortions. To reduce these errors,
we further process ei using the correction network
illustrated in Fig. 2. The correction network first
converts ei into the Horizontal/Vertical-Intensity
(HVI) color space (Yan et al. 2025). In RGB space,
luminance and chrominance information are cou-
pled, making it difficult to correct structural and
color distortions separately. In contrast, the HVI
color space separates luminance from chromatic
information, allowing the network to model struc-
tural and color deviations more effectively. The
transformation is formulated as
si = T (ei),
(3)
where T (·) denotes the HVI transform and si =
[hi, v i, d i] ∈Rh×w×3 is the HVI representation of
ei. hi and v i encode the chromatic information
and d i represents the luminance information.
Subsequently, the HVI representation si is
projected into a high-dimensional feature space
through a convolutional layer and processed by
a U-Net to correct residual structural and color
distortions. The encoder progressively extracts
multi-scale features to capture both local details
and contextual information. At the bottleneck, a
correction block integrates depthwise convolution,
a gating mechanism, and channel attention to fur-
ther refine the encoded features. Depthwise con-
volution captures local spatial information, while
the gating mechanism adaptively emphasizes fea-
tures relevant to distortion correction. Channel
attention further recalibrates feature responses
across channels to improve the modeling of struc-
tural and color information. The decoder progres-
sively restores the spatial resolution and integrates
encoder features through skip connections to pre-
serve fine-grained details. Finally, the corrected
HVI representation bsi is converted back to RGB
space through the inverse HVI transformation,
yielding the corrected view bci:
bci = T −1(bsi).
(4)
During training, the MLLM remains frozen,
and only the lightweight correction network with
approximately 4M parameters is optimized. By
combining the pretrained visual priors of the
frozen MLLM with few-shot supervised correc-
tion, the proposed branch can effectively refine
the enhanced views using only a few paired clean
images. The training objective combines a Char-
bonnier loss LChar(·, ·) and a frequency-domain
loss LFreq(·, ·). The former minimizes pixel-level
reconstruction errors, while the latter encourages
the preservation of image details in the frequency
domain. The overall loss is defined as
Len = LChar( bci, ci) + λLFreq( bci, ci),
(5)
where ci (i ∈{1, · · · , m}) denotes the correspond-
ing clean ground-truth image and λ balances the
two loss terms. After training, the enhancement

3.3 Fast 3D Scene Reconstruction
After view enhancement, the corrected views serve
as pseudo-GT supervision for 3D scene reconstruc-
tion. However, residual artifacts and cross-view

inconsistencies may still affect Gaussian optimiza-
tion and induce redundant densification, increas-

3DGS. First, the conservative screen-space bound-

with negligible Gaussian contributions, resulting
in unnecessary rasterization operations. Second,
adaptive densification and pruning can scatter
spatially neighboring Gaussians across memory,
reducing memory-access efficiency. Third, updat-
ing different Gaussian attributes through sepa-
rate optimization kernels introduces additional
kernel launches and global memory accesses.
Inspired by Faster-GS (Hahlbohm et al. 2026),
we develop a fast 3D scene reconstruction branch
that accelerates 3DGS optimization from three
aspects: rasterization, memory layout, and param-
eter updates.

3.3.3 Parameter Updates
Standard 3DGS (Kerbl et al. 2023) optimizes
Gaussian attributes, including position, rotation,
scale, opacity, and spherical harmonic coeffi-
cients, using Adam (Kingma and Ba 2014). These
attributes are organized into separate parameter
groups, and their updates typically involve mul-
tiple kernel launches and global-memory accesses.
Each update requires reading gradients, optimizer
states, and parameter values from memory and
writing the updated results back. As the num-
ber of Gaussian primitives increases, these oper-
ations introduce considerable computational and
memory-bandwidth overhead. Inspired by Faster-
GS (Hahlbohm et al. 2026), we fuse the Adam
updates of different parameter groups into a sin-
gle CUDA kernel and use contiguous buffers for
unified parameter updates, reducing the overhead.
Specifically, we fuse the Adam updates of
different Gaussian parameter groups into a sin-
gle CUDA kernel and organize the corresponding
parameters and optimizer states in contiguous
memory buffers. Each thread loads the parame-
ter value, gradient, and moment estimates into
registers, where the Adam moment updates, bias
correction, and parameter update are performed
using fused arithmetic operations. The updated
parameters and optimizer states are then writ-
ten back to global memory. By reducing kernel
launches and redundant global-memory accesses,
this strategy improves the efficiency of Gaussian
parameter updates, particularly when optimizing
a large number of Gaussian primitives.

Evaluation Metrics: To evaluate render-
ing quality, we report Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio
(PSNR), Structural Similarity Index Measure
(SSIM) (Wang et al. 2004), and Learned Per-
ceptual Image Patch Similarity (LPIPS) (Zhang
et al. 2018) for quantitative evaluation. Specif-
ically, PSNR measures pixel-level reconstruction
fidelity, while SSIM evaluates structural similar-
ity between the rendered and reference images.
Higher values (↑) of these two metrics indicate
better reconstruction quality of the novel view.
LPIPS measures the perceptual similarity between
the rendered and reference images in a deep fea-
ture space. A lower value (↓) indicates better
perceptual quality. In implementation, LPIPS is
computed using the AlexNet (Krizhevsky et al.
2012) backbone following the official code (Zhang
et al. 2018). Furthermore, to evaluate computa-
tional efficiency and representation complexity,
we additionally report GPU memory consumption
(VRAM), training time (Training), and the final
number of Gaussian primitives (#Gs).

Effectiveness of Rasterization Optimiza-
tion: To evaluate the effectiveness of raster-
ization optimization, we replace the standard
3DGS (Kerbl et al. 2023) rasterization pipeline

shown in Table 5, the number of Gaussians

gation strategy reconstructs the blending pro-

redundant gradient computations and memory
accesses. In this way, these designs improve ras-
terization efficiency and reduce the computational
overhead of 3D reconstruction.

5 Conclusion
This paper proposes MLLM-GS, an MLLM prior-
guided framework for fast novel view synthesis in
extreme environments. It consists of two branches:
MLLM prior-guided view enhancement and fast
3D scene reconstruction. The former combines
a frozen MLLM with a lightweight correction
network to reduce pixel misalignment and color
distortions, providing reliable pseudo-GT super-
vision using only a few paired clean views. The
latter accelerates 3DGS optimization from three
aspects: rasterization, memory layout, and param-
eter updates. Extensive experiments demonstrate
18


that MLLM-GS achieves high-quality novel view
synthesis across diverse severe degradations while
substantially reducing reconstruction time and
GPU memory consumption.
