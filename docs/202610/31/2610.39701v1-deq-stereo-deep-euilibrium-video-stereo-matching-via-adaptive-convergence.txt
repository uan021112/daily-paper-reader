DEQ-Stereo: Deep Equilibrium Video Stereo
Matching via Adaptive Convergence
Kiljoon Han1 , Kyumin Hwang2 , Wonhyeok Choi1 , Jeonghoon Kim1 , and
Sunghoon Im2,†
1 Daegu Gyeongbuk Institute of Science and Technology, Daegu, South Korea
2 Korea Advanced Institute of Science and Technology, Daejeon, South Korea
Abstract. Iterative refinement models have significantly improved stereo
matching accuracy, but their rigid, fixed update schedules incur heavy
computational costs and a strict trade-off between speed and accuracy.
This challenge is magnified in video streams, where traditional online
methods resort to reducing feature channels or relying on expensive
temporal warping. To address this, we propose DEQ-Stereo, a novel
video stereo matching framework based on the Deep Equilibrium (DEQ)
paradigm. Instead of unrolling a fixed number of iterations, DEQ-Stereo
reformulates the refinement as an implicit fixed-point problem, adap-
tively terminating once convergence is met. To ensure fast and stable
intra-frame convergence, we introduce the Convolutional Simplified
Gated Unit (ConvSGU), which eliminates unnecessary non-linearities
by modeling the latent state as a gated, element-wise sum of inputs. Fur-
thermore, to exploit cross-frame temporal coherence, we propose Tem-
poral State Transfer (TST), a warm-start strategy that propagates
the converged hidden state to the next frame without requiring explicit
pose or warping priors. Extensive evaluations demonstrate that DEQ-
Stereo delivers state-of-the-art performance with a superior accuracy-to-
latency trade-off.
Keywords: Stereo Matching · Deep Equilibrium Models · Video Depth

Introduction
Stereo matching is a fundamental problem in computer vision, aimed at esti-
mating depth by computing disparities between a pair of images. It underpins
various 3D perception tasks, including autonomous driving [14,31], robotics [45],
augmented reality [12], and 3D scene reconstruction [5]. Recent learning-based
approaches, particularly those based on iterative refinement [24, 26, 40], have
significantly improved accuracy. These methods refine the disparity map across
multiple steps, progressively enlarging the receptive field and enabling robust
predictions in challenging areas like textureless regions [24, 47] or thin struc-
tures [9, 37]. Their generalization ability and optimization-like behavior have
made them widely adopted in both static and sequential stereo settings. How-
ever, their inference time increases linearly with the number of iterations, which
can be a bottleneck in time-sensitive environments.

While stereo matching is often benchmarked on individual image pairs, many
real-world applications require continuous stereo video processing. Applying it-
erative models independently to each frame leads to redundant computation and
temporal inconsistencies across frames. Recent video stereo methods incorporate
temporal cues to improve both runtime and coherence. These can be broadly cat-
egorized into offline and online approaches. Offline methods [19,20] exploit full
sequences for improved accuracy but are impractical for real-time use, as they
rely on future frames. Online methods [25,42,46] are designed for real-time pro-
cessing by using lightweight models and previous-frame information, but suffer
from accumulated errors and require reliable pose and disparity priors.
To address the limitations of existing video stereo matching methods, we
propose DEQ-Stereo, a novel framework based on the Deep Equilibrium (DEQ)
model [3], designed for efficient and temporally consistent stereo matching in
video sequences. Unlike conventional approaches that rely on a fixed number
of iterative updates, DEQ-Stereo directly solves for the fixed point of the up-
date module, eliminating the need for explicit unrolling and enabling adaptive
inference based on a convergence criterion as shown in Fig. 1. This yields more ef-
ficient, stable, and scalable inference across frames. To support this equilibrium-
based formulation, we propose two complementary strategies aimed at improving
intra-frame convergence stability and enhancing robustness across frames.
First, we introduce the Convolutional Simplified Gated Unit (ConvSGU),
designed to accelerate and stabilize per-frame convergence. Inspired by Recur-
rent Additive Networks (RAN) [23], ConvSGU simplifies the gating structure
of ConvGRU by expressing the latent state as a gated, element-wise sum of in-
puts, thereby removing unnecessary non-linearities. This simplification facilitates
faster and more stable convergence to equilibrium states within the DEQ formu-

lation. Furthermore, to exploit temporal coherence in video, we propose Tempo-
ral State Transfer (TST), a mechanism that reuses the converged hidden state
from the previous frame to initialize the current one. This warm-start strategy
encourages consistency over time and improves convergence speed, particularly
in scenes with low inter-frame motion. By integrating the DEQ paradigm with
ConvSGU and TST, DEQ-Stereo produces temporally consistent, high-quality
disparity estimates while significantly reducing computational cost. Extensive
evaluation across multiple benchmarks including SceneFlow [29], TartanAir [36],
KITTI2015 [31], and ETH3D SLAM [33], demonstrates that our method achieves
the best trade-off between efficiency and accuracy.
Our main contributions can be summarized as follows:
1. We propose DEQ-Stereo, a novel stereo matching framework leveraging
Deep Equilibrium models for adaptive and accurate iterative refinement.
2. To improve convergence from both intra-frame and inter-frame perspectives,
we propose ConvSGU enhancing convergence stability within each frame,
and TST accelerating convergence across video frames.
3. Our method achieves state-of-the-art performance across multiple online
video stereo benchmarks, delivering the best trade-off between inference
speed and accuracy.

Related Work
2.1
Per-frame Stereo Matching Networks
Early deep learning stereo methods centered on building a cost volume and reg-
ularizing it with computationally expensive 3D convolutions to directly regress
disparity [7, 16, 21, 44]. While these methods laid the groundwork, their high
computational cost motivated further refinements in cost aggregation strate-
gies [27,39]. A significant shift occurred with the introduction of iterative refine-
ment architectures, which adapted RAFT [34] from optical flow for stereo match-
ing [26]. This design, while powerful, relies on a fixed number of iterations—a
core limitation forcing a trade-off between accuracy and efficiency. Subsequent
work has focused on improving performance within this fixed iteration, either
by enhancing frameworks with hierarchical processing or better geometric repre-
sentations [24,40,43], or by designing specialized recurrent modules to preserve
high-frequency details [9, 37, 47]. However, despite these advances, the funda-
mental issue of fixed iteration remains.
2.2
Video Stereo Matching Networks
In video stereo matching, methods leverage temporal information for efficiency
and consistency. While offline approaches [18–20] yield high-quality, temporally
smooth results, their non-causal nature makes them unsuitable for real-time ap-
plications. Online methods [12,25,42] are therefore more common, ranging from
early RNN-based designs [48] to lightweight temporal modules and adaptations


4
K. Han et al.
of iterative refinement architectures prominent in per-frame matching. Focusing
on the latter, these iterative approaches operate with a low, fixed number of
iterations, supplementing their limited refinement capabilities by warping the
depth from the previous frame to provide a strong initial estimate. This warping
strategy, however, necessitates auxiliary camera parameters, and its direct injec-
tion of prior estimates hinders the model’s ability to correct errors, leading to
their accumulation over time. This specific vulnerability of fixed, low-iteration
schemes directly motivates our adaptive approach.
2.3
Implicit Deep Learning
Unlike traditional fixed-sequence layers, Implicit Deep Learning networks—such
as Deep Equilibrium Models (DEQ) [3] and Neural ODEs [8]—define outputs
as fixed-point solutions of a parameterized process, enabling effectively infinite
depth. By adaptively determining the iterations needed to reach equilibrium,
DEQ overcomes the inefficiencies of fixed-iteration stereo matching, yielding sig-
nificant computational savings without sacrificing refinement quality. This im-
plicit paradigm has been successfully applied across diverse domains, including
diffusion models [6,32], object detection [35], and graph networks [15]. In dense
correspondence, DEQ-Flow [2] pairs the DEQ framework with ConvGRU for
optical flow, reusing both the predicted flow and hidden state from the previous
frame to accelerate convergence. While our TST shares this warm-start intu-
ition, it differs crucially by propagating only the hidden state. Empirically, we
find that this selective propagation reduces sensitivity to complex scene dynam-
ics, ultimately improving temporal stability in video stereo matching.

Iterative methods excel by aggregating features across multiple recursive
steps, effectively combining local matching cues with global context. A key lim-
itation is their reliance on a fixed iteration count N, chosen empirically rather

sive iterations increase inference time. This balance becomes even more critical
in video stereo settings, where real-time constraints and dynamic scene content
demand adaptive iteration control to optimize both speed and accuracy.

Convolutional Simplified Gated Unit
In DEQ-based networks, the convergence speed of the residual function criti-

a streamlined variant of ConvGRU inspired by the Recurrent Additive Network
(RAN) [23]. ConvSGU simplifies recurrent dynamics while retaining modeling
capacity, defined as:

Experiments
5.1
Implementation Details
Training Setup. We implement DEQ-Stereo in PyTorch, training on eight
NVIDIA TITAN RTX GPUs and evaluating on a single RTX 3090. We use
AdamW [28] with gradients clipped to [−1, 1]. For pretraining, we separately
train on SceneFlow [29] and the TartanAir TemporalStereo split [36,46] for 200K
steps with a one-cycle learning rate schedule with a learning rate of 2×10−4 and
a total batch size of 8. For KITTI fine-tuning, we initialize from the TartanAir
model and fine-tune on KITTI raw [13] using LEAStereo [11] pseudo-labels,
following TC-Stereo [42]. We train for 50K steps with a maximum learning rate
of 1 × 10−4. The TartanAir model is directly used for zero-shot evaluation on
ETH3D SLAM [33]. All training is performed on a per-frame basis, while the
video mode during inference is determined by whether TST is enabled.
DEQ Solver & Inference. The DEQ solver uses Broyden’s method with
Armijo backtracking for stable convergence, with an Armijo constant of 1×10−4,
initial step size of 0.5, and minimum step size of 1 × 10−2. It terminates when
max(||gθ(s, C)||) falls below 1 × 10−3 during training or 30 during inference, or
after 16 iterations. Without TST, each frame is initialized from scratch; with
TST, the converged state is propagated to the next frame to exploit temporal
coherence. All other solver settings remain identical between the two modes.
5.2
Comparison with State-of-the-Art Methods
We evaluate our method against state-of-the-art methods on the TartanAir,
SceneFlow, and KITTI2015 datasets. For a fair comparison, video-based base-
lines are restricted to online methods, excluding offline ones that exploit fu-
ture frames; representative per-frame methods are also reported for reference.
We evaluate our method on the TartanAir using the train-test split provided
by TemporalStereo to compare against other online video methods reported in
Tab. 1. Our approach exhibits its most significant advantage in occluded regions,
decisively outperforming online video methods that falter in these areas due to
their reliance on unreliable temporal warping. This highlights the robustness of
our method, which effectively handles ill-posed areas, even in the presence of
misleading or missing temporal priors.

both accuracy and inference speed further improve, indicating that our adaptive
equilibrium formulation effectively benefits from task-specific adaptation.

6
Conclusion
We introduce DEQ-Stereo, an efficient iterative stereo matching framework rooted
in the Deep Equilibrium (DEQ) paradigm. By replacing traditional fixed-iteration
update loops with an implicit fixed-point formulation, our architecture adap-
tively balances accuracy and computational efficiency. Specifically, the proposed
ConvSGU accelerates and stabilizes per-frame convergence by eliminating un-
necessary non-linearities, while the TST mechanism enables effective cross-frame
warm-starting. Together, these components allow DEQ-Stereo to achieve state-
of-the-art or highly competitive performance across multiple challenging bench-
marks, demonstrating strong robustness in occluded and dynamic regions with-
out relying on explicit, computationally expensive temporal warping.
