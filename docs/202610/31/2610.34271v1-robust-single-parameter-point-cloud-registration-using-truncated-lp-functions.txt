Robust Single-Parameter Point Cloud Registration
using Truncated Lp Functions
Seong Hun Lee1, Patrick Vandewalle2 and Javier Civera3
Abstract— We propose a novel method for robust point cloud
registration with only one tunable parameter. Our method,
dubbed TLP, uses truncated Lp functions with different values
of p to assign priority scores to correspondences, select the
hypothesis, and refine the final model. By leveraging the robust-
ness of truncated functions, TLP effectively handles the high
outlier rates commonly encountered in real-world 3D feature
matching. We also exploit GPU parallelization to evaluate
hypotheses efficiently, enabling registration from thousands of
correspondences in under a second on average. With only one
tunable parameter, our method offers greater practicality than
multi-parameter methods that require dataset-specific tuning of
several parameters. Evaluations on public benchmark datasets
demonstrate that TLP achieves higher accuracy than state-of-
the-art single-parameter methods. Furthermore, it outperforms
two widely used multi-parameter methods on three of the four
evaluated datasets while maintaining competitive runtime.
I. INTRODUCTION
Point cloud registration estimates the rotation and transla-
tion needed to align point clouds captured from different
viewpoints, enabling 3D reconstruction, localization, and
robotic perception [1], [2]. A common pipeline first estab-
lishes correspondences using local descriptors, such as hand-
crafted FPFH [3] or learned FCGF [4], and then estimates
the transformation. Correct correspondences allow the least-
squares alignment to be computed in closed form [5], but
partial overlap, ambiguous geometry, and noise can cause
outliers to greatly outnumber inliers [6], [7]. Reliable regis-
tration therefore requires identifying geometrically consistent
correspondences among sets dominated by outliers.
RANSAC addresses this problem by generating hypothe-
ses from random minimal samples and evaluating their
consensus [8], but its efficiency deteriorates as the probability
of drawing an all-inlier sample decreases. Recent methods
exploit compatibility graphs, correspondence rankings, and
consistency filtering to make hypothesis generation more
selective [6], [9], [2], [10]. Others combine robust objec-
tives with specialized optimization or parameter search [7],
[11], [1]. Together, these advances motivate a coordinated
approach to correspondence scoring, hypothesis selection,
and refinement.
Coordinating these stages requires accounting for their
different objectives: correspondence scoring should preserve
degrees of geometric agreement, hypothesis selection should

In this work, we propose TLP, a novel method that uses
a common family of truncated Lp costs and complementary
scores for correspondence ranking, hypothesis selection, and
refinement while requiring users to tune a single parameter.
For correspondence ranking, it combines the truncated L3
compatibility score with the support aggregation introduced
in HERE [1], preserving degrees of pairwise geometric
agreement before aggregation. Hypothesis selection uses the
limiting score as p →∞, equivalent to maximizing the
inlier count, while refinement successively uses the costs
corresponding to p = 2, the logarithmic endpoint p →0+,
and p = 0.4. With fixed, stage-specific exponents and trun-
cation thresholds derived from a single distance parameter,
this formulation accommodates the different objectives of
each stage in a unified manner.
Our main contributions are as follows:
• A robust point cloud registration framework that uses
truncated Lp functions with different exponents for corre-
spondence scoring, hypothesis selection, and refinement.
• A correspondence scoring scheme that incorporates contin-
uous truncated L3 compatibility into support aggregation,
preserving the degree of pairwise agreement.
• An efficient implementation combining deterministic hy-
pothesis generation, parallel batch evaluation, and staged
refinement, with only one user-tuned parameter.

II. RELATED WORK
A. Compatibility-Based Correspondence Scoring
Pairwise distance consistency allows us to assess corre-
spondence correctness without first estimating the rigid body
transformation. ROBIN uses such invariants to construct
compatibility graphs and reject outliers through graph prun-
ing [12]. CLIPPER retains graded consistency in a weighted
graph and seeks a dense, mutually consistent subset [13].
SC2-PCR strengthens pairwise evidence through second-
order spatial compatibility [6], whereas MAC extracts mul-
tiple maximal cliques to generate transformation hypothe-
ses [9]. HERE ranks correspondences by aggregating the
support of their compatible neighbors [1]. In our method,
we adopt this aggregation with continuous truncated L3
arXiv:2610.34271v1  [cs.CV]  25 Oct 2026


compatibility, retaining the degree of pairwise agreement
when computing correspondence priorities.
Learned estimators also exploit relations among matches.
DGR predicts correspondence confidence using a 6D convo-
lutional network [14], while PointDSC incorporates spatial
consistency into learned outlier rejection [15]. Our method
requires no estimator training and accepts both handcrafted
and learned correspondences.
B. Hypothesis Generation and Consensus Selection
RANSAC estimates models from random minimal samples
and selects them by consensus [8]. Subsequent methods
improve different parts of this process. PROSAC exploits
correspondence rankings by progressively enlarging the pool
from which samples are drawn [16]. LO-RANSAC refines
promising hypotheses through local optimization [17], and
Graph-Cut RANSAC incorporates spatial coherence into
this refinement through graph-cut-based inlier labeling [18].
MAGSAC marginalizes over a range of noise scales to score
and refine models, reducing dependence on a single inlier
threshold [19].
For point cloud registration, PCR-99 uses ranked cor-
respondences to guide deterministic three-point hypothe-
sis generation [2]. TCF improves the input to three-point
RANSAC through successive length- and angle-based con-
sistency filtering [10], while HERE uses selected correspon-
dences to guide a decomposed parameter search [1]. Our
method combines ranking-guided generation with GPU batch
evaluation, inlier-count selection, and local refinement.
C. Robust Objectives and Refinement
Robust estimation controls the influence of incorrect cor-
respondences through the residual objective. FGR uses a
Geman–McClure penalty and graduated non-convexity to
optimize alignment [20]. More generally, graduated non-
convexity couples robust costs with non-minimal solvers by
progressively introducing non-convexity [21]. TEASER uses
truncated least squares [7], whereas TEAR minimizes trun-
cated entry-wise absolute residuals through a decomposed
search [11].
Parameterized loss families, such as Barron’s [22], provide
a complementary approach to robust estimation by contin-
uously adjusting the shape of the penalty. IRLS optimizes
robust objectives by solving a sequence of weighted least-
squares problems, and its convergence has been studied in the
context of non-smooth robust regression [23]. Our method
unifies correspondence scoring, hypothesis selection, and
refinement through a normalized, truncated, smoothed Lp
family with fixed, stage-specific exponents.

IV. METHOD
A. Overview of TLP
Alg. 1 summarizes the pipeline. TLP first ranks corre-
spondences using truncated L3 compatibility and support
aggregation. These rankings guide deterministic three-point
hypothesis generation, and GPU batch evaluation selects the
two hypotheses with the largest consensus (p →∞). Each
undergoes L2 refinement followed by smoothed L0+ refine-
ment via iteratively reweighted least squares (IRLS) [23][24,
Chapter 9], with inlier sets updated between model fits. Batch
processing continues until the stopping criterion is met, and
the best model receives final L0.4 refinement. The distance
threshold τ is the only dataset-dependent parameter; all other
settings remain fixed.

We generate three-point samples deterministically from

0, . . . , n−1 to the n point correspondence using the method
proposed in [2]. This method enumerates triplets (i, j, k)
satisfying 0 ≤i < j < k < n in increasing order of
their index sum s = i + j + k. For each sum, i and then
j are traversed in ascending order. The enumeration begins
as follows:
• All triplets with sum 3: (0, 1, 2).
• All triplets with sum 4: (0, 1, 3).
• All triplets with sum 5: (0, 1, 4), (0, 2, 3).
• All triplets with sum 6: (0, 1, 5), (0, 2, 4), (1, 2, 3).
• ...
• Unless an early termination condition is met, enumera-
tion continues up to an index sum of min(1000, 3n−6),
reaching the final triplet (n −3, n −2, n −1) when
3n −6 ≤1000.

Thus, mAA averages accuracy over integer angular thresh-
olds from 1◦to K◦, rewarding smaller errors within this
range without penalizing the magnitude of errors beyond
it. As a result, mAA reflects both estimation accuracy and
robustness to outliers while reducing dependence on any
single evaluation threshold. The score lies in [0, 1] and
is independent of point cloud scale, allowing the same
thresholds across datasets. The main limitation of rotation-
based mAA is that it does not measure translation accuracy:
a high score alone does not guarantee correct alignment.
In practice, however, accurate rotation estimates tend to be
strongly correlated with accurate translation estimates. Thus,
rotation-based mAA serves as a useful, scale-independent
proxy for overall registration quality.
C. Registration Accuracy
Table II summarizes the registration accuracy results.
Some baseline evaluations could not be completed under our
experimental conditions. MAC ran out of memory for both
FPFH configurations. TEASER incurred excessive runtimes
with FCGF, exceeding two hours for some 3DMatch pairs
and frequently exceeding half an hour for KITTI pairs. These
entries are reported as unavailable, and the corresponding
TEASER threshold entries are marked N/A in Table I.
Among the available results, TLP achieves the highest
mAA in 10 of the 12 dataset–descriptor–cutoff combinations.
It consistently outperforms all evaluated single-parameter
baselines. It also outperforms all evaluated multi-parameter
baselines, except on 3DMatch with FPFH at the 10◦and 15◦
cutoffs, where SC2-PCR achieves higher accuracy. Fig. 3
presents five examples from 3DMatch with FPFH, where
TLP achieves more accurate alignment than the evaluated
single-parameter baselines.


D. Ablation Study
Table IV summarizes the results of our ablation study. It
shows that the default configuration achieves the highest or
joint-highest mAA in 9 of the 12 settings. Removing indi-
vidual components generally reduces accuracy on 3DMatch
with FPFH, whereas the effects on KITTI are small.
E. Runtime
Table III reports the runtimes. Among the nine methods,
TLP ranks third, first, third, and third in runtime across
the four dataset–descriptor combinations, respectively. Thus,
TLP consistently ranks among the three fastest methods
while achieving superior accuracy in most evaluated settings.
VI. CONCLUSION
We presented TLP, a robust point cloud registration
method that uses truncated Lp functions with different expo-
nents for correspondence scoring, hypothesis selection, and
refinement. By combining continuous compatibility scores
with deterministic hypothesis generation, parallel batch eval-
uation, and staged refinement, TLP requires only one user-
tuned distance threshold, with all other settings fixed across
datasets. Experiments on 3DMatch and KITTI with FPFH
and FCGF descriptors show that TLP achieves the highest
rotation-based mAA in 10 of the 12 evaluated settings among
the available baseline results, while ranking among the three
fastest methods in all four dataset–descriptor configurations.
These results demonstrate that the proposed combination
provides accurate and efficient registration with limited pa-
rameter tuning.
