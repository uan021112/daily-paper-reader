Abstract
Generating interactive 3D assets from a single image requires realistic appearance, fidelity to the depicted
object, and plausible physical behavior. Yet detailed geometry alone does not determine how an object should
move or deform. We introduce PhysAg3D, which couples 3D generative models with agentic reasoning and
tool use. A vision-language agent first infers semantic parts, candidate interactions, and physical priors from
the image; a 3D generator then produces a textured whole-object mesh from the same image. A hierarchical
interaction representation links these revisable priors to mechanisms and the generated surfaces they control,
organizing interaction construction and refinement. Guided by this representation and observations of the mesh,
the agent uses geometric and physical tools to construct parts, joints, deformable regions, and surface bindings
while retaining useful generated geometry and materials. Rendered interactions and execution measurements
guide targeted revisions within a fixed budget. The framework accommodates both rigid articulation and
compliant deformation. Quantitative and qualitative evaluations on real photographs and external benchmarks
demonstrate detailed, image-aligned assets with plausible interactions. Ablations support bounded refinement
and source-surface preservation. Project Page: supra-3d.github.io/PhysAg3D.

1 Introduction
Generating interactive 3D assets from a single image offers a scalable way to populate virtual environments and
embodied simulations with objects from the real world (Mildenhall et al., 2020; Kerbl et al., 2023; Mo et al., 2019;
Xia et al., 2020; Xiang et al., 2020). A useful asset should capture the depicted instance, retain realistic geometric
and material detail, and respond to actions in ways appropriate to the object. These goals concern different aspects of
quality: a visually convincing reconstruction can remain static, while a functional mechanism can belong to an asset that
no longer resembles its reference. The task therefore requires bringing appearance, instance alignment, and physical
interaction into a coherent realization.
Recent advances provide strong foundations for this task. Image-conditioned generators synthesize detailed geometry
and materials (Poole et al., 2023; Jun & Nichol, 2023; Nichol et al., 2022; Lin et al., 2023; Qian et al., 2023; Liu et al.,
2023a; Shi et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2023b; Hong et al., 2024; Long et al., 2024; Tochil et al., 2024; Xu et al., 2024; Du
et al., 2024a; Voleti et al., 2024; Tang et al., 2024; Xiang et al., 2025b;a); articulated and physical generation methods
infer part structure, motion, and material behavior (Mo et al., 2019; Xia et al., 2020; Yu et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2019;
Xiang et al., 2020; Liu et al., 2025; 2026; Cao et al., 2026; Makoviychuk et al., 2021; Hu et al., 2020); and agentic
systems construct and revise assets through executable programs, visual grounding, and feedback (Ahn et al., 2022; Li
et al., 2023a; Huang et al., 2023b;a; Driess et al., 2023; Brohan et al., 2023; Singh et al., 2023; Valmeekam et al., 2023;
Le et al., 2025; Zhou et al., 2026; Wang et al., 2026). A remaining challenge is to maintain agreement between what an
object is expected to do, how that behavior is implemented, and which visible surfaces actually respond. A single image
underdetermines hidden mechanisms and physical parameters, and a generated mesh need not expose the semantic parts
required for interaction. Furthermore, a valid joint or deformable simulation does not ensure that the corresponding
detailed surface moves with it. Addressing these discrepancies can require changes to geometry and physical structure,
creating a further tension between functional construction and preservation of the depicted appearance.
We introduce PhysAg3D, a framework for single-image interactive asset generation through generative–agentic coupling.
Our central idea is to combine the instance-specific geometry and appearance supplied by a pretrained 3D generator
with an agent’s ability to reason about object behavior and construct executable physical models. The generated asset
provides a detailed visual foundation; agentic reasoning turns this foundation into an interactive realization and revises it
in response to observed behavior. This division of capabilities allows physical construction to make use of rich generated
surfaces while remaining responsive to the structure and interactions of the depicted object. Figure 1 illustrates this
goal through mixer-head articulation, compass-lid closure, telescope reorientation, and local ear-pad deformation. The
examples associate actions with specific moving or deforming regions while retaining recognizable object structure and
surface detail.
Specifically, a vision-language agent first infers revisable semantic and physical priors from the image, and a 3D generator
produces a textured whole-object mesh from the same image. We propose a hierarchical interaction representation
for this coupling, connecting semantic requirements, physical mechanisms, and concrete asset realizations. Explicit
correspondences associate intended interactions with the structures and visible surfaces that implement them. Guided by
these relationships and observations of the mesh, the agent uses geometric and physical tools to construct parts, joints or
compliant regions, and surface bindings while retaining useful generated geometry and materials. Rendered interactions
and execution measurements then guide targeted revisions with bounded construction and validation attempts. The
representation expresses both rigid articulation and compliant deformation through their respective relationships between
physical state and visible appearance. Our contributions are threefold:
• A generative–agentic framework that grounds interaction construction in image-derived priors while retaining useful
surfaces and materials from detailed whole-object generation.
• A hierarchical interaction representation that connects requirements, mechanisms, and concrete surface realizations
to organize construction and feedback-guided refinement.
• Quantitative and qualitative evaluations on real photographs and external benchmarks, assessing appearance, image
alignment, and interaction attributes, with ablations of bounded refinement and source-surface preservation.
2 Related Work
Image-conditioned 3D asset generation.
Early text-to-3D systems distilled diffusion priors into 3D representa-
tions (Poole et al., 2023; Jun & Nichol, 2023; Nichol et al., 2022; Lin et al., 2023; Qian et al., 2023; Tang et al., 2023;
Du et al., 2026); feed-forward and multi-view methods improved single-image consistency and speed (Liu et al., 2023a;
Shi et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2023b; Hong et al., 2024; Long et al., 2024; Du et al., 2024b; Tochil et al., 2024; Xu et al.,
2024; Voleti et al., 2024; Tang et al., 2024). TRELLIS introduces structured 3D latents, and TRELLIS.2 supports
detailed geometry and physically based materials (Xiang et al., 2025b;a). UniLat3D unifies geometry and appearance in
a shared latent space for single-stage image-to-3D generation (Wu et al., 2026). SINGAPO conditions articulated parts
on images and part relationships (Liu et al., 2025); PAct jointly generates geometry, composition, and articulation (Liu

et al., 2026); PhysX-Omni covers rigid, deformable, and articulated objects (Cao et al., 2026). Shape, surface, and
texture reconstruction further improve appearance (Gao et al., 2022; Li et al., 2023b; Huang et al., 2023c; Cao et al.,
2023; Chen et al., 2024; Guo et al., 2020; Müller et al., 2022; Laine et al., 2020). More recently, SUPRA-TEX generates
relightable materials faithful to reference images by anchoring visible-surface observations in material latent space while
completing unobserved regions (Yang et al., 2026). PhysAg3D studies agent-driven construction and revision around a
detailed whole-object reconstruction, linking image-derived requirements to mechanisms and visible surfaces.
Articulation and physical modeling of 3D assets.
PartNet, PartNet-Mobility, GAPartNet, and Shape2Motion support
research on parts, affordances, and mobility (Mo et al., 2019; Xia et al., 2020; Yu et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2019).
Articulate AnyMesh segments movable parts and refines joints for open-vocabulary meshes (Qiu et al., 2025). Particulate
predicts segmentation, kinematics, and motion constraints in one pass (Li et al., 2025); Instruct-Particulate adds
semantic and point-prompt conditioning, combining 3D generation with vision-language inference for image-to-asset
articulation (Li et al., 2026b). PhysAg3D focuses on connecting intended interactions, physical entities, exported
bindings, and observed surface responses, including compliant regions whose simulation and visible geometry differ.
Agentic 3D modeling and refinement.
Articulate-Anything uses vision-language reasoning and actor–critic refine-
ment for articulated modeling (Le et al., 2025). Language-guided robotics connects instructions to executable actions
and closed-loop spatial reasoning (Ahn et al., 2022; Li et al., 2023a; Huang et al., 2023b;a; Driess et al., 2023; Brohan
et al., 2023), while planning studies emphasize replanning and verification (Singh et al., 2023; Valmeekam et al., 2023).
ArtiCraft provides a part-and-joint SDK, object-specific tests, and structured execution feedback (Zhou et al., 2026).
aDSL unifies hierarchical modeling and spatial relations for planning, execution, and critique, combining procedural
scaffolds with TRELLIS through SpaceControl (Wang et al., 2026). Shape programs and CAD languages offer related
compositional abstractions and persistent references (Jones et al., 2020; 2025; Cascaval et al., 2023). PhysAg3D links
requirements, mechanisms, and concrete realizations to generated surfaces. Visual and execution evidence guides local
revision while preserving useful geometry.
Simulation and visual grounding substrates.
MuJoCo, PyBullet, Isaac Gym, and differentiable simulators provide
complementary dynamics and contact substrates (Todorov et al., 2012; Coumans & Bai, 2016; Makoviychuk et al.,
2021; Hu et al., 2020). Segment Anything, DINOv2, and CLIP provide visual grouping and image–text alignment
primitives (Kirillov et al., 2023; Oquab et al., 2024; Radford et al., 2021). These form contextual foundations for
PhysAg3D’s explicit interaction-to-surface representation.
3 Method
3.1 Task Definition and Framework Overview
Given a single image I, PhysAg3D generates a textured 3D asset with object-specific interactions. An asset contains
visible geometry and materials, physical entities and parameters, controls, and bindings from physical state to visible
surfaces. We evaluate appearance, image alignment, and physical behavior separately; construction targets the declared
interactions, since hidden structure, scale, and material parameters are not determined by one image.
Figure 2 summarizes the workflow. Image interpretation produces revisable semantic and physical hypotheses, while
a pretrained generator provides a detailed whole-object mesh. Mechanism reasoning connects these sources through
interaction intent S (what should happen), physical structure K (which mechanism produces it), and concrete realization
X (the implementing asset entities and bindings). Asset construction realizes these mechanisms and bindings; evidence
attribution links observations to the responsible regions for local revision. These are functional roles in one agentic loop.
Appendix A details the interaction representation, construction procedures, validation checks, and candidate-selection
policy.
3.2 Generative–Agentic Coupling
Image priors and generated geometry.
A vision-language agent infers provisional hypotheses Π0 = Φ(I) about
parts, materials, connections, candidate interactions, and scale, while retaining ambiguities. A pretrained generator
produces a textured whole-object mesh M0 = G(I); our implementation uses TRELLIS.2 (Xiang et al., 2025a). The
agent inspects rendered views and geometry of M0 before fixing partitions or mechanisms. Priors guide construction but
do not condition G, so the coupling occurs through interpretation, construction, and feedback without joint training.

Hierarchical interaction representation.
For candidate version v, we define
Rv = (Sv, Kv, Xv, Γv, ρv),
Hv = (Rv, Ev),
(1)
where S, K, and X encode semantic requirements, mechanisms, and concrete realizations; Γ links their entities; ρ
records correspondence to visible regions of M0; and E stores candidate observations. The revision index v is distinct
from execution time t.
A requirement is
r = (Ur, ar, Or, ψr, Dr),
(2)
where Ur is the target region, ar the action protocol, Or the observation, ψr the response test, and Dr the initial
conditions and scope. Thus, lid opening is measured relative to its support, while local compliance is measured after
removing common rigid motion. Material compliance and articulation are separate attributes.
The mechanism level K specifies rigid or compliant domains, supports, connections, degrees of freedom, joint frames,
limits, and material assumptions. The realization level X identifies actual meshes, materials, scene nodes, controls,
simulation entities, and exported bindings, while retaining construction or loading failures. An intended mechanism can
therefore remain valid even when its realization is incomplete. In Figure 2, “Interaction intent” denotes S, “Physical
structure” denotes K, and “Visible surfaces” depicts the surface component of X, whose full scope includes the
implementing entities and bindings.
Correspondences across levels and surfaces.
The relations Γ connect requirements to mechanisms, mechanisms to
realized entities, semantic parts to visible regions, and physical drivers to bindings. The source map ρ records retained,
modified, added, deleted, and unresolved surface/material regions relative to M0. In Figure 2, the inter-level links
illustrate Γ, the native surface and material anchors illustrate ρ, and attributed observations contribute to E. These maps
distinguish intended relationships from exported ones: an intended hinged panel may be found attached to the base.
Topology changes require an updated correspondence rather than reuse of obsolete face indices. The hierarchy expresses
progressively stronger commitments about one candidate, not a fixed sequence of agent roles; requirements can select
mechanisms, and observations can retrieve the associated entities for inspection.

3.4 Refinement with Visual and Physical Feedback
Each candidate is evaluated with complementary evidence: renders assess image alignment, retained appearance, part
separation, and visible response; source checks assess preserved surfaces and materials; execution checks assess actions,
ranges, contacts, supports, and applicable deformation or recovery. MuJoCo (Todorov et al., 2012) is used for supported
physical models. A moving proxy, a joint reaching a target, and the intended visible surface responding are distinct
observations. Each Ev record stores the requirement, target entities, candidate identity, action, initial state, runtime,
and assessment configuration, with outcomes marked unassessed, pass, fail, or inconclusive. Errors without trajectories
provide no measured response, and earlier dynamic evidence is invalidated after a build change.
For an unmet requirement, feedback combines the discrepancy or missing evidence with the related mechanisms, realized
entities, and bindings obtained through Γv. Given remaining allowances bv, the agent proposes
ov ∼π(I, Rv, Ev, bv),
Xv+1 = Apply(Xv, ov),
Rv+1 = Reconcile(Rv, ov, Xv+1; M0).
(5)
Correspondences localize inspection but do not guarantee a causal diagnosis: failures may arise from partitions, frames,
supports, controls, material assumptions, bindings, or observation definitions. Reconciliation records the actual export
and keeps unresolved discrepancies; accepted requirements remain traceable across revisions, so a reduced motion range
cannot silently satisfy a full-closure requirement.
A declared policy bounds construction and validation and preserves prior candidates when revisions fail. The system
returns the best eligible candidate under a fixed evidence-based selection rule, together with its interactive preview and
unresolved requirements. A preview may be useful without a valid simulation trajectory, while its dynamics remain
unverified. Appendix A gives the implementation budgets, checks, and selection policy.

Common protocol.
All comparisons fix input lists, method versions, and inference configurations; image-conditioned
methods receive the same source image. We record auxiliary conditioning, output support, and computational cost.
Candidate selection uses only within-run evidence, without benchmark annotations or final evaluation scores. Refinement
permits at most two Builder repairs after initial construction and returns the best available candidate. All attempted
inputs remain in the outcome accounting. Static assets are evaluated for input consistency; missing or invalid interaction
videos count as failures within the fixed action denominator.

4.2 Single-Image Interactive Generation in the Wild
Benchmark and comparisons.
Our benchmark contains more than 200 real-object photographs; evaluation uses
a fixed subset of 50 objects with 110 predefined semantic actions, spanning rigid, compliant, and mixed objects.
Development cases are identified separately, and method-specific preprocessing is documented. We compare PhysAg3D
with Particulate, PhysX-Omni, PAct, ArtiCraft, and aDSL (Li et al., 2025; Cao et al., 2026; Liu et al., 2026; Zhou et al.,
2026; Wang et al., 2026). Additional qualitative comparisons appear in Appendix C.
Evaluation metrics.
For input-view consistency, a fixed spatial grid search selects the rendered camera view closest to
the input; foreground IoU, PSNR, and CLIP similarity are computed on the aligned pair. Action Completion Rate (ACR)
measures correct semantic completion. Strict Mis-Motion Rate (MMR) counts missing, incomplete, incorrectly related,
structurally broken, or spuriously coupled actions. Video-based Range Achievement Rate (RAR) scores the maximum
valid visible range in {0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1} after the ACR semantic gate. These three metrics are macro-averaged over
objects. Function-and-Visible-Stability Pass (FVSP) is the fraction of objects completing all expected actions without
video-visible instability. It assesses rendered video evidence, not simulator-log stability.
PhysAg3D leads all reported input-consistency and interaction metrics in Table 1, reaching 91.3% ACR and 88.0%
FVSP versus 66.8% and 42.0% for aDSL, the strongest baseline on these two measures. Figure 3 compares appearance
and visible interaction states under consistent rendering conditions.

4.3 Evaluation on External Benchmarks
Reference-based evaluation.

5 Conclusion
We presented PhysAg3D for generating interactive 3D assets from a single image. Its hierarchical representation
connects interaction requirements, physical mechanisms, and visible surfaces to guide construction and feedback-based
refinement. Experiments support appearance preservation and plausible interactions, with ablations indicating benefits
from refinement and surface preservation. Recovering hidden structure and material properties remains a limitation.

A Method and Implementation Details
A.1 Interaction Representation
The representation in Section 3.2 separates interaction intent, mechanism design, and concrete assets. The semantic
level S identifies the target region, action, expected response, and observation frame. The mechanism level K specifies
bodies or compliant domains, connections, degrees of freedom, supports, and parameter assumptions. The realization
level X identifies the meshes, materials, scene entities, controls, and exported bindings. The relations Γ connect these
levels; ρ records the origin and modification of source surfaces; and E associates observations with the candidate that
produced them. These correspondences link interaction requirements to mechanisms, realized entities, and surface
bindings for construction and feedback-guided revision, as defined in Section 3.2. The following subsections detail the
associated construction and validation procedures.
For example, a lid-opening requirement refers to the lid region and measures its motion relative to the base. Its
mechanism identifies the hinge axis and permitted range, while its realization identifies the moving body and the
visible lid faces assigned to it. This correspondence allows the hinge configuration and visible-surface assignment to be
inspected separately; it localizes the relevant entities without by itself establishing the cause of a failure. For a padded
region, the corresponding records identify the load, support, physical discretization, and visible skin. These examples
explain the representation without assuming that hidden mechanisms or material parameters are uniquely recoverable
from the input image.
A.2 Geometry, Materials, and Surface Binding
The initial TRELLIS.2 reconstruction supplies the detailed visible geometry and materials. Geometric refinement uses
rendered views and measurements to identify actionable regions: mesh connectivity alone does not determine which
parts should move, and a texture boundary need not be a mechanical boundary. Bounded edits preserve useful source
faces, UV coordinates, and material assignments. When topology changes, source correspondence must be updated
rather than inherited from obsolete face indices.
For rigid articulation, visible vertices follow the transform of their assigned body. Correct body ownership, local
transforms, parent–child relationships, joint frames, and limits are all needed to produce the intended visible motion.
Collision geometry can differ from the detailed render mesh, but motion of a collision proxy alone does not verify
motion of the visible part.
For compliant regions, the physical discretization and visible skin may also differ. The displacement binding in Eq. 4
transfers physical-node displacements to visible vertices in a common frame. Its node identities, dimensions, weights,
and rest geometry are checked against the exported asset. Supports and loading conditions are specified separately
from the skin binding. A local deformation observation removes common rigid motion before assessing shape change;
otherwise, movement of the entire object could be mistaken for local compliance. The ear-pad example uses an
approximate compliance model rather than a material calibration recovered from the image.
A.3 Feedback and Candidate Records
Rendered observations assess appearance, part separation, and visible response. Execution observations assess the
requested action, achieved range, contacts, supports, and applicable deformation or recovery. Each record identifies
the requirement, candidate, target region and entities, action, initial state, runtime and assessment configurations, and
assessment outcome. The four outcomes are unassessed, pass, fail, and inconclusive. An execution error supplies no
measured trajectory, and a changed build requires renewed dynamic evidence.
Feedback associates a discrepancy with the relevant mechanism, realized entity, or binding. Local repairs can address
motion range, tracking, surface assignment, or assembly; structural replanning addresses topology, ownership, joint-axis,
or material contradictions. Previous candidates remain available when a revision fails. The budgets and selection
procedure below bound this loop.

The original interval remains the reference across repairs. The retained span must be at least 0.2(h0 −ℓ0) and, for a
hinge, at least 10◦. A reduced interval is considered only after a completed failed sweep with a valid rest pose. The
checks include rest and both motion directions where applicable. A colliding rest pose, missing clearance information,
incomplete sweep, or execution error terminates this search. The achieved range is retained explicitly; reducing travel
does not satisfy an unchanged full-closure requirement.
Let D be the positive asset diagonal in meters. The documented acceptance profile uses a contact-proxy penetration
tolerance of 0.005D, an angular endpoint tolerance of 5◦, and a translational endpoint tolerance of 0.005D. Addi-
tional checks cover finite states, solver warnings, joint limits, measured interaction, and applicable compliant-region
requirements. Geometric sweep checks apply to sampled configurations and do not replace dynamic execution.
For a declared compliant region, a least-squares rigid alignment removes common motion from the simulation nodes.
Let dmax be the maximum residual over recorded samples and phase endpoints. The response criterion requires
dmax ≥0.001D together with a pairwise-distance-change check. When recovery is required, the residual after
unloading must not exceed max(0.001D, 0.35dmax). These criteria are deformation heuristics on simulation nodes;
exported-surface response is assessed separately through the binding and visible observations.
A.6 Output Selection
Selection excludes unloadable, empty, nonfinite, externally dependent assets and detected source-identity conflicts. In
autonomous runs, eligible candidates are ranked by appearance evidence, native retention, semantic evidence, absence of
unsupported additions, display checks, declared bodies, supported physics acceptance, and recency, in that order. Native
retention includes source identity, face accounting, geometry, and UV/material checks. As specified in Section 4.5,
Full pipeline and w/o surface preservation use the same selection rule. In the latter, source-geometry, UV, and material
preservation constraints are removed from construction, while the shared ranking criteria and physical-to-visible bindings
remain. This intervention therefore tests the construction constraints under a fixed output-selection policy. Supported
physics acceptance requires a passing report, at least three trajectory states, and matching asset and trajectory identities.
A preview can be returned without a valid dynamic trajectory, with that status kept distinct. If no refined candidate

ACR measures correct semantic action completion. Strict MMR counts missing, incomplete, incorrectly related,
structurally broken, or spuriously coupled actions. RAR scores the maximum valid visible range in {0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1}
after the ACR semantic gate. FVSP is the percentage of objects that complete every expected action without video-visible
instability. It is a joint functional-and-visible-stability criterion based on rendered videos, not simulator logs.
Support marks indicate declared or evaluated capabilities, not successful outputs. Static assets remain evaluable for
input consistency; missing or invalid interaction videos count as failures within the fixed action denominator. Failed and
inconclusive attempts remain in the outcome accounting, with unsupported output types distinguished in the records.
Development examples remain separate from the benchmark comparison.
B.2 Inputs, Baselines, and Scoring
Section 4.1 specifies the common comparison protocol. Image-conditioned methods receive the same photograph;
mesh-conditioned methods require an identified reconstruction and any auxiliary conditioning. Format and coordinate
conversions are distinguished from geometry repair or inference of missing physical parameters, which would change the
evaluated method. PhysXGen denotes the framework introduced in PhysX-3D (Cao et al., 2025a). aDSL reports image-
to-shape generation and articulated creation as a downstream application, motivating its inclusion in the single-image
comparison.
Reference-based comparisons use compatible alignment, pose, sampling, distance normalization, and scoring settings.
Shape normalization must preserve the separately assessed absolute-scale error. Table 2 follows the PhysXVerse/PhysX-
Mobility split reported by PhysX-Omni; its kinematic score is not a dynamics success rate. Missing scores, marked by a
dash or NR, are unreported entries rather than measured zeros.
PhysX-Bench uses fixed evidence generation, evaluator configuration, and prompts across methods. Joint playback
supplies articulation evidence, while material response and static appearance are separate observations; playback does
not establish dynamic contact stability. The selected states in Figure 4 likewise demonstrate visible articulation and
surface appearance. Table 7 summarizes the evaluation settings.
B.3 Ablation Configuration
The three variants in Section 4.5 share inputs, cached reconstructions, initial priors, model and decoding settings,
construction tools, and final scoring. Full pipeline and w/o surface preservation share the two-repair limit and candidate-
selection rule. One-shot returns the initial candidate and receives final scoring without feeding it back for repair. All
attempted inputs, including failed and inconclusive runs, remain in the outcome accounting.
The source-preservation ablation removes construction-time geometry, UV, and material constraints while retaining the
initial reconstruction and physical-to-visible bindings. It keeps output selection fixed, including the native-retention
ranking criterion. The One-shot comparison measures refinement as a whole, including additional repair and candidate-
selection opportunities; it does not isolate an individual feedback signal.
