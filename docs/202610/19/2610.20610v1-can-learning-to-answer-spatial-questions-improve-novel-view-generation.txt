ABSTRACT
Generating novel views from limited observations requires preserving both the
appearance and spatial structure of a scene. Although recent approaches exploit
fine-grained geometric cues, they do not explicitly model high-level semantic and
spatial properties such as object identity, attributes, and inter-object relationships.
In this work, we investigate whether 3D language understanding can provide com-
plementary supervision for novel-view generation. We introduce Ask4NV, built
on a pretrained unified multimodal model with a Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT)
architecture. Our model incorporates camera geometry into both VAE and ViT
representations and leverages understanding features during generation through
joint self-attention with a modality-aware attention masking strategy. Experiments
demonstrate strong image quality and geometric consistency in novel-view gener-
ation, alongside competitive 3D language understanding. Further analysis shows
that joint training improves cross-view correspondence in VAE representations
and similarity in a 3D-aware feature space.

language driving (Wang et al., 2025c).
While such fine-grained geometric cues reveal the scene structure and dense correspondences across
viewpoints, they do not explicitly offer the interpretation of high-level scene properties, such as
object identities, attributes, and inter-object relationships. Such high-level understanding can also
guide novel-view generation by informing how objects and their spatial arrangements should appear
from a different viewpoint. For example, recognizing a red chair beside a wooden table can guide
the model to preserve the chair’s identity, attributes, and relationship to the table, even as its visible
shape and image position change with the viewpoint. Learning these semantic and spatial properties
could therefore complement fine-grained geometric information, helping the model preserve scene
content and represent its spatial organization consistently across views.
However, the potential of explicitly learning such high-level properties for novel-view generation
remains largely unexplored. We then ask: How can we provide such high-level semantic and spatial
supervision, and how can it be incorporated into joint learning with novel-view generation? We
investigate this direction through 3D language understanding tasks (Hong et al., 2023; Huang et al.,
2023; Zheng et al., 2025b), which involve identifying and localizing objects and reasoning about
their attributes and spatial relationships. Fig. 2 highlights the shared semantic and spatial proper-
ties underlying 3D language understanding and novel-view generation, suggesting the potential of
language-based supervision to support generation. To jointly learn novel-view generation and 3D
language understanding, we adopt pretrained unified multimodal models (Xie et al., 2025; Team,
2024), which support both visual generation and multimodal understanding within a single model.
Concretely, we introduce Ask4NV, which builds on a pretrained Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT)
architecture (Liang et al., 2024; Deng et al., 2025) and introduces a small set of architectural modifi-
cations to jointly model 3D language understanding and novel-view generation. The MoT architec-
ture provides task-specific parameters together with joint self-attention between the two transform-
ers, allowing the two tasks to be learned within a shared framework without introducing a separate
cross-task fusion module. For novel-view generation, we use VAE latents alongside complementary
ViT features from the understanding transformer and incorporate camera geometry into both rep-
resentations through Pl¨ucker ray embeddings. We further design an attention masking strategy to
facilitate information exchange across different token modalities during novel-view generation.
Our analyses examine how joint training with 3D language understanding affects the model’s visual
representations and novel-view generation capabilities. Extensive experiments show that our model
achieves strong novel-view generation performance in both image quality and geometric consistency
across multiple evaluation settings. It also performs competitively on 3D language understanding
despite the limited performance of its pretrained backbone on these tasks. Ablation studies further
validate our architectural and attention design choices.

Novel-view generation.
We follow a common formulation of prior novel-view generation ap-
proaches (Gao et al., 2024; Kwon et al., 2025), which generate target images given a set of source

these approaches aim to synthesize unseen views that preserve scene appearance and maintain ge-
ometric consistency, including interpolated and challenging extrapolated viewpoints. Formally, let
Isrc denote a set of N source images and Csrc their corresponding camera parameters. Given the
camera parameters Ctgt for M target views, the model generates the corresponding target images:

3D language understanding. We consider 3D question answering (Ma et al., 2022; Azuma et al.,

Shared scene properties across tasks. Fig. 2 presents examples of 3D language understanding
and novel-view generation concerning the same underlying scene properties. In the top example,
answering the question requires distinguishing the cabinet’s color (attributes), which novel-view
generation should also preserve when predicting the cabinet from a different viewpoint. In the
middle example, the question is about the stool’s relative position (spatial relationship), while gen-
eration requires depicting this arrangement from the target viewpoint. In the bottom example, iden-
tifying the chair as an armchair (object category) requires recognizing characteristic features that
novel-view generation should preserve across viewpoints. These examples suggest that learning to
interpret scene properties through language can provide complementary supervision for novel-view
generation. We use this supervision during training to develop representations useful for generation,
without requiring question answering when generating novel views.

3.2
ENHANCING NOVEL-VIEW GENERATION THROUGH 3D LANGUAGE UNDERSTANDING
We incorporate visual representations processed by the understanding transformer into novel-view
generation, allowing generation to explicitly utilize semantic and spatial information. Specifically,
we extract ViT features from all source views and include them alongside the VAE tokens. The
understanding transformer processes these ViT tokens, and the generation transformer accesses their
representations through joint self-attention.
Similar to the VAE representations, we incorporate camera information into the ViT features using
Pl¨ucker ray embeddings. We preserve the pretrained ViT projector’s joint spatial merging and chan-
nel projection by adding ray embeddings to its output. For each output visual token, we compute
the ray at the center of its corresponding image region and map it into the language model’s hidden
space using a two-layer MLP:
 \ti

(7)
where rvit ∈RNvit×6 contains the rays corresponding to all source-view ViT tokens, and the MLP
is applied independently to each ray.
We concatenate the camera-aware source VAE and ViT tokens, noisy target VAE tokens, and text
tokens into an input sequence. The two Transformers process their respective tokens through joint
self-attention, with an attention mask controlling information flow among token groups. Noisy target
tokens can attend to both source VAE and ViT tokens, accessing source appearance and represen-
tations from the understanding transformer. Source ViT tokens attend bidirectionally to clean VAE
tokens but do not directly attend to noisy target tokens. The complete attention pattern, including
the bidirectional VAE attention described in Sec. 3.1, is illustrated in Fig. 3(c).
The resulting target hidden states are projected to velocity predictions, and the model is trained
with the flow-matching objective. At inference, we initialize the target latents with Gaussian noise,
integrate the predicted velocity field, and decode the resulting latents into target images using the
VAE decoder.

EXPERIMENTS
5.1
EXPERIMENTAL SETTING
Implementation and training details. We build our model on Lance-Video-3B (Fu et al., 2026),
a pretrained MoT-based unified multimodal model, and fine-tune it while freezing the ViT encoder
and the VAE encoder and decoder. Our 3D language understanding training data comprise ScanNet-
based datasets, including SQA3D (Ma et al., 2022), ScanQA (Azuma et al., 2022), ScanRefer (Chen
et al., 2020), and Multi3DRefer (Zhang et al., 2023). We therefore use ScanNet (Dai et al., 2017)
for novel-view generation to align the scene domains across the two tasks. Given its limited number
of scenes, we supplement the generation training data with DL3DV (Ling et al., 2024). We use
multimodal sequence packing, with each pack containing three novel-view generation samples and
one 3D language understanding sample. This 3 : 1 ratio approximates the pretraining task balance
within our computational budget. The understanding task uses V = 32 uniformly sampled frames
per scene. For novel-view generation, we sample 8 frames, using 2–4 as source views and the
remainder as target views. Additional implementation details are provided in Appendix A.3.

CONCLUSIONS
In this work, we investigate how joint training with 3D language understanding can enhance novel-
view generation. We introduce Ask4NV, which adapts a pretrained MoT-based architecture through
ViT conditioning and a tailored attention mask. Experiments demonstrate improvements in image
fidelity and geometric consistency across multiple benchmarks, while maintaining competitive 3D
language understanding performance. Our analyses show improved cross-view correspondence in
VAE representations and stronger alignment with ground-truth views in a 3D-aware feature space.
Ablation studies further validate the benefits of joint training and our architectural choices. These
findings highlight the potential of 3D language understanding as complementary supervision for
generative scene modeling.

A.1
DATASET DETAILS
Novel view generation. We use ScanNet (Dai et al., 2017) for both training and evaluation, as
our 3D language understanding datasets are also based on ScanNet. Given the limited number of
scenes in ScanNet, we supplement the training data with DL3DV (Ling et al., 2024) to increase
scene diversity and reduce the risk of overfitting. We additionally evaluate on RealEstate10K (Zhou
et al., 2018) and ScanNet++ (Yeshwanth et al., 2023), neither of which is used for training, to assess
cross-dataset generalization.
3D language understanding. We use the preprocessed ScanNet-based datasets provided by Video-
3D-LLM (Zheng et al., 2025b), including SQA3D (Ma et al., 2022), ScanQA (Azuma et al., 2022),
ScanRefer (Chen et al., 2020), and Multi3DRefer (Zhang et al., 2023).
Camera normalization. For both tasks, we express camera poses relative to the first input camera
and normalize camera centers by their mean pairwise distance. For novel-view generation, this
distance is computed over both source and target camera centers. We apply the same coordinate
transformation and scale normalization to the associated 3D points and object boxes.

A.3
TRAINING AND ARCHITECTURE DETAILS.
Training configuration. We train our model using the Adam8bit optimizer (Dettmers et al., 2021)
on 4 A100 GPUs, and accumulate gradients over 64 micro-steps per optimizer update. We train for
728 optimizer steps, corresponding to one epoch over the 3D language understanding data, with a
linear warmup over 40 steps to 5 × 10−5, followed by a constant learning rate (held at 5 × 10−5).
For novel-view generation, we use a timestep shift of 4.0 and apply conditioning dropout with a
probability of 0.1 to each of the text and camera conditions. For 3D visual grounding, we set the
temperature hyperparameter τgrd = 0.07. Following our backbone, we set the loss weights λgen and
λund to 1.0 and 0.25, respectively. For a fair comparison, Lance-FT, Lance-FT + ViT, and Ask4NV
are trained for the same number of optimizer steps and observe the same number of novel-view
generation samples.
Architectural details. We expand the input dimension of the pretrained projector Pvae from Cz
to ˜Cz = Cz + Cr to accommodate the concatenated ray representations. We retain the pretrained
weights for the latent channels and zero-initialize the weights for the additional ray channels. We
resize input images to 480 × 480 for the VAE encoder and 476 × 476 for the ViT encoder, ensuring
compatibility with the VAE’s spatial compression factor of 16 and the ViT’s patch size of 14 × 14.
We apply temporal padding for VAE encoding to eliminate the effect of its temporal compression
while maintaining a correspondence between input views and latent representations.

EVALUATION DETAILS.
Camera-pose accuracy. In addition to PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS, we evaluate camera-pose accu-
racy and multi-view consistency. For camera-pose evaluation, we jointly estimate poses from the
generated target views using a fixed VGGT-Ω-1B-512 checkpoint (Wang et al., 2026), without in-
cluding source images or ground-truth target images. We align the estimated camera centers to the
corresponding ground-truth centers using a least-squares Sim(3) transformation computed with the
Umeyama algorithm.
We report Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) as the root mean squared error (RMSE) of the aligned
target camera centers. We also compute Relative Pose Errors for rotation (RPEr) and translation
(RPEt) between consecutive target views, reusing the same Sim(3) alignment. Rotation error is
measured by the geodesic angle in degrees, and translation error by the Euclidean norm of the
translation component of the relative-pose error. We report RMSE across the evaluated pairs, with
ATE and RPEt expressed in ground-truth coordinate units.
Multi-view consistency. We evaluate multi-view consistency using a variant of MEt3R (Asim et al.,
2025) that replaces its original point-map reconstruction backbone with VGGT-Ω, using the same

reconstructed point maps, we reproject and render both feature maps into a common viewpoint at

Setting of baselines. The evaluated baselines differ in their input requirements, including their
reliance on external modules for geometric estimation or number of source or target views. For a
fair comparison on our benchmarks, we follow a common evaluation protocol while preserving the
conditioning requirements of each method. Below, we describe the method-specific preprocessing
and inference settings used for evaluation.
We focus our comparison on methods that generate novel views without explicit depth-based warp-
ing or point-cloud rendering as inference-time conditioning. Accordingly, we exclude MVGenMas-
ter (Cao et al., 2025), GEN3C (Ren et al., 2025), and ViewCrafter (Yu et al., 2024), which use such
geometric guidance during generation.
CAT3D (Gao et al., 2024): Since the official implementation is not publicly available, we use the
reproduction provided by CAMEO (Kwon et al., 2025). This reproduction is trained with camera
parameters at the scale of VGGT (Wang et al., 2025b), so we use VGGT-estimated camera param-
eters during evaluation as well. As the model is trained exclusively on Re10K, we treat DL3DV,
ScanNet, and ScanNet++ as out-of-domain evaluation datasets.
NVComposer (Li et al., 2025): We use source images at 512 × 512 resolution and ground-truth
camera parameters. Since the model processes a fixed set of 16 views, we fill the remaining slots
with cameras interpolated between the first source view and the target views, and evaluate only the
target views. As ScanNet and ScanNet++ are unseen during NVComposer’s training, we treat them
as out-of-domain evaluation datasets.
CAMEO (Kwon et al., 2025): As CAMEO shares the architecture and inference pipeline of the
CAT3D reproduction, we likewise use camera parameters estimated by VGGT (Wang et al., 2025b)
during evaluation. It is also trained exclusively on Re10K, so we treat DL3DV, ScanNet, and Scan-
Net++ as out-of-domain evaluation datasets.
Matrix3D (Lu et al., 2025): The model generates the target images in a single pass, conditioned
on the source images and the ground-truth camera parameters of all views. It is trained on Ob-
javerse (Deitke et al., 2023), MVImgNet (Yu et al., 2023), CO3D-v2 (Reizenstein et al., 2021),
Re10K (Zhou et al., 2018), Hypersim (Roberts et al., 2021), and ARKitScenes (Baruch et al., 2021).
We therefore treat DL3DV, ScanNet, and ScanNet++ as out-of-domain evaluation datasets.
Omni-View (Hu et al., 2026): Omni-View is a unified multiview 3D model that couples understand-
ing, texture (appearance), and geometry modules. Novel views are synthesized by its texture mod-
ule. Its novel-view generation training uses only Re10K. Thus, DL3DV, ScanNet, and ScanNet++
are outside its generation training distribution, although ScanNet-based datasets are used for under-
standing training. We evaluate its texture module using four source views at 512 × 512 resolution
and the ground-truth source and target camera parameters.

B
RELATED WORK
B.1
NOVEL VIEW GENERATION
Novel-view synthesis (NVS) aims to predict the appearance of a scene from unseen viewpoints
given observed views. Reconstruction-based approaches, such as NeRF (Mildenhall et al., 2021)
and 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023), model scenes using 3D representations from which
novel views are rendered. Recent advances in generative modeling (Ho et al., 2020; Rombach et al.,
2022; Peebles & Xie, 2023) have enabled novel-view generation from sparse observations by lever-
aging learned visual priors. To adapt these priors to 3D scenes, existing methods explore cam-
era conditioning (Gao et al., 2024; Sargent et al., 2024), geometric guidance (Cao et al., 2025; Li
et al., 2025), cross-view consistency (Kwon et al., 2025; M¨uller et al., 2024), and geometry-aware
latent representations (Jang et al., 2026). While these approaches primarily focus on view synthe-
sis, Omni-View (Hu et al., 2026) jointly models novel-view synthesis, 3D language understanding,
and geometric estimation, emphasizing how generation facilitates understanding. Our work instead
investigates how semantic and spatial supervision from 3D language understanding can improve
novel-view generation. We pursue this direction through joint training on overlapping scene do-
mains and explicitly use the understanding expert’s visual representations during generation.
B.2
3D LANGUAGE UNDERSTANDING
Understanding 3D scenes through natural language involves diverse tasks such as 3D question an-
swering (Azuma et al., 2022; Ma et al., 2026; 2022; Yang et al., 2025), 3D dense captioning (Chen
et al., 2021; 2023; 2024a), and 3D visual grounding (Chen et al., 2020; Zhang et al., 2023). A
line of open-vocabulary 3D understanding methods lifts semantic knowledge from 2D foundation
models, particularly CLIP (Radford et al., 2021) and LSeg (Li et al., 2022), into 3D representations
such as point clouds (Jatavallabhula et al., 2023; Yang et al., 2024; Zhou et al., 2025), neural radi-
ance fields (Engelmann et al., 2024; Kerr et al., 2023), and 3D Gaussians (Jun-Seong et al., 2025;
Qin et al., 2024; Shi et al., 2024). Building on recent developments in 2D Multimodal Large Lan-
guage Models (MLLMs) (Bai et al., 2025; Hurst et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2023), numerous efforts aim
to extend multimodal understanding to 3D environments by introducing geometric knowledge into
these models in various ways. Some works incorporate spatial information through 3D positional
embeddings (Zheng et al., 2025b; Zhu et al., 2024), voxelized scene features (Fu et al., 2024a), or
bird’s-eye-view representations (Qi et al., 2026), whereas others inject geometric priors through dis-
tillation (Huang et al., 2026) or fusion (Fan et al., 2026; Zheng et al., 2025a) of 3D-aware features.
Ross3D (Wang et al., 2025a) and G2VLM (Hu et al., 2025) take a different approach by combining
3D language understanding with reconstruction, exploiting their complementary signals to improve
spatial understanding. Imagine3D-LLM (Jung et al., 2026) improves 3D language understanding by
learning compact Gaussian scene representations through reconstruction supervision.
B.3
UNIFIED MULTIMODAL MODEL
Unified multimodal models integrate multimodal understanding and visual generation within a sin-
gle framework. Chameleon (Team, 2024) and Emu3 (Wang et al., 2024) unify these capabilities
through autoregressive next-token prediction. Janus (Wu et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2025b) uses sep-
arate visual encoders for understanding and generation while retaining a unified Transformer back-
bone. Other approaches combine next-token prediction with diffusion-based visual generation (Chen
et al., 2025a; Xie et al., 2025). Bagel (Deng et al., 2025) and Lance (Fu et al., 2026) adopt Mixture-
of-Transformers (MoT) architectures with specialized Transformer experts interacting through joint
self-attention. Recent studies also investigate knowledge transfer between understanding and gen-
eration (Kang et al., 2026). Building on this line of research, we focus on improving novel-view
generation through semantic and spatial supervision from 3D language understanding.

ADDITIONAL ABLATION AND ANALYSIS
C.1
ABLATION ON JOINT TRAINING DESIGN.
We examine how joint training and visual conditioning affect novel-view generation and 3D lan-
guage understanding. Joint denotes training on both tasks. Without joint training, the model is
trained only on the task being evaluated. Gen-ViT denotes ViT conditioning for novel-view gen-
eration, while Und-VAE denotes additional VAE conditioning for 3D language understanding. All
configurations with Gen-ViT use the same generation-side ViT conditioning, Pl¨ucker-ray embed-
dings, and attention mask. Thus, the Joint ✗and Gen-ViT ✓setting corresponds to Lance-FT + ViT
and differs from our final model only in the absence of 3D language understanding training. We use
the same datasets and evaluation protocols as in the main experiments. Our model enables Joint and
Gen-ViT without Und-VAE.
Novel-view generation. Table 7 shows that adding ViT conditioning improves PSNR, SSIM, and
LPIPS across all four datasets. Joint training without Und-VAE further improves all three image
fidelity metrics and most geometric consistency metrics compared with generation-only training
with ViT conditioning. However, additionally enabling Und-VAE reduces image fidelity on every
dataset and worsens most geometric metrics, despite improvements in rotation RPE on ScanNet and
ScanNet++ and translation RPE on ScanNet and ScanNet++. Our configuration achieves the best
image fidelity across all four datasets, supporting the use of joint training with ViT conditioning
without additional VAE conditioning for understanding.
3D language understanding. Table 8 shows relatively small variations in understanding perfor-
mance across the evaluated configurations. Under joint training, additional VAE conditioning yields
modest gains on SQA3D and visual grounding, while our configuration performs better on most
ScanQA metrics. Thus, Und-VAE provides no consistent benefit across understanding benchmarks.
Our configuration maintains broadly comparable understanding performance while achieving better
novel-view generation quality.

ADDITIONAL ANALYSIS ON CORRESPONDENCE OF VAE.
We extend the analysis in Sec. 4 by examining how cross-view correspondence develops during
training and visualizing the cosine similarity of VAE token representations across views. We mea-
sure token-level correspondence using the attention-output features at layer l = 26.
Correspondence throughout training. Figure 6 compares Ask4NV with Lance-FT throughout
training on ScanNet. Across all three thresholds, Ask4NV reaches the final correspondence accuracy
of Lance-FT substantially earlier and achieves higher accuracy throughout training after the initial
stage. These results suggest that our joint training design promotes the development of cross-view
consistent VAE representations.
Cross-view feature similarity. We visualize cross-view feature similarity by computing cosine sim-
ilarity between a query VAE token’s attention-output feature and those of all VAE tokens in another
view, without camera conditioning. The resulting similarity maps are overlaid on the corresponding
images. Figure 7 presents the examples of overlaid images of Lance-FT and Ask4NV. Compared
with the diffuse responses of the baseline, Ask4NV exhibits more localized feature responses around
corresponding scene regions, illustrating its ability to capture cross-view correspondences more ac-
curately.

C.3
ABLATION ON BIDIRECTIONAL ATTENTION BETWEEN VISUAL TOKENS.
Novel-view generation in Ask4NV involves three groups of visual tokens: source ViT tokens, clean
source VAE tokens, and noisy target VAE tokens. We extend the ablation in Table 6 by examining the
effects of allowing bidirectional attention between different visual token groups. ViT–VAE bidir.
denotes bidirectional attention between source ViT and clean source VAE tokens, while Noise–
Clean bidir. denotes bidirectional attention between clean source and noisy target VAE tokens. Our
model enables both forms of bidirectional attention.
Enabling both forms of attention achieves the best results across all reported metrics on DL3DV and
RealEstate10K, and most metrics on ScanNet++. On ScanNet, enabling only Noise–Clean bidirec-
tional attention yields better PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS, while our configuration achieves better ATE,
translation RPE, and MEt3R. Nevertheless, enabling both connections provides more consistent per-
formance across datasets, including the out-of-domain RealEstate10K and ScanNet++ benchmarks,
supporting our attention mask design.

D
LIMITATIONS AND FUTURE WORK
Our experiments demonstrate improvements in novel-view generation under the evaluated multi-
view settings. Extending these benefits to longer camera trajectories and more diverse in-the-wild
scenes remains an important direction, requiring consistent preservation of scene appearance and
geometry across broader viewing conditions. Our current training and evaluation also use a fixed
image resolution. Supporting multiple resolutions and higher-resolution outputs would broaden
the method’s applicability while introducing additional challenges in preserving fine visual details
across views. Larger models and more diverse training data would enable further investigation of
how the benefits of 3D language supervision scale. Although a performance gap remains relative to
leading 3D language understanding models, future unified backbones with stronger understanding
capabilities may help narrow this gap and provide richer supervision for novel-view generation.
