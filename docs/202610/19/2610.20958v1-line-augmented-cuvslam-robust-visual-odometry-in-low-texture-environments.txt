# Line-Augmented cuVSLAM: Robust Visual Odometry in Low-Texture Environments 

Dara Molloy ∗, Terry Wu ∗, Wonju Lee ∗, Abhilash Nand Kumar ∗, David Adley ∗,Conor Ferguson ∗, Hesam Rabeti †∗Analog Devices †NVIDIA 

Dara.Molloy@analog.com 

Abstract —Autonomous mobile robots (AMRs) in warehouses and production facilities operate in precisely the environments that defeat point-feature visual odometry: featureless aisles, uniform walls, repetitive racking and specular floors. Such environments are, however, rich in straight-edge structure: rack uprights, door frames, wall junctions and cabinet outlines present high-contrast linear edges stable across wide viewpoint changes. A line-feature extension to cuVSLAM [1], a CUDA-accelerated visual-SLAM system, is presented that recovers this signal. Lines are parameterised as Pl ¨ ucker coordinates for geometric transport and as the minimal 4-DOF orthonormal representation of Bartoli and Sturm [2] for optimisation; a Cauchy-robust point-to-line residual with analytic Jacobians is folded directly into cuVS-LAM’s per-frame Gauss–Newton pose solve, and the same line state is jointly refined in the Schur-complement sliding-window bundle adjustment. An adaptive information weight causes lines to assume more of the estimation burden exactly as point features become scarce, and the extension adds residual blocks to existing solvers rather than introducing a second estimator, reducing exactly to the unmodified point pipeline when disabled. Evaluated in RGB-D mode under SE(3) Umeyama alignment with no scale correction, line features reduce mean absolute trajectory error by 38.5% over the 22 sequences of the TUM RGB-D freiburg3 collection and by 30.1% over the 8 Replica room and office sequences, where median translation error falls by 38.9% and median rotation error by 66.4%, with zero tracking failures in any configuration tested. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Cameras are inexpensive, compact and information-dense, making visual odometry an attractive alternative to LIDAR-based localisation for autonomous mobile robots, provided it can match LIDAR’s robustness in the industrial environ-ments where point-feature tracking degrades. cuVSLAM [1] represents a modern, state-of-the-art visual-SLAM system: CUDA-accelerated, supporting up to 32 cameras across RGB, depth and stereo-inertial pipelines, and running in real time on NVIDIA Jetson edge hardware. As in ORB-SLAM3 [3], VINS-Mono [4] and other deployed visual or visual-inertial odometry (VIO) systems, its front end tracks sparse point features: corners and textured patches carried across frames by descriptor matching or optical-flow tracking. cuVSLAM’s own point path uses the latter: GFTT corner detection followed by GPU Lucas–Kanade optical-flow tracking with normalised-cross-correlation validation, not descriptor re-matching. Point features are precisely the primitive that indoor struc-tured environments lack. A warehouse aisle or production fa-cility presents long stretches of uniformly painted wall, repet-itive shelving, specular flooring and low-contrast or distant ceiling structure. Corner detectors yield few correspondences in these conditions, and the surviving matches are often poorly conditioned or attached to moving inventory. These environments are, however, rich in straight-edge structure. Rack uprights, door frames, wall–floor junctions, pallet edges and cabinet outlines are abundant, high-contrast and stable across wide viewpoint changes. A line is also a better-conditioned measurement than a point in these scenes, as it aggregates gradient evidence along its full length, which increases robustness to noise, motion blur and partial occlusion relative to a small tracked point patch. The point-line VIO literature converges on this observation, reporting that line features yield the largest gains in degenerate, low-texture and structured scenes [5]–[7]. This paper carries the point-line VIO formulation estab-lished by PL-VINS [5] and its lineage into cuVSLAM’s CUDA-accelerated pose solve and bundle adjustment, and ex-tends it with five departures aimed at the industrial degradation regime above. The extension augments rather than replaces the point pipeline and preserves the low-latency front end/back end separation that makes cuVSLAM deployable on edge hardware. The contributions are:  

> •

A persistent, cross-window line landmark registry 

that keeps a landmark alive after its originating frame leaves the bundle-adjustment sliding window, so an early, wide-baseline observation can still be paired against a much later one for triangulation, in place of PL-VINS’s window-scoped landmarks, which are deleted outright once their originating frame marginalises out.  

> •

A trimmed-mean multi-view line fusion that ranks each landmark’s recently triangulated endpoint pairs by mutual agreement and discards a fixed worst-agreeing fraction before averaging, in place of PL-VINS’s landmark state, which is otherwise a free variable updated only by the joint bundle adjustment, with no separate fusion step.  

> •

An adaptive, clamped point/line weight that scales inversely with surviving point observations and saturates at fixed floor/ceiling bounds, in place of PL-VINS’s sin-gle fixed information matrix shared unweighted between points and lines.  

> •

A cuVSLAM integration of point-to-line residuals 

into both the per-frame front end pose solve, with a Cauchy robust loss, and the windowed back end bundle arXiv:2610.20958v1 [cs.RO] 14 Oct 2026 adjustment, with a Student’s-t robust loss.  

> •

An evaluation on two public benchmarks , TUM RGB-D and Replica, under strict SE(3) alignment with no scale correction, paired with an analysis of which scene properties predict benefit. II. R ELATED WORK 

Early visual-SLAM systems were formulated as recursive filters, with camera poses and map features maintained in a state updated around a local linearisation [8]. Nonlinear factor-graph optimization subsequently became standard, with bundle adjustment providing a sparse pose-landmark formulation for joint refinement [9]. Keyframe-based systems such as ORB-SLAM3 [3] established this point-centric paradigm across monocular, stereo, RGB-D, and visual-inertial configurations. cuVSLAM [1] extends this lineage to CUDA-accelerated, multi-camera, real-time edge deployment. Early point-line systems commonly represented a mapped segment by two 3D endpoints. In the original PL-SLAM implementation [10], the endpoint coordinates were optimized directly, and the residual was formed from the projected endpoints and the corresponding observed infinite image line. This representation simplified initialization and association, but endpoint locations remained sensitive to occlusion, contrast variation, and partial visibility. Metric depth does not remove this issue in RGB-D systems, because endpoint locations re-main affected by image truncation. Later formulations replaced endpoint coordinates with geometric line states while retaining endpoint-to-line reprojection constraints, as in PL-VIO [6]. The geometric foundation was provided by the Pl¨ ucker representation, which supports rigid transport and projection but uses six coordinates subject to the Klein constraint. A min-imal four-degree-of-freedom orthonormal representation was established for line reconstruction and bundle adjustment by Bartoli and Sturm [2]. Pl¨ ucker coordinates can consequently be retained for transport while the orthonormal chart is used for optimization, avoiding the rank deficiency of direct constrained optimization. This representation was incorporated into tightly coupled point-line estimation by PL-VIO [6] and PL-VINS [5]. Line measurements complement point measurements but do not remove the underlying unobservable directions [11]. The Pl¨ ucker–orthonormal formulation has been imple-mented in visual-inertial estimation [12], wheel-odometry-aided odometry [13], and RGB-D SLAM [14]. Structural-prior methods such as StructVIO [7] obtain additional constraints from Manhattan-world regularity; no such axis-alignment as-sumption is imposed here. In this work, the established line geometry is integrated into cuVSLAM’s CUDA-accelerated frontend and backend, with PL-VINS serving as the principal architectural reference. Three limitations of the inherited design motivate correspond-ing extensions for persistent line estimation in low-texture, structure-rich environments:  

> •

Landmark persistence Line landmarks are scoped to the active sliding window. When the frame associated with a landmark is marginalized, its identity is not retained for later re-association; a subsequent observation of the same edge is therefore treated as a new landmark. Long-lived structural edges cannot consequently accumulate observations across successive windows.  

> •

Multi-view reconstruction In the inherited monocular formulation, a line landmark is initialized from a se-lected observation pair and subsequently refined as a free bundle-adjustment variable. No separate multi-view fu-sion step is applied, so an erroneous association or poorly conditioned reconstruction can influence the landmark state.  

> •

Residual weighting Point and line residuals share a fixed information matrix under a Huber norm [15], without a relative point-line weight. Their relative authority there-fore remains constant as point support varies. Sections V-B and IV-D address these three points in turn. 

a) Detection and description: LSD [16] is used for its subpixel localization and false-detection control; EDLines [17] and ELSED [18] provide alternative accuracy–runtime trade-offs. Detection is exposed through an interchangeable interface (Section V-A), and association is performed with the Line Band Descriptor [19], as in the PL-VIO/PL-VINS lineage [5], [6]. III. S YSTEM OVERVIEW 

cuVSLAM [1] separates a low-latency front end, which tracks features and estimates the per-frame pose against a local odometry map, from an asynchronous back end, which refines that map through sliding-window bundle adjustment and maintains global consistency through pose-graph optimi-sation and loop closure. This split enables real-time operation on Jetson-class hardware, and our design preserves it without modification. The line path attaches at both levels without altering the separation (Fig. 1). A persistent line-landmark registry is maintained outside the active bundle-adjustment state and stores each landmark’s identity, geometric state, descriptor, and recent depth-based reconstructions. Landmarks leaving the sliding window can therefore be re-associated through geomet-ric prediction and descriptor matching, enabling their accu-mulated endpoint observations to reinforce current estimates. Their residuals are incorporated into the existing Gauss– Newton pose solve and sliding-window bundle adjustment. Schur marginalization thus removes only the active variable; the registry entry is retained, and no parallel estimator or pose-graph arbitration is required. IV. L INE -F EATURE FORMULATION

E. Back-End Bundle Adjustment 

Line landmarks are jointly refined with camera poses and point landmarks in the sliding-window bundle adjustment. The original cuVSLAM back-end structure is retained and extended with line-landmark states; the resulting sparse pose-landmark system is solved by Schur complement [9]. Because the orthonormal representation is minimal, each line con-tributes a non-singular 4 × 4 diagonal block to Hmm , exactly analogous to a point’s 3 × 3 block, marginalised by the same block-diagonal inversion; the raw Pl¨ ucker form would instead give 6 × 6 blocks with rank deficiency two, requiring ex-plicit constraint handling. Minimality is therefore what makes line marginalisation numerically sound without changing the Schur-complement elimination structure. The back end applies a Student’s-t robust loss [23] to both point and line residuals rather differently from the frontend’s Cauchy loss: the two solves address different failure modes. The frontend rejects gross single-frame association errors before they enter the pose estimate, while the back end’s heavier-tailed loss down-weights landmarks whose accumulated residual across many observations indicates a persistent, rather than one-off, error. V. I MPLEMENTATION 

A. Detection, Reconstruction and Association 

Detection sits behind an interchangeable detector interface, so the choice of detector is a configuration decision rather than an architectural one. LSD [16] is the default used for all results reported here. Detections are filtered by length and image-border artifacts, with the minimum length scaled to image diagonal, and are capped at the strongest responses. On the RGB-D path, depth is sampled along each segment and a Pl¨ ucker line formed directly by back-projection, with a silhouette filter rejecting segments straddling a depth discon-tinuity, since such edges are viewpoint-dependent occlusion boundaries rather than physical structure. Observations are then associated against entries in the persistent line-landmark registry using LBD descriptors [19] under a geometric prior: each active landmark is projected via equations 2–3, and only candidates within an angular and perpendicular distance gate of that prediction are considered, suppressing the repetitive-structure mismatches that dominate failures in tracking en-vironments. Survivors are matched by Hamming distance under a ratio test. Re-associated entries are instantiated in the active optimization state, whereas entries that leave the sliding window remain in the registry until a bounded unobserved lifetime expires; the registry is capped in number to bound memory and solve cost. 

B. Robust Multi-View Line Fusion 

A landmark’s 3D state is not fixed at initialization; it is refined on every re-observation. The landmark is represented as an infinite line plus a 1D extent, an anchor point, a unit direction, and an arc-length interval [smin , s max ], rather than as two stored endpoints. Endpoints are a line’s least observable property: a detector routinely truncates or extends a segment from one frame to the next as occlusion or contrast changes, so endpoint motion reflects measurement noise rather than scene change. Each observation is reconstructed independently from sam-pled depth and appended to a per-landmark history buffer, with a widest-parallax-preserving eviction policy once the buffer is full: among buffered observations, the eviction candidate is the one whose removal least reduces the spread of viewing angles subtending the landmark, preserving the baseline diversity that constrains the line’s direction. Fusing the buffer into a single endpoint estimate is per-formed with a fixed-fraction, residual-ranked trimmed mean in the tradition of the α-trimmed mean filter [24]. The buffered estimates are ranked by distance to the pool mean, the worst-agreeing fraction is discarded, and the remainder is averaged. The resulting closed-form estimator limits the influence of mismatched associations and unreliable depth-based recon-structions without requiring a per-landmark optimization loop, while preserving a continuous estimate as additional agreeing observations are accumulated. Replica carries no sensor noise or depth artefacts, so scene geometry is the only variable that separates its sequences. Two sequences show isolated regressions, indicating that line detection can introduce noise in certain contexts; on room2, RPE translation RMSE rises from 0.0007 m to 0.0042 m while its median falls, and on office0, APE rotation regresses from 0.652 deg to 1.242 deg at unchanged translation accuracy. Taken across the 30 sequences of both datasets, translation accuracy improves on 26 and no sequence loses tracking, so line features gain accuracy and robustness together, with the residual cost confined to per-step precision. VII. D ISCUSSION 

Line features deliver their gain in the error mode that gov-erns autonomous mobile robot (AMR) operation: accumulated drift, which sets how far the platform travels before it requires relocalisation. Translation accuracy improves on 26 of the 30 sequences and rotation accuracy on 7 of 8 Replica sequences, with no tracking failure in any configuration. Accuracy and robustness therefore improve together; an estimator that trades occasional track loss for accuracy is not deployable at fleet scale. The geometry of the constraint explains where the gain comes from. The translation block of equation 7 leaves motion along a line unobserved, and parallel lines share that null direction however many are tracked (Section IV-C), so lines contribute in proportion to how well their directions comple-ment the angular distribution of the surviving point observa-tions. The Replica office sequences supply that complement through wall, door and furniture edges on surfaces carrying almost no point texture, and they show the largest gains of the 30 sequences. Structured indoor environments supply this evidence densely, which establishes the operational case for the modality. The adaptive weight of equation 9 is self-limiting, sitting at its floor throughout well-textured scenes such as most of TUM RGB-D, so the feature need not be restricted to known feature-poor routes. All runs completed without tracking failure on the two evaluated datasets. Within these experiments, the observed trade-off was therefore in accuracy rather than track completion, and downstream recovery of a bounded accuracy change is preferable to recovery after a lost track. Persistent line landmarks bound the accumulated error, and they do so through a measurement path that is deliberately conservative. The perpendicular endpoint-to-line residual of equation 6 is invariant to position along the line, so the endpoint truncation that dominates segment instability never reaches the solver. The results indicate that per-frame LSD re-detection introduces additional variation into the line residuals in some sequences, which can limit short-term accuracy while improving robustness to accumulated drift. On the full TUM set, mean one-frame RPE translation increases from 0.0108 m to 0.0136 m, while APE translation improves on 19 of 22 sequences (Section VI-B). Across both datasets, translation accuracy improves on 26 of the 30 sequences. Coupling per-frame detection with direct line tracking, so that a landmark’s image support is propagated instead of refitted, removes this noise at its source and is the next step for the front end. The exceptions are three TUM sequences and two Replica metrics, all small in absolute terms: sitting static shifts by 5.7 mm on a 0.259 m trajectory, room2 loses 0.8 mm of trans-lation accuracy, and office0’s rotation error rises at unchanged translation accuracy. These cases are consistent with a failure mode in which the point-count-based weight increases line authority while repetitive-edge associations are less reliable. Future work will explore context-aware per-observation un-certainty, which weighs each line measurement on its own evidence rather than transferring authority on point count alone. The observed accuracy trade-off is therefore associated with the measurement path and the apportionment of estimation au-thority between the point and line modalities, rather than with the Pl¨ ucker–orthonormal formulation or its Schur-complement marginalisation. VIII. C ONCLUSION 

An extension of cuVSLAM with persistent line landmarks, trimmed-mean multi-view fusion, and a clamped adaptive point-line weight has been presented for structured indoor environments. Lines are incorporated as additional residual blocks, leaving the point pipeline unchanged when the line terms are disabled. The Pl¨ ucker–orthonormal parameterisation provides minimal 4 × 4 landmark blocks that are marginalised by the existing Schur complement without the rank deficiency of raw Pl¨ ucker coordinates. Under SE(3) Umeyama alignment with no scale correction, mean APE translation RMSE is reduced by 38.5% over the 22 TUM RGB-D sequences and by 30.1% over the 8 Replica sequences. Translation improves on 19 of 22 TUM sequences and 7 of 8 Replica sequences, while no tracking failures are observed. On the ten-sequence focus subset, the line-augmented pipeline reaches 0.0357 m, below the published cuVSLAM Odom and ORB-SLAM3 values. The gain is concentrated in accumulated drift, with a small rise in one-frame RPE. The improvement is not unconditional. Regressions occur in four of thirty sequences, in low-texture, untextured near-range scenarios where repetitive-edge associations become unreliable; the point-count-based weighting rule increases line authority precisely in these regimes, amplifying rather than suppressing detection noise. Future work should explore max-imising the line signal through more accurate line detection and matching to improve robustness in such challenging cases. ACKNOWLEDGMENT
