Q-MAGIC: Quad-only Mesh Autoregressive Generation via
Singularity-Aware Circular Relative Offset Tokenization
Yuming Zhao1†, Zezeng Li2,†, Weimin Wang1‡, Xiang Wu1, Na Lei1,‡
1Dalian University of Technology, China
2École Centrale de Lyon, France
Abstract
Autoregressive models have proven effective for triangular mesh generation by transforming it into predicting the next face,
converting the non-differentiable connectivity prediction into a sequence of token predictions. However, generating quad-only
meshes remains challenging because quad faces impose stricter topological constraints than triangles. The model must not
only serialize quad faces into a valid token sequence, but also support unambiguous connectivity reconstruction during de-
serialization. To address this, we propose Q-MAGIC, a novel autoregressive model for quad mesh generation. We first introduce
a tailored serialization scheme for quad meshes, which requires at least two new vertices for the next face and is less flexible than
triangles due to strict wiring constraints. To meet these requirements and reduce serialization length, we propose a singularity-
aware serialization that prioritizes wiring from singular points, leading to more coherent traversals. Additionally, we propose
a circular relative offset tokenization that encodes geometry mainly via local relative offsets. Built upon this representation,
an autoregressive Transformer learns the underlying distribution of quad meshes via next-token prediction, enabling effective
quad mesh generation. Extensive experiments demonstrate that Q-MAGIC produces more compact sequences while consistently
improving geometric fidelity and mesh quality compared to state-of-the-art methods.

1. Introduction
Quad meshes are a widely used surface representation in produc-
tion workflows such as animation, CAD, and physical simulation,
where pure quadrilateral meshes are particularly favored for their
compatibility with standard modeling pipelines and support for
downstream applications, including computational mechanics and
geometric analysis. Consequently, developing robust and practical
methods for generating high-quality pure quad meshes remains an
important problem in both academia and industry.
Most current methods generate quad meshes via a two-stage
pipeline. These methods first create a triangle mesh and then ap-
ply quadrangulation or remeshing as post-processing. This in-
termediate triangle mesh can be produced either from continu-
ous 3D representations [RMA∗24, MHS∗21, PFS∗19, WLL∗21,
HTC∗24, XCG∗24] or from triangle-based autoregressive mesh
generators [WZL∗25,CWL∗25]. However, this conversion adds ex-
tra stages and heuristics, and it does not reliably guarantee quad-
only outputs, often requiring additional cleanup or refinement.

Inspired by triangular mesh generation, Liu et al. [LWG∗25] pro-
posed a mixed triangle and quad face mesh generative model via a
united hybrid polygon mesh serialization approach. While this seri-
alization method is capable of handling both triangular and quadri-
lateral faces simultaneously, it suffers from two key limitations.
First, it fails to account for the unique mesh wiring constraints
and singular structures inherent to quadrilaterals. Second, the ex-
cessively redundant sequence length substantially increases the dif-
ficulty of model training and optimization.
Compared to triangular meshes, quad meshes are more imbal-
anced in structure. Most faces consist of regular valence-4 connec-
tivity, yet the global layout is controlled by a small set of singular
vertices and their neighborhoods. These singular structures steer
the overall edge flow and indicate where regularity must break.
Learning a quad mesh as a uniform sequence over-emphasizes the
regular patterns and underfits these rare singularities. As a result,
errors in early singularity decisions can propagate along loops, pro-
ducing inconsistent layouts and reduced editability.
To address these issues, we propose Q-MAGIC, a quad-only au-
toregressive model that follows a layout-first design. We partition a
mesh into patches and serialize them in a singularity-aware order,
so topology-critical regions are generated early. Within each patch,

2. Related Works
2.1. Autoregressive Model for Triangle or Mixed Mesh
Following MeshGPT [SAA∗24], recent autoregressive mesh gen-
eration methods have moved towards stronger conditional control-
lability and better scalability. Some methods introduce richer shape
conditions or control signals to impose stronger constraints on the
output without changing the autoregressive paradigm [CHH∗25,
HRLL24]. Other methods address the bottleneck of training and
inference caused by long sequences, proposing scalable network
architecture and training designs for meshes with more patches and
higher resolution [HRLL24,CCP∗24,WWZ∗25b].
Another important direction is mesh serialization, which directly
affects sequence length, local connectivity modeling, and gener-
ation stability. EdgeRunner [TLH∗25] borrows the idea of con-
nectivity compression from EdgeBreaker [Ros99] to achieve loss-
less patch compression and reduce long-range dependencies. Me-
shAnythingV2 [CWL∗25] uses adjacent-face encoding to reduce
vertex redundancy. OCTGPT [WWZ∗25a] uses a multi-scale struc-
ture for scalable 3D shape serialization. BPT [WZL∗25] com-
presses meshes at both vertex and face levels via blocked and
patchified representations to reduce sequence length. MeshRip-
ple [LLG∗26] aligns the generation order with surface topology
through frontier-aware traversal, and Mesh Silksong [SZW∗26]
emphasizes topology-preserved serialization. FACE [WGL∗26]
represents each face as a compact token, while SATO [XQQ∗26]
introduces strip-based tokenization for unified triangle and quadri-
lateral representation.
Deepmesh and Mesh-RFT [ZYW∗25, LXG∗25] further ex-
plore preference-based post-training (e.g., DPO [RSM∗23]) to im-
prove the generation quality. Researchers are also exploring ac-
celerated decoding and progressive level-of-detail generation to
achieve a better balance between inference speed and output quality
[SZT∗25,SLWR25,CQL∗25,ZSA∗25].
Recently, autoregressive modeling has been extended to quadri-
lateral meshes. QuadGPT [LWG∗25] and Mesh-Pro [ZLL∗26]
present end-to-end quadrilateral dominant mesh generation frame-
works that employ unified hybrid polygon serialization and reward-
based refinement strategies. QuadLink [ZZS∗26] formulates quad-
dominant mesh generation as a point-relation learning problem,
predicting vertices and face centroids and assembling polygo-
nal faces through centroid-conditioned vertex links and a quad-
first strategy. In general, existing autoregressive methods are ei-
ther dominated by triangular meshes or produce hybrid meshes,
whereas native quad-only mesh generation remains underexplored.
2.2. Quad Mesh Generation
Quad mesh generation has long been studied in computer graphics,
with traditional methods mainly formulated as remeshing, layout
extraction or field-aligned optimization. For example, Marinov and
Kobbelt [MK04] proposed anisotropic quad-dominant remeshing,
while Pietroni et al. [PNA∗21] extracted field-coherent quad lay-
outs from cross fields. Although these methods can produce high-
quality quad structures, they typically rely on carefully constructed
inputs, such as clean surfaces, reliable directional fields, or well-
initialized layouts. Moreover, since quad connectivity is mainly ob-
tained through hand-designed remeshing or layout extraction pro-
cedures, these methods are less flexible in modeling diverse quad-
only meshes in a data-driven generative setting.
Beyond
traditional
optimization-based
pipelines,
recent
learning-based methods have also explored quad mesh generation,
although most of them still rely on intermediate fields, templates,
or local assembly procedures. Many works first predict a local
directional frame field for quadrangulation and then generate
quad meshes via field-aligned parameterization or subsequent
remeshing. This pipeline imposes strong requirements on the
predicted field, which should be globally smooth for stable
parameterization, while still aligning with geometric features to
support reliable quad extraction [YLGL25,DWX∗25b,DWX∗25a,
LLL∗25b, QQX∗25, CLZ∗26, DSS∗26]. Another line of work
builds on PDE-constrained formulations, e.g., incorporating PDE
residuals into the learning objective to learn mappings from a
parametric domain to the physical domain for structured mesh
generation [CLW∗22, PCL25]. MeshONet [XCP∗26] further
formulates structured mesh generation as an operator-learning
problem for mapping computational domains to physical do-
mains. Other approaches use flow or ODE-based template
deformations to fit geometry while largely preserving topol-
ogy [GC20, LSCdG∗21, ZLW24]. Meanwhile, some approaches
cast meshing as a sequential decision-making problem and
use reinforcement learning to optimize element placement or
advancing-front updates, with rewards designed around element
quality and topological preferences [PHCZ22,TQHZ23,LFWN20].
Overall, these methods tend to work best in simple planar ge-
ometries or topologies with clean inputs, and their applicability
remains limited for complex shapes.
In addition, researchers have explored directly generating quad
meshes from point clouds. Point2Quad [LQW∗25] directly pro-
duces quad-only surface meshes by predicting and selecting lo-
cal quadrilateral faces for mesh assembly. However, it follows
a candidate-face prediction paradigm. Therefore, the recoverable
quad connectivity is bounded by whether valid faces are included
in this candidate set. Our method instead represents quad meshes as
autoregressive sequences, where quad faces are generated through
the learned sequence and the corresponding de-serialization rule,
reducing the reliance on pre-enumerated local face candidates.

3. Method
As shown in Fig. 1, the proposed Q-MAGIC centers on a com-
pact serialization for quad-only meshes, which transforms mesh
geometry and connectivity into a token sequence for autoregres-
sive generation. While Q-MAGIC adopts the block–offset index-
ing introduced by BPT [WZL∗25], BPT is designed for triangular
meshes, whose local serialization exploits the fact that every pair
of vertices within a triangle is directly connected by an edge. This
property does not hold for quadrilateral faces, where diagonally op-
posite vertices are not edge-connected. Consequently, directly ex-
tending BPT-style traversal to quad meshes becomes insufficient
for preserving valid connectivity, particularly around singular re-
gions with non-consecutive local face configurations. The serial-
ization follows a singularity-aware traversal and uses circular rela-
tive offset tokens within local patches to produce shorter sequences
while retaining topology-critical structures.

3.2. Singularity-aware Serialization
Block–offset indexing. Although the naive quad mesh serialization
described above is simple and directly compatible with autoregres-
sive models, it treats each quantized coordinate as an independent
token and repeats shared vertices for every incident face. Existing
methods, such as QuadGPT [LWG∗25], adopt similar coordinate-
based tokenizations, which can result in long and redundant se-
quences and a vocabulary size that grows cubically with the coordi-
nate resolution. To address these issues, we follow BPT [WZL∗25]
and convert quantized coordinates into a block–offset representa-
tion, as shown in Fig. 2.
Given the vertex sequence S, we discretize each 3D position into
a block-offset index. Along each coordinate axis, we partition the
coordinate range into B blocks and subdivide each block into O
offsets. So the resolution per axis is B · O. For a quantized vertex

vertices whose valence is not equal to 4 as singular vertices, and de-
note the set of such vertices as Virr. During traversal, patch centers
are first selected from Virr until all singular vertices are covered.
After the center vertex c is determined, we collect all quadrilat-

4.1. Experiment Settings
Datasets. We construct our experimental dataset with the obj files
in Thing10K dataset [PNA∗21] and apply a fixed, automated filter-
ing pipeline to ensure mesh quality and reliable evaluation. We re-
tain only meshes that satisfy the following criteria: all-quadrilateral
faces, valid vertex coordinates, no degenerate faces, and no non-
manifold edges. Since some meshes have excessively high resolu-
tion and an excessive number of faces, we retopologize and sim-
plify them using Quad Remesher [Max24], limiting the face count
to under 4,700 to guarantee stable training and manageable com-
putational overhead. After filtering and simplification, we obtain
2,100 quad meshes. We randomly split the dataset into 80% for
training and 20% for testing. To improve robustness to object ori-
entation, we apply rotation augmentation to the training data.
Baselines. We compare Q-MAGIC with two types of methods.
The first type consists of autoregressive triangle or hybrid mesh
generators, including BPT [WZL∗25], TreeMeshGPT [LLL25a],
and QuadGPT [LWG∗25]. For a fair evaluation, we convert the
outputs of the autoregressive baselines into quad meshes using
the Blender Quad Remesher [Max24], an automatic remeshing
tool that converts an input surface into a quad-based mesh, and
we apply the same post-processing settings across methods. Dur-
ing this quad remeshing process, the algorithm may automati-
cally fill small holes, which can lead to the presence of a lim-
ited number of triangular faces in the vicinity of the filled re-
gions. The second type is a direct quad-only reconstruction method,
Point2Quad [LQW∗25]. All point cloud conditioned methods take
4,096 uniformly sampled surface points as input. As QuadGPT
does not provide a pretrained model, we use the official API
(https://3d.hunyuan.tencent.com/) to produce its re-
sults. The generation process can be viewed as resembling a
topology-optimization-style pipeline.

(CD) and Hausdorff distance (HD) computed on uniformly sam-
pled 1024 points from the generated and ground-truth meshes,

Implementation Details. Q-MAGIC is a compact autoregres-
sive Transformer with 8 decoder layers and a hidden size of 1024.
For mesh tokenization, we set the block size to B = 8 and the
offset size to O = 16, resulting in a per-axis spatial resolution of
B·O = 128. The maximum sequence length is about 20K tokens.
For point-cloud conditioning, we employ a frozen pretrained
Michelangelo Encoder to extract 768-dimensional point-cloud
features. The extracted features are projected into the 1024-
dimensional latent space of the Transformer and incorporated into
the autoregressive decoder through cross-attention.
For remeshing, we convert the triangular meshes generated by
BPT into quad meshes using Blender Quad Remesher. We set the
target face count to 4,500 and the adaptive size to 30%, allowing the
remesher to adjust the local face density according to the geometric
characteristics of each mesh.
We first sample 16,384 surface points per shape and randomly
subsample 4,096 points during training. All experiments are trained
on a single NVIDIA A100 GPU for approximately 7 days. The
model is trained using the standard next-token cross-entropy loss.
The model is optimized with AdamW using β1 = 0.9 and β2 =
0.95, with a base learning rate of 5×10−5 and a batch size of 4. We
adopt a LambdaLR schedule with a linear warmup for the first 10%
of training steps, followed by a cosine decay to a minimum learning
rate ratio of 0.5. During inference, we autoregressively generate
mesh tokens using multinomial sampling with top-k sampling (k =
50) and a temperature of 0.7.
4.2. Quantitative Results
Table 2 reports the quantitative comparison between Q-MAGIC
and Point2Quad, TreeMeshGPT, QuadGPT, and BPT. Overall, Q-
MAGIC achieves the best results in geometric fidelity, as measured
by CD and HD, and element quality, as reflected by the Scaled Ja-
cobian, while preserving high mesh validity in terms of Watertight-
ness and Manifoldness. These results demonstrate that Q-MAGIC
produces more regular quad meshes with more accurate surface fit-
ting, without sacrificing closedness or manifold consistency.
Geometric Fidelity. We evaluate geometric fidelity using CD
and HD, where lower values are better. Q-MAGIC achieves the
lowest errors on both metrics, showing that direct quad mesh gen-
eration preserves geometric accuracy. Although some baselines ob-
tain competitive results by first generating triangle meshes and then
remeshing them into quads, their final quality remains limited by
the intermediate triangle representation. Errors in the generated
triangle mesh can propagate through remeshing, as observed in
TreeMeshGPT. By directly predicting quad structures, Q-MAGIC
avoids this propagation and improves geometric fidelity.
Mesh Validity. Watertightness and manifoldness are used to as-
sess the topological validity of generated meshes, where higher
values indicate more closed and manifold structures. As shown in
Table 2, Q-MAGIC achieves near-perfect scores on both metrics,
confirming that direct quad generation preserves strong structural
validity. Compared with Point2Quad, Q-MAGIC obtains better ge-
ometric fidelity and element quality while maintaining compara-
ble watertightness and manifoldness. This suggests that Q-MAGIC
achieves a more favorable balance between surface accuracy, mesh
regularity, and topological consistency.
Element Quality. We evaluate element quality using the Scaled
Jacobian, where higher values indicate better-shaped elements.
This metric reflects the distortion and regularity of quad faces. Q-
MAGIC achieves the highest score, suggesting that its generated
quads are closer to ideal elements with lower distortion. This im-
provement is important for downstream applications such as simu-
lation and geometric processing, since element quality directly af-
fects both practical usability and numerical stability.
User Study. We conducted a user study to evaluate perceptual
mesh quality. For each test case, the outputs of all compared meth-
ods were presented in a randomized order with method names hid-
den, forming a blind evaluation protocol. Testers rated the outputs
of all methods on a 0–10 scale, where higher scores indicate better
visual quality. Considering the cost of participant involvement, we
performed the study on a limited set of ten cases with 50 testers.
Although the number of cases is limited, the observed preferences
provide supporting evidence for the effectiveness of Q-MAGIC. As
shown in Table 2, our method achieves the highest mean score,
indicating a perceptual preference for our results. This is consis-
tent with the qualitative comparisons, where Q-MAGIC produces
more continuous boundary loops, fewer unnecessary openings and
holes, and more regular quad layouts with coherent local structures
in detail-sensitive regions. These advantages lead to higher subjec-
tive ratings.

4.4. Ablation Study
To validate the effectiveness of the core designs in our Q-MAGIC
pipeline, we conduct ablation studies on the center vertex selec-
tion and the serialization scheme, with results reported in Table 3
and Table 4. Qualitative visualizations are provided in Figure 5 and

Table 3 shows that singularity-aware traversal consistently im-
proves mesh validity and element quality over random traversal.
Since singularity regions determine local valence distributions and

edge-loop organization, errors introduced there are difficult to cor-
rect later. Random traversal often establishes connectivity in regu-
lar regions first, forcing the model to satisfy singularity constraints
within an already generated structure, which can lead to disordered
layouts and broken loops. In contrast, singularity-aware traver-
sal anchors critical connections early, providing stronger structural
guidance and reducing error accumulation.
Table 4 shows that the circular relative offset representation is
more robust than the block-offset representation. By shifting pre-
diction from global positions to local displacements, circular rel-
ative offsets operate within a bounded value range and avoid the
large jumps introduced by global discretization. In contrast, abso-
lute offsets are more susceptible to drift across regions, and the
resulting errors can be amplified during de-serialization, leading to
reduced mesh validity and geometric accuracy.
We further analyze the computational sensitivity to the block
size B and offset size O. Increasing (B,O) from the default (8,16)
to (16,32) raises the per-axis spatial resolution from 128 to 512,
while substantially enlarging the corresponding token spaces. Un-
der the same training setting, the peak GPU memory increases and
the training time increases by approximately 53%. Therefore, we
adopt (B,O) = (8,16) as a practical configuration that provides
sufficient spatial resolution while maintaining manageable memory
and training costs.
5. Limitations and Future Work
Despite the promising results, Q-MAGIC still has several limita-
tions. As an autoregressive framework, its computational cost in-
creases with sequence length, which may limit scalability to highly
complex or high-resolution meshes. In addition, global connectiv-
ity across locally generated regions is not always guaranteed for
complex shapes. Our current evaluation demonstrates good gen-
eralization to diverse shapes within the Thing10K distribution;
however, cross-domain generalization to CAD-oriented or artist-
designed quad meshes with different topology and edge-flow pat-

6. Conclusion
In this paper, we introduce Q-MAGIC, an autoregressive Trans-
former framework for quad-only mesh generation, which takes the
undifferentiable connectivity prediction as the next face token pre-
diction task. To shorten token sequences and enhance the overall
regularity of the global mesh, we introduce a singularity-aware se-
rialization to prioritize topology-critical wiring and improve quad
mesh validity. Furthermore, to compactly encode geometry and
handle mesh-density variation, we propose circular relative offset
tokenization, which encodes geometry via local relative offsets into
discrete tokens, achieving mesh-density adaptation. Experimental
results demonstrate that our Q-MAGIC outperforms baseline meth-
ods in both mesh validity and reconstruction quality.
