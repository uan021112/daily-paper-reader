# TGrasp6D: Task-Informed Vision-Language Priors and Pose Refinement enable Zero-Shot Task-Oriented 6DoF Grasping 

Manav Kulshrestha 1, Apoorva Vashisth 1, Damon Conover 2, Aniket Bera 1

Abstract — Task-Oriented Grasping (TOG) has emerged as an important skill for purposeful robotic manipulation. Given the task of using a knife to “slice bread,” a robot must choose to grasp from the handle, whereas for “hand[ing] the knife over,” it is preferable for the handle to remain unobstructed for safe handoff. Current approaches for TOG either focus on 2D planar grasping, remain limited by their reliance on task-agnostic grasp generators, or are constrained by reliance on finite, manually-annotated, task-specific grasp datasets that cannot cover all possible grasp-task pairs. To that end, we present TGrasp6D, a vision-language based approach for generating task-oriented 6DoF grasps in a zero-shot manner. Given a task and a single RGB-D image of an associated object, TGrasp6D reasons about the appropriate semantic affordance for the given task and object geometry to produce an initial task-informed prior. This generated prior is further refined via 6DoF pose optimization for collision avoidance while preserving task consistency. Our approach achieves state-of-the-art success rate of 94% on the TaskGrasp dataset, with the best baseline at 74%. We also deploy our approach on a Franka Research 3 robot to demonstrate zero-shot generalization to real world objects. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Robotic manipulation, and specifically robotic grasping, is a fundamental capability for enabling robots to physically interact with, and operate, in the real world [1, 2]. Recent methods have significantly advanced this field [3–5] by prediction of stable grasps for diverse objects. However, a stable grasp solves half the problem: it does not consider whether the resulting hand-object configuration will support the intended task. Task-Oriented Grasping (TOG) captures this coupling between grasp and intent, requiring joint rea-soning over object affordances and task semantics, rather than treating grasp selection as a purely geometric problem. This matters wherever robots operate around humans or chain manipulation steps together – an assistive robot handing over a tool, or an industrial arm that must re-grasp a part differently depending on whether it’s inserting or fastening. Various methods have been proposed for TOG. Training-based approaches [6–9] rely on large human-annotated datasets of object-task-grasps labels [9]. However, due to the boundedness of these datasets in the space of valid grasps and possible tasks, even large annotation efforts [8] yield sparse coverage. Hence, their performance [6–9] is capped by the dataset size, and generalization to novel task-object

Many approaches [6, 7, 10– 12] attempt to bypass the generative aspect of grasp synthesis by coupling with a task-free generator [13–15] and then scoring the resulting grasps for the given task, essentially reducing the problem to that of score regression. By injecting task semantics onto task-agnostic grasp priors, these methods are bottlenecked by the coverage of the upstream generator: if no candidate near the task-relevant region was proposed, no downstream semantic reasoning can recover it. Finally, planar-grasp methods [16–19] restrict prediction to the image plane, discarding a broad class of feasible grasps with out-of-plane approach directions. In contrast, large vision-language models offer a different foundation for TOG. Pretrained on internet scale image-text corpora, they encode priors over object semantics and functional affordances without requiring the exhaustive su-pervision that prior TOG methods depend on. These priors, however, are expressed in the image and text modalities that the models were trained on. Extending their reasoning requires situating the model in the three-dimensional, geo-metric space where manipulation actually occurs. Hence, we present TGrasp6D, a framework that leverages VLMs to synthesize 6DoF task-oriented grasps in a zero-shot manner, requiring no task-specific training or fine-arXiv:2610.20901v1 [cs.RO] 14 Oct 2026 tuning. Given a single RGB-D observation of an object and a natural language task instruction, TGrasp6D first reasons jointly over the object’s geometry and semantics to identify the task-relevant grasp affordance, then localizes this affordance within the image. To bridge the resulting image-space localization into 3D, we lift it into a plane consistent with the camera’s projective geometry, yielding a task-informed grasp prior around the localized affordance. Candidate grasps sampled within this prior are then refined through pose optimization that trades off collision avoidance against consistency with the task-relevant region, yielding grasps that are simultaneously geometrically feasible and appropriate to the task at hand. > •

A. Task-Agnostic 6DoF Grasp Synthesis 

Early methods [3–5, 13–15] focused on generating stable 6DoF grasps conditioned only on object geometry, regard-less of the downstream task. Sundermeyer et al. [13] root grasp contact points directly in the observed point cloud to reduce the search space for cluttered-scene grasping. Fang et al. [15] learn dense, temporally-consistent grasp perception from large-scale supervision, incorporating object center-of-mass to improve stability. Yuan et al. [14] unify grasping and placement in a single transformer that can optionally condition on a language goal, though this selects which object to act on rather than where on it to grasp. Iwase et al. [5] instead couple grasp prediction with 3D shape reconstruction, arguing that reasoning jointly about occluded geometry benefits both. Bukhari et al. [4] learn a probabilistic latent space over object shapes and use the inferred features to guide SE (3) diffusion-based grasp synthesis, improving robustness to noisy or partial observations over deterministic shape encoders. Most recently, Kulshrestha et al. [3] removes the need for grasp-specific training by querying a VLM to generate a candidate antipodal axis in image space, then lifting it into 3D via depth alignment. While these methods differ in representation and supervision, all optimize purely for stability and feasibility; we instead condition the search for a feasible pose on a task-relevant region identified before optimization begins. 

B. Task-Oriented Grasping 

A first line of work learns task-conditioned grasp selection from annotated data. Murali et al. [9] introduce TaskGrasp, the largest TOG benchmark to date, and propagate a semantic knowledge graph over objects and tasks to generalize to held-out instances. Tang et al. [6] replace this closed-world graph with open-ended knowledge queried from an LLM to generalize to novel concepts, and [7] extends the idea to both LLMs and VLMs over an expanded dataset. Deshpande et al. [8] scale data rather than knowledge sources, fine-tuning a VLM on a large synthetic corpus of cluttered, realistically-instructed scenes. Chu et al. [20] instead incorporate ex-plicit physical-property reasoning over multi-step, flexible language instructions to condition 6DoF grasp generation. Since each method depends on a bespoke annotated or synthesized TOG corpus, generalization is bounded by that corpus’s coverage of the object-task-grasp space. A second line of work couples task reasoning with an existing task-agnostic generator, applying semantics only after a candidate set has been produced. Dong et al. [10] retrieve grasp constraints from human demonstration videos and use them to filter task-agnostic candidates. Huang et al. [11] fold similar human-derived constraints directly into a guided diffusion sampler rather than filtering after the fact, while Tang et al. [12] use a VLM to ground task-relevant affordance regions but still select among poses returned by a fixed generator [15]. Because semantics are applied downstream of an already-fixed candidate distribution, a task-appropriate grasp the generator never proposed cannot be recovered by any amount of subsequent reasoning. A third line of work restricts prediction to the image plane. Vuong et al. [16] treat grasp detection as a diffusion-based generative problem conditioned on language, Holomjova et al. [17] predict a task-relevant grasp rectangle via one-shot learning, Tang et al. [18] ground task and object jointly through a 2D vision-language model restricted to top-down grasping, and Li et al. [19] decompose objects into convex parts for LLM-guided part selection but likewise execute largely top-down grasps. A task-relevant affordance need not lie in the image plane, so planar prediction structurally ex-cludes a broad class of otherwise feasible grasps. TGrasp6D avoids all three limitations, requiring no task-specific dataset, no upstream task-agnostic generator, and no restriction to the image plane. 

C. Vision-Language Models for Affordance Reasoning in Manipulation 

Beyond grasping, VLMs increasingly ground task-level affordances for general manipulation. Huang et al. [21] show an LLM can infer free-form affordances and, by querying a VLM, compose them into 3D value maps that a planner optimizes over, demonstrating that language-model priors transfer zero-shot to full trajectory synthesis. Yuan et al. [22] instruction-tune a VLM to output image keypoint affordances directly, generalizing across navigation, manipulation, and augmented reality, though the output remains a 2D point requiring a separate module to become an executable action. Our approach, TGrasp6D, consists of 3 major components: (1) VLM based object-centric task-oriented reasoning and localization of the appropriate object part affordance, (2) gen-eration of a vision-language-based grasp-pose prior around the localized object part in image space and utilization of camera extrinsics for lifting it into 3D, and (3) sampling of candidate grasps from the grasp-pose prior and batched 6DoF pose refinement for collision avoidance and task-region consistency via optimization. The overview of our approach is detailed in Fig. 2.

V. R ESULTS 

A. Setup 

Dataset and Metrics . We evaluate on the TaskGrasp dataset [9], which provides up to 25 annotated task-oriented grasps per object–task pair. We retain pairs with at least 

20 ground-truth grasp annotations, yielding 310 evaluation instances. A predicted grasp is considered successful if its gripper sweep volume attains at least 0.1 IoU with any ground-truth grasp for the corresponding object–task pair, similarly to [16]. For all evaluations, we report grasp success rate (SR) and generation failure rate (GFR), where the latter measures the fraction of inputs for which no grasp is produced. For generated grasps classified as failures, we additionally report the orientation error (fOE) and position error (fPE) relative to the nearest ground-truth grasp. 

Hyperparameters . We tune the hyperparameters for our approach using grid search over the principal parameters governing grasp-prior sampling and pose refinement. The final configuration is chosen based on performance on a small validation set and is used across all reported experiments. Unless otherwise stated, the same hyperparameter setting is applied to all object–task pairs. 

Hardware Configuration . We conducted our experiments on a workstation equipped with an NVIDIA RTX 5080 GPU and a 20 -core Intel Core i7 CPU. Our baselines requiring re-training were trained locally on the RTX 5080, while author-provided pretrained weights were used for others. Real-world experiments were conducted using a Franka Research 3 (FR3) robotic manipulator equipped with the parallel-jaw gripper. All grasp synthesis and pose-refinement components were executed on the same workstation.

C. Ablation Studies 

We conduct ablation studies along two principal dimen-sions of our approach: (i) the contribution of 6 DoF grasp-pose refinement, and (ii) the choice of vision-language model used for affordance reasoning, localization, and grasp-axis prediction. The former isolates whether geometric refinement meaningfully improves the task-informed prior by resolving collisions and correcting small localization or orientation errors, while the latter evaluates how sensitive the overall pipeline is to the semantic and spatial reasoning capabilities of the underlying VLM. 

Effect of Grasp-Pose Refinement . We analyze the con-tribution of the 6DoF pose-refinement stage by evaluating TGrasp6D without refinement. As illustrated in Fig. 4, removing pose refinement decreases the success rate from 

94 .5% to 89 .4% , indicating that the vision-language prior alone provides a strong initialization, while geometric refine-ment recovers an additional 5.1 percentage points. To better understand this improvement, we further decompose unsuc-cessful grasps according to their dominant failure mode: incorrect task-affordance selection, grasp-position error, or grasp-orientation error. Pose refinement substantially reduces position failures from 3.5% to 1.6% and orientation failures from 3.9% to 0.6% , but not affordance failures which remain at 3.2% . This behavior is expected: pose refinement can cor-rect geometric inaccuracies in the VLM-derived grasp prior, but cannot fully recover from an incorrectly inferred task-relevant affordance. TGrasp6D currently relies on a single RGB-D observa-tion, making grasp synthesis sensitive to depth noise, self-occlusion, and incomplete point clouds, which can cause the refinement stage to accept poses that collide with unobserved geometry. The method also depends on large VLMs, intro-ducing inference latency and, for hosted models, API cost, while locally deployed alternatives can be computationally expensive. In addition, the current formulation localizes a single task-relevant region and therefore tends to concentrate grasp generation around one mode of the valid grasp space. Finally, the refinement objective does not explicitly model contact mechanics or closed-loop execution, motivating fu-ture work on multi-view perception, multiple affordance hypotheses, and feedback-driven grasping. VII. C ONCLUSION 

In this work, we presented TGrasp6D, a zero-shot frame-work for task-oriented 6DoF grasp synthesis from a sin-gle RGB-D observation and natural-language task instruc-tion. By grounding task-relevant affordances with a vision-language model, lifting them into 3D grasp priors, and refining candidate poses for collision avoidance and task con-sistency, TGrasp6D removes the need for task-specific train-ing data and task-agnostic grasp generators. On TaskGrasp, our method achieves a 94 .5% success rate, outperforming the strongest baseline by over 20 percentage points, while real-robot experiments demonstrate strong zero-shot transfer across diverse object–task pairs.
