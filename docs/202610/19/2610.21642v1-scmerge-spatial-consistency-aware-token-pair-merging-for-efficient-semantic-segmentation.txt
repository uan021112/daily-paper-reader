# Abstract 

Vision Transformer architectures have showcased impres-sive performance on semantic segmentation, yet their quadratic attention complexity hinders practical applica-tions. Recent works have focused on merging tokens to reduce sequence length and thus improve transformer efficiency. However, existing token merging methods primarily rely on feature similarity to identify similar tokens, overlooking spatial consistency, which may in-troduce segmentation errors in semantic segmentation. In this work, we propose SCMerge, a training-free to-ken merging method that jointly considers feature simi-larity and spatial consistency in pairing similar tokens. The pairwise tokens are then merged within the encoder, and the resulting token sequences are reconstructed onto the original patch grid after decoding. The proposed method is flexible and can be plugged into existing vision Transformer-based semantic segmentation pipelines. Ex-tensive experiments on the benchmark datasets ADE20K, Pascal Context, and Cityscapes show that SCMerge con-sistently achieves a better trade-off between segmentation accuracy and efficiency than baseline methods. 

# 1. Introduction 

Semantic segmentation has made significant progress with the recent introduction of Vision Transformers (ViTs) [8, 24, 29, 37]. However, ViTs with self-attention suffer from quadratic computational complexity and long latency, limiting their application to high-resolution im-ages. A primary avenue for addressing this issue is to reduce token sequence length in self-attention and stream-line computation [1, 17, 23, 25, 28, 30, 36]. Although most existing token reduction methods work well for image classification, they often struggle in dense predic-tion tasks, such as semantic segmentation. The main challenge is to reduce the token sequence length while preserving the spatial and semantic details required by the segmentation decoder. This work focuses on ViT-based semantic segmenta-tion and aims to present a training-free token merging method to improve the efficiency of existing pipelines. Existing training-free token reduction methods have shown that merging similar tokens can effectively reduce the token sequence length and computational complex-ity. ToMe performs bipartite matching in feature space and merges similar tokens via averaging [1]. Mutual Pair Merging (MPM) employs mutual nearest-neighbor matching and combines the matched tokens, yielding computation and latency improvements without modi-fying segmentation decoders [26]. In addition, ALGM employs a segmentation-specific local-to-global merging schedule that combines local and global token match-ing [22]. Despite their potential, existing methods mainly match tokens based on feature similarity, which can cause segmentation errors when local feature content is similar but the tokens are spatially or globally different. To-kens near semantic boundaries may be incorrectly paired with interior tokens from neighboring regions, leading to boundary blurring. In addition, interior tokens from different objects can have similar features and be mis-takenly identified as redundant. More recently, several works [13, 19] have proposed to address this issue by introducing spatial information or learned policies, but at the cost of additional inputs or training. In this paper, we propose SCMerge, a training-free token merging method to reduce the token length in ViT-based semantic segmentation pipelines, aiming to achieve a better trade-off between segmentation accuracy and computational cost. To guide the merging process, We introduce a token-pairing criterion that jointly consid-ers local feature similarity and spatial consistency. By merging only tokens that are similar in both feature and spatial consistency, this mechanism improves the effi-ciency–accuracy trade-off. Specifically, we first compute the cosine similarity between all token pairs. A local variance measure is then used to identify tokens that 1arXiv:2610.21642v1 [cs.CV] 15 Oct 2026 are near semantic boundaries or lie in a smooth interior region. For each candidate pair, we compute a spatial-consistency penalty that discourages merging of locally similar but boundary-sensitive tokens, as well as tokens belonging to different objects. Finally, paired tokens are merged via feature averaging, and the reduced token sequence is passed through the segmentation decoder be-fore being mapped back to the original token grid. This design refines the token pairing process to preserve se-mantic boundaries and neighborhood spatial consistency, improving segmentation efficiency. Our SCMerge does not require additional supervision, extra input modalities, or modifications to the segmen-tation head, allowing it to be plugged into ViT-based semantic segmentation pipelines. We evaluate the effec-tiveness of the proposed token merging module using ViT-based segmentation models [29] on three semantic segmentation datasets, including ADE20K [38], Pascal Context [21], and Cityscapes [5]. Experimental results demonstrate that plugging our SCMerge module into ViT-based semantic segmentation models improves the trade-off between computational complexity and segmen-tation accuracy. Our contributions can be summarized as follows: • We propose a novel training-free token-pair merg-ing method that incorporates a spatial-consistency penalty into feature-similarity-based matching. This method prevents the merging of locally similar but spatially incompatible tokens and improving the accu-racy–efficiency trade-off. • We design a local feature variation metric to iden-tify boundary-sensitive tokens and introduce a spatial-consistency penalty, comprising a boundary-sensitive term and a neighborhood consistency term, to reduce incorrect merging near semantic boundaries and pre-serve fine spatial detail. • Extensive experiments demonstrate that our method can be integrated into ViT-based semantic segmen-tation models to achieve a better accuracy-efficiency trade-off than baseline methods relying only on feature similarity. 

# 2. Related Work 

Efficient ViT Segmentation. Vision Transformer-based segmentation models, including SETR [37], Seg-menter [29], DPT [24], and Mask2Former [4], have achieved promising performance in dense prediction tasks, but they suffer from high computational costs when processing high-resolution images. A possible solution is to redesign the architecture or attention mechanism. SegFormer [34] adopts a hierarchical Transformer en-coder with a lightweight all-MLP decoder. Swin Trans-former [18] restricts self-attention to shifted local win-dows, and PVT [32] progressively reduces spatial res-olution to build pyramid features. FlashAttention [6] accelerates attention by reducing memory input and out-put between GPU high-bandwidth memory and on-chip memory through tiled computation. This work focuses on reducing redundant tokens inside a pretrained ViT encoder , preserving the compact representation through decoding, and reconstructing the dense prediction grid without changing the segmentation head. 

Token Reduction in Vision Transformers. Many recent methods accelerate ViTs by reducing the to-ken sequences passed to subsequent layers. Dynam-icViT [25] learns to prune uninformative tokens dynam-ically, while TokenLearner [28] learns a compact set of informative tokens. A-ViT [36] introduces adaptive token halting, and IA-RED 2 [23] reduces redundancy with interpretability-aware token selection. Patch Slim-ming [30] and SPViT [15] study efficient token pruning, while ATS [9] samples tokens adaptively. Token Pool-ing [20], STViT [12], HeatViT [7], and DiffRate [3] further explore pooling, sampling, pruning, or learnable compression rates. These methods either target image classification or require learned policies, retraining, or architecture-specific modifications. Unlike classification tasks, preserving thin structures and fine boundaries is critical in dense prediction tasks. This depends on having a sufficient number of tokens for accurate reconstruc-tion and precise predictions. This work introduces a training-free token merging method designed for dense prediction. 

Training-free Token Merging. Token merging has emerged as an effective technique for improving the effi-ciency of ViTs. By combining similar tokens rather than discarding them, token merging methods substantially reduce computational complexity and latency in image classification and semantic segmentation. ToMe [1] is a training-free token merging method that matches feature-similar tokens and averages them progressively inside the encoder. Several works have extended token merging with learned or adaptive mechanisms, including learned patch merging [27], efficient token merging [10], delayed spatial merging [11], and learned pruning-and-merging thresholds [2]. In addition, fusion-based or saliency-driven methods, such as token fusion [14], multi-criteria fusion [16], spectrum-preserving merging [31], learnable token merging [33], and saliency-driven merging [35] have been studied for token merging. Mutual Pair Merg-ing (MPM) [26] forms mutual nearest-neighbor token pairs and preserves a reconstruction map before the dense prediction decoder. These methods can significantly im-prove ViT efficiency by averaging similar tokens, but their token grouping strategies mainly rely on feature similarity, which neglects spatial consistency when mea-suring token similarity. Our SCMerge addresses this 2limitation by using local feature variation to pair tokens in a more spatial-aware manner. 

Spatial-aware Token Merging for Semantic Segmen-tation. Several recent methods have incorporated task-specific or spatial information to improve token merg-ing for semantic segmentation. CTS [19] learns a content-aware token-sharing policy to preserve semantic structures. ALGM [22] designs an adaptive local-to-global merging schedule for segmentation backbones. ToSA [13] introduces spatial awareness into token merg-ing so that merging decisions consider both visual feature similarity and spatial information. These methods demon-strate the importance of spatial information for semantic segmentation, but they often rely on learned policies, specialized schedules, additional spatial information, or method-specific implementations. SCMerge follows the same motivation of spatial-aware token merging, but it is training-free and can be plugged into existing ViT-based semantic segmentation pipelines. 

# 3. Method 

As illustrated in Figure 1, SCMerge is a training-free module that can be inserted into multiple blocks of a ViT encoder to reduce the token sequence length from N to M (M < N ), while maintaining a merge map for reconstructing compact segmentation head out-puts onto the original token grid after decoding. is indexed by a representative coordinate ci for local neighborhood statistics, while a merge map tracks recon-struction assignments across merging stages. At each insertion, SCMerge first computes a feature-similarity matrix S to measure content similarity between tokens. It then calculates local context variation within a 3 × 3

token neighborhood and uses this variation to assign a spatial-consistency penalty score when forming match-ing pairs. The matched token pairs are merged into a reduced sequence Y = [ y1, . . . , y M ] ∈ RM ×d. The merged tokens are then propagated through the remain-ing backbone layers, while the corresponding merge map is stored. The merged tokens are then fed into the de-coder and segmentation head of the original ViT-based model to produce compact prediction logits. Finally, the merge map is used to reconstruct these compact logits onto the final semantic segmentation results. 

3.1. Preliminaries 

We follow existing semantic segmentation models [29] and use a standard Vision Transformer (ViT) encoder [8] and Mask Transformer decoder as our backbone. Given an input image of size H × W and patch size P , the im-age is divided into N0 = H/P × W/P non-overlapping patches. Each patch is linearly projected into a d-dimensional embedding, which is added to a learned posi-tional embedding, resulting in the initial token sequence 

X0 ∈ RN0×d. These tokens are then processed by a stack of Transformer blocks consisting of self-attention and feed-forward layers. The decoder then jointly processes the tokens from the encoder together with C learnable class embeddings, where C denotes the number of semantic classes. After the Transformer decoder layers, the output image and class embeddings are projected and normalized, and the pairwise inner products between the image embeddings and the class embeddings produce patch-level class logits. In the original full-token model, these logits correspond to the N0 patch locations and are reshaped into an H/P ×

W/P patch grid. They are then upsampled to the input resolution via bilinear interpolation. 

SCMerge Insertion. SCMerge is inserted after se-lected ViT encoder blocks and operates on the current token sequence. At each insertion, SCMerge reduces the token sequence length from N to M (M < N ). To support multiple merge layers, SCMerge maintains a rep-resentative coordinate for each token and a merge map. The coordinate indicates the token’s position in the sparse token layout used for variation computation, while the merge map records, for each original patch location, the token that represents it. The merge map is updated af-ter each merge stage. The reduced token sequence is then propagated through the remaining encoder blocks and passed through the Mask Transformer decoder. The decoder architecture and parameters remain unchanged, while only the number of input tokens is reduced. After decoding, the merge map restores the reduced logits to the original patch grid, followed by bilinear upsampling to the input resolution.

3.3. Decoding and Reconstruction 

The reduced token sequence Y is passed through the segmentation decoder without spatial expansion, and the original token grid is reconstructed only after decoding for dense prediction. The segmentation decoder pro-cesses the merged token sequence Y and produces class logits H ∈ RM ×C , where C is the number of seman-tic classes. The merge map maps each merged token’s logits back to the original patch locations, reconstruct-ing the N0 patch logits. These logits are then reshaped into the original patch grid and bilinearly upsampled to the input resolution. For the Mask Transformer de-coder, the M reduced tokens are jointly processed with 

C class embeddings, resulting in a sequence of M + C

tokens. Thus, the token reduction is preserved across the entire encoder–decoder pipeline. The decoder remains unchanged in architecture and parameters, introducing no extra learnable parameters. 

# 4. Experiments 

This section evaluates the effectiveness and generaliza-tion of our method on semantic segmentation. We plug our token merging method into existing ViT-based se-mantic segmentation models and present quantitative and qualitative comparisons on three benchmark semantic segmentation datasets among full-token baseline models, models with feature-similarity-based token merging, and models with our method. Later, we also provide a com-prehensive ablation study supporting our design choices. 

4.1. Experimental Setup 

Datasets and Models. We conduct experiments on three datasets: ADE20K [38], Pascal Context [21], and Cityscapes [5], which respectively cover scene parsing, object and stuff segmentation, and high-resolution street scenes. Our experiments use Segmenter [29] as a baseline full-token model. To provide a fair comparison, we re-tain its released encoder and Mask Transformer decoder and keep the input resolution and validation protocol of each checkpoint unchanged. We insert the token merging operation after the second and fifth encoder blocks by default. 

Implementation Details. We fix the deterministic channel-sketch width to ds = 48 across all datasets and backbones, using zero padding only when the fea-ture dimension is not divisible by ds. After each merge stage, the remaining tokens are scattered onto a zero-filled sparse grid at their retained representative coordi-nates; only token features are averaged during merging. We use zero-padded 3 × 3 average pooling with a fixed denominator to compute the first and second moments, and derive the variance as the centered second moment. Assignment maps are combined across merge stages and used to reconstruct the dense logits after decoding the reduced tokens. 

Baselines. We use the officially released Segmenter checkpoint as the full-token baseline model and as the backbone of all training-free token merging methods: ToMe [1], MPM [26], and SCMerge. For ToMe [1], we use r = 200 for the 1024-token Segmenter-B/16 setting and scale r proportionally with the initial token count for other resolutions and backbones, preserving the same per-layer reduction ratio. To compare our method with MPM under a similar token budget, we configure MPM to merge the top 76% of token pairs, and refer to this configuration as MPM-top.

# 5. Conclusion 

In this work, we presented SCMerge, a training-free to-ken merging method that conducts token merging based on both feature similarity and spatial consistency for se-mantic segmentation. The method is flexible and can be plugged into existing ViT-based semantic segmentation models for improving computation efficiency with less quality drop. Extensive comparison experiments on three 8benchmark semantic segmentation datasets ADE20K, Pascal Context, and Cityscapes demonstrate that our SCMerge can achieve better accuracy-latency trade-off compared with existing state-of-the-art training-free to-ken merging methods. Although our SCMerge with the spatial-consistency penalty mitigates boundary blurring, very small objects and extremely thin structures may still be smoothed due to information aggregation. In addition, since SCMerge is training-free and introduces no learn-able parameters, its merging decisions are based on fixed, hand-designed criteria. This may limit its adaptability to diverse or unseen visual patterns. Addressing these limitations is a direction for our future work while pre-serving the plug-and-play and training-free advantages of SCMerge.
