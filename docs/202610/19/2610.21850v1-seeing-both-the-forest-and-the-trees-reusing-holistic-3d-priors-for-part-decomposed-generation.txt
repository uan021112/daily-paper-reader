Abstract
Generating 3D assets with explicit parts requires two properties at once: geometric
detail inside each part and structural coherence across the whole object. Recent
image-conditioned 3D generation models adopt a structured 3D representation
that anchors tokens to explicit spatial positions, capturing both properties, but their
output remains a single fused mesh. We ask how a part-decomposed generation
model can inherit both properties of these holistic priors, and answer with STRUCT-
PARTS, a framework whose core technical contribution is a dual-frame coordinate
interleaving mechanism: each shape latent is interpreted under both a part-local
and an object-global reference frame, and processed through a global-local inter-
leaved transformer that simultaneously reuses the prior’s geometric detail at the part
scale and its structural coherence at the whole-object scale. As its structural input,
STRUCTPARTS uses a segmented mesh scaffold, a coarse mesh paired with a face-
level segmentation mask kept entirely within the prior’s native 3D representation.
With only lightweight finetuning, both intra-part detail and inter-part coherence
emerge from the same pretrained weights. On PartObjaverse-Tiny, STRUCTPARTS
matches or surpasses prior part-decomposed models at a fraction of their training
cost, while naturally supporting part-mesh refinement and part-level editing.

Introduction
Part-decomposed 3D asset generation is essential for downstream tasks such as localized editing and
animation. A part-decomposed generation model must produce two properties at once: geometric
detail inside each part, and structural coherence across the whole object. Recent image-conditioned
3D generation models [3, 4] adopt a structured 3D representation that anchors tokens to explicit
spatial positions, jointly scaling to high-fidelity global structure and local geometric detail. The native
3D priors of such holistic models therefore already encode what part-decomposed generation needs,
even though they emit a single fused mesh. Yet directly extending these priors to part-decomposed
generation has proven non-trivial.
Approaches that adapt holistic 3D generation models to part-decomposed generation can be grouped
into three families, and none of them fully inherits both properties of the holistic prior. (i) Methods
that feed a point cloud [5, 6, 7], sometimes with extra abstract layout such as bounding boxes [5],
supply an input the holistic prior was never trained to take; the part model must therefore relearn
its conditioning almost from scratch, incurring substantial retraining cost [5, 7]. (ii) Two-stage
methods [8, 9] repurpose the holistic generation model itself to predict structural abstractions such
as bounding boxes or cuboid meshes in the first stage; the resulting objective shift drags the prior
into a non-geometric latent space and erases the very geometric detail the pretraining was meant
to provide. (iii) Methods that keep the original holistic input but predict each part directly in the
unnormalized object frame [10, 11] preserve the input format but mismatch the prior’s normalized
pretraining space at the output side, where tiny parts occupy only a small fraction of the canvas and
degrade accordingly. Across all three, the prior is forced to operate outside the conditions it was
pretrained on, leaving neither its geometric detail nor its structural coherence fully inherited. This
motivates our central question:
How can a part-decomposed generation model inherit both the structural coherence and the geometric
detail of a holistic 3D prior?
Answering this question requires recognizing a fundamental characteristic of 3D data: its inherent
hierarchical structure. A hat is both a component of a human character and a complete shape in
its own right. This dual identity is not incidental but ubiquitous, and a holistic 3D prior trained on
diverse 3D assets has implicitly observed each part as both a piece and a whole. To honor this duality,
a part-decomposed model should not inherit the prior at one scale only: it should expose both scales
simultaneously, treating each part as a complete shape locally (so per-part geometric detail is reused
exactly as during pretraining) and as part of the whole object globally (so structural coherence is
preserved across the assembly). We summarize this principle as seeing both the forest and the trees:
just as a tree is itself a complete shape and at the same time part of a larger forest, a model should
reason at the part scale and the object scale at once. The duality is mutually reinforcing: global
context constrains parts toward coherence, while high-quality parts elevate the geometric fidelity of
the whole.
Honoring this duality requires the model to read the prior at both scales simultaneously, which in
turn forces a careful choice of structural input. We propose STRUCTPARTS, whose first design
choice is to keep the structural input format that the holistic prior was already pretrained on. Recent
holistic 3D generation models [3, 12] condition shape synthesis on a coarse 3D mesh via voxel-
anchored positional embeddings; we recognize that this same format already carries everything a
part model needs to anchor its latents in 3D space, with the addition of a single face-level segmenta-
tion mask sufficing to expose part boundaries. Together, the coarse mesh and segmentation mask
form a segmented mesh scaffold: a structural input the holistic prior reads without any objective
shift, and one that off-the-shelf upstream stages produce as a by-product of holistic generation and
segmentation.
With the scaffold in place, the core mechanism of STRUCTPARTS is dual-frame coordinate inter-
leaving, which inherits both prior properties at once. Each scaffold-anchored latent is interpreted
under two complementary frames: a local frame normalized within each part, and a global frame
normalized across the whole object. These yield distinct local and global positional embeddings,
processed through a global-local interleaved transformer that alternates between intra-part and
inter-part attention. In the local frame, each part is processed as a standalone shape, reusing the
prior’s geometric detail as during pretraining; in the global frame, the parts are coordinated as one
anchored assembly, preserving the prior’s structural coherence. Both properties therefore emerge
2


from the same pretrained weights with only lightweight finetuning, rather than requiring extensive
retraining [7, 11, 5].
In practice, our pipeline stays simple. We train on segmented ground-truth watertight meshes; at
inference, scaffolds come from a single image via structure generation [13] and 3D segmentation
models [14, 15]. We retain the image condition alongside the scaffold; together with the holistic prior
for geometric details, this enables part-level mesh refinement even when the scaffold is coarse. The
scaffold also supports part-level editing via merge/split through segmentation regrouping in a training-
free manner. On PartObjaverse-Tiny, STRUCTPARTS matches or surpasses prior part-decomposed
models with substantially lower training costs, showing that strong part-decomposed 3D generation
can emerge from a minimal reinterpretation of a holistic prior rather than a part-specific model trained
from scratch; we demonstrate generation examples from STRUCTPARTS in Fig. 1.
In summary, our contributions are three-fold:
• We propose a dual-frame coordinate interleaving mechanism that lets a part-decomposed
generation model inherit both the geometric detail and structural coherence of a holistic 3D
prior with only lightweight finetuning.
• We adapt the coarse-mesh conditioning from holistic 3D generation models by adding a face-
level segmentation mask, yielding a segmented mesh scaffold that exposes part boundaries
while remaining in the prior’s native 3D representation.
• On PartObjaverse-Tiny, our method matches or surpasses prior part-aware methods at a
fraction of their training cost, while supporting part-mesh refinement and part-level editing.

Related Work
3D holistic shape generation. Recent 3D shape generation has converged on two representation
paradigms. Sparse-Voxel methods [16, 4] restrict computation to active surface voxels, yielding
a structured latent space well-suited for editing, but the active-voxel sequence grows rapidly with
resolution and demands sparse convolution and specialized attention. VecSet-based approaches [17,
18, 19, 20, 21, 13] compress a shape into a small set of feature vectors via cross-attention between a
dense point cloud and sparser point queries, fitting cleanly into standard transformer attention. Recent
localizable VecSet variants such as LATTICE [3, 12] anchor each token to an explicit 3D position,
inheriting both VecSet’s efficiency and Sparse-Voxel’s spatial grounding and capturing both global
structure and local geometric detail. Despite this progress, all such holistic models still emit a single
fused mesh, motivating a part-decomposed counterpart that directly reuses these native 3D priors
rather than disrupting them.
3D parts reasoning. 3D part reasoning extracts part structure from existing 3D shapes. Traditional
supervised methods [22] train segmentation networks on labeled benchmarks but struggle to gen-
eralize beyond seen categories. 2D-lifting methods [23, 24] render multiple views and propagate
masks from 2D foundation models [25, 26] back to the 3D surface; this enables open-vocabulary
segmentation but suffers from view inconsistency and unstable post-processing. Native 3D data-
engine methods [27, 15, 28, 14] instead distill 2D foundation features into a 3D backbone for direct
prediction. None of these synthesize new geometry; they yield part masks on a given mesh. Our
work directly exploits this readily available signal at inference time, treating the segmented mesh
as a structural scaffold injected into a holistic generation model, rather than training a separate
part-prediction head from scratch or coupling decomposition and geometry generation within a single
model [11, 10].
3D decomposed shape generation. Existing part-decomposed generation models acknowledge
native 3D priors but exploit them only indirectly, falling into four patterns. (i) Conditioning a
learned model on a point cloud, sometimes with additional abstract layout [5, 6, 7]; because such
input formats differ from how holistic shape models are conditioned, the prior is not preserved and
substantial retraining is required [5, 7]. (ii) Repurposing the generation model to first synthesize
structural abstractions like bounding boxes or cuboid meshes [8, 9]; the first-stage objective shift
drags the prior into a latent space sharply different from the native shape prior. (iii) Keeping the
original holistic input but predicting each part in the unnormalized object frame [10, 11, 29]; this
spatial-resolution mismatch hurts tiny parts that occupy only a small fraction of the object volume.
(iv) Lifting 2D multi-view part predictions to 3D [30]; this sidesteps the native 3D latent at the cost
3


of view consistency and post-processing complexity. In contrast, STRUCTPARTS stays in the native
geometric domain, exposes part boundaries through a segmented mesh scaffold, and uses dual-frame
coordinate interleaving to reason at both scales with only lightweight finetuning rather than full
retraining of the underlying generation model.
3
Method
This section instantiates STRUCTPARTS. As illustrated in Fig. 2, given a single image and a segmented
mesh scaffold, we sample per-part shape latents anchored to the scaffold, attach dual-frame positional
embeddings (one local to each part, one global across the object), and process them with a global-local
interleaved transformer; the image condition is retained throughout, so fine detail flows from the
image into each part even when the scaffold is coarse. We review the structured latent representation
we build on (Sec. 3.1), detail the scaffold-guided global-local interleaving (Sec. 3.2), and describe its
training-free applications (Sec. 3.3).
3.1
Preliminaries: structured shape latents for 3D diffusion
Structured shape latents. We build on recent 3D shape generation models [3, 12] that adopt a
structured 3D representation, in which each latent token is anchored to an explicit spatial position.

X ∈RN×7 (positions, normals, sharp-edge flags). We quantize X onto a regular grid of resolution R,
yielding a query set X0 ∈RN0×7 with integer voxel indices V0 ∈{0, . . . , R−1}N0×3 (one token
per occupied cell), providing stable spatial information for the 3D DiT’s positional embeddings (PE).

that holistic 3D models already accept with only a face-level mask, keeping the input in the prior’s
native 3D representation.

3.3
Downstream tasks
Because STRUCTPARTS retains the holistic prior intact and is scaffold-guided, two capabilities
emerge without any additional training:
Part-mesh refinement from a coarse scaffold. The dual-frame embeddings only require coarse
voxel indices, and the image condition is retained at every block. As a result, the model can refine a
5


low-fidelity scaffold into high-quality decomposed parts at inference time, with fine geometric detail
recovered directly from the image rather than from the scaffold itself.
Part-level editing via scaffold regrouping. The segmentation mask L in the scaffold gives users
a direct handle on decomposition granularity. Inspired by RePaint [34] and X-Part [5], we support
training-free merge and split operations: merge fuses adjacent scaffold segments into a single coherent
part by relabeling their faces in L, while split subdivides an existing segment into distinct sub-parts.
At inference, the modified L determines which latents are resampled by the flow-matching process;
tokens in unedited regions are kept so that the rest of the object remains consistent.
4
Experiments
We first detail our implementation (Sec. 4.1) and experimental setup (Sec. 4.2). Next, we evaluate
our approach against state-of-the-art methods through comprehensive quantitative and qualitative
comparisons (Sec. 4.3). We then validate our core design choices via ablation studies (Sec. 4.4), and
finally showcase the versatility of our pipeline through various downstream applications (Sec. 4.5).
4.1
Implementation Details
Backbone and initialization. STRUCTPARTS uses a 21-layer DiT backbone following Hunyuan3D-
2.1 [13], with self-attention layers alternating between intra-part (local frame, 1283 part-normalized
RoPE) and inter-part (global frame, 5123 shape-global RoPE) positional conditioning. We initialize
from open-source Hunyuan3D-2.1 [13] weights and finetune on part-level data.
Training data. We train on Partverse-XL [9] (∼36K objects, ∼240K parts). Raw part meshes are
remeshed to watertight via cuBVH [11] and 24 conditioning views per object are rendered following
Trellis [16]; segmented watertight scaffolds for the full object are obtained by baking multi-view
rendered segmentation labels onto a watertight remesh of the whole shape (full details in App. A.1).
Training compute. The DiT is trained for 40K steps on 8 H200 GPUs over ∼4 days, for a total
allocation of 768 H200-hours. Full network configuration (hidden dim, MoE setup, NTK-aware
RoPE base) and hyperparameters (optimizer, precision, gradient clipping, per-part token budget) are
deferred to App. A.1.
4.2
Experimental Setup
We evaluate on PartObjaverse-Tiny [27] (200 high-quality objects with detailed segmentation labels),
whose UIDs are strictly excluded from our training set.
Metrics. We report Chamfer Distance (CD) and F-Score at thresholds 0.05 and 0.10 (F-Score@0.05,
F-Score@0.10) at two granularities: part-level, where each part is normalized into a [−1, 1] bounding
box, and shape-level, where the assembled object is normalized into a [−1, 1] bounding box.
Settings and baselines. Because baselines differ in whether they accept layout controls, we use
two complementary protocols. (i) GT-layout-conditioned, part-level. Methods that consume a
layout (HoloPart [6], X-Part [5], FullPart [9], OmniPart [8]) receive ground-truth bounding boxes
or segmentation masks, yielding a one-to-one correspondence between predicted and ground-truth
parts and a direct part-level geometric assessment. (ii) Image-to-decomposed-parts, shape-level.
Direct-generation baselines [8, 10, 11] synthesize parts from a single image with uncontrollable
output layouts, so all methods are evaluated on the assembled shape; the layout-dependent methods
are run with a standardized predicted pipeline using Hunyuan3D-2.1 [13] for the scaffold mesh and
P3-SAM [14] for the part labels.
Matched-budget comparison. In addition to released baseline checkpoints, we retrain X-Part with
the same Hunyuan3D-2.1 initialization, Partverse-XL data, 512 tokens per part, and 768-H200-hour
allocation as STRUCTPARTS. Tab. 1 reports this result separately; implementation details, training
schedules, checkpoint comparisons, and per-object compute are given in App. A.2.

4.3
Main Results
Layout-conditioned evaluation (part-level). Tab. 1 shows STRUCTPARTS achieves the best de-
composition quality across all metrics, with qualitative comparisons in Fig. 3. The advantage also
holds over X-Part retrained under the same GPU-hour budget, providing a controlled comparison of
training efficiency. FullPart and OmniPart often fail on complex structures even with ground-truth
layouts. HoloPart and XPart follow the given layouts but introduce input signals that disrupt the
native 3D prior, yielding missing geometric detail (XPart, row 2), incomplete shapes (row 1 for both),
or floaters within part bounding boxes (XPart, row 3). STRUCTPARTS avoids these failure modes by
reusing the holistic prior in its native representation.
Image-to-decomposed-parts evaluation (shape-level). Tab. 2 shows STRUCTPARTS is comparable
to released X-Part and outperforms the other baselines by a clear margin; the matched-budget
comparison is reported separately in Tab. 1. Fig. 4 reveals each baseline’s failure mode: PartPacker and
PartCrafter cannot produce semantically meaningful decompositions as they synthesize parts without
explicit controllability; FullPart and OmniPart’s bbox-conditioned formulation causes collisions and
mismatches at part boundaries that propagate into the assembled shape. STRUCTPARTS, XPart and
HoloPart share the same scaffold input, yet only STRUCTPARTS leaves the holistic prior undisturbed,
preserving fine geometric detail in the final decomposed shape generation.
Additional validation. On a fixed 200-object ABO subset evaluated with identical inputs and metrics,
STRUCTPARTS obtains the lowest mean and median CD and matches released X-Part at F@0.05.
Category-wise results, sample counts, and paired bootstrap intervals are reported in App. A.4. A
controlled comparison of Hunyuan3D-2.1 and UltraShape initialization at the same training point is
given in App. A.5.

4.5
Applications
As discussed in Sec. 3.3, the scaffold-conditioned formulation naturally supports downstream tasks.
We demonstrate two: decomposed-mesh refinement and part-level editing.
Decomposed-mesh refinement. As shown in Fig. 6, when the input scaffold [13] is coarse, X-Part [5]
rigidly follows the input geometry and fails to recover detail, while STRUCTPARTS synthesizes fine
geometric details that faithfully match the input image. By aligning each part with the holistic shape
latent space, our dual-frame design inherits the detail-recovery ability of the underlying generation
prior rather than forcing the model into a new part-specific interface, suggesting potential to scale to
high-quality mesh generation.

Technical appendices and supplementary material
A.1
Implementation Details
Dataset and scaffold construction. We train on Partverse-XL [9], which contains 36K objects with
about 240K parts in total. Because the raw per-part meshes in Partverse-XL are not watertight, we
apply cuBVH-based watertight remeshing [11] to each part before training. For each object, 24
conditioning views are rendered following Trellis [16].
To obtain segmented watertight scaffolds for the full object, we (i) watertight-remesh the entire
shape, (ii) bake multi-view rendered segmentation labels from the raw (non-watertight) mesh onto
the watertight surface using a multi-view depth-fusion procedure [16], and (iii) filter out edge labels
that fall outside the ground-truth per-part bounding boxes to clean up boundary noise. The result is a
3D-consistent watertight mesh whose face labels match the original part decomposition.
Network architecture. Our DiT backbone follows Hunyuan3D-2.1 [13]: 21 layers, hidden dimension
2048, 16 attention heads. Self-attention alternates by layer parity: even layers are intra-part (tokens
attend only within their own part) and odd layers are inter-part (tokens attend across all parts). Each
token carries a 3D voxel coordinate encoded by two-scale rotary positional embeddings: a part-local
1283 grid in intra-part layers and a shape-global 5123 grid in inter-part layers. The shape-global
RoPE uses NTK-aware scaling [35, 32] with rotary base θ = 40k, a 4× scaling of the default
θ = 10k chosen to accommodate the larger 5123 position range. The last 6 blocks replace the dense
feed-forward layer with a Mixture-of-Experts feed-forward (8 experts, top-2 routing) to enhance
latent-space capacity. All attention layers use FlashAttention-3 kernels.
Training procedure. The DiT is initialized from open-source Hunyuan3D-2.1’s ShapeDiT [13]
weights and fine-tuned on part-level data using a rectified-flow-matching objective with a linear noise
schedule and velocity prediction. We use AdamW with learning rate 1e−4 and weight decay 10−2,
in bfloat16 mixed precision (forward/backward in bf16; master weights and optimizer states in fp32)
with gradient-norm clipping at 1.0. During training we fix the per-part token budget to 512, though
the model can scale to more tokens at inference. The DiT is trained on 8 GPUs for 40K steps within
a 768-H200-hour allocation (8 H200 GPUs for four days).
VAE finetuning. The VAE component is finetuned with a token length of 512 to ensure it can encode
and decode individual parts effectively at the per-part scale used during DiT training.
Inference pipeline. At inference, the segmented mesh scaffold is obtained from a single image
via two off-the-shelf upstream stages: a holistic 3D generation model [13] produces the coarse
mesh, and a 3D segmentation model [14] provides the per-face part labels. The image is encoded
by DINOv2 [26]; per-part shape latents are sampled by the rectified-flow scheduler conditioned on
dual-frame RoPE indices derived from the scaffold and on the image features. Because the dual-frame
embeddings only require coarse voxel indices and the image condition is retained in every block,
low-fidelity scaffolds are sufficient: missing geometric detail is recovered directly from the image
(see the part-mesh refinement application in Sec. 3.3).
A.2

To verify training efficiency under a controlled resource budget, we retrain X-Part [5] using the same
initialization, training data, and token budget as STRUCTPARTS (Sec. 4.2). Both runs use 8 H200
GPUs for four days. The X-Part implementation and hyperparameters follow the setup confirmed
with its authors; the retrained model is distinguished from its released checkpoint throughout the
evaluation.
Computation and conditioning. To measure the cost of the two adaptation strategies, we profile
both methods with the same PyTorch profiler, including forward and backward computation. Tab. 4
summarizes the shared training allocation and per-object costs. STRUCTPARTS uses 32.5% fewer
TFLOPs per object and less memory. X-Part uses learned global-object and per-part local-geometry
conditioners, fuses semantic features into both, and injects two 4,096-token streams through separate
cross-attention branches in every DiT block. STRUCTPARTS instead derives dual-frame coordinates
from scaffold voxel indices and reuses object-level image conditioning across parts. Computing
X-Part conditioning online per part partly explains its approximately 1.5× longer step time. We

A.3
Scaffold Robustness and Geometry Preservation
Geometry preservation and whole-shape sensitivity. To verify geometry preservation and quantify
sensitivity to upstream errors, we evaluate the input Hunyuan scaffold and generated outputs against
the same GT objects with identical normalization and surface sampling. Tab. 6 reports the input
scaffold, clean output, and outputs under two controlled perturbations. With all other settings fixed,
we add normal-direction vertex noise at 1%, 2%, or 5% of the scaffold bounding-box diagonal, or
relabel 5%, 10%, or 20% of boundary voxels. The clean output preserves overall geometry; aggregate
metrics do not isolate high-frequency detail, which is assessed qualitatively in Fig. 6. Geometry noise
causes little change at 1%, moderate degradation at 2%, and substantial degradation at 5%. Label
corruption changes whole-shape metrics only slightly, indicating that assembly can conceal incorrect
part boundaries.
Part-level sensitivity. To expose segmentation errors hidden by assembly, we apply the same label-
corruption rates to GT part labels and compare each generated part with its corresponding GT part.
GT labels retain a known correspondence, whereas P3-SAM part IDs cannot be reliably matched
to GT part IDs. Tab. 7 shows nearly unchanged performance at 5% corruption and progressively
worse part consistency at 10–20%. These controlled perturbations assess local error sensitivity; the
dependence on reasonable coarse topology and the effects of natural structural failures are discussed
in Sec. 5.

A.5
Initialization Dependence and Method Comparison
Alternative initialization. To verify dependence on the Hunyuan3D-2.1 prior, we also initialize
STRUCTPARTS from UltraShape [12], whose DiT is pretrained with a coarse-mesh anchor. Since
the compatible UltraShape DiT requires retraining, we compare both initializations at the same 20K
halfway training point using part-level metrics. UltraShape achieves mean/median CD of 0.123/0.015
and F@0.05/F@0.10 of 0.64/0.77; the Hunyuan3D-2.1 baseline is the 20K STRUCTPARTS checkpoint
in Tab. 5. The nearly identical F-scores support adaptation of both initializations at this common
training point.
Comparison with UniPart. To clarify the distinction from dual-space part representations, we
compare the two formulations. UniPart first generates and segments a whole-object geometry–
segmentation latent, then predicts separate global and canonical part latents through two-stage
diffusion. STRUCTPARTS instead processes one latent within one DiT under alternating part-local and
object-global frames, accepting an editable segmented scaffold for training-free merge/split editing.
This comparison is conceptual; UniPart code was unavailable at the time of evaluation, preventing a
matched quantitative comparison.
A.6
Social Impact
Positive impact. STRUCTPARTS produces explicitly part-decomposed 3D assets, enabling part-aware
downstream workflows such as localized editing, animation rigging, retopology, and semantic asset
reuse. By reusing a pretrained holistic 3D prior with only lightweight finetuning, the method avoids
the energy and data costs of training a part-aware generative model from scratch, lowering the carbon
footprint of part-decomposed 3D research and making this capability accessible to teams without
large-scale compute infrastructure. The scaffold-conditioned interface and training-free editing
17


operations (merging and splitting parts via segmentation regrouping) further lower the barrier for non-
experts to iterate on 3D content, with potential applications in education, indie game development,
and rapid prototyping for design and engineering.
Negative impact and mitigation. Like other generative 3D models, STRUCTPARTS can be misused,
for example to produce unauthorized reproductions of copyrighted 3D assets or to fabricate misleading
3D media. Because the model inherits a pretrained holistic prior, it also inherits any biases present in
the upstream training data, such as over-representation of common object categories or culturally
skewed design styles. To mitigate these risks, we train and release our model only with publicly
available, license-compliant datasets, and we encourage downstream users to attribute reused assets
and to verify generated outputs for intellectual-property and ethical concerns before deployment.
The model is not designed for, and we do not recommend its use in, identity-related or surveillance
applications.

NeurIPS Paper Checklist
The checklist is designed to encourage best practices for responsible machine learning research,
addressing issues of reproducibility, transparency, research ethics, and societal impact. Do not remove
the checklist: The papers not including the checklist will be desk rejected. The checklist should
follow the references and follow the (optional) supplemental material. The checklist does NOT count
towards the page limit.
Please read the checklist guidelines carefully for information on how to answer these questions. For
each question in the checklist:
• You should answer [Yes], [No], or [N/A].
• [N/A] means either that the question is Not Applicable for that particular paper or the
relevant information is Not Available.
• Please provide a short (1–2 sentence) justification right after your answer (even for [N/A]).
The checklist answers are an integral part of your paper submission. They are visible to the
reviewers, area chairs, senior area chairs, and ethics reviewers. You will also be asked to include it
(after eventual revisions) with the final version of your paper, and its final version will be published
with the paper.
The reviewers of your paper will be asked to use the checklist as one of the factors in their evaluation.
While [Yes] is generally preferable to [No], it is perfectly acceptable to answer [No] provided a
proper justification is given (e.g., error bars are not reported because it would be too computationally
expensive” or “we were unable to find the license for the dataset we used”). In general, answering
[No] or [N/A] is not grounds for rejection. While the questions are phrased in a binary way, we
acknowledge that the true answer is often more nuanced, so please just use your best judgment and
write a justification to elaborate. All supporting evidence can appear either in the main paper or the
supplemental material, provided in appendix. If you answer [Yes] to a question, in the justification
please point to the section(s) where related material for the question can be found.
