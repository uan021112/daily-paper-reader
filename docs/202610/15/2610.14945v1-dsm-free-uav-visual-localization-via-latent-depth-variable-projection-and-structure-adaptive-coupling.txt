DSM-Free UAV Visual Localization via Latent-Depth Variable
Projection and Structure-Adaptive Coupling
Yibin Ye, Xichao Teng, Shuo Chen, Xiaokai Song, Yuli Sun, Qifeng Yu, Zhang Li, Member, IEEE
Abstract—Visual localization enables unmanned aerial vehi-
cles (UAVs) to maintain absolute positioning in GNSS-denied
environments. A common approach matches UAV images with
geo-tagged orthographic maps and estimates camera pose using
Perspective-n-Point (PnP) with elevations from Digital Surface
Models (DSMs). However, accurate and up-to-date DSMs are
often unavailable in practice. Orthographic–perspective geometry
(OPG) enables DSM-free localization in scenes with varying
elevations, yet existing methods typically estimate pose indirectly
through intermediate matrix estimation and decomposition. To
address this limitation, we propose Latent-Depth Variable Projec-
tion (LDVP), which treats the unknown scales along perspective
viewing rays as latent depths. By analytically eliminating these
depths and solving for the horizontal camera center in closed
form given the rotation, LDVP reduces nonlinear estimation
to a rotation-only problem, enabling direct recovery of metric
horizontal position without assuming a planar scene. To im-
prove localization robustness across different scene structures,
we further introduce Structure-Adaptive Coupling (SAC), which
uses relative scene structure inferred from monocular depth
estimation to select LDVP when the matched points are not
dominated by a single plane, and Planar-PnP otherwise. Our
local observability analysis reveals how scene elevation variation
affects the geometric constraints of OPG, providing a basis for
this adaptive strategy. Extensive experiments show that LDVP
consistently outperforms existing OPG-based DSM-free methods,
while SAC further improves robustness across diverse scenes and
substantially narrows the gap to DSM-based localization. Code
will be released at https://github.com/UAV-AVL/LDVP-SAC.
Index Terms—UAV Visual Localization, Digital Surface Model,
Orthographic–Perspective Geometry, Variable Projection.
I. INTRODUCTION
Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) are increasingly used
in applications such as inspection, emergency response, and
mapping [1]. Most outdoor UAV systems rely on the Global
Navigation Satellite System (GNSS), together with Inertial
Measurement Units (IMUs), for global positioning and naviga-
tion. However, GNSS can become unreliable in environments
with signal blockage or interference, while inertial navigation
inevitably accumulates drift over time [2]. Visual localization
therefore plays an important role in maintaining absolute
positioning capability and operational autonomy when GNSS
is degraded or unavailable [3].
Map-based absolute UAV visual localization commonly
matches onboard UAV images against geo-tagged orthographic
reference maps, such as aerial orthophotos or orthorectified

satellite imagery, and recovers the UAV pose from the geo-
metric relationship between the two views [4], [5]. The choice
of pose estimation model is closely related to the elevation
structure represented by the matched points. When most points
lie on or near a common plane, the planar assumption closely
matches the scene geometry and provides an effective way to
estimate pose without per-point elevation. This condition is
often encountered in relatively flat, expansive areas, such as
plains, or in scenes dominated by planar structures, including
roads and rooftops. Accordingly, planar models have been
widely used in UAV localization [3], [6], [7]. In low-altitude
flight, however, the validity of this assumption becomes
strongly scene dependent. Matched points may span buildings,
vegetation, sloped terrain, and other structures at different
elevations, producing parallax that cannot be explained by a
single plane. A more general solution is to recover the 3D
coordinates of the matched map points from a Digital Surface
Model (DSM) [4], [8], [9] and estimate the camera pose using
a Perspective-n-Point (PnP) solver [10].
Although DSM-based PnP can handle general 3D scene
structure, obtaining elevation data that meet the requirements
of high-accuracy UAV localization remains difficult in prac-
tice. High-quality DSM generation usually relies on dedicated
stereo photogrammetry, airborne LiDAR, or other specialized
mapping procedures. Widely available global elevation prod-
ucts are easier to obtain, but their spatial resolution is often
much coarser than that of the 2D reference maps used for
visual localization. For example, AW3D30, a widely used
global DSM, provides a spatial resolution of approximately
30 m [11], whereas reference maps used for UAV localization
are commonly available at meter-level or finer resolution [4],
[5]. This resolution gap makes it difficult to assign sufficiently
accurate elevations to individual matched points. Moreover,
in rapidly changing urban areas or disaster-affected regions,
buildings and local terrain may change after the DSM is
acquired, leading to geometric inconsistency between the
elevation data and the 2D reference map [12]. Previous studies
have also shown that degraded or missing DSM information
can noticeably reduce the accuracy of low-altitude UAV lo-
calization [5], [13]. These limitations motivate methods that
can recover accurate pose directly from 2D orthographic maps
without requiring DSM elevations.
Orthographic–perspective geometry (OPG) [14] provides
a promising way to address this problem beyond the pla-
nar assumption. By eliminating unknown scene elevations,
OPG derives pose constraints directly from correspondences
between perspective UAV images and orthographic refer-
ence maps. Larsson et al. introduced orthographic–perspective
epipolar (OPE) geometry and derived minimal solvers for

the corresponding essential matrix [14]. More recently, Li et
al. proposed the Antisymmetric-Homography (AH) constraint
for DSM-free horizontal localization [13]. However, existing
OPG-based methods typically recover pose indirectly through
intermediate matrix estimation and decomposition. Moreover,
OPG constraints are inherently sensitive to scene elevation
structure. Our local observability analysis shows that elevation
variation strengthens pose constraints, whereas near-planar
structures lead to weaker observability. These challenges high-
light the need for both direct metric pose estimation and
adaptation to different scene structures.
To enable direct metric pose estimation without a DSM,
we propose Latent-Depth Variable Projection (LDVP), a ge-
ometric formulation based on OPG. By treating the unknown
scale along each perspective viewing ray as a latent depth,
LDVP analytically eliminates these variables and solves for
the horizontal camera center in closed form given the rotation.
This reduces nonlinear optimization to camera rotation and
enables recovery of the metric horizontal position without
intermediate matrix estimation and decomposition. Unlike
planar methods, LDVP handles elevation variations without
requiring per-point height information.
To improve DSM-free localization across different scene
structures, we further introduce Structure-Adaptive Coupling
(SAC). Since the observability of OPG depends on scene
elevation variation, LDVP may become less reliable in ap-
proximately planar scenes. For such scenes, we consider
planar PnP (Planar-PnP), which estimates camera pose by
assigning a common elevation to the matched map points. SAC
uses relative scene structure inferred from monocular depth
estimation (MDE) [15] to select LDVP when the matched
points are not dominated by a single plane, and Planar-PnP
otherwise. By adapting the pose estimation method to scene
structure, SAC improves localization robustness across diverse
scenes without requiring a DSM.
We evaluate the proposed framework on the AnyVisLoc
and OrthoLoC benchmarks [5], [4], which cover diverse low-
altitude environments and orthographic reference maps. Ex-
periments show that LDVP consistently outperforms existing
OPG-based DSM-free methods, validating the effectiveness
of direct metric pose estimation. The results also confirm
the complementary behavior of LDVP and Planar-PnP across
different scene structures. By selecting between them ac-
cordingly, SAC further improves localization robustness and
substantially narrows the performance gap between DSM-free
and DSM-based localization. Overall, the main contributions
of this work are summarized as follows:
• We propose Latent-Depth Variable Projection (LDVP), a
direct metric formulation for DSM-free UAV localization.
By analytically eliminating latent ray depths and solving
for the horizontal camera center in closed form given the
rotation, LDVP reduces nonlinear estimation to camera
rotation.
• We analyze the local observability of orthographic–
perspective geometry, revealing how scene elevation vari-
ation affects pose constraints and providing a geometric
basis for structure-adaptive pose estimation.
• We develop Structure-Adaptive Coupling (SAC), which
uses relative scene structure inferred from monocular
depth estimation to select between LDVP and Planar-PnP.
Experiments demonstrate improved robustness across di-
verse scenes and a substantially reduced gap to DSM-
based localization.
II. RELATED WORK
A. DSM-based UAV Visual Localization
Map-based absolute UAV visual localization usually starts
from establishing correspondences between UAV images and
2D reference maps. Early approaches mainly relied on hand-
crafted local features, such as SIFT, SURF, and ORB, which
provide efficient feature matching under moderate viewpoint
and appearance variations [16], [17], [18]. With the develop-
ment of deep learning, learned feature representations have
gradually become dominant. Sparse feature-based methods,
including SuperPoint and LightGlue, improve feature detec-
tion and matching robustness through learned representations
[19], [20]. More recent detector-free and dense matching
approaches, such as LoFTR, DKM, and RoMa, further en-
hance correspondence estimation under large viewpoint and
appearance changes [21], [22], [23]. These advances provide
increasingly reliable correspondences for subsequent geomet-
ric localization.
To recover metric UAV poses from image correspondences,
many existing approaches introduce external elevation infor-
mation. DSM-based localization methods lift matched ref-
erence pixels to 3D with DSMs and exploit the resulting
2D–3D correspondences for camera pose estimation, typically
through Perspective-n-Point (PnP) solvers with robust estima-
tion strategies such as Random Sample Consensus (RANSAC)
[8], [9], [5], [4]. Several studies further improve localization
robustness by incorporating gravity-direction constraints [24]
or inertial attitude priors [25]. More recently, AeroMap3D
combines bare-earth DEMs with OpenStreetMap semantics to
filter correspondences affected by unmodeled buildings before
DEM-based 2D–3D lifting and PnP-RANSAC pose estimation
[12]. Beyond DSM-based approaches, other external 3D scene
representations, including Level-of-Detail (LoD) 3D maps
[26], 3D meshes, and Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) [27],
have also been explored to provide geometric constraints for
visual localization. However, methods in this category rely
on accurate and up-to-date external geometric information,
which is difficult to acquire and maintain in practical UAV
applications. In contrast, this work focuses on metric UAV lo-
calization using 2D reference maps without external elevation
or 3D scene models.
B. DSM-free UAV Visual Localization
To avoid reliance on external elevation information, DSM-
free UAV localization methods attempt to recover camera
poses from correspondences between UAV images and 2D
reference maps. A widely adopted strategy is to assume
that the matched scene is approximately planar. Under this
assumption, many existing methods estimate homographies
between UAV images and reference maps [28], [29], [1],

while camera pose can also be estimated by applying PnP to
coplanar 3D points, referred to here as Planar-PnP [30]. These
approaches are effective when the planar assumption closely
matches the scene structure, but their accuracy can degrade
when matched points span substantial elevation variation [5].
Beyond the planar assumption, several studies have ex-
plored more general geometric formulations for DSM-free
localization. For street-view localization, Sekii et al. modeled
each 2D reference point in an aerial image as a vertical
3D line with unknown altitude and estimated camera pose
from point-to-line constraints [31]. Larsson et al. developed
Orthographic–Perspective Epipolar (OPE) geometry for pose
estimation against a 2D reference map without explicit eleva-
tion information [14]. More recently, Li et al. introduced the
Antisymmetric-Homography (AH) formulation and its associ-
ated essential matrix for metric UAV localization without prior
map elevation [13]. Unlike planar methods, OPE and AH elim-
inate the unknown scene elevations and allow pose estimation
from orthographic–perspective correspondences with varying
elevations. However, these approaches recover pose indirectly
through intermediate matrix estimation and decomposition,
rather than directly estimating the metric pose. Furthermore,
the pose observability of OPG depends on scene elevation
variation and may weaken in approximately planar scenes.
Existing OPG-based methods do not explicitly account for this
scene-dependent limitation.
C. Monocular Depth-guided Visual Localization
Recent advances in monocular depth estimation (MDE)
have substantially improved cross-scene generalization and
geometric prediction, making monocular depth an increasingly
practical geometric prior for image matching and visual local-
ization [32], [33], [34]. Toft et al. used predicted depth to
identify planar regions for perspective rectification [35], while
Huang et al. introduced depth-aware homography estimation
to handle non-planar scenes [36]. Other methods incorporate
depth-derived curvature, 3D coordinates, or surface normals
into feature representation and matching [37], [38], [39]. More
recently, DECO exploits monocular depth to infer local surface
geometry and co-visibility between UAV images and ortho-
graphic maps, suppressing geometrically invalid keypoints
before feature matching [40]. While these studies demon-
strate the value of monocular depth for understanding scene
structure, existing approaches mainly exploit depth cues for
feature matching, geometric filtering, or scene representation.
Using relative scene structure to guide pose solver selection
without treating predicted depth as metric elevation remains
less explored in DSM-free UAV localization.
III. METHODOLOGY
This section presents the proposed DSM-free UAV localiza-
tion framework for recovering the metric horizontal camera
position from a perspective UAV image and a geo-tagged
orthographic reference map. We first formulate Latent-Depth
Variable Projection (LDVP), which treats the unknown scale
along each perspective viewing ray as a latent depth and
eliminates it analytically for direct metric pose estimation.
LDVP provides a geometric solution for scenes with suffi-
cient relief where planar assumptions become inaccurate. We
then analyze the local observability of this formulation and
show how scene relief affects the underlying orthographic–
perspective geometry. Based on this analysis, we introduce
Structure-Adaptive Coupling (SAC), which uses relative scene
structure inferred from monocular depth to adaptively select
between LDVP and Planar-PnP. An overview of the complete
framework is shown in Fig. 1.

IV. EXPERIMENTS
A. Experimental Setup
Evaluation protocol. We evaluate the proposed method
through synthetic and real-world experiments. The synthetic
experiments isolate the effect of scene relief to examine
how elevation variation affects LDVP and the complementary
behavior of LDVP and Planar-PnP. The real-world experiments
evaluate localization accuracy and robustness across diverse
scene structures and viewing conditions on AnyVisLoc [5] and
OrthoLoC [4].
For all methods, localization accuracy is evaluated using the
horizontal camera-position error
epos = ∥bc −cgt∥2 ,
(36)
where bc and cgt denote the estimated and ground-truth hor-
izontal camera centers, respectively. We report the median
position error and the localization accuracy A@τ, defined as
the percentage of UAV images whose horizontal position error
does not exceed τ meters (τ ∈{1, 3, 5}).

Compared methods. We compare the proposed LDVP and
LDVP+SAC with four representative localization methods.
DSM-PnP uses reference-map elevations to construct metric
2D–3D correspondences and estimates the camera pose using
PnP-RANSAC [5]. With meter- to decimeter-level DSM spa-
tial resolution, it serves as a reliable reference for evaluating
the impact of unavailable elevation information. Planar-PnP
assigns a common zero elevation to the matched map points
and provides a direct DSM-free solution under the planar
assumption. OPE [14] and AH [13] are representative OPG-
based DSM-free methods. We use their respective robust
estimation procedures in all experiments. All methods are
evaluated using the same UAV-image–reference-map corre-
spondences within each test instance. DSM elevations are
provided only to DSM-PnP; the remaining methods operate
without DSM information.
Implementation details. The experiments are conducted on
Ubuntu 22.04 using Python 3.10 and PyTorch, with an
NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090 GPU and an Intel Xeon Plat-
inum 8370C CPU. Unless otherwise specified, UAV-image–
reference-map correspondences are obtained using RoMa
[42], a strong dense matching method that provides reliable
correspondences under large viewpoint changes. Monocular
depth maps used by SAC are predicted using MoGe2-Aerial
[15], which is specifically adapted to aerial imagery and is
therefore well suited for inferring the relative scene structure
used by SAC. DSM-PnP uses P3P-based RANSAC, while
Planar-PnP uses IPPE-based RANSAC, both implemented
with OpenCV. For LDVP, five-correspondence hypotheses are
evaluated within MSAC using a 5 m point-to-line residual
threshold. The initial rotation search uses angular steps of 30◦,
45◦, and 45◦for tilt, azimuth, and roll, respectively, followed
by local rotation refinement. For SAC, the dominant plane is
estimated using three-point RANSAC with 2000 iterations and
a dimensionless inlier threshold of τπ = 0.003. The planar-
support threshold is set to τS = 0.5 in the main experiments.

C. Real-World Experiments
1) Datasets: AnyVisLoc [5] comprises 20,077 UAV im-
ages collected across 24 diverse urban and natural environ-
ments. The dataset covers flight altitudes from 6 to 500 m and
camera down-tilt angles from 5◦to 90◦, providing both oblique
and near-nadir observations under varied scene structures. It
provides georeferenced aerial and satellite reference maps. We
use the aerial reference maps, whose corresponding DSMs
have spatial resolutions ranging from 0.15 to 1.98 m, and
follow the reference-map rotation and cropping protocol of
[40] to construct the UAV-image–reference-map pairs.
OrthoLoC [4] contains 16,427 UAV samples from 51 loca-
tions, including 10,923 same-domain and 5,504 cross-domain
samples. Its flight altitudes range from 23 to 201 m, and most
observations have down-tilt angles between 60◦and 90◦. The
dataset provides georeferenced orthophotos and corresponding
DSMs with spatial resolutions ranging from 0.04 to 0.73 m.
The same-domain reference maps are generated from aerial
photogrammetry, whereas the cross-domain setting introduces
reference data from different sources and acquisition con-
ditions. Following [40], we use all provided samples and
restrict the yaw difference between UAV image and reference
map to 45◦. These two datasets provide complementary scene
structures, viewing conditions, and reference-map settings for
real-world evaluation.
2) Comparison with Existing Methods: Table I reports the
real-world localization results using RoMa correspondences.
DSM-PnP provides a DSM-based reference and is therefore
excluded when ranking the DSM-free methods. Among the
OPG-based DSM-free methods, LDVP consistently outper-
forms AH and OPE on both datasets. The improvement is
particularly clear at A@1, supporting the effectiveness of the
proposed direct metric pose formulation. The proposed SAC
further improves LDVP on both datasets, with particularly
clear gains at A@3 and A@5. On AnyVisLoc, SAC improves
A@3 by 6.10 percentage points. On OrthoLoC, SAC also
improves A@3 and A@5, although A@1 decreases slightly.
This indicates that the Planar-PnP branch mainly helps reduce
larger localization errors for near-planar queries, while its
benefit can be less evident for queries that LDVP already
localizes accurately.

as metric 3D input for pose estimation.
V. CONCLUSION
This work presented a DSM-free UAV localization frame-
work for direct metric pose estimation without requiring
external elevation information. We proposed LDVP to di-
rectly recover the metric horizontal camera position under
OPG by analytically eliminating latent ray depths. Our local
observability analysis revealed how scene elevation variation
affects the pose constraints of OPG, motivating SAC to
adaptively select between LDVP and Planar-PnP using relative
scene structure inferred from monocular depth estimation.
Experiments demonstrated that LDVP outperforms existing
OPG-based DSM-free methods, while SAC further improves
robustness across diverse scenes and substantially narrows the
gap to DSM-based localization.
The demonstrated accuracy of geometric pose estimation
without DSM information, together with the ability of monoc-
ular depth to capture scene structure, suggests the potential for
end-to-end DSM-free localization. Future work could integrate
scene-structure understanding and pose estimation into a uni-
fied learned framework, with the geometric insights developed
here providing a basis for its design.

APPENDIX C
FIVE-CORRESPONDENCE HYPOTHESIS SIZE
Before eliminating the latent scales, N correspondences
provide 2N scalar equations in N + 5 unknowns: N scales
{λi}N
i=1, three rotation parameters, and the two-dimensional
horizontal center. The generic degree-of-freedom count there-
fore gives the necessary condition
2N ≥N + 5,
(62)
or equivalently N ≥5. After variable projection, the same
count is seen directly: each correspondence contributes one
scalar incidence constraint ei(R, c) = 0 on the five pose
degrees of freedom.
Accordingly, LDVP uses five correspondences to generate
each pose hypothesis. For a sampled set, rotation candidates
are evaluated by the structured search described in the main
paper, while the horizontal center and per-correspondence
scales are eliminated analytically. Each resulting hypothesis
is scored over all correspondences by MSAC. Robust center
profiling and continuous rotation refinement are then applied
only in the final refit on the selected consensus. The counting
argument motivates the five-correspondence hypothesis size; it
does not guarantee a unique or valid solution for every five-
correspondence configuration.
