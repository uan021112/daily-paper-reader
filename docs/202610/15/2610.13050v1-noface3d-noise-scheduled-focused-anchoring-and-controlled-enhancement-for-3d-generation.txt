ABSTRACT
Recent image-to-3D models generate complete, high-quality assets from a sin-
gle image, with global shape and appearance that are already convincing; what
remains is the fine scale, where small, thin, elongated, or detail-rich regions are
reconstructed softly and under-resolved. This gap can arise in generators with
fixed-resolution spatial grids: the same spatial sampling density is used for a large,
smooth, low-curvature surface and for a thin, high-curvature structure that carries
most of the perceived detail. To this end, we introduce NOFACE3D, a training-
free framework that refines these detail-critical regions while preserving the rest
of the asset. Given a generated mesh, its input image, and a refinement box, NO-
FACE3D extracts the selected submesh, scales it up to fill the generator’s input
volume, and re-encodes it into the full-resolution latent grid. Conditioned on the
image crop, it refines the region through flow-based sampling with a spatially
varying noise schedule that anchors boundary latents. The refined region is then
integrated back into the original asset, preserving the surrounding geometry and
appearance. We implement NOFACE3D on TRELLIS.2 and its sparse voxel rep-
resentation; all of it operates purely at inference time on the frozen pretrained
generator. We evaluate NOFACE3D on PAniC-3D, TexVerse and BIGDETAIL, a
new benchmark of 339 assets with annotated detail-critical regions. On all three,
it generates sharper geometry and finer texture detail than the baselines. Project

INTRODUCTION
Single-image 3D generation now produces complete, textured assets in seconds. Feed-forward re-
construction models predict 3D representations directly from image features (Hong et al., 2024;
Tang et al., 2024; Xu et al., 2024), while native 3D latent generative models trained on large asset
collections (Deitke et al., 2023) synthesize objects with convincing proportions, silhouettes, and ap-
pearance (Zhang et al., 2024; Li et al., 2025b; Zhao et al., 2025). Structured-latent generators such
as TRELLIS and TRELLIS.2 (Xiang et al., 2025b;a) attach a local code to each occupied voxel of a
sparse grid. Their output is already convincing on large, low-curvature surfaces.
However, fine details such as fingers, springs, and engravings often appear soft or blurred. This gap
can arise from how spatial capacity is allocated: a fixed-resolution grid provides the same sampling
density to large, smooth surfaces and small, high-curvature structures. For example, one full-body
character generated by TRELLIS.2 (Xiang et al., 2025a) occupies approximately 4,000 voxels in the
643 shape-latent grid (1.5% of its cells), with only about 1,400 voxels (0.5%) representing the head
crop. The challenge is therefore to give detail-critical regions more spatial capacity while preserving
the parts that are already well reconstructed.
Increasing the global grid resolution offers finer spatial sampling, but also increases computational
cost across the entire asset, even when only a small region needs refinement (Wu et al., 2025).
Refiners trained on synthetic mesh degradation may not fully address detail lost through limited
spatial sampling during generation (Deng et al., 2024). Render-space enhancement can improve
appearance, but does not directly increase the latent capacity allocated to a selected 3D region (Ryu
et al., 2025).
To address this challenge, we propose NOFACE3D, a training-free framework built on a frozen
image-to-3D generator. We extract the target submesh, rescale it to the generator’s input cube, and
re-encode it with the pretrained encoders. The generator’s full-resolution latent grid now represents
this region alone, increasing its effective resolution in the original asset’s coordinates. The input
image crop guides regeneration.
A spatially varying noise schedule anchors latents near attachment boundaries, with stronger regen-
eration farther away. Finally, we map the refined region back to the original coordinate frame and
combine it with the surrounding mesh.
Our evaluation focuses on whether refinement produces sharper geometry and finer texture detail
while preserving overall asset quality. We evaluate NOFACE3D on PAniC-3D and TexVerse, and
introduce BIGDETAIL to assess localized refinement on diverse objects with detail-critical regions
annotated in 2D and 3D. Where artist ground truth is available, we report appearance metrics and
pointwise geometry measures. We interpret Chamfer distance and F-score as geometry checks,
since they are dominated by large, smooth surfaces (Wang et al., 2018; Chen et al., 2025) and
may not reflect fine detail that artist references carry in textures rather than geometry. To assess
visible improvements in geometry and texture, we use qualitative comparisons and GPTEval3D
evaluations across all three datasets, alongside a human preference study on PAniC-3D. Across
these evaluations, NOFACE3D outperforms the original TRELLIS.2 results and the refinement and
editing baselines.
Our contributions are as follows.
• NOFACE3D, a training-free framework for localized refinement that scales up a selected
region to fill the generator’s input volume and re-encodes it into the full-resolution latent
grid.
• A spatially varying noise schedule for flow-based sampling that anchors boundary latents
and increases refinement strength with distance from attachment boundaries.
• BIGDETAIL, a benchmark of 339 assets reconstructed from generated images, with detail-
critical regions annotated in both 2D and 3D. Qualitative comparisons and GPTEval3D
evaluations on PAniC-3D, TexVerse, and BIGDETAIL demonstrate sharper geometry and
finer texture detail.

RELATED WORK
2.1
TEXT- AND IMAGE-TO-3D GENERATION
Early text-to-3D methods lift 2D diffusion priors into 3D by score distillation, optimizing a per-scene
representation so its renderings match a pretrained image model (Poole et al., 2023; Lin et al., 2023;
Wang et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2023a); they are flexible but slow, prone to view inconsistency, and
tend toward oversmoothed geometry, which multi-view diffusion mitigates by generating several
consistent views before lifting (Liu et al., 2023a; Shi et al., 2024; Long et al., 2024). Feed-forward
large reconstruction models instead predict a 3D representation in a single pass, trading per-scene
optimization for speed (Hong et al., 2024; Li et al., 2024; Tang et al., 2024; Xu et al., 2024). The
now dominant family trains native 3D latent generators on large asset collections, using compact
vector-set latents (Zhang et al., 2023; Zhao et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2024; Li et al., 2025b; Zhao
et al., 2025) or structured sparse-voxel latents (Xiang et al., 2025b;a). Because dense volumetric ca-
pacity scales cubically, recent work attacks this cost with sparse-volume attention (Wu et al., 2025)
and non-uniform representations that place resolution where occupancy is (Xiong et al., 2025; Ren
et al., 2024), while part-based generators disperse capacity across semantic parts so each is generated
at higher effective resolution (Lin et al., 2025; Yang et al., 2025a;b; Sun et al., 2026). A parallel au-
toregressive line generates meshes as triangle token sequences, targeting artist-like topology directly
(Siddiqui et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2024; Tang et al., 2025). NOFACE3D shares the motivation of
the capacity-aware methods, but instead of redesigning the representation or retraining, it reallocates
the existing budget at inference time to a single semantic region.
2.2
MESH AND GEOMETRY REFINEMENT
A dedicated line enhances the geometry of an existing coarse asset. DetailGen3D models coarse-
to-fine enhancement as a data-dependent latent flow with token matching, trained on synthetically
degraded pairs (Deng et al., 2024). Elevate3D refines view by view, alternating a high-frequency-
preserving SDEdit (Meng et al., 2022) variant for texture with monocular-geometry updates for
shape, explicitly targeting the trade-off a single global noise level imposes (Ryu et al., 2025).
MeshReGen unifies enhancement, reconstruction, and editing as one regeneration problem and re-
ports that re-encoding degraded geometry into a structured latent and resampling does not recover
fine detail (Park et al., 2026). A related line bridges through predicted normals to raise detail fidelity
before geometry synthesis (Ye et al., 2025a), while no-ground-truth surface metrics (Lee et al., 2026)
and region-annotated character benchmarks (Chen et al., 2023b) support scoring such refinements.
NOFACE3D shares this goal but is training-free and localized: it learns no degradation-specific
mapping, operates on a frozen generator, and refines one region at increased effective resolution
while leaving the geometry outside it essentially unchanged.
3
METHOD
NOFACE3D takes a generated asset, its conditioning image, and a bounding box marking a detail-
critical region, and regenerates only that region with the frozen generator. The region is scaled up
to fill the generator’s input volume and re-encoded into its full-resolution latent grid (Section 3.2);
a boundary-distance field controls the noise strength and release time of each latent token (Sec-
tion 3.3); geometry and texture are resampled under that field (Sections 3.4, 3.5); and the result is
cut back into the asset in mesh space (Section 3.6). Implementation details and hyperparameters for
every step are in Appendix A.

3.2
REGION SELECTION AND LOCAL RE-ENCODING
The refinement region is specified by a 3D bounding box B, with a corresponding 2D bounding box
Ωin the conditioning image. The 3D box can be supplied directly, as in BIGDETAIL, or derived
from the image region. We extract the submesh selected by B without splitting triangles, then center
it and scale it uniformly to fit the generator’s input cube, preserving its proportions.
Only this rescaled submesh is converted to the generator’s 10243 sparse voxel representation and re-
encoded with the pretrained encoders to obtain the base latents for each stage. Rescaling increases
the region’s effective spatial resolution across all latent stages and the decoded voxel representation.
Regeneration is conditioned on the crop of the input image within Ω, passed through the generator’s
standard preprocessing.
3.3
BOUNDARY-DISTANCE RELEASE FIELD
Our refinement requires at least one attachment boundary: a crop-box face crossed by the base
mesh where the selected region connects to the surrounding asset. We anchor nearby latents to the
base asset and allow stronger regeneration farther away. Each latent token receives a release level
τ ∈[0, τmax]. The same τ sets its initial noising time t = τ and determines when it stops following
the noised base latent and follows the learned flow instead (Section 3.4). Larger τ means stronger
initial noising and earlier release. Tokens with τ = 0 remain anchored and are restored to their
base values at the end; tokens with τ = 1 start from pure noise. The per-stage maximum τmax ≤1
controls refinement strength.

box extent in that direction, and take the smallest value d(u). We set τ = 0 within an anchored band
of width g, then increase it smoothly over a transition band of width r:

3.5
TEXTURE REFINEMENT
Texture is refined after geometry, on the refined shape coordinates; the decoded refined mesh is never
re-encoded. The base texture SLat is encoded in the same local frame from the crop seed and the
base asset’s PBR attributes, and the texture model uses the same boundary-distance field, evaluated
with the texture-stage g and r, and the same re-anchored sampling of Eq. 4. By Eq. 3, the texture
anchored and transition bands lie inside the shape SLat’s anchored band, where base texture values
are available at matching coordinates. The result decodes to PBR channels on the refined 10243
voxels.
3.6
REINTEGRATION IN MESH SPACE
The refined region is decoded to a mesh with per-vertex PBR values and mapped back to the original
coordinate frame using the inverse transforms. This restores the region’s position, orientation and
scale without an additional alignment fit. The cut is made in mesh space with the same crop box B
used for selection: base faces whose centroid cf lies inside B are removed, and the refined mesh
takes their place,
M = {f ∈Mbase : cf /∈B} ∪Mref.
(5)
The two parts are concatenated, with no mesh boolean, vertex welding or stitching. The cut is binary
although τ is smooth: the transition band controls how strongly latents are released, while anchoring
encourages compatible geometry and a smooth transition in color at attachment boundaries. The
joined mesh is then re-voxelized at 1024³ and remeshed for export.

4
EXPERIMENTS
We compare NOFACE3D with refinement and editing methods on PAniC-3D and TexVerse, and
with TRELLIS.2 on BIGDETAIL, and in a human study (Section 4.2). We then ablate the noise
schedule (Section 4.3) and show that the method also supports localized 3D editing (Section 4.4).
4.1
SETUP
Datasets and baselines. PAniC-3D (Chen et al., 2023b) pairs single images of full-body characters
with artist meshes. Their head and upper body carry much of the perceived identity but occupy
only a small fraction of the latent grid, so the marked region is a semantic crop of that part. We
compare with baselines on 329 of the 4,892 assets, because Elevate3D takes about 25 minutes per
asset on an NVIDIA RTX A6000 (Ryu et al., 2025). NOFACE3D runs the TRELLIS.2 cascade
once per region, close to the cost of one TRELLIS.2 generation, which takes about 17 s at 10243
on an NVIDIA H100 (Xiang et al., 2025a). TexVerse (Zhang et al., 2025) adds 151 general objects
with authored PBR materials, none from Objaverse. We also propose BIGDETAIL, a benchmark of
objects whose detail sits in a small region that a uniform latent grid under-resolves. Each subject
pairs a large, smooth body with a compact cluster of named detail parts (e.g. a steel tank with its
valve and trigger assembly), spanning everyday and industrial objects. We generate one image per
subject with Nano Banana 2, reconstruct it with the original TRELLIS.2 model, and annotators
mark each detail-critical region as an image rectangle and an oriented 3D box. The resulting 339
assets have no artist mesh, so they are judged pairwise against the original TRELLIS.2 result. The
baselines, DetailGen3D (Deng et al., 2024), Elevate3D (Ryu et al., 2025) and Easy3E (Hu et al.,
2026), are each applied to the original TRELLIS.2 result. We do not compare with VoxHammer (Li
et al., 2025a), a training-free 3D editing method, because it edits at the generator’s native resolution
and does not increase it.
Metrics. Against the registered GT mesh, we report Chamfer distance, F-score, surface IoU at 1283,
and Sharp Normal Error (SNE) (Chen et al., 2025), following prior refinement and editing work
(Deng et al., 2024; Li et al., 2025a; Ye et al., 2025b). On matched RGB views we report PSNR,
SSIM(Wang et al., 2004) and LPIPS (Zhang et al., 2018), as in 3D editing benchmarks (Li et al.,
2025a; Ye et al., 2025b), plus DINOv2-large (Oquab et al., 2024) cosine similarity (DINO) and FID
(Heusel et al., 2017), following Xiang et al. (2025b) and Li et al. (2025a). We also use GPTEval3D
(Wu et al., 2024): a vision-language judge (GPT-4o (OpenAI, 2024) or GPT-5.6) compares pairs of
unlabeled assets on six criteria, and we report its 1–10 win share, for which chance is 5.0. Data splits,
registration, and the metric, judging and user-study protocols are in Appendix B; the construction of
BIGDETAIL is in Appendix C.

Refinement and editing methods. NOFACE3D is best on nearly every metric on both datasets
(Table 1); TRELLIS.2 leads Chamfer on PAniC-3D by 0.001. DetailGen3D is best on SNE because
it sharpens the whole surface, but this also warps the shape (TexVerse Chamfer 35.4 vs. 7.22).
Easy3E and Elevate3D fall below TRELLIS.2 on most metrics.

4.3
NOISE SCHEDULING ABLATION
Figure 5 compares refinement with and without noise scheduling. In the ablated variant, we re-
generate the selected region without boundary anchoring or the spatially varying noise schedule.
Regeneration alone does not reliably produce a smooth transition to the retained asset, leaving vis-
ible discontinuities in geometry and appearance at attachment boundaries. With noise scheduling,
the regenerated region connects more coherently to its surroundings.

CONCLUSION
We presented NOFACE3D, a training-free framework that refines the detail-critical regions of an
asset produced by a pretrained image-to-3D generator. It reallocates the latent budget rather than
enlarging it: the detailed region’s 3D content is rescaled to fill the generator’s latent grid, re-encoded
there, and regenerated under a boundary-distance field that pins the interface latents and restores
them exactly, after which the refined region is combined with the surrounding mesh. The method
needs only the frozen generator, the input image and a box around the target region, so it is not
tied to a category or body part, and conditioning the same process on an edited crop turns it into a
localized 3D editor.
Limitations. Because NOFACE3D is training-free and runs on the frozen TRELLIS.2 generator,
it inherits that generator’s prior: re-encoding adds latent capacity but does not change what the
generator can produce, so text such as printed labels and lettering, which TRELLIS.2 struggles
to render legibly, often remains distorted after refinement. Post-training the generator on text-rich
samples may alleviate this.

IMPLEMENTATION DETAILS
Generator. We use the released 4B TRELLIS.2 model in its default 1024 cascade configuration.
DINOv3 features are extracted at 512 pixels for the first two stages and at 1024 pixels for the last two.
The SS latent decodes to 643 occupancy, max-pooled to the sparse 323 support of the low-resolution
shape SLat; the shape decoder upsamples that SLat, and its predicted occupancy is quantized to
the sparse 643 grid of the high-resolution shape SLat. The texture SLat is sampled on the same
643 coordinates with the shape SLat concatenated to its input, and the texture decoder, guided by
the shape decoder’s intermediate subdivisions, lifts it to six PBR channels (base color, metallic,
roughness, alpha). We use Euler sampling with time rescaling and classifier-free guidance; the time
ladders are specified below.
Cost. With τmax = 1, NOFACE3D samples each stage of the 10243 cascade once per region
with the released 12-step ladder, so its shape stages cost the same as in one TRELLIS.2 generation.
Xiang et al. (2025a) report about 17 s for a 10243 asset on an NVIDIA H100, 10 s for shape and
7 s for material. The only additional cost is voxelizing and encoding the crop seed, which uses
the generator’s own conversion routines and encoders. NOFACE3D loads the same models as
TRELLIS.2 and runs on the same hardware, which Xiang et al. (2025a) specify as a GPU with at
least 24 GB of memory.
Crop box and condition. For PAniC-3D and TexVerse, we use square image bounding boxes and
align the base asset to the image by its frontal silhouette. Since a single image cannot constrain
depth, the crop box B starts at the front-most surface under Ωand its depth equals the image box’s
side length, retaining the front-facing portion when the object extends farther back. This is an
experimental setting, not a requirement on the shape of the refinement box. The condition crop
passes through the generator’s standard preprocessing (background removal and recentering). In
our PAniC-3D and TexVerse experiments it is a crop of the input image and is not upscaled, so no
detail enters through an image model rather than through NOFACE3D. The crop of a user image
can be low-resolution; it can then be upsampled by image interpolation or by a vision-language
image model such as Gemini Flash Image, which touches only the 2D condition. NOFACE3D also
runs on generated inputs: the BIGDETAIL images are generated with Gemini, and the editing crops

B
EVALUATION DETAILS
Data. We use the Genshin (PMX) and VRoid (VRM) subsets of PAniC-3D. On these characters the
head and upper body carry much of the perceived identity while occupying little of the latent grid,
which is why the marked region is a crop of that part. Of the 5,631 PAniC-3D assets with completed
original TRELLIS.2 results and refinement (132 Genshin, 5,499 VRoid), the 4,892 that complete
every evaluator form the large-scale set, and the 329 on which every baseline also completed (120
Genshin, 209 VRoid) form the comparison set. The original TRELLIS.2 results are generated once
and cached, so they differ from ours only by the refinement. GT, original and refined meshes are
registered and rendered with a shared camera and normalization.
Baselines. On PAniC-3D and TexVerse every method sees the same single image. DetailGen3D
outputs untextured meshes, so its appearance metrics are not reported. Easy3E receives the image
crop as its target view and the crop box as its 3D mask.
Metrics. Chamfer distance and F-score use 250,000 points per mesh. SNE uses 22 views on a
Fibonacci sphere at 5122, and the appearance metrics use four 10242 views at azimuths 30◦, 120◦,
210◦, 300◦and pitch 30◦. DINO and FID measure semantic and distributional similarity rather
than pixel-wise agreement. FID is computed over the set of rendered views and has no per-asset
deviation.
Judged evaluation. Following GPTEval3D (Wu et al., 2024), the judge is GPT-4o or GPT-5.6,
which we use as a scalable proxy for human preference; the user study of Section 4.2 agrees with
it. The six criteria are fidelity and completeness, 3D plausibility, geometry–texture alignment, tex-
ture detail, geometry detail, and overall quality; the judge is not told which asset is refined. The
15


Preprint
geometry–texture alignment criterion asks whether the geometric detail is consistent with the image
and texture, that is, how realistic the geometry looks, not whether it matches GT. The judge sees four
RGB views and four normal maps of each asset at azimuths 0◦, 90◦, 180◦, 270◦. In the pairwise
protocol it picks the better of two assets or a tie; in the monadic protocol it rates each asset from 1
to 10 alone, and pairwise outcomes are derived by comparing the independent ratings. Both report
the win share 10 (wins + 0.5 ties)/games, for which chance is 5.0. Side-by-side judging is position-
biased: in a pilot, GPT-4o chose the left panel 68.5% of the time. The comparison with baselines
therefore counts both orders of every pair, and the large-scale tournament (1,000 assets, 8,000 judge
calls) keeps only decisions on which both orders agree. The labeled trial shows the artist mesh as a
named reference and asks which of the two assets matches it better; as in the tournament, only assets
on which both orders agree are counted.
Human study. The study in Section 4.2 pairs TRELLIS.2 and NOFACE3D on 200 PAniC-3D
assets from the Genshin and VRoid subsets. Each trial shows rotating textured and normal-map
views of both assets and the conditioning image of the full object and of the marked region; the
options are unlabeled, with left/right assignment and orientation randomized. Reviewers rate 1–9
(left to right preference) or answer neither when the base generation is too poor to judge. Ratings
are mapped so that 9 favours NOFACE3D, and the signed score is the mapped rating minus 5.
Sixteen reviewers gave 1,188 responses (7–181 judged ratings each, median 35); no demographic
information was collected.

C
BIGDETAIL CONSTRUCTION
TRELLIS.2 already reconstructs Objaverse-style assets well, such assets may overlap its training
data, and few of them concentrate their detail in a small volume that a uniform latent grid under-
resolves. Every BIGDETAIL subject is designed around that case. The images are generated, so
no artist mesh exists and none of the reference-based metrics can be computed; BIGDETAIL is
evaluated with pairwise GPTEval3D on the whole asset, the original TRELLIS.2 result against the
refined asset for the same input.
Subjects and images. Each subject is specified by an object, its smooth mass (e.g. “red cylin-
drical steel tank”), and a compact detail cluster with named parts (e.g. “valve and trigger as-
sembly: squeeze lever, carry handle, pressure gauge, safety pin, tamper seal”).
Subjects span
everyday and industrial objects: stationery and office equipment, hand and power tools, kitchen
and household appliances, furniture and lighting, musical instruments, sports gear, cameras and
laboratory instruments, vehicles, boats and aircraft, railway, firefighting, utility and space hard-
ware, and buildings. For each subject we generate one 4096×4096 image with Nano Banana 2
(gemini-3.1-flash-image) and reconstruct it with the original TRELLIS.2 model.
Region labels. Annotators mark each detail-critical region as a pair of boxes, a rectangle on the
image and an oriented 3D box on the mesh, with no assumed camera; an asset may carry several
regions, or be marked as needing no refinement and excluded. The evaluation set is the 339 refined
assets.
Refinement. We use the annotated bounding boxes directly: the 3D box specifies the selection and
its orientation, whole triangles whose centroids fall inside it are kept, and the attachment boundaries
G are the box faces crossed by the base mesh, as in Eq. 6. The per-stage band widths are those of
Appendix A, with independently configurable release maxima. The condition is the exact image
crop, resized with Lanczos interpolation and padded to a white 10242 square before the generator’s
background removal, without generative image sharpening. Each labeled region is refined in its own
run.

LARGE-SCALE COMPARISON WITH TRELLIS.2
Table 4 compares NOFACE3D with the original TRELLIS.2 results on all 4,892 PAniC-3D assets.
Every appearance metric improves, most clearly FID (13.97 →7.43) and DINO (0.812 →0.830),
the fine F-score and surface IoU rise (F1 at 0.25% from 0.141 to 0.144, IoU from 0.373 to 0.381),
and pointwise geometry otherwise changes little: Chamfer differs by 0.33%, F1 at 1% by less than
0.1% and SNE by 0.2%. This is consistent with the rest of the asset being preserved. The refinement
shows in appearance against GT and in judged and human preference.
Under the monadic protocol, both judges prefer NOFACE3D to TRELLIS.2 on all six criteria, with
overall quality 4.81 vs. 4.22 on GPT-4o and 5.02 vs. 3.53 on GPT-5.6 (Table 5); the margins are
moderate because most of each asset is the preserved remainder. A pairwise tournament on a 1,000-
asset subset, keeping only decisions on which both orders agree, gives the same ordering. Finally, a
labeled trial shows the artist mesh as a named reference and asks which of the two matches it better:
of the 871 assets on which both orders agree, the judge chose NOFACE3D on 681 and TRELLIS.2
on 188, 78.4% of the 869 decided assets, and judged the remaining 2 a tie.
