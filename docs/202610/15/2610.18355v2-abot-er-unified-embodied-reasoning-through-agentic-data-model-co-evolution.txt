## AMAP CV Lab Alibaba Group ABot-ER: Unified Embodied Reasoning through Agentic Data–Model Co-Evolution 

## Abstract 

We introduce ABot Embodied Reasoning (ABot-ER) , a unified embodied reasoning foundation model that breaks the tension between breadth and depth in embodied reasoning. Embodied intelligence demands the joint mastery of perception, planning, and execution, yet reconciling generality with specialization across this spectrum remains an open challenge. Within a single unified model, ABot-ER commands specialist-level depth across all three competencies, spanning spatial, scene, and temporal reasoning in perception; long-horizon action organization in planning; and point grounding and affordance in execution. The Agentic Data-Model Self-Evolution Engine 

continuously adapts data strategies to the model’s evolving capability state. The Task-Conditioned Adaptive Training Strategy uses task tokens to activate lightweight low-rank residuals over a shared backbone, separating updates from heterogeneous output formats while retaining shared embodied knowledge. Across a systematic evaluation on twelve benchmarks covering three core competencies and five capability dimensions, ABot-ER achieves state-of-the-art overall performance across perception, planning, and execution within a unified parametric framework, demonstrating that breadth and depth can coexist in unified embodied reasoning.

Embodied intelligence studies agents that act on instructions in the physical world. An instruction such as 

rinse off a ladle and move it to the table requires more than recognizing objects: determining which objects and scene states are relevant, what sequence of actions is needed, and where each action should be directed. These interdependent judgements span perception, planning, and execution, and together constitute embodied reasoning . Execution here means identifying grounded interaction targets for a downstream action model rather than generating motion commands. Moving beyond a fixed task requires coordinating these judgements as tasks, scenes, and embodiments change, which makes embodied reasoning central to general-purpose behaviour in the physical world. Embodied reasoning (ER) models serve as a reasoning interface between multimodal observations and downstream control. Recent embodied foundation models reflect a broader shift from isolated perception or control tasks toward reasoning across multiple stages of physical interaction [3, 12, 18, 21, 44]. An ER model infers spatial relations, scene states, and temporal changes, translates high-level goals into ordered action plans, and identifies where interaction should occur [1, 16, 39, 61, 69]. Its outputs are task-relevant assessments, action plans, and interaction targets that downstream systems use to guide and parameterize execution. However, existing ER models still exhibit uneven capabilities across perception, planning, and execution. Unifying these competencies raises two related challenges, one about data and one about optimization: which types of supervision should be prioritized at each training stage, and how heterogeneous task outputs can be learned jointly through shared parameters while limiting interference. The first, which we term Data-Model Drift , arises because capabilities develop at different rates: the relative utility of each type of training data can change over the course of training, so a fixed mixture may become less well aligned with the model’s evolving learning needs [59]. This mismatch can arise even when the underlying corpus remains unchanged. The second, which we term Modality Entanglement , arises when heterogeneous outputs, namely semantic descriptions, structured action sequences, and spatial coordinates, impose competing demands on shared parameters, so an update that benefits one output type may degrade another [67]. Section 4 fixes the boundary these outputs are separated along. ABot-ER addresses data adaptation and optimization interference with an Agentic Data-Model Self-Evolution Engine and a Task-Conditioned Adaptive Training Strategy , respectively. The engine pursues recursive self-improvement (RSI) at the level of training data, and turns corpus construction into a closed loop driven by evaluation feedback, with the aim of improving performance across capability dimensions: the current model’s errors are diagnosed and attributed to specific capability gaps, targeted supervision is constructed to address them and audited for relevance and quality, the training-data mixture across capability dimensions is adjusted, the model is trained on the revised corpus, and the resulting evaluation opens the next round. What recurses is the corpus, not the learning algorithm or the architecture, and what enters each round is decided by deterministic gates rather than by the model itself. The loop is governed by continuous screening of each corpus revision for overlap with evaluation data, under quality-control rules calibrated through expert review in the initial rounds; subsequent rounds proceed automatically under those rules. The training strategy uses a task token to activate lightweight low-rank residuals selected by the task type of each training record. All tasks share a backbone for cross-task representation learning, while the residuals provide dedicated capacity for type-specific adaptation, reducing interference between heterogeneous outputs. We systematically evaluate ABot-ER on twelve benchmarks covering three core competencies and five capability dimensions. Within a unified parametric framework, it achieves state-of-the-art overall performance , with the highest aggregate score in each of perception, planning, and execution, while remaining competitive on individual benchmarks, demonstrating that breadth and depth can coexist in unified embodied reasoning. The main contributions of this report are as follows: • We design the Agentic Data-Model Self-Evolution Engine , which realizes RSI over the training corpus by coupling evaluation-driven capability diagnosis with targeted data revision and iterative training. By adapting supervision and training-data mixtures to the model’s evolving capability needs, the engine mitigates Data-Model Drift . The recursion is bounded rather than open-ended, and Section 6 reports 3where it converges. • We propose the Task-Conditioned Adaptive Training Strategy , which uses task tokens to activate dedicated lightweight low-rank residuals for semantic and action outputs while retaining a shared backbone for cross-task learning. This provides output-specific adaptation capacity to mitigate the optimization interference associated with Modality Entanglement .• We systematically evaluate ABot-ER on twelve benchmarks spanning three core competencies and five capability dimensions. Within a unified parametric framework, it achieves state-of-the-art overall performance , with the highest aggregate score in each of perception, planning, and execution. • We demonstrate the reusability of the engine-produced corpus : from the same ancestor checkpoint, a single post-training run on this corpus matches the six-round iterative model in mean performance across the twelve benchmarks, with a fifth of the accumulated post-training. The same corpus and post-training recipe are also applied to backbones ranging from 2B to 27B parameters, without rerunning the data-revision loop for each backbone. To support further research, we will open-source our pre-training and post-training datasets , together with a multi-scale ABot-ER model family comprising 2B, 4B, 9B, and 27B models, as well as a 35B-A3B MoE model. The remainder of this report is organized as follows: Section 2 states the role of an embodied reasoning model in a robot system and the decisions ABot-ER is designed to make; Section 3 reviews related work; Section 4 formalizes the multi-competency unification problem and presents the unified model architecture, with the Task-Conditioned Adaptive Training Strategy in Section 4.3; Section 5 details the Agentic Data-Model Self-Evolution Engine; Section 6 reports experimental results and in-depth analysis; Section 7 concludes and discusses future directions. 

## 2 The Role of an Embodied Reasoning Model 

An embodied reasoning model supplies task-relevant judgements throughout a physical interaction. Given observations and a task instruction, it reasons about spatial relations, scene states, temporal events, action sequences, and interaction targets, which together support both deciding what to do next and assessing what has already happened. Depending on the query, it returns a semantic assessment, an ordered action plan, or grounded image coordinates, and it does not generate motor commands. The agent runtime manages task progression: it decides when to query the model, acts on the returned assessments and targets, and schedules and supervises the proposed plans. Downstream action policies carry out the motion, navigation [19, 26] and manipulation [9, 65] alike. ABot-ER instantiates this role (Figure 2; physical deployment in Section 6.6). This section defines the five capability dimensions the role requires and explains why we serve them from one model. 

2.1 What the Model Answers for the Robot 

We organize these reasoning requirements into five capability dimensions, summarized in Table 1 and formalized in Section 4. The first three, spatial and relational reasoning, scene and state understanding, and temporal and event reasoning, fall under perception. The remaining two address planning and execution-oriented grounding, and we refer to their outputs as action-space outputs, distinguished from semantic outputs by their task function and interface requirements. The five form one set rather than five separate problems, because a single action draws on several of them at once. Placing an object needs free space (spatial), a clear surface (state), and a release point (point grounding); recovering from a failed grasp needs the failure recognized (temporal), the object re-localized (spatial), and the plan revised (long-horizon). The answers must also agree with one another: a release point is only useful if it lies in the region the state assessment just called clear.

At deployment this means one model to serve rather than a specialist per dimension, and the physical system of Section 6.6 runs it on a single NVIDIA RTX 5090D.

3.1 Embodied Vision-Language Foundation Models 

Earlier embodied learning studies often focused on specific task settings, including embodied question answering [13], manipulation [52], and vision-and-language navigation [2]. Their different task interfaces and supervision objectives motivate input-output alignment when constructing a unified model. A recent line of work consolidates multiple embodied capabilities under a single vision-language model. Two useful distinctions are how sensor observations enter the model and whether it directly generates robot-control actions. PaLM-E [15] embeds sensor observations directly into the token space, so that a large language model performs cross-task embodied reasoning over them. RT-2 [6] places robotic action in the same output vocabulary as vision-language pretraining within a single autoregressive framework. EmbodiedGPT [41] uses embodied chain-of-thought for high-level planning and extracts planning-related features to support closed-loop control, keeping plan generation and control in separate stages. LEO [24] integrates 3D vision, language, and action modeling in a unified embodied agent, and the RoboBrain [3] series builds multimodal foundation models covering spatial understanding, temporal reasoning, and task planning through progressive multi-stage training. ABot-ER follows this line of multi-capability modeling, but exposes semantic assessments, action plans, and interaction targets rather than directly generating motor commands. 

3.2 Embodied Perception and Interaction Grounding 

Embodied perception extracts structured information from visual observations in support of physical interaction decisions. Interaction grounding builds on this visual understanding but is treated as execution-oriented reasoning in our capability taxonomy (Section 2.1). In spatial relation reasoning, SpatialVLM [7] endows vision-6language models with quantitative spatial reasoning through large-scale synthetic spatial question answering, and SpatialRGPT [11] combines region-level reference with depth information to improve relative position judgement. On the evaluation side, EmbSpatial-Bench [16] assesses spatial relations from an embodied viewpoint, whereas VSI-Bench [61] assesses spatial understanding from video. In interaction grounding, VRB [4] learns affordance representations from human videos, ManipLLM [28] predicts manipulation-oriented end-effector poses from RGB observations and text prompts, and Where2Place, introduced with RoboPoint [69], evaluates valid placement prediction under spatial constraints. In scene state understanding, OpenEQA [39] is an open-ended embodied question-answering benchmark, and RoboVQA [50] constructs large-scale question answering over robot and human videos, covering planning, success judgement, affordance, past description, and future prediction. In temporal reasoning, Cosmos-Reason1 [44] targets physical common sense and embodied reasoning over video. These studies emphasize different capabilities and use task-specific supervision and output conventions, so integrating them requires explicit alignment of task definitions and interfaces. 

3.3 Task Planning and Feedback-Guided Execution 

Task planning translates high-level goals into action choices and sequences while accounting for feasibility, ordering constraints, and feedback. The paradigm of LLM-as-planner established several routes to this: Say-Can [1] combines semantic knowledge with value-function feasibility for task decomposition; ProgPrompt [53] and Code-as-Policies [31] cast planning as code generation, using program structure for sequencing and control flow; and Inner Monologue [25] introduces environment feedback as an online correction signal, so that plans are revised during execution rather than fixed in advance. At the execution level, benchmarks such as ALFRED [51] and simulation platforms such as Habitat [49] require a model to select actions condi-tioned on successive observations during interaction. These approaches use different perception interfaces, plan representations, and feedback mechanisms, providing complementary foundations for unified embodied modeling. 

3.4 Adaptive Data Construction and Multi-Task Training 

The two methodological components of this report, one concerning which supervision to train on and one concerning how heterogeneous outputs share parameters, each have a line of prior work.  

> Data construction, self-improvement, and mixture weighting.

Self-Instruct [57] generates and filters instruction data with the model itself. Self-Rewarding Language Models [68] use model-generated judgements as the reward signal in iterative training. RoboCat [5] self-improves from self-generated robotic interaction data, so that data and model advance together across generations. DoReMi [59] optimizes data-domain weights with a proxy model and applies the resulting mixture to subsequent training. ABot-ER’s engine differs from these in what drives the revision and in what is revised. The engine diagnoses weaknesses at the capability-dimension level from evaluation evidence and revises both supervision content and cross-dimension mixtures within each round, with capability gains evaluated alongside retention. Whereas DoReMi learns sampling weights over existing data domains, our engine also constructs and revises supervision in response to capability-level diagnosis.  

> Parameter-efficient adaptation and multi-task interference.

Adapter modules [22] add small task-specific components to a pretrained network, while LoRA [23] represents weight updates through low-rank factors. In their standard transfer-learning formulations, the pretrained backbone remains frozen. A separate line addresses multi-task interference through gradient-based optimization: PCGrad [67] projects away conflicting components of task gradients, while CAGrad [32] maximizes the worst-case local improvement across tasks within a neighborhood of the mean task gradient. Our Task-Conditioned Adaptive Training Strategy uses low-rank residual parameterization but jointly trains the shared backbone and residual branches, without explicit gradient-conflict correction. A task token selects the branch according to the sequence’s output regime (semantic or action-space) rather than allocating a separate branch to each benchmark or capability dimension (Section 4). Against this background, ABot-ER studies unified embodied reasoning across perception, planning, and execution-oriented grounding. Its data engine couples capability diagnosis with targeted supervision revision and capability-aware mixture adjustment over multiple training rounds, with gains evaluated alongside 7retention. Its task-conditioned training strategy provides dedicated low-rank residual capacity for semantic and action-space outputs while retaining a shared backbone. These components are studied together as a framework for adaptive data construction and joint training across the five capability dimensions.

4.1 Capability Coverage and Unified Modeling Objective 

We refine the three core capabilities of perception, planning, and execution into five capability dimensions: • Spatial and Relational Reasoning (Perception): Judging direction, distance, and multi-view spatial layouts among objects. • Scene and State Understanding (Perception): Recognizing object attributes, inferring interaction states, and performing open-ended scene question answering. • Temporal and Event Reasoning (Perception): Recognizing actions and events in video, determining temporal order, and detecting state changes. • Long-Horizon Action Organization (Planning): Generating ordered action plans under task preconditions and cross-step state constraints given high-level goals, and selecting subsequent actions from available observations and interaction history. • Point Grounding and Affordance (Execution): Localizing manipulation targets and predicting placement positions under affordance constraints, expressed as image coordinates for downstream action policies. The evaluation benchmarks and metrics corresponding to each capability dimension are listed in Table 7. Although the five capability dimensions differ in their reasoning requirements and output formats, they share core embodied knowledge at the foundational level, including object, spatial, state, and affordance 8representations. We cover all five dimensions within a single unified model and formulate the supported tasks within a shared conditional generation framework. The five dimensions organize capability coverage rather than define separate training losses: we optimize causal cross-entropy over unmasked assistant tokens, normalized within each training sequence, and with a per-device microbatch size of one, gradient accumulation and data-parallel averaging combine these sequence-normalized losses across the effective batch. Inputs, media, and padding carry no supervision, and the assistant tokens of a multi-turn sample are normalized together. Relative exposure across capabilities is controlled by the data construction and sampling strategy of Section 5 rather than by per-task loss weights. At inference, all dimensions use this same generation procedure, without loading separate task-specific checkpoints and without an additional decoding stage. This objective is constrained in practice by two obstacles: Modality Entanglement , under which heterogeneous supervision from the five dimensions competes over shared parameters, and Data-Model Drift , under which a fixed training-data mixture may become misaligned with the model’s evolving learning needs. We first present the unified architecture that accommodates heterogeneous inputs and outputs, then introduce the Task-Conditioned Adaptive Training Strategy to mitigate interference between heterogeneous output types; Data-Model Drift is addressed by the Agentic Data-Model Self-Evolution Engine in Section 5. 

4.2 Model Architecture 

To accommodate heterogeneous input modalities and output semantics within a single parameter space, ABot-ER adopts a unified decoder-only vision-language architecture based on the Transformer [56] comprising three core components: a vision encoder E, a modality projector P, and an autoregressive language model 

G. All five dimensions share the same autoregressive backbone and output head; task-conditioned residuals are introduced within selected attention projections rather than as separate per-dimension decoders. We instantiate this architecture on the pretrained multimodal model Qwen3.5-9B (approximately 9B parameters). Its language backbone comprises 32 decoder blocks with a hidden dimension of d = 4096 and a hybrid attention architecture: three Gated DeltaNet linear-attention blocks followed by one full-attention block, repeated eight times, so that the full-attention blocks are those with zero-based indices 3, 7, . . . , 31 . When the task provides a visual observation I (an image or a video frame sequence), the visual branch extracts zv = P(E(I)) , whereas text-only tasks bypass the visual branch and require no placeholder visual input. The data loader injects the task conditioning token c ∈ { [EQA] , [TASK_PLAN] } into the content of the first user message, once per sample, without rewriting the frozen benchmark prompt body. That message is then rendered by the model’s chat template, which supplies the role delimiters and an optional system message, and zv is inserted at the media placeholders the template emits. Within the message, text and media keep their original interleaved order, so the sequence reaching G opens with the user role delimiter, then c, then the interleaved content: the task token sits inside the first user message rather than at the head of the input sequence. Writing zin for this input sequence, the output y = G(zin ) is generated autoregressively token by token, whether it comprises multiple-choice answers, coordinate points, natural language descriptions, or ordered action sequences. All dimensions use the same generation procedure without a separate rationale-decoding stage. We map the five capability dimensions onto the two task families the training corpus is collected as, and condition on those families. Grouping at this granularity keeps conditioning coarse, with two branches rather than one per dimension, so that most parameters stay shared across all tasks. [EQA] covers embodied question answering, which supplies the spatial, scene-state and temporal supervision together with the interaction coordinates, and [TASK_PLAN] covers task planning, which supplies ordered action plans. The families are 9defined by how the corpus was collected, not by the form of the answer: a coordinate is a question-answering record here, even though it is an action-space output. The two families share object, spatial, state, and affordance knowledge, but differ in supervision scale, answer length, output protocol, and reasoning horizon, and it is this difference that the conditional residuals are given capacity for. The route is determined from record-level task-family metadata and shared across all turns in a sequence; it does not enforce a uniform answer format across turns, and each family also carries auxiliary supervision in other answer forms. Because the task family of a query is known before inference, routing is a deterministic lookup rather than a learned router. To remove format inconsistencies at the data level, ABot-ER separates the numeric protocol from the serialization protocol. The numeric coordinate convention is shared across tasks: coordinates are continuous values normalized to the [0 , 1] interval relative to the original image dimensions, with three decimal places. This is the single coordinate convention the model has to learn. Serialization, by contrast, follows a fixed benchmark-specific format to comply with the corresponding evaluation interface. Section 5.3 describes both layers and the conflict they resolve. 

4.3 Task-Conditioned Adaptive Training Strategy 

The Task-Conditioned Adaptive Training Strategy augments the attention query and value projection modules of selected full-attention blocks with task-conditioned low-rank residuals, using a parameterization inspired by low-rank adaptation [23]. Each residual operates on the corresponding projection input and is added to its output before subsequent attention computation. We place them in the last four full-attention blocks, whose zero-based indices form the set S = {19 , 23 , 27 , 31 }. For l ∈ S , a projection p ∈ { qproj , v proj }, and the hidden state xl entering that projection: 

ez lp,c = Wlpxl + blp + αr Blp,c Alp,c xl (2) where Alp,c ∈ Rr×d, Blp,c ∈ Rdout ,p ×r , r ≪ d, and α is a scaling coefficient. The output dimension dout ,p follows the projection itself and therefore differs between the two. In this backbone qproj jointly produces query features and attention output gates, which are split downstream; the residual is added to the full projection output before that split and therefore applies to both parts. These attention output gates are internal to the backbone and unrelated to the task routing described above. Each projection holds one such pair of factors per route, so that c selects between the two task-family sets. The route is resolved once per sequence and applies to all of its tokens. All parameters other than the route-specific low-rank factors are shared across tasks. The shared language backbone and the residuals are trained jointly, so the shared weights continue to receive gradients from all tasks; the residuals provide a task-family-specific adaptation path intended to mitigate interference between the two families, rather than isolating task gradients. The rank r, the scaling coefficient α, the residual layer indices, and the detailed per-stage training configuration are reported in Table 18. At inference time, the task marker selects the residual branch before response generation, and the selected branch remains active throughout decoding without requiring additional generated routing tokens. 5.1 Evaluation-Driven Capability Diagnosis 

Diagnosis decides what the next corpus revision should change. A round freezes the evaluation contract so that scores are comparable across its checkpoints, aggregates results at the granularity of the five capability dimensions, classifies the underlying errors, and passes the resulting gap profile to the coordinating agent, which returns a data requirement together with candidate sources. The contract fixes the prompt text, media renderer, output parser, and scorer of every benchmark, together with the chat template and decoding mode, and is identified by a content hash. It provides a stable target for training-label alignment, which label construction and parser round-trip checks then enforce. Any change to a prompt, a required point count, a parser, or a scorer produces a new contract version, and scores obtained under different versions are not treated as directly comparable without re-evaluation under a common 11 contract. Because biases introduced early in a closed loop compound over later rounds, the initial rounds are human-calibrated: an expert reviews the agent’s diagnoses and the resulting revisions before they enter training, and those judgements are encoded as the deterministic rules and gates that later rounds run against. 

5.1.1 From Symptom to Fault 

The choice of diagnostic granularity is consequential. Using a single benchmark’s score as the evolution signal carries a risk associated with Goodhart’s Law [40]: when a metric is continuously optimized, it may cease to faithfully reflect the capability it was designed to measure. Targeted augmentation for a specific benchmark may teach the model that benchmark’s idiosyncratic answering patterns, improving the score without strengthening spatial reasoning. We therefore separate the symptom , which is necessarily observed at benchmark level, from the conclusion , which is drawn at capability-dimension level. Dimension scores are fixed equal-weight averages over the benchmark groups of Table 7. A capability gap is admitted only when the benchmarks within a dimension exhibit consistent trends, or when a benchmark-specific symptom is independently traced to a mechanical cause such as a protocol violation. For a dimension represented by a single benchmark, cross-benchmark agreement is unavailable, and diagnosis relies on item-level error analysis instead. Which of these applies is decided by the error classification, not by inspection of the scores alone. Raw model outputs and error logs are classified by fixed rules into six classes, each read off the record itself rather than inferred from the score: parser and format failures; point misses separated from coordinate protocol violations; multiple-choice category errors; numeric parsing errors; open-ended judge deductions; and task-plan errors, subdivided into illegal actions, truncated plans, and executable plans that fail to reach the goal state. These classes are then mapped onto the five dimensions to obtain an attributed gap profile. Table 2 lists representative mappings actually used, each with the check that separates a capability conclusion from a benchmark-specific artifact; where a symptom is traced to the contract, no such check is needed. Deterministic checks classify observable errors and establish audit findings. The coordinating agent uses this evidence to propose root-cause hypotheses and corresponding data requirements. The fault categories organize intervention targets. A diagnosis may identify a primary intervention while requiring coordinated revisions to supervision content, protocol, and exposure: content evolution constructs or repairs the required targets, protocol evolution aligns their representation with task-specific interfaces, and mixture evolution adjusts how much exposure they receive. Increasing exposure does not repair incorrect targets or incompatible output conventions, while valid supervision may remain ineffective when insufficiently sampled. Two of the five categories concern evaluation-interface compliance and evaluation integrity instead. A contract fault indicates non-compliance with the frozen evaluation interface; output and training-label checks determine whether the violation originates in the labels or occurs despite compliant supervision, and the labels are rebuilt, without adding samples, only when a training-label mismatch is confirmed. A contaminated evaluation means the score no longer reflects capability; an anomalous score triggers a contamination audit and is classified this way only when the audit confirms overlap, after which the implicated sources are quarantined (Section 5.5). The other three place the intervention on the supervision. Missing or incomplete targets primarily motivate content construction (Section 5.2). Contradictory supervision may require content repair when target semantics disagree, protocol alignment when representations disagree (Section 5.3), or both. 

Under-exposure motivates mixture reallocation under the relevant content and protocol checks (Section 5.4). Section 5.4 traces a case in which quota adjustment was followed by persistent supervision conflicts, motivating further content and protocol revisions.

5.2 Content Evolution: Targeted Supervision 

Content evolution turns a diagnosed failure into a specification of the supervision to construct or repair. For point grounding, this means targets tied to verified task-relevant regions rather than motion endpoints. For planning, it means complete trajectories with checked preconditions and verified terminal states. The aim is 13 to supply the missing or corrected learning signal, not merely to enlarge the corpus. Case B shows how a failure becomes such a specification. In an archived record from the R3 diagnostic round, six actions succeeded without completing the task, and later planner calls returned unparseable output: the initial plan omitted the placement action, while a repetitive state description left the subsequent JSON unclosed. These observations motivated requirements for terminal-state-complete trajectories verified by simulator replay, feedback-conditioned recovery examples, bounded state descriptions, and parser-valid labels. Table 3 summarizes the construction method used for each dimension. Two risks accompany targeted construction. The first is supervision reliability : agent-assisted supervision may contain unverified fabrications that would inject systematic noise. We therefore adopt evidence grounding, so that every training target is derived from input media, environment state records, or trusted annotations, with the agent acting only as a candidate proposer and its outputs entering the formal pool only after independent verification. Deterministic release audits then enforce the specified constraints on format, provenance, coverage, and exposure that every build must pass. The second is diminishing marginal returns , which made the acceptance criteria themselves a target of revision. A construction requirement stated as an exact count makes the last few percent of a target as expensive as the first ninety, because the items still missing are the ones the available sources cover least well, and a build can spend far longer closing that remainder than it spent producing the bulk. Construction targets are therefore specified as an elastic band rather than an exact count, and the engine tracks the cost of each additional accepted item as the round proceeds. When the measured construction difficulty for the remainder is high, the band widens and the round concludes on a documented exception, provided the residual gap does not materially alter capability coverage or risk levels.

5.3 Protocol Evolution 

Protocol evolution revises inconsistent training representations to satisfy task-specific output requirements. It unifies the numeric coordinate convention while preserving the point cardinalities, serialization formats, and action schemas that different tasks require. Training data for different dimensions often inherits from independently developed annotation systems, producing incompatible representations of the same underlying quantity. For point supervision, three conventions were in simultaneous use: legacy supplementary data using a 0–1000 coordinate range with eight points wrapped in XML or JSON; historical training rewrites using a 14 single [0 , 1] tuple [(x,y)] ; and the official evaluator, which requires [0 , 1] coordinates, exactly eight points, and the XML form <Answer><point>[[x1,y1], ..., [x8,y8]]</point></Answer> . With conflicting targets acting on shared parameters for the same image-question pairs, the model has no single output convention to fit. Case A shows why the two are separable. In an archived point-grounding record, all eight predicted points were parseable and contract-compliant, yet none fell inside the target region: a representation that satisfies the interface can still carry the wrong target. Historical supervision mixed target medoids, motion endpoints, and arm or hand paths, while the training-data audit separately identified the incompatible ranges, counts, and wrappers above. Before the combined revision, VABench-P scores spanned a range of 45 .17 points across the evaluated checkpoints of one run. The rebuild retained verified target-region supervision and aligned its representation with each task’s requirements, after which the within-run range narrowed to 3.46 points, an order of magnitude tighter. Mixture evolution adjusts exposure for supervision whose content and output requirements have already been checked. It addresses insufficient sampling without treating reweighting as a substitute for correcting targets or protocols. The point-grounding history illustrates the distinction: quota adjustment was followed by a higher dimension score, but substantial checkpoint variation remained until the content and protocol revisions were also applied. Sample counts alone do not capture differences in input size or supervision length. During corpus construction the system therefore uses a build-time token proxy as a common budget metric, computed from the characters of all conversation-message contents together with per-image and per-video allowances; it is a character heuristic rather than a tokenizer count. No single accounting basis is sufficient, and the three used in this report answer different questions: unique and physical records reveal repeated exposure and source coverage; consumed samples , equal to optimizer steps times global batch, reveal how much data the model actually saw and govern streaming shard safety; 

assistant supervision tokens , measured with the model’s own tokenizer, quantify supervised output volume. Because the objective is normalized within each training sequence (Section 4), a supervision-token share describes the corpus rather than a proportional weight in the training objective. The bases can diverge sharply. In R6, the single-trunk development release of Table 5 rather than either stage of the released model, the question-answering-to-planning ratio is 75 : 25 by unique records but 29 .71 : 70 .29 by assistant supervision tokens, because planning labels are long structured plans and many question-answering labels contain only a single token. Every mixture figure in this report therefore names its basis. In R6, the question-answering partition is allocated over unique records in three streams. A fine-grained capability stream, targeted at approximately 45% , distributes budget equally across fine-grained capability channels so that training contribution is decoupled from raw volume. A benchmark guarantee stream, approximately 15% , sets a minimum quota for each of the nine question-answering benchmarks so that none can be starved by the fine-grained allocation; its budget is filled by drawing train-split supervision from those benchmarks, and is reweighted toward the dimension that diagnosis identifies as marginally weak. A foundational capability stream, approximately 40% , covers four general knowledge categories in equal proportion so that the model retains broad competence beyond evaluation-specific distributions. Planning supervision sits outside these three streams and is allocated as separate replay-verified trajectory pools. To prevent degenerate allocations, boundary constraints are imposed on the question-answering mixture. Per-benchmark and per-source shares are bounded within the question-answering token-proxy budget, counting materialized exposures so that repetitions accumulate, and no sample is exposed more than four times. The numbers themselves are set by hand: target quotas per dimension and per benchmark are read off the evaluation diagnosis of the round and recorded as literals in its build configuration, while the coordinating agent supplies the qualitative data requirement and the candidate sources rather than the quotas. Deterministic programs then validate those quotas against the bounds above and realize them by capped stratified sampling, drawing samples stratified by source and by group under the exposure caps, where all variants of the same scene, episode, or trial share one group budget; within the manually fixed stream budgets, the pretraining mixture allocates the benchmark-by-source cells with a linear program. If any capacity or format gate is unsatisfied, the build fails and is rolled back. Each release records an ordered SHA-256 over its serialized records, and rebuilding from the same seed reproduces that hash. Learned mixture optimization [59] and influence-based data selection [58] address the same allocation problem with automatic criteria and represent a natural direction for replacing the manually set quotas used here. The point grounding and affordance dimension is the engine’s clearest under-exposure fault, and also its clearest illustration of why exposure adjustment alone is insufficient when supervision conflicts remain. In the earliest round the dimension took whatever share source frequency gave it and scored 50 .58 . The first build with explicit quota constraints raised the two point benchmarks to 15 .04% of the total token proxy, and the dimension scored 57 .39 in that round. That quota-adjusted build nevertheless showed substantial checkpoint variation on VABench-P, with a within-run range of 45 .17 points (Table 4). Following protocol alignment and the Case A target-semantics rebuild, the reported VABench-P range narrowed to 3.46 points. The released model reached a point grounding and affordance dimension score of 67 .06 , with Stage B allocating 25 .53% 

of its physical records and 29 .96% of its assistant supervision tokens to point supervision. The ordering is 16 the lesson: the dimension carried an under-exposure fault and a contradictory-supervision fault at once, and quota decides only the first of the two. 

5.5 Data Governance and Evaluation Integrity 

Data governance protects the evidence the evolution loop runs on. Each build is checked for source eligibility, supervision consistency, media integrity, and overlap with evaluation data under the specified audit rules. Deduplication proceeds at three levels: intra-source exact identity matching; cross-source media and question hashing, which catches the same annotation set mounted under two source names; and quarantine of conflicting answers for the same image and question, adjudicated rather than silently discarded. The four audited risks are the following. • Evaluation contamination. If training data covers test content, evaluation loses diagnostic validity [46] and the entire feedback loop is corrupted. Two controls apply within the loop (Section 5.1): evaluation runs in an execution context separate from the coordinating agent, and evaluation items are excluded as source material for supervision construction. Leakage detection then screens at instruction, trajectory, and environment granularity. Even absent direct answer overlap, training data that covers all test environments and supervises environment-specific identifiers is flagged as a potential risk. • Format interference. Invalid coordinate values or labels inject spurious gradient signal; format compliance verification intercepts such samples before they enter the training pool. • Reference integrity. Broken media references cause the model to receive blank visual input; media integrity verification requires every record to resolve to an existing file. • Semantic and provenance compliance. Sources are checked against eligibility and licensing constraints, and semantic near-duplicates and anomalous perfect scores are escalated for case-by-case adjudication against the criteria calibrated in the initial rounds rather than resolved by threshold alone.

This subsection describes the two-stage recipe used for the released model; earlier development rounds used their own configurations, documented in Appendix A. Both stages train on corpora this engine produced: Stage A on the release that the content, protocol, and mixture revisions of the earlier rounds converged on, and Stage B on the point-heavy release specified by the Case A rebuild together with the under-exposure diagnosis of Section 5.4. Training first establishes competence across the joint capability mixture, then reallocates supervision toward the remaining bottlenecks while retaining other-task data to mitigate forgetting; the selected checkpoint, rather than the final training step, is carried forward. The two-stage design is motivated by asymmetric convergence observed in joint training. Stage A , cross-dimension foundation, starts from the pretrained vision-language model and trains on the joint mixture, comprising the three-stream question-answering partition and the separately allocated planning supervision, with a large budget. Its objective is to establish baseline competence across all five dimensions, internalize the unified numeric protocol and the frozen per-benchmark serializations, and learn task-conditioned output switching. After sufficient iterations Stage A exhibits asymmetric convergence: perception performance saturates first while planning and execution continue improving. Planning and execution require longer exposure to establish stable generation patterns for complete action sequences and precise coordinates. This asymmetric progress motivates Stage B , targeted post-training. It is supervised fine-tuning rather than reinforcement learning, and proceeds from the selected Stage A checkpoint on a set rebalanced toward the bottleneck dimensions. Because concentrated training risks catastrophic forgetting, it uses a substantially reduced learning rate, retains a proportion of saturated-dimension data as anti-forgetting anchors, and runs for a much shorter schedule. The released model uses 462 ,390 physical records for Stage A, split 90 .44 : 9 .56 by physical records between question answering and planning, and 104 ,960 physical records for Stage B, of which point supervision accounts for 25 .53% by physical records and 29 .96% by assistant supervision tokens. Stage B reduces the decoder learning rate from 4.0 × 10 −6 to 3.0 × 10 −7 while retaining other-task data as anti-forgetting anchors. In each stage an earlier checkpoint is carried forward rather than the final one. The complete jobs consume approximately 826 GPU hours on 32 GPUs, excluding data construction and full-suite evaluation, which is what makes a revision expensive to assess and worth diagnosing before it is made. Full compositions and configurations are reported in Appendix A.  

> Checkpoint selection.

Model selection evaluates several checkpoints of a run under the frozen contract, filters them by guardrails, and ranks the survivors in a fixed order. A guardrail is a benchmark-level retention bound rather than a capability dimension: each round fixes a floor on the nine-benchmark question-answering mean, bounds on the permitted drop of the task-planning mean and of the individual benchmarks the round is not targeting, a parser-reliability floor, and zero detected overlap with evaluation data. These bounds are stated per round against the checkpoint the run started from, so no single tolerance applies across the history; those in force for the released model are listed in Appendix A. Among the checkpoints that clear them, selection compares in order the number of leading results at that checkpoint, then the mean over the benchmarks the round targets, then the nine-benchmark question-answering mean. A leading result is a benchmark on which the checkpoint strictly exceeds the best score of a frozen set of public models, counted within a single checkpoint so that a profile assembled from several checkpoints cannot stand in for one model. The peak of any single dimension is not used as the criterion, because in joint learning an extreme score on one dimension typically accompanies significant degradation elsewhere. Targets and guardrails together span all twelve benchmarks, so no capability dimension goes unwatched: the released run maximizes a benchmark in four of the five dimensions and bounds every remaining benchmark against regression, with long-horizon action organization covered by bounds on the planning mean and on each of its three benchmarks.  

> Retrospective selection and round closure.

The engine applies no fixed numerical convergence threshold. Each run is trained to its configured length, and the selection window is determined retrospectively from the evaluated checkpoints: a round is closed, and its checkpoint either selected or rolled back, when two successively evaluated checkpoints fail to increase the number of leading results on the targeted benchmarks and either the targeted mean declines or a guardrail is breached. When that condition does not arise, the round closes once the configured budget is exhausted, and selection proceeds over all checkpoints evaluated 18 in the run. When in addition the remaining bottlenecks cannot be addressed by revising the mixture or the source pool, the data version is frozen and the release proceeds to comprehensive pre-release evaluation. Figures 5 and 6 in Section 6 report the five capability summaries at the selected observation points of this history.  

> Cross-round continuation.

The development history is not a single continuation chain. Revisions that changed the coordinate protocol, the source eligibility rules, or the grounded-planning supervision were retrained from the pretrained model, since prior-protocol knowledge creates interfering priors; incremental reweightings continued from an earlier checkpoint. The R6 development runs of Table 5 resumed from the R4 checkpoint, deliberately bypassing the fragmented R5 weights. The released model instead followed the two-stage recipe above: Stage A was initialized from the pretrained model on the open-source release derived from R4, and Stage B from the selected Stage A checkpoint. Since R4 itself restarted from the pretrained model after the implicated sources had been removed, no weights trained on those sources were carried into either branch. All continuations reset optimizer and scheduler state. The complete lineage is documented in Appendix A.

The source of gains changes structurally across the history, and round-to-round change is not monotonic: improvement on one dimension is frequently accompanied by regression on another.

## 6 Experiments 

We first assess the overall competitiveness of a unified embodied model across twelve benchmarks and five capability dimensions, then apply the same training recipe to backbones from 2B to 27B parameters, followed by analyses of the Task-Conditioned Adaptive Training Strategy and data-model co-evolution. We examine whether competitiveness across tasks coexists within one model, whether task conditioning is associated with cross-task gains, and how iterative updates balance improvement with retention. Real-world demonstrations then illustrate the utility of model outputs within robotic systems. 

6.1 Experimental Setup 

6.1.1 Capability Dimensions and Benchmarks 

We organize twelve benchmarks by the five dimensions introduced in Section 4. Perception comprises spatial and relational reasoning, scene and state understanding, and temporal and event reasoning. Planning is assessed through long-horizon action organization, while execution-oriented evaluation focuses on point grounding and affordance prediction. The latter measures spatial grounding for manipulation and does not, by itself, measure physical task completion. The nine-benchmark Embodied Question Answering (EQA) suite and the three-benchmark planning suite are evaluation collections rather than a partition into the three core competencies: EQA includes both perceptual judgments and execution-oriented pointing tasks.  

> Spatial and Relational Reasoning.

EmbSpatial [16], VSI [61], and ERQA [18] provide complementary assessments of spatial understanding. EmbSpatial focuses on spatial relations from embodied viewpoints. VSI tests spatial layout, distance, size, and related reasoning from video observations. ERQA (Embodied Reasoning Question Answering) evaluates embodied reasoning through multiple-choice questions with interleaved images and text, including spatial and multi-view understanding. Its placement in this group indicates the principal emphasis of our analysis rather than an exhaustive characterization of its question types.  

> Scene and State Understanding.

OpenEQA [39], UniEQA [55], and RoboVQA [50] assess whether a model can identify question-relevant evidence and form appropriate semantic judgments. OpenEQA covers open-ended scene questions; UniEQA spans multiple embodied questions and skills; and RoboVQA evaluates action, state, outcome, and task understanding from videos of robotic and human activity. These benchmarks overlap in scope, so the grouping organizes the analysis without assuming that each benchmark measures an isolated capability. 20 Temporal and Event Reasoning. Cosmos [44] evaluates the understanding of events, actions, and temporal relations in physical scenes. Relevant questions in RoboVQA and VSI offer additional evidence of temporal reasoning. Each benchmark contributes only once to benchmark-level aggregates; dimension-level scores, when reported, require a frozen mapping from questions to capabilities.  

> Long-Horizon Action Organization.

ET-Plan-Bench [71] and the EB-ALFRED and EB-Habitat tasks from EmbodiedBench [62] assess goal-directed reasoning through complete-plan generation and feedback-conditioned action selection. The ET-Plan-Bench archive used here is marked official-agent, with success inherited from the upstream exploration run; we do not equate this configuration with feedback-free, one-shot complete-plan generation. EB-ALFRED and EB-Habitat provide current observations, action history, and available feedback during interaction and assess subsequent action selection and task completion. These settings probe complementary aspects of action organization; claims about closed-loop behavior rely on the interactive evaluations.  

> Point Grounding and Affordance.

VABench-Point (VABench-P) [42, 70] and Where2Place-point [69] evaluate instruction-conditioned localization of actionable targets or valid placement regions. The model predicts normalized coordinates, and scoring measures their agreement with the official target region. VABench-P defines target regions through bounding-box annotations, whereas Where2Place uses region masks. These scoring annotations are distinct from the point outputs requested from the model. 

6.1.2 Training Data 

Both training stages use the corpora that the self-evolution loop of Section 5 converged on, and no model in this section was trained on data revised for it specifically. Stage A is a joint release of about 462 k physical records over 295 k unique identities, split 90 : 10 by physical records between question answering and planning; it was derived from the decontaminated release of Section 5 by removing 681 quarantined or invalid records and then aligning 47 k VABench-P samples to the frozen eight-point XML evaluation contract. Stage B is a joint post-training release of about 105 k physical records, each unique identity materialized exactly twice; it rebalances toward planning recovery rather than toward point grounding alone, so task planning takes a quarter of its physical records and close to two thirds of its assistant supervision tokens. Table 3 gives the construction method per capability dimension, and Appendix A the realized composition of each stage together with the released record counts. The corpora are assembled from public train splits and simulator rollouts, organized by the same five capability dimensions used for diagnosis and evaluation. Only train splits enter them, and the sources below are named by what each contributes rather than by the benchmark it shares a name with.  

> Spatial and relational supervision.

EmbSpatial [16] contributes multiple-choice spatial relations posed from embodied viewpoints, and VSI [61] layout, distance and size questions answered from video of a walked-through scene. Euclid30K [29] adds geometric problems used as a surrogate task for spatial perception. RefSpatial [72] provides spatial referring expressions with reasoning traces, HANDAL [20] manipulable object categories with pose and affordance annotations, and RoboRefIt [37] language-conditioned object referring in cluttered indoor scenes.  

> Scene and state supervision.

RoboVQA [50] contributes long-horizon question answering over robot and human activity video, and Robo2VLM [8] question answering derived from in-the-wild robot manipulation datasets. EgoPlan-IT [10] adds planning questions over egocentric activity video, RoboFAC [66] robot failure cases with analysis and correction annotations, and EO-Data [47] instruction-following data from a unified embodied foundation model. MM-IFInstruct [14] supplies multimodal instruction-following data, and LLaVA instruction data [33] general visual instruction tuning, which keeps competence outside the embodied distributions the other sources cover.  

> Temporal and event supervision.

Cosmos-Reason [44] supplies physical common-sense and event items over short video, here grounded mainly in RoboVQA clips.  

> Point grounding and affordance supervision.

FSD [70] is the main source of eight-point supervision, contributing manipulation data that bridges reasoning and decision. RoboPoint and Where2Place [69] 21 contribute spatial affordance prediction, namely target points and valid placement regions. HOI4D [34] adds egocentric category-level human-object interaction, and CoSyn [64] code-guided synthetic text-rich images.  

> Long-horizon supervision.

ALFRED [51] contributes household instruction-following trajectories in simula-tion, and Habitat [49] simulator rollouts, which are retained only when replay reaches the target state. Public planning corpora and in-house planning annotations complete the partition.

Open-ended answers use the LLM-based evaluators in the current evaluation implementation. Pointing tasks measure the fraction of predicted points inside the target region. The planning benchmarks use their respective task-success metrics, with format validity, task progress, and invalid-action rates retained as diagnostics. ABot-ER uses one set of model weights across tasks and selects an internal branch through an input condition. Following Section 4, the condition is the data task family of the record: [EQA] for embodied question answering and [TASK_PLAN] for task planning. Point-grounding benchmarks are evaluated under the former condition: their supervision is collected as part of the question-answering family, even though the answer is a coordinate rather than text. The token is injected by the loader rather than stored in the records. The audited point predictions use coordinates normalized to [0 , 1] relative to the original image. Where2Place uses tuple lists, whereas the current VABench archive uses tagged eight-point lists; all 300 answers repeat one location. These cannot be interpreted as eight independent predictions. Evaluation adapters apply deterministic numerical or format conversions, maintaining consistency between target regions and image preprocessing coordinates. The audited EQA configurations specify greedy autoregressive decoding without multiple-sample ensembling. EB archives contain a reasoning_and_reflection field, so an absence of explicit reasoning is not assumed across all evaluations; the provenance of its original online calls remains to be verified, precluding inferences about native closed-loop behavior. A single generation episode still requires token-by-token decoding and does not imply a single network forward pass.

6.6 Real-World Deployment 

We further examine how ABot-ER’s embodied perception supports physical interaction on a Galaxea R1 Pro, a wheeled dual-arm robot. The platform has 26 degrees of freedom, including two 7-DoF arms with parallel grippers, a 4-DoF torso, and a 6-DoF omnidirectional chassis; it stands approximately 1.7 m tall and supports a rated payload of 3.5 kg per arm at a reach of 0.6 m. 1 ABot-ER serves as the visual question answering (VQA) component of ABot-AgentOS [54], our robot agent runtime, deployed through vLLM on an external host with a single NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5090D and taking 1920 × 1536 images from the robot’s head camera. ABot-AgentOS orchestrates the interaction and invokes ABot-M [9, 65], a separate vision-language-action (VLA) model, for physical manipulation.

The second is where to act: a grasp point on the target block, and a placement point inside a free region to the right of the tens digit large enough to receive the ones digit. ## 7 Conclusion and Future Work 

This report presented ABot-ER, a unified embodied reasoning model that resolves, from one set of weights, the questions a robot faces between seeing and acting: where things are, what state the scene is in, what just 30 happened, what to do next, and exactly where to act. It is a decoder-only vision-language architecture with a single normalized coordinate protocol, per-benchmark output contracts frozen into the training labels, and a task conditioning mechanism, so that one body of embodied knowledge serves perception, planning, and execution without switching weights or adding a decoding stage at inference. Two obstacles stand between that design and a model that realizes it. Against Data-Model Drift , the Agentic Data-Model Self-Evolution Engine turns corpus construction into a closed loop driven by evaluation feedback, and its central move is to diagnose which aspect of the training data is at fault rather than to answer every weakness with more samples: one shared diagnosis coordinates three revisions that cannot substitute for one another, supplying supervision that is missing, repairing supervision that is contradictory, and reallocating budget to supervision that is under-exposed, with a coordinating agent proposing each diagnosis and deterministic programs deciding what enters training. Against Modality Entanglement , the Task-Conditioned Adaptive Training Strategy overlays lightweight low-rank residuals on the shared backbone, routed by the task family of each record, so that heterogeneous supervision can share representations while receiving the task-specific adaptation intended to reduce interference between them. In a systematic evaluation across twelve benchmarks covering three core competencies and five capability dimensions, ABot-ER leads all three competency means, namely perception, planning, and execution, among the public models compared, demonstrating that breadth and depth can coexist in unified embodied reasoning. The converged corpus carries much of what the loop produces: a single post-training pass on it reaches a similar twelve-benchmark mean to a chain of six rounds at a fifth of the accumulated post-training, and the same one-pass recipe transfers to backbones from 2B to 27B parameters without any size-specific diagnosis. The same recipe on a mixture-of-experts backbone that activates 3B parameters per token surpasses the 9B dense model on the twelve-benchmark mean, with the conditioning’s contribution there concentrated on complete-plan generation. Task conditioning pays while a corpus is still being made internally consistent and returns less once it is: on that backbone it improved eleven of twelve benchmarks before the output protocol was unified and neither suite mean after, with a consistent gain on complete-plan generation throughout. On a wheeled dual-arm platform the model serves as the perception and judgement component of an agent pipeline that delegates motion to a separate action model. Interactive planning is the dimension the aggregate mean describes least well: it is where a single pass on the converged corpus recovers the least of the six-round chain, and where targeted post-training contributes the most. That gap sets the first of three directions we plan to pursue. The most direct is to extend self-evolution from supervised fine-tuning to learning from environment feedback, using the observed outcomes of model-proposed actions executed by downstream policies. The second is to couple the reasoning and control tiers more tightly while keeping them separate, so that grounded coordinates and plans carry more of the information a vision-language-action model needs, and feedback from execution returns to the reasoning tier as evidence. The third follows from the corpus transferring across backbones: the artifact worth maintaining is that corpus together with its evaluation contract and audit trail, not only the checkpoint trained on it. We release the model weights and this corpus, and of the two we expect the corpus to be the more durable. We hope ABot-ER is useful as a reasoning layer for robot systems, and that the account of how its training data was diagnosed, repaired, and audited is as useful as the model itself. 31 8 Contributions
