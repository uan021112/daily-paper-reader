Abstract
Reconstructing 3D scenes from sparse views without per-scene optimization re-
mains highly challenging, especially for recovering accurate geometry and fine
textures. While recent feedforward approaches leverage generalizable 3D Gaussian
Splatting (3DGS) for scene generation, they typically assign one or multiple Gaus-
sians to each pixel. Such uniform allocation not only produces highly redundant
representations by wasting primitives in homogeneous areas, but also fails to ex-

structural modeling. To address these limitations, we present CompactSplat, a
novel feedforward framework that enables a content-aware allocation of 3D Gaus-

prior methods on both standard metrics and high-resolution rendering consistency,
achieving high-fidelity reconstructions with significantly fewer primitives.

Introduction
Recovering geometrically accurate and visually consistent 3D scenes from sparse views is a funda-
mental problem for immersive VR, gaming, and digital content creation. The recent success of 3D
Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) Kerbl et al. (2023) has significantly advanced this field by representing
scenes as collections of 3D Gaussians, enabling real-time rendering with high visual fidelity.
Existing methods for constructing such representations generally follow two paradigms. Traditional
approaches rely on dense multi-view captures and COLMAP-based Schönberger and Frahm (2016)
point cloud initialization, followed by computationally expensive per-scene optimization. To avoid
this bottleneck, feed-forward approaches have recently gained increasing attention. By leveraging
pretrained models, they directly predict 3D Gaussians from sparse inputs in a single forward pass,
eliminating per-scene optimization and enabling fast, scalable 3D reconstruction.
Despite their rapid inference, many existing feed-forward methods remain inefficient in their primitive
allocation. Natural scenes exhibit substantial spatial variation in texture and geometric complexity,
yet prior methods typically adopt a rigid pixel-wise prediction paradigm, assigning one Chen et al.
(2024c); Xu et al. (2024b); He et al. (2026) or multiple Charatan et al. (2024); Tang et al. (2024)
Gaussians to every pixel. This uniform allocation forces the network to populate large, textureless
regions with thousands of primitives. Consequently, the generated 3D representations can suffer from
substantial spatial redundancy. This redundancy increases representation storage costs and motivates
more compact 3D priors for downstream tasks.
To address this issue, we introduce CompactSplat, a novel feed-forward framework for high-fidelity
3D reconstruction with content-aware Gaussian allocation. Instead of retaining a primitive at every
pixel, CompactSplat allocates Gaussians according to image texture, using sparse primitives in
homogeneous regions while concentrating denser predictions in areas with rich texture. To achieve
this adaptive allocation, we decouple the primitive generation process into two complementary
components. First, a Texture-Aware Partitioner (TAP) analyzes the input views to estimate regional
texture complexity and partition each image with a quadtree. Second, a Hierarchical Pyramid
Sampler (HPS) uses this guidance to sample explicit Gaussian attributes from a pooled multi-
resolution attribute pyramid. Together, these components enable CompactSplat to adapt its primitive
density to the input texture, reducing spatial redundancy while retaining strong reconstruction quality.
Crucially, CompactSplat provides a unified paradigm for controllable inference. Owing to the scale-
aware synergy between TAP and HPS, our framework naturally supports dynamic sparsity control at
inference time. By modulating a single partition threshold, the model can select different trade-offs
between rendering fidelity and primitive count—without requiring any network retraining or
auxiliary modules. This intrinsic adaptability provides an on-the-fly mechanism to select different
representation sizes using a single set of pretrained weights.
Ultimately, CompactSplat establishes a more compact paradigm for 3D representation. By replacing
rigid pixel-wise allocation with texture-driven primitive allocation, our approach adapts to spatial
variations in image content. In our experiments, it delivers high-fidelity reconstructions with fewer
primitives. This allocation transforms dense, redundant outputs into compact 3D representations with
a tunable primitive count.

Related Works
2.1
3D Gaussian Splatting
Since the advent of Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) Mildenhall et al. (2021), novel view synthesis has
advanced rapidly, evolving from implicit MLPs to grid-based Müller et al. (2022); Fridovich-Keil et al.
(2022) and anti-aliased Barron et al. (2021, 2022) representations. To further accelerate rendering,
recent paradigms shifted towards explicit point-based architectures. Notably, 3D Gaussian Splatting
(3DGS) Kerbl et al. (2023) revolutionized the field by modeling scenes with semi-transparent,
anisotropic ellipsoids, enabling photorealistic, real-time rendering via differentiable rasterization.
Consequently, a proliferation of extensions has emerged to refine 3DGS. These include optimizations
for training stability Cheng et al. (2024); Zhang et al. (2024); Radl et al. (2024); Diolatzis et al. (2024),
anti-aliasing techniques Yan et al. (2024); Yu et al. (2024); Song et al. (2024); Liang et al. (2024), and
adaptations for dynamic scenes Luiten et al. (2023); Wu et al. (2023); Wan et al. (2024); Huang et al.
(2023); He et al. (2024). Additionally, several works focus on compressing these representations to
reduce storage and accelerate inference Girish et al. (2024); Navaneet et al. (2024); Niedermayr et al.
(2024); Lee et al. (2024); Fan et al. (2024); Chen et al. (2024b).
2.2
Generalizable 3D Reconstruction
To bypass the computationally expensive per-scene optimization of traditional 3DGS, recent literature
has explored feedforward models that directly predict Gaussian attributes from sparse inputs. Splatter
Image Szymanowicz et al. (2024) pioneered the translation of 2D images into Gaussian parameter
maps. Subsequent frameworks integrated geometric priors to enhance spatial understanding; for
instance, pixelSplat Charatan et al. (2024) utilized epipolar geometry, while MVSplat Chen et al.
(2024c) and DepthSplat Xu et al. (2024b) leveraged cost volumes and cross-task depth interactions.
Further advancements addressed specific challenges, such as expanding synthesis ranges Wang et al.
(2024b), improving geometric surfaces via 2DGS He et al. (2026), and handling unposed inputs Kang
et al. (2025); Jiang et al. (2025). VGGT Wang et al. (2025) and MV-DUSt3R Tang et al. (2025)
likewise reconstruct uncalibrated views, while CompactSplat assumes known camera intrinsics and
extrinsics. Other pipelines introduced triplane representations Zou et al. (2024); Xu et al. (2024a)
and canonical space generation Ye et al. (2024) for feedforward 3D reconstruction and generation.
SplatFormer Chen et al. (2024a) instead uses a point transformer to refine an already optimized
Gaussian scene for novel-view extrapolation.
Pixel-wise prediction strategies, including multi-layered approaches like HiSplat Tang et al. (2024),
can generate redundant primitives in homogeneous regions. Recent methods explore alternatives:
TokenGS Ren et al. (2026) uses learnable queries to decouple Gaussian count from image resolution
and view count, while C3G An et al. (2025) predicts a compact Gaussian set from unposed multi-
view images using learned queries. VolSplat Wang et al. (2026) lifts features using predicted depth
and decodes Gaussians from occupied voxels. Off The Grid Moreau et al. (2026) detects subpixel
primitive positions and adapts patch density using image entropy. CompactSplat adopts a content-
aware allocation paradigm based on an image-space quadtree. TAP concentrates finer partitions
in textured regions, while HPS samples pooled Gaussian attributes and modulates primitive scales
according to block footprints. This spatial hierarchy supports inference-time quality–compactness
control with one checkpoint, reducing per-view redundancy while preserving detail at the selected
operating point.

3.1
Model Architecture
Figure 2: Overview of CompactSplat. A dual-path encoder first extracts and fuses multi-view
and single-view features from sparse inputs, which are then processed by a multi-scale U-Net.
Concurrently, the Texture-Aware Partitioner (TAP) evaluates scene complexity based on a user-
defined threshold to generate multi-level spatial masks. Guided by these masks, the Hierarchical
Pyramid Sampler (HPS) adaptively extracts features across decoder levels. Ultimately, this paradigm
allocates sparse samples (S.S.) from low-resolution (L.R.) maps to flat regions, and dense samples
(D.S.) from high-resolution (H.R.) maps to texturally complex areas.
Building upon the robust feature extraction paradigms established in recent feed-forward models He
et al. (2026); Xu et al. (2024b), CompactSplat employs a dual-path encoder to capture both multi-
view correspondences and single-view geometric priors. For the single-view branch, we utilize
a pretrained monocular depth backbone (e.g., Depth Anything V2 Yang et al. (2024)) to extract
structural and semantic cues, which are particularly beneficial for ill-posed regions lacking cross-view
overlaps. Concurrently, the multi-view branch processes the inputs through self- and cross-attention
mechanisms Vaswani et al. (2017); Liu et al. (2021b) to construct plane-sweep cost volumes Chen
et al. (2024c); Collins (1996), enforcing strict multi-view consistency. The complementary features
from both branches are then concatenated to form a unified volumetric representation.
This fused feature volume is subsequently fed into a multi-scale 2D U-Net Ronneberger et al. (2015)
architecture. Unlike prior works that deterministically upsample all features into a single, full-
resolution map for pixel-wise Gaussian prediction, our architecture decouples primitive generation to
eliminate spatial redundancy, as illustrated in Fig. 2. Specifically, we introduce the Texture-Aware
Partitioner (TAP), which analyzes spatial frequency and regional complexity from the inputs to
produce a set of hierarchical partition masks. These masks dynamically dictate the appropriate Level
of Detail (LoD) for representing different regions of the scene.
Guided by the TAP masks, our Hierarchical Pyramid Sampler (HPS) predicts explicit Gaussian
attributes—including 3D center, scale, rotation, opacity, and spherical harmonics—directly from the
multi-scale intermediate features of the U-Net decoder. Crucially, large, homogeneous regions are
assigned to lower-resolution feature levels, allowing a single, scale-modulated Gaussian to seamlessly
tile broad planar surfaces. In contrast, structurally complex areas are routed to the highest-resolution
decoder features to preserve intricate geometric details. This multi-scale routing paradigm not only
captures fine geometry but also naturally enables dynamic sparsity control, resulting in a highly
compact yet expressive 3D representation.
The core motivation behind the Texture-Aware Partitioner (TAP) is to replace redundant pixel-wise
generation with adaptive, density-aware primitive allocation. To this end, we formulate the image
partitioning as a top-down quadtree decomposition driven by local textural complexity. This design
is motivated by the inherent coupling of geometry and appearance within a single 3D Gaussian
primitive. In natural environments, geometric complexity (e.g., depth discontinuities, occlusions, and
structural boundaries) almost inevitably manifests as high-frequency textural variance. Although the
converse is not strictly true—intricate textures frequently lie on structurally flat surfaces—relying on
textural complexity as a subdivision proxy guarantees a conservative and sufficient upper bound. It
ensures that all complex geometries receive dense primitive allocation, while aggressively pruning
homogeneous regions where neither geometry nor texture demands high-resolution modeling.

While the Texture-Aware Partitioner (TAP) determines the
optimal spatial layout and density of the primitives, the
Hierarchical Pyramid Sampler (HPS) maps these abstract
spatial partitions into explicit 3D Gaussian attributes. To
this end, HPS performs scale-conditioned sampling from
a foundational scene representation.
Instead of forcing the network to implicitly predict multi-
scale attributes across different decoder layers, our ar-
chitecture utilizes a DPT head to predict a unified, high-
fidelity Gaussian attribute field Abase at full image resolu-
tion. However, directly sampling from this high-frequency
field for large, homogeneous partitions would inherently
lead to aliasing artifacts and a mismatch in the receptive
field. To address this, we construct a discrete attribute
scale space A = {AL−1, . . . , A1, A0}. Starting with
the full-resolution field AL−1 = Abase, each subsequent
coarser level Al is derived by progressively applying a
spatial low-pass filter (e.g., local aggregation). This multi-scale representation ensures that features
at lower Level of Detail (LoD) indices naturally encapsulate broader regional contexts, perfectly
mirroring the TAP quadtree hierarchy.

It enables a single Gaussian, sampled from a deeper level of the scale space, to morphologically
expand and seamlessly tile expansive planar surfaces. This fundamentally breaks the representational
redundancy of rigid pixel-wise predictions while rigorously preserving the rendering quality of

3.4
Training Strategy
A core objective of CompactSplat is to establish a unified paradigm for controllable inference,
enabling dynamic trade-offs between rendering fidelity and sparsity without network retraining. To
achieve this, the model must be exposed to varying partition thresholds τ during the training process.
However, jointly optimizing for multi-view geometric consistency and dynamic primitive pruning
from scratch introduces severe training instability. To mitigate this, we propose a tailored two-stage
curriculum learning strategy.
Stage 1: Dense Geometric Initialization. In the initial phase, we strictly fix the partition threshold
to τ = 0. Under this condition, the Texture-Aware Partitioner (TAP) behaves conservatively,
decomposing the feature maps at the highest Level of Detail (LoD) across the entire scene. This
dense supervision isolates the learning objective, forcing the dual-path encoder and the multi-scale
U-Net to focus exclusively on establishing robust multi-view correspondences and extracting accurate
geometric priors, completely free from the confounding effects of adaptive sparsity.
Stage 2: Sparsity-Aware Fine-Tuning. Once the network has converged to a reliable geometric
understanding, we introduce stochastic thresholding. In this subsequent stage, τ is randomly sampled
from a predefined uniform distribution τ ∼U(0, τmax) at each training iteration. This strategy exposes
the model to a continuous spectrum of pruning ratios. Crucially, it forces the Hierarchical Pyramid
Sampler (HPS) to accurately learn the geometric scale multipliers for lower-resolution Gaussians,
ensuring they can morphologically expand to seamlessly tile broad, homogeneous regions under
aggressive pruning scenarios. Through this two-stage curriculum, a single set of pretrained weights
inherently acquires the versatility to reconstruct scenes at arbitrary sparsity levels on the fly.

demonstrate the efficiency of our feedforward architecture, we introduce two representation-specific
metrics: the average Number of Gaussians(GS Num.), and the Gaussian Per Pixel(GPP), which
explicitly highlights the compactness(CPT) of primitives compared to the unpruned baseline.
Baselines.Our framework is compared against leading sparse-view feedforward 3DGS models, in-
cluding PixelSplat Charatan et al. (2024), MVSplat Chen et al. (2024c), TranSplat Zhang et al. (2025),
HiSplat Tang et al. (2024), DepthSplat Xu et al. (2024b), SurfSplat He et al. (2026) and YonoSplat Ye
et al. (2025). While architectures like PixelSplat and HiSplat indiscriminately predict multiple
Gaussians per pixel, methods such as MVSplat, TranSplat, DepthSplat, SurfSplat and YonoSplat
restrict their generation to a single primitive per pixel. Because our core contribution lies in decisively
breaking the 1:1 pixel-to-primitive barrier to eliminate spatial redundancy, benchmarking against this
latter group establishes a highly rigorous baseline for evaluating representational efficiency.
Qualitative Comparisons. Visual inspections of the rendered novel views in Figure 3 demonstrate
that CompactSplat achieves photorealistic quality that is highly competitive with, and frequently
surpasses, current state-of-the-art feed-forward methods. This superiority becomes particularly
evident under high-resolution magnification. While baseline models often struggle with spatial voids,
noisy boundaries, and spurious artifacts due to their rigid primitive allocation, CompactSplat maintains
strict structural integrity. By leveraging complexity-aware allocation, our method seamlessly tiles flat
planar surfaces with scale-modulated primitives while accurately preserving high-frequency edges.
Consequently, it yields strictly watertight geometries and visually coherent structures that remain
exceptionally robust even upon close inspection. Additional qualitative comparisons across various
scenes are provided in our supplementary video.
Quantitative Results. The objective metrics presented in Table 1 and Table 2 further corroborate the
transformative efficiency of our architecture. Across standard fidelity metrics (e.g., PSNR, SSIM,
LPIPS) and the structure-aware HRRC metric, CompactSplat consistently matches or exceeds the
performance of leading baselines. Crucially, it achieves this high-fidelity synthesis while drastically
reducing the total Number of Gaussians. By pioneering a departure from the conventional pixel-wise
prediction paradigm, CompactSplat establishes a highly compact representation. Furthermore, this
compactness is inherently controllable; our architecture allows users to dynamically modulate the
pruning ratio on the fly, offering an unprecedented capability to balance rendering quality and memory
footprint without requiring any network retraining.

Conclusion
In this paper, we presented CompactSplat, a novel feed-forward framework designed to overcome
the severe spatial redundancy inherent in current sparse-view 3D Gaussian Splatting models. By
synergizing a Texture-Aware Partitioner (TAP) with a Hierarchical Pyramid Sampler (HPS), our
approach decisively breaks away from the rigid pixel-wise prediction paradigm, adaptively allocating
primitives based on regional complexity. This scale-aware representation not only achieves state-
of-the-art rendering fidelity across multiple datasets but also inherently supports dynamic sparsity
control during inference. Consequently, CompactSplat delivers highly compact 3D priors, offering
flexible, on-the-fly trade-offs between reconstruction quality and memory footprint.

A.3
Implementation Details.
CompactSplat is implemented in PyTorch Paszke et al. (2019) and optimized using the AdamW
optimizer Loshchilov and Hutter (2017) coupled with a cosine annealing learning rate schedule. For
the core components of our proposed architecture, we set the maximum block size in the Texture-
Aware Partitioner to Smax = 16. During the sparsity-aware fine-tuning stage of our curriculum, the
dynamic partition threshold is sampled uniformly up to τmax = 0.02.
Following standard practices He et al. (2026); Xu et al. (2024b), we instantiate our model using
varying capacities of the Depth Anything V2 Yang et al. (2024) monocular backbone (ViT-S, ViT-B,
and ViT-L), denoting these variants as Ours-S, Ours-B, and Ours-L, respectively. All models are
trained on NVIDIA A100 GPUs. Specifically, Ours-S is trained for 300K iterations with a batch size
of 16, whereas Ours-B and Ours-L are trained for 600K iterations with a batch size of 8. The learning
rate is configured at 2 × 10−6 for the pretrained monocular backbone and 2 × 10−4 for all newly
initialized layers.

A.7
Limitations and Future Directions.
While CompactSplat significantly improves representational efficiency, it leaves exciting room for
future exploration. First, although our quadtree-based partitioning is highly efficient, it relies on
rigid orthogonal grids. Exploring more sophisticated, shape-aware, or semantic-driven subdivision
strategies could further push the boundaries of the Pareto frontier, extracting even higher visual
fidelity at extreme pruning ratios. Second, our current formulation primarily eliminates spatial
redundancy within individual views, leaving the inherent inter-view redundancy largely unexplored.
Since multiple source cameras inevitably observe overlapping geometries, future research could
investigate cross-view primitive pruning or multi-view fusion mechanisms to further condense the
global 3D scene representation.
