Receding-Horizon Next-Best-View Planning for
Highly Occluded Target Perception
Ayse Gul Yildiz1, Rekha Raja∗1
Abstract—Robotic systems that must acquire information
about a specific target often face severe occlusion from sur-
rounding structures or from the target itself, such that a
single camera viewpoint provides only partial information. Active
perception must therefore select viewpoints that progressively
reveal unobserved regions. However, conventional local next-
best-view (NBV) methods typically select viewpoints according
to their immediate utility, which can overlook views that provide
limited information initially but enable more informative obser-
vations from subsequent viewpoints. We formulated local target
perception as a short-horizon sequential view-planning problem
and proposed a Receding-Horizon Next-Best-View (RH-NBV)
planner that evaluates candidate sequences of future viewpoints
rather than isolated views. RH-NBV is evaluated on a UR5e
manipulator with an eye-in-hand RGB-D camera in simulation
as well as on physical robot against a gradient-based NBV
planner, and random search, using a coffee mug and Stanford
bunny under controlled occlusion and a tomato plant with
natural self-occlusion. Under heavy occlusion, RH-NBV achieves
substantially higher target information recovery, reaching 63.8%
ROI coverage versus 34.2% and 85.5% occluded-surface recall
versus 7.4% for the single-step NBV baselines on the most
occluded mug configuration. A controlled horizon ablation shows
that this improvement arises from multi-step reasoning rather
than additional computation. RH-NBV provides the greatest
benefit for strongly occluded targets, while myopic NBV remains
competitive and substantially cheaper when visibility is less
constrained.

I. INTRODUCTION
Active perception enables a robot to acquire information
that cannot be obtained from a fixed sensor configuration
[1]. By moving its sensor to different viewpoints, a robot
can improve the visibility of task-relevant regions and acquire
complementary observations of the environment [2], [3]. This
capability is particularly important when a target is partially
occluded by surrounding structures or by itself, as in object
inspection, manipulation, harvesting, and active 3D perception.
Next-best-view (NBV) planning provides a framework for
actively selecting such viewpoints according to their expected
utility. While many NBV approaches address global scene
exploration or reconstruction, local target-centric perception
requires sensing to be focused on a specific region of interest
(ROI). Under occlusion, however, viewpoint utility can be
sequential: a view with limited immediate information may

reveal regions that enable more informative subsequent obser-
vations. Conventional local NBV methods typically select the
viewpoint with the highest immediate utility and therefore do
not explicitly account for these downstream effects.
We formulated local target perception under occlusion as a
short-horizon sequential view-planning problem and proposed
a Receding-Horizon Next-Best-View (RH-NBV) planner. RH-
NBV samples candidate sequences of future viewpoints and
evaluates each sequence using a semantic, occlusion-aware
utility while propagating the belief state through the sequence.
The planner executes only the first viewpoint of the selected
sequence, incorporates the acquired observation into the belief,
and recomputes the planning horizon. This formulation enables
the planner to account for the future visibility that a viewpoint
can create while retaining the feedback and adaptability of
conventional single-step planning. We evaluate RH-NBV on
a UR5e manipulator with an eye-in-hand RGB-D camera
against a gradient-based NBV planner, and random search.
Experiments use a coffee mug and the Stanford bunny under a
controlled six-stage occlusion ladder, as well as a tomato plant
exhibiting natural self-occlusion. The results show that RH-
NBV provides its greatest advantage under strong occlusion,
while myopic NBV remains competitive and substantially
cheaper when the target is more readily visible.
The paper makes several contributions: (i) a formulation
of local target perception under occlusion as a short-horizon
sequential NBV problem, where a viewpoint depends on the
future visibility it enables; (ii) RH-NBV combines seman-
tic target information, occlusion-aware utility, and in-horizon
belief-state propagation; (iii) an evaluation across controlled
and natural occlusion conditions showing that the benefit of
non-myopic planning increases as target visibility becomes
more constrained, (iv) implemented the method on a physical
systems and showed the validation and effectiveness of the
proposed method.
II. RELATED WORK
The next-best-view (NBV) problem was introduced for
automated 3D reconstruction by Connolly [4], who iteratively
selected viewpoints expected to expose the largest amount of
previously unmapped volume. Subsequent approaches have ex-
tended NBV using information gain, visibility, uncertainty, and
geometric reconstruction objectives. A common characteristic
is that candidate viewpoints are evaluated with respect to the
current reconstruction state and the highest-utility viewpoint
is selected for execution. Such formulations are therefore
arXiv:2610.19082v1  [cs.RO]  13 Oct 2026


myopic: they optimize immediate observations without con-
sidering how a viewpoint affects future information, which is
particularly limiting under occlusion.
Receding-horizon approaches address this limitation by
evaluating multiple future viewpoints before executing the next
action. Bircher et al. [6] introduced a receding-horizon NBV
strategy for autonomous exploration of unknown 3D environ-
ments, constructing a tree of collision-free future configura-
tions and selecting branches according to expected unmapped
volume and path cost. Zhang and Zhang [7] similarly applied
receding-horizon planning to active 3D reconstruction with
a manipulator, evaluating candidate sequences using occu-
pancy entropy accumulated along their views. These methods
demonstrate the value of non-myopic view planning, but
primarily address global exploration or reconstruction rather
than information acquisition focused on a semantically defined
target whose visibility evolves throughout the sequence.
Occlusion-aware and target-specific NBV provide comple-
mentary capabilities. Delmerico et al. [8] incorporated vis-
ibility into volumetric information gain by weighting voxel
uncertainty according to ray reachability. For target-centric
reconstruction, restricting the objective to a task-relevant ROI
can prevent sensing effort from being spent on irrelevant scene
geometry [5]. GradientNBV [9] is particularly closely related
to this work. It combines semantic target information with
an occlusion-aware information objective for local perception
of plant nodes, representing occupancy and semantic confi-
dence in a voxel map and using gradient ascent to efficiently
identify informative camera viewpoints. Its differentiable for-
mulation achieved reconstruction performance comparable to
a sampling-based semantic planner while substantially reduc-
ing ray-tracing evaluations. However, GradientNBV remains
single-step: it optimizes the utility of the immediate viewpoint
and does not explicitly account for the future information that
a viewpoint may enable. This distinction is important under
severe occlusion, where successive viewpoint utilities depend
on information acquired earlier.
Together, prior work establishes the key components of
informative active perception.
The remaining gap is their combination for local perception
of a semantically defined target under severe occlusion, where
viewpoint value depends on preceding observations. RH-NBV
addresses this gap by evaluating short viewpoint sequences
with semantic and occlusion-aware utility and predicted belief-
state propagation through the horizon.
III. METHOD
A. Problem Formulation
We consider a 6-DoF manipulator equipped with an eye-
in-hand RGB-D camera operating in a bounded workspace
VT ⊂R3 containing a target object and potentially occluding
structures. The objective is to acquire information about a
target within a predefined region of interest (ROI), VROIT ⊂
VT , while avoiding unnecessary sensing of the surrounding
scene. The ROI location is assumed known, whereas the target
geometry and occupancy are initially unknown. Semantic
information from an instance-segmentation module identifies
the target and distinguishes it from surrounding structures. Let
VC ⊂R3 denote the admissible camera-position space, with
VC ∩VT = ∅. A viewpoint is parameterized by its camera

IV. EXPERIMENTAL SETUP
A. Simulation and Physical Setup
RH-NBV is implemented in ROS 2 Jazzy and evaluated
in both simulation and on a physical UR5e manipulator
equipped with an eye-in-hand Intel RealSense D455 RGB-
D camera. The target ROI is extracted in 3D by combining
HSV-based RGB segmentation with aligned depth data. RH-
NBV evaluates candidate viewpoints using a voxel belief map
of the workspace, while MoveIt 2 executes the selected cam-
era motions. The simulation environment is implemented in
Gazebo, while the physical setup uses the same planning and
perception pipeline with a real camera and robot. Simulation
uses a Stanford Bunny and coffee mug for controlled object-
level occlusion and a tomato plant for natural self-occlusion
as shown in Fig. 4. Physical experiments are performed with
the coffee mug to validate RH-NBV on a real robot and RGB-
D sensor. Fig. 2 summarizes the perception–planning–action
loop, and Fig. 3 shows the physical experimental setup.

D. Evaluation Metrics
We evaluate target information recovery, reconstruction
quality, and motion efficiency using four primary metrics. ROI
coverage is the fraction of target-ROI voxels observed by
the executed viewpoints. F1 reconstruction score quantifies
geometric agreement between the reconstructed target and
reference geometry. Occluded-target recall is the fraction of
target surface points initially hidden from the camera that are
subsequently observed. Trajectory distance is the cumulative
Euclidean distance traveled by the camera. As secondary
metrics, we report motion-success rate, the fraction of com-
manded camera motions successfully executed by MoveIt 2;
and ray-tracing calls, which quantify the computational effort
of viewpoint evaluation.
E. Experimental Protocol
For each target and occlusion configuration, all planners
start from the same initial pose and use the same sensing
budget, target ROI, and semantic target definition. RGB-D
observations are fused into a common reconstruction pipeline
before each subsequent viewpoint is selected. RH-NBV exe-
cutes only the first viewpoint of each selected sequence and
replans from the updated state; the remaining viewpoints are
discarded.
The experiments address three questions: (i) whether multi-
step planning improves target information recovery over my-
opic viewpoint selection, (ii) whether this advantage increases
with occlusion severity, and (iii) whether the improvement
arises from the planning horizon rather than increased com-
putational effort. Controlled occlusion, natural self-occlusion,
and horizon-ablation experiments are used to evaluate these
effects. Default planner parameters are reported in Table I.

E. Real-World Experiment
RH-NBV (K=10, H=3) was evaluated on a physical UR5e
equipped with an eye-in-hand Intel RealSense D455 and
compared with GradientNBV and Random on a coffee mug
under two occlusion configurations. The easy configuration
places panels behind and to the right of the mug, whereas the
hard configuration encloses the target with four panels (Fig. 3).
Each planner was evaluated after 10 executed viewpoints;
results are reported in Table VI.
Under easy occlusion, GradientNBV achieves the highest
ROI coverage (99.24%), whereas RH-NBV obtains the highest
reconstruction F1 score (0.367 vs. 0.180 for GradientNBV and
0.318 for Random). Under hard occlusion, RH-NBV achieves
both the highest ROI coverage (90.24%) and F1 score (0.222),
outperforming GradientNBV (78.64%, 0.040) and Random
(87.96%, 0.018). The same overall trend was observed in
the simulation experiments, where the advantage of RH-NBV
became more pronounced as occlusion increased.

VI. DISCUSSION
Overall, the experiments show that the advantage of RH-
NBV emerges primarily under strong target occlusion. In the
controlled occlusion experiments, RH-NBV achieves substan-
tially higher occluded-target recall in the more challenging
configurations, while its advantage is smaller when the tar-
get is more visible. The tomato-plant experiment shows the
same trend within a single scene: RH-NBV is more effective
gaining information in occluded fruits, whereas GradientNBV
becomes preferable for the largely unoccluded whole-plant
target. The horizon ablation further indicates that this im-
provement comes from multi-step planning rather than simply
evaluating more viewpoints, as H = 3, K = 10 outperforms
the matched-evaluation-budget H = 1, K = 30 case. These
results suggest that RH-NBV is most useful when occlusion
creates sequential dependencies between informative view-
points; when such dependencies are weak, myopic planning
remains a simpler and more computationally efficient choice.
VII. CONCLUSION
This paper presented RH-NBV, a receding-horizon next-
best-view planner for acquiring information about occluded
targets. The key idea is to move beyond myopic viewpoint
selection by evaluating short sequences of future views while
propagating a predicted belief state, thereby capturing the se-
quential dependence between observations. Across controlled
external and natural self-occlusion experiments, RH-NBV pro-
vided its greatest benefit when target visibility was strongly
constrained, improving recovery of otherwise difficult-to-
observe target regions, while myopic NBV remained com-
petitive when the target was more readily visible. Horizon
ablations further showed that this improvement resulted from
multi-step reasoning rather than simply evaluating a larger
number of single-step candidates. Real-robot experiments on a
UR5e with an eye-in-hand RGB-D camera demonstrated that
the approach transfers to physical hardware, with the improved
perception performance accompanied by higher computational
and motion cost. Future work will investigate adaptive plan-
ning horizons that adjust the extent of look-ahead according to
the estimated visibility and uncertainty of the target, with the
goal of retaining the benefits of non-myopic planning while
reducing its computational overhead.
