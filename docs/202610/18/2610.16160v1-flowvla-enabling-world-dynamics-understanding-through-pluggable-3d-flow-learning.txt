https://mmai-laboratory.github.io/flowvla/ Abstract: Recent advances in 3D reconstruction enable the extraction of temporally consistent 3D motion from RGB observations; however, current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models still predominantly rely on 2D image-text priors. Therefore, they do not accurately represent the fundamental dynamics of the 3D world as-sociated with physical actions. This over-reliance on static 2D visual cues limits the capacity of VLA models in achieving robust robot manipulation. To address these limitations, we introduce FlowVLA , a reconstruction-guided, pluggable 3D flow learning framework aimed at empowering VLA models with an inherent understanding of action-relevant dynamics. Instead of requiring explicit 3D sen-sor inputs or altering the inference-time architecture, our method is designed to utilize temporally aligned 3D flow only during training. Our method allows the model to learn the dynamic evolution of the physical scene while preserving the computational efficiency of conventional 2D VLA backbones. To bridge scene-level dynamics with robotic manipulation, we propose a modular 3D flow learning framework defined by a dual-level flow supervision design. Specifically, point-level flow guides the model to forecast the future displacements of task-critical 3D points, while TCP-level flow aggregates these motions to align with the robot’s Tool-Center-Point (TCP) trajectories. This dual-level flow supervision encourages the model to focus on semantically meaningful dynamic regions, effectively cou-pling scene-level motion with precise control signals. Extensive evaluations across simulation and real-world experiments demonstrate that FlowVLA consistently improves manipulation performance, zero-shot robustness, and optimizes data efficiency. FlowVLA adds only lightweight auxiliary heads during training and dis-cards them at inference. Therefore, FlowVLA introduces no additional 3D sensor inputs, architectural modifications, or inference-time computation. 

Keywords: Motion-relevant 3D Dynamics, 3D Scene Understanding, Training-only Supervision 

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

Reliable robotic manipulation should consider interaction within a dynamic, three-dimensional physical world. This dynamic interaction is addressed through the emergence of VLA models as a strong paradigm, transferring the rich semantic knowledge of pre-trained LLMs and VLMs to physical robot controls [1, 2, 3, 4, 5]. By integrating large-scale multimodal backbones with an action decoder, recent VLAs have achieved remarkable performance across diverse robotic tasks, including complex scene reasoning [6, 7], cross-embodiment generalization [8, 4, 9], and language-instructed manipulation [10, 3, 11]. Despite their impressive capabilities, most existing VLA models remain constrained by their de-pendence on multimodal representations derived from 2D image-text pretraining. Although these 2D representations provide rich semantic priors, they are not inherently designed to capture action-

Consequently, action policies often overfit to static visual cues—such as backgrounds, static camera viewpoints, or absolute global geometry—rather than prioritizing action-relevant 3D interactions and dynamics. To mitigate these limitations, recent approaches [12, 13, 14, 15] have explored 3D-aware VLAs that integrate explicit 3D signals, including depth maps or point clouds. Although these approaches improve physical manipulation by leveraging spatial understanding, they also present new challenges. In their ap-proaches, auxiliary 3D sensors are required, and costly retraining on paired 3D-action datasets is demanded. More crucially, these approaches emphasize the extraction of static 3D geometry from single observations; as a result, they still overlook the dynamic evolution of the scene that is directly tied to physical manipulation. To address these challenges, we propose FlowVLA , a reconstruction-guided, pluggable 3D flow learning framework that injects action-relevant 3D motion cues into VLA representations. Our core insight is to leverage 3D flow exclusively as a privileged signal during training to inject manipulation-relevant 3D dynamics directly into the model’s latent representations. Instead of providing static snapshots, 3D flow provides direct supervision on how the physical environment moves and evolves under robot actions. Therefore, we employ 3D flow not as an input modality, but as a training-only module, while preserving the architecture of VLAs during inference. To achieve this, we derive temporally aligned 3D point trajectories from consecutive frames [16]. By computing the positional differences between adjacent frames, we formulate 3D flow that highlights regions of real physical movement within the scene. To effectively bring this spatial motion into the learning process, we establish a dual-level scene-to-control 3D flow learning framework comprising scene-level and control-level modules. The scene-level module captures dense 3D scene dynamics by supervising the future displacements of motion-salient 3D points, while the control-level module distills these dense motions into TCP-aligned flow that reflects the robot end-effector trajectory. By jointly optimizing these two modules, our framework bridges holistic environmental changes in 3D space with control-relevant robot motion. We comprehensively validate FlowVLA through extensive evaluations on standard benchmarks, such as LIBERO and LIBERO-Plus, and real-world robot experiments. Empirical results demonstrate that our 3D sensor-free framework improves diverse VLA models with zero inference overhead. By improving task success rates in both simulated and real-world environments, the proposed approach highlights the potential of FlowVLA as a practical framework for 3D-aware policy learning. 

## 2 RELATED WORK 

2.1 Vision-Language-Action Models 

VLAs integrate visual observations, proprioceptive states, and language instructions to generate robot actions. The viability of language-model-based sequence modeling for robot control was established by early studies [17, 18, 19], leading to RT-1 [1]. RT-2 [2] further showed that the semantic knowledge acquired from internet-scale pretraining can transfer effectively to robot manipulation. Recent advancements have concentrated on scaling, generalization, and expressive action generation. Octo [11] and OpenVLA [10] utilize large-scale robot datasets [8, 20] to improve adaptation to novel environments. Other works extend VLA models with continuous action generation via flow matching or diffusion [3, 4, 21], internal reasoning mechanisms [6, 7], and world modeling [22, 23]. Even with these advances, the current VLA paradigm still relies on representations inherited from 2D image-text pretraining. This makes existing models highly effective at semantic grounding, but leaves action-relevant 3D dynamics insufficiently modeled [15, 24, 25]. 

2.2 3D-Aware VLA Models 

To improve spatial grounding in VLA models, recent works have incorporated explicit 3D information into their pipelines. Models such as LLaVA-3D [26], SpatialVLA [12], and DM0 [27] exploit 3D representations to enhance spatial understanding, whereas 3DVLA [15] injects 3D features into the 2VLM embedding space for improved reasoning capabilities. It is demonstrated by these methods that useful physical grounding for robot manipulation can be provided by 3D structures. However, incorporating 3D information into 2D-pretrained backbones poses significant challenges. Dedicated alignment mechanisms are required due to the domain gap between pixel-based and geometric representations [14, 28, 13, 29]. Furthermore, high-quality 3D-action paired datasets are remarkably scarce compared to large-scale 2D corpora, making the computational cost of retraining prohibitively expensive [15, 30]. Several recent methods therefore explore more efficient ways to integrate 3D information. PointVLA [14] introduces lightweight modules for injecting 3D information without retraining the entire backbone, while GeoVLA [31] employs a parallel 3D embedding network to preserve multimodal alignment. BridgeVLA [13] instead projects 3D point clouds into multiple 2D views so that they can be processed within a unified representation space. Despite their effectiveness, these approaches typically rely on 3D sensor-derived inputs or 3D representations, and primarily emphasize static 3D geometry while providing limited supervision on how motion-salient regions physically evolve under robot actions. In contrast, FlowVLA uses reconstruction-derived 3D flow as a training-only privileged signal, encouraging existing 2D VLA policies to encode action-relevant 3D motion cues without the need for auxiliary 3D sensors or additional inference-time computation. 

## 3 METHOD 

As shown in Fig. 1, we present FlowVLA , a reconstruction-guided 3D flow learning framework that injects action-relevant 3D dynamics into VLA models through training-only supervision. Temporally aligned 3D point trajectories are derived from RGB sequences by our FlowVLA using a feed-forward dynamic reconstruction model [16]. Then, it converts 3D point trajectories into future 3D flow targets. These targets supervise the VLA with a dual-level scene-to-control objective: the action latent is encouraged by point-level flow to encode dense 3D scene motion, while motion cues are distilled into a control-oriented representation aligned with end-effector displacement by TCP-level flow.

## 4 SIMULATION EXPERIMENTS 

Settings. The proposed 3D flow supervision is evaluated across heterogeneous policy designs to confirm the generalization ability of FlowVLA. We instantiate FlowVLA on two representative VLAs with substantially different action-generation policies: Seer [22] and π0.5 [4]. Seer adopts a two-layer MLP-based policy that reconstructs future observations and predicts actions, whereas π0.5 employs a PaliGemma-based [32] VLM backbone with a flow-matching action policy. For π0.5, we follow the official fine-tuning recipe and perform LoRA adaptation [33]. For both backbones, we attach 3D flow objectives only during training while preserving their model architectures and inference pathways. Further details on the simulation settings and datasets are provided in Appendix A. 

4.1 Simulation Results LIBERO. Tab. 1 summarizes the results on the LIBERO benchmark. Our scene-to-control 3D flow supervision yields consistent improvements on recent VLA models, with particularly large gains in long-horizon manipulation. It is notable that FlowVLA achieves particularly large gains on LIBERO-Long , which requires sustained spatial-temporal reasoning over extended horizons. ## 6 CONCLUSION 

We present FlowVLA , a reconstruction-guided 3D flow learning framework that injects action-relevant 3D dynamics into existing VLA models through training-only supervision. By deriving 8temporally aligned 3D point trajectories from RGB observations into future 3D flow targets, FlowVLA couples scene-level point motion with control-relevant TCP displacement through dual-level flow supervision. Experiments on LIBERO, LIBERO-Plus, and real-world tasks show consistent improve-ments in performance and robustness across different VLA backbones. FlowVLA notably operates without auxiliary 3D sensors, architectural modifications, or inference-time computation, as all 3D flow modules are used only during training. These results suggest that proposed 3D flow supervision serves as an effective approach to impart action-relevant 3D dynamics to 2D VLA models. 

Limitations. FlowVLA requires an offline reconstruction step to generate 3D point trajectories for training. Although this does not affect inference-time efficiency, the quality of the auxiliary supervision depends on the reconstruction model. In addition, while the visualization in Appendix C.2 suggests that magnitude-based point selection tends to capture dynamically active regions, it does not explicitly guarantee task relevance. This suggests future extensions with semantic or contact-aware point selection.

A.1 Datasets LIBERO. LIBERO [35] is a standardized benchmark for robot manipulation. It consists of 4 task suites—LIBERO-Spatial, LIBERO-Object, LIBERO-Goal, and LIBERO-Long—where each suite contains 10 tasks and 50 expert demonstrations per task. These suites are designed to evaluate complementary aspects of manipulation capability, including spatial reasoning, generalization to unseen object instances, language-conditioned manipulation, and long-horizon planning. 

LIBERO-Plus. LIBERO-Plus [36] extends LIBERO to provide a more fine-grained evaluation of robustness under distribution shifts. It introduces 7 perturbation dimensions and contains 10,030 test episodes organized across 5 difficulty levels. These perturbations include changes in camera viewpoint, robot initialization, language instructions, lighting, background, layout, and sensor noise, thereby enabling a detailed analysis of zero-shot robustness beyond standard in-distribution evaluation. Sensitivity to Noisy 3D Flow Supervision. To evalu-ate the robustness of FlowVLA to imperfect reconstruc-tion targets, we conduct a noise sensitivity analysis on LIBERO-Long. Offline Reconstruction Cost. For Seer [22] experiments on LIBERO-Long, VDPM [16] pre-processing takes approximately 4 wall-clock hours to generate 3D flow supervision, compared to approximately 13 wall-clock hours for policy training excluding this preprocessing. This offline cost is about 30% of the policy training time, is incurred only once before training, and the gener-ated supervision targets are cached for subsequent experiments. Since the 3D flow supervision is training-only, it does not introduce any additional inference-time computation.
