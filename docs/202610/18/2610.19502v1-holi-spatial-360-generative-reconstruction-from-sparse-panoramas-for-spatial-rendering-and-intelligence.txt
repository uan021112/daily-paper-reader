ABSTRACT
Spatial intelligence requires scalable 3D environments that are both photoreal-
istic and semantically grounded, yet existing indoor datasets are dominated by
room-scale captures and costly LiDAR acquisition. Online real-estate platforms
offer abundant panoramas of diverse multi-room homes, but sparse viewpoints
and texture-poor regions make reconstruction severely under-constrained. We in-
troduce Holi-Spatial 360, a generative reconstruction and data curation framework
for converting these panoramas into annotated multi-room 3D environments. Be-
cause reconstruction quality bounds subsequent generation, our Correspondence-
Guided Geometric Optimization converts feed-forward reconstruction priors into
reliable cross-view constraints and builds a coherent 3D Gaussian Splatting repre-
sentation as 3D spatial memory. Adapted single-image and video priors then re-
store appearance and complete continuous trajectories while preserving observed
content. The reconstructed scenes support arbitrary RGB-D rendering and an au-

INTRODUCTION
Spatial intelligence aims to equip large models (Bai et al., 2025a; Team et al., 2023; Bai et al.,
2025b) with the ability to perceive, ground, reason, and act in the 3D world. Its progress depends
on scalable 3D environments that are both photorealistic and semantically grounded. Multi-room
homes are particularly important for embodied applications, where understanding must extend be-
yond individual objects to layouts and relationships across interconnected spaces.
However, existing indoor datasets remain difficult to scale. ScanNet (Dai et al., 2017) and Scan-
Net++ (Yeshwanth et al., 2023) are dominated by room-scale captures, while building-scale datasets
such as Matterport3D (Chang et al., 2017) and HM3D (Ramakrishnan et al., 2021) rely on dedicated
capture and processing. SAGE-3D (Miao et al., 2026) provides semantically annotated 3DGS envi-
ronments but builds upon designed 3D assets and expert annotation. Holi-Spatial (Gao et al., 2026)
automates spatial data curation from videos, yet relies on densely captured trajectories with contin-
uous camera motion and sufficient view overlap. Diverse, real-world multi-room environments with
both high-quality reconstruction and semantic supervision therefore remain costly to obtain.
Online real-estate platforms offer a largely untapped alternative: abundant 360-degree panoramas
of diverse multi-room homes. Yet these panoramas are captured at only a few widely separated
locations. Limited overlap, texture-poor surfaces, and extensive unobserved regions make cross-
view matching and geometric reconstruction unreliable (Ren et al., 2025; Jin et al., 2026). Recent
image- and video-based generative methods can repair artifacts in novel views (Wu et al., 2025a;
Yin et al., 2025; De Lutio et al., 2026; Wang et al., 2026), but they do not by themselves establish re-
liable geometry from sparse, multi-room panoramas. When the initial reconstruction is misaligned,
generated views may drift from the observed scene and reinforce errors when distilled back into 3D.
To address these challenges, we introduce Holi-Spatial 360, a generative reconstruction and data
curation framework that transforms sparse panoramas into semantically annotated multi-room
3D environments. Our key insight is that reconstruction quality sets the ceiling for generation.
Correspondence-Guided Geometric Optimization uses priors from feed-forward large recon-
struction models (Wang et al., 2025; Lin et al., 2025) to establish cross-view constraints and optimize
a coherent initial 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) representation. This representation serves as per-
sistent spatial memory for generative trajectory densification: image and video priors restore and
complete novel views, which are distilled back into 3D while remaining grounded in the observed
panoramas. The scenes support RGB-D rendering and automated spatial data curation, includ-
ing verified 3D instances, grounding annotations, and spatial question-answer pairs. The pipeline
requires only RGB panoramas, without depth capture or human-in-the-loop labeling.
From publicly available real-estate and rental panoramas, we construct Holi360: over 1K multi-
room 3DGS scenes, dense RGB-D renderings and meshes, more than 100K verified 3D instances,
and 2M spatial question-answer pairs. Reconstruction and annotation use only RGB panoramas;
RealSee3D (Li et al., 2026) LiDAR and semantic maps are reserved for evaluation. Experiments
show improved rendering, geometry, and grounding, while VLM fine-tuning on Holi360 boosts
spatial reasoning.

2.2
GENERATIVE RECONSTRUCTION FROM SPARSE VIEWS
Generative priors have become a powerful remedy for sparse-view reconstruction.
Recon-
Fusion (Wu et al., 2023) regularizes NeRF optimization with image diffusion, while 3DGS-
Enhancer (Liu et al., 2024), Difix3D+ (Wu et al., 2025a), and GenFusion (Wu et al., 2025b) distill
restored novel views into 3D representations. Video-based methods further improve temporal con-
sistency through scene- or reference-grounded guidance (Zhong et al., 2025; Yin et al., 2025), long
autoregressive generation (De Lutio et al., 2026), or large-scale restoration training (Wang et al.,
2026). Although these methods refine the resulting 3D representation, their main advances center
on the generator, leaving the initial reconstruction largely unchanged. Beyond static scenes, Neo-
Verse (Yang et al., 2026) and One4D (Mi et al., 2025) extend coupled generation and reconstruction
to dynamic 4D scenes from monocular videos or sparse frames. PanoWorld (Jia et al., 2026) sim-
ilarly maintains a dynamic 3DGS cache, but relies on single-image diffusion to update it, making
independently generated views susceptible to cross-view inconsistencies similar to those observed
in DiFix. In contrast, we globally optimize a unified 3DGS from observed panoramas and use gen-
eration to densify missing evidence while preserving scene identity along continuous trajectories.

indoor surfaces. LRMs provide substantially denser matches, although the number of forward–

3.4
AUTOMATED SPATIAL DATA CURATION
Given the densely rendered RGB-D trajectories, we automatically transform each reconstructed en-
vironment into spatially grounded training data. We first sample representative views throughout
the scene and prompt Qwen3.5-VL-27B to identify visible, spatially groundable object categories.
Its predictions are canonicalized and merged into a scene-specific label vocabulary, avoiding the
limited coverage of a predefined indoor taxonomy. Conditioned on this scene-specific vocabulary,
Boxer (DeTone et al., 2026) processes posed RGB-D observations to propose globally aligned 3D
object instances and bounding boxes.
We subsequently apply geometric and semantic verification to remove unreliable predictions. Geo-
metric filtering rejects boxes with insufficient multi-view support, implausible dimensions, or incon-
sistent projections, while a second VLM pass examines their multi-view crops to eliminate incorrect
categories and duplicate instances. The remaining annotations form a verified instance set contain-
ing canonical labels, captions, and world-coordinate 3D bounding boxes.
Finally, following the question-answer templates of Holi-Spatial (Gao et al., 2026), we combine ap-
pearance descriptions generated by the VLM from multi-view object crops with geometric attributes
and spatial relations computed from the verified 3D bounding boxes. These complementary anno-
tations are used to instantiate large-scale 3D grounding samples and spatial question-answer pairs.
This automated label–ground–verify pipeline scales semantic annotation to new environments with-
out human-in-the-loop labeling.

CONCLUSION
In this paper, we presented Holi-Spatial 360, an automated pipeline that turns sparse panora-
mas of multi-room homes into high-fidelity 3D environments and holistic spatial annotations.
Correspondence-guided 3DGS reconstruction provides a coherent geometric foundation, while
adapted image and video generative priors restore appearance and complete continuous views with-
out losing the observed scene content. The resulting Holi360 dataset contains over 1K scenes,
100K instance annotations, and 2M spatial question-answer pairs. Experiments show improved re-
construction and annotation quality under sparse panoramic inputs, and demonstrate that Holi360
benefits VLM spatial reasoning.

IMPLEMENTATION DETAILS
Data collection and splits.
We ensure that no scene overlaps between the training data and any
of the evaluation sets, including RealSee. We collect approximately 1,000 real-world multi-room
scenes from publicly available online real-estate and rental platforms across multiple geographic
regions. From this collection, we reserve 30 scenes exclusively for testing and exclude them from
training. The test scenes are divided into three batches, denoted as Batch 1, Batch 2, and Batch 3,
with 10 scenes in each batch. Since these panoramas are captured under real-world conditions, dif-
ferent views may exhibit exposure and illumination inconsistencies. Consequently, a reconstruction
may recover the correct scene structure while showing a slight color discrepancy from the reference
image, resulting in relatively low PSNR values across all methods.
RealSee evaluation.
We additionally evaluate on RealSee because, to the best of our knowledge,
it is the only publicly available dataset of real-world multi-room indoor scenes that provides both Li-
DAR measurements and semantic annotations. Nevertheless, RealSee has several limitations. First,
its LiDAR observations are relatively sparse and therefore capture only part of the scene geometry.
Second, its semantic annotations do not follow a fully unified category taxonomy. For example,
labels such as wall and background wall, door and swing door may refer to closely related
semantic categories. We lift the 2D semantic annotations into 3D using the available depth observa-

ABLATION VISUALIZATION
In Fig 11, We qualitatively visualize and quantitatively evaluate how the designs of our reconstruc-
tion affect the overall pipeline quality. It‘s clear that after the LRM corresononded-guided Gaussian
depth can under stable geometry in the sparse view setting, if without it, even under the same initial-

correspondences and weakening multi-view constraints. Consequently, the model overfits the RGB
observations and produces more floaters. In detail, as shown in the w/o correspondence-guided
case, removing the matching constraint can produce clearly incorrect correspondences even when
the reprojection constraint is satisfied. Existing multi-view optimization typically estimates corre-
spondence errors directly between pairs of frames. Once an erroneous match is introduced, it may be
repeatedly reinforced during optimization, especially under sparse-view settings where only a few
nearby observations are available. For example, the blue point on the panel adjacent to the wall is
matched to an incorrect location in the other view, although the resulting correspondence still yields
a low reprojection error. This ambiguity motivates our correspondence-guided design, which pro-
vides additional constraints to suppress geometrically plausible but semantically incorrect matches.

object and occasional misclassifications. Our post-processing pipeline is designed to address these
issues, further improving its accuracy and robustness.
