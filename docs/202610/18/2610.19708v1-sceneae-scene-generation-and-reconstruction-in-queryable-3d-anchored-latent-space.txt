# ABSTRACT 

Recent video world models have increasingly adopted 3D memory mechanisms to integrate historical observations into a lightweight and persistent scene repre-sentation. This naturally raises a complementary question: if 3D representations can serve as an effective mechanism for memorizing observed scenes, can they also be directly used as the latent space for reconstructing and generating scenes? Motivated by this idea, we represent a scene as a compact and unstructured 3D-anchored latent space ( SceneAE ). Each latent combines an explicit 3D position with an implicit content feature and is spatially grounded to describe its local scene region, forming a sparse latent point cloud. Unlike conventional video-VAE latents that describe how a scene appears from particular viewpoints, our repre-sentation directly describes the scene itself, making it naturally applicable to both reconstruction and generation. For reconstruction, once the scene has been gener-ated, our persistent 3D representation can be projected and decoded into RGB-D observations by different target cameras, without the need to regenerate a video for each new camera trajectory. For generation, we leverage the geometry-content separation to first generate camera-controlled 3D geometry and then synthesize content conditioned on it, reducing camera-appearance entanglement and improv-ing camera controllability. Experiments show that this 3D latent achieves superior generation quality, computational cost, and camera controllability.

# 1 INTRODUCTION 

Recent video generation models (Wan et al., 2025; Hong et al., 2022; Yang et al., 2025; Zheng et al., 2024) encode videos into dense spatiotemporal latent grids and learn their distributions with dif-fusion transformers. Building on this paradigm, camera-controllable world models generate future observations from an initial view and a prescribed camera trajectory, often predicting RGB together with depth to improve spatial understanding. For example, Gen3R (Huang et al., 2026) jointly mod-els the appearance latents of Wan VAE and the geometric features of VGGT (Wang et al., 2025), while GLD (Jang et al., 2026) performs diffusion directly in the feature space of a geometry foun-dation model (Lin et al., 2025). These approaches combine scalable video- generation priors with explicit geometric information, producing compelling and geometry-aware RGB-D sequences. Despite architectural differences, these methods use viewpoint-aligned latent grids that describe individual observations rather than the scene itself. Their outputs are therefore trajectory-specific videos that cannot be directly queried from novel viewpoints as shown in Figure 1(a). Overlapping views also introduce latent redundancy, while appearance- centric representations make geomet-ric consistency harder to learn. SceneTok (Asim et al., 2026) compresses the scene into compact unstructured 1D tokens and supports multi-view decoding, thereby alleviating the fixed-viewpoint limitation. However, its tokens lack explicit spatial localization, and the model must learn the scene region corresponding to each token through dense token-pixel interaction, which increases optimiza-tion difficulty as well as encoding and decoding costs. Our core idea is to anchor latent features at explicit 3D positions, guiding them to learn the corresponding local 3D regions and thereby forming a sparse latent point cloud, which is shown in Figure 1(b). Unlike view-aligned dense grids, this rep-resentation describes the scene directly in a shared 3D coordinate system, so that the same physical region needs to be encoded only once and can be reused across different viewpoints, which avoids the redundancy of repeated encoding while effectively ensuring geometric consistency across view-points. Furthermore, due to the properties of the 3D representation, for any target camera within the field of view , the latents can be projected onto its image plane and decoded into per-pixel RGBD information through a deep learnable decoder, which allows our representation to directly perform the scene reconstruction task and thereby support fast world roaming. Furthermore, existing camera-controllable generation methods typically inject camera features di-rectly into view-aligned appearance latents. Since camera conditions and appearance features de-scribe different factors, this implicit conditioning requires the model to infer geometric transforma-tions, occlusion relationships, and the emergence of newly revealed regions while simultaneously generating texture, which easily leads to feature entanglement and makes camera control difficult. 3D memory methods such as GEN3C (Ren et al., 2025), Voyager (Huang et al., 2025), and Mi-rage (Wang et al., 2026) have shown that more direct geometric control can be achieved when camera control acts directly on explicit scene geometry (e.g., a point cloud memory) rather than on appearance latents. However, the 3D memory in these methods is mainly used to store and query scene content that has already been observed. Inspired by this, we further extend camera con-trol over explicit geometry to future scene generation, and propose a two-stage Geometry-Content Decoupled DiT. Specifically, the future 3D anchor layout is first generated according to the target camera trajectory, and then the predicted point positions are used to provide spatial relationships through 3D RoPE to guide the generation of the corresponding latent content. This establishes a direct control path from camera trajectory to scene geometry and then to scene content, reducing camera-appearance entanglement and achieving more accurate trajectory following. Extensive experiments on multiple real-world datasets demonstrate that our method achieves supe-rior scene reconstruction and generation quality with a substantially more compact latent space and lower computational cost. It consistently reconstructs geometrically coherent RGB-D observations across query viewpoints and more faithfully follows target camera trajectories. 

# 2 RELATED WORK 

Scene Latent Space. Conventional image and video VAEs (Wan et al., 2025; Yang et al., 2025) compress appearance into dense latent grids, but their limited geometric awareness can cause cross-view inconsistency. GLD (Jang et al., 2026) introduces stronger geometry by generating dense foundation-model features, at the cost of a high-dimensional latent space. Both remain viewpoint-2SceneAE: Scene Generation and Reconstruction in Queryable 3D-Anchored Latent Space aligned and redundantly encode overlapping regions. SceneTok (Asim et al., 2026) uses compact, unstructured tokens for query-view decoding, but their lack of spatial grounding requires token-pixel interactions to learn token-region correspondence, increasing optimization difficulty and cost. 

Camera-Controllable Scene Generation. Recent video diffusion models (Zhang et al., 2025; Wu et al., 2026) have been extended to camera-controllable scene generation by incorporating camera poses, ray embeddings, or geometric features into the generation process. Aether (Zhu et al., 2025) jointly models and generates image latents, depth latents, and camera pose latents. PRoPE (Li et al., 2025) encodes relative projective relationships directly in attention. GLD (Jang et al., 2026) per-forms diffusion in the feature space of a geometry foundation model. Other methods maintain a persistent 3D memory: GEN3C (Ren et al., 2025) renders an explicit point-cloud cache as condi-tioning, and Mirage (Wang et al., 2026) stores and queries historical video latents in 3D space. 

3D Scene Reconstruction. Neural scene representations, such as NeRF (Mildenhall et al., 2021; Chen et al., 2022; Johari et al., 2022) and 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2024), encode scenes in 3D and support novel-view rendering, but typically re-quire per-scene optimization and are not designed as latent spaces for generative modeling. There-fore, some feed-forward reconstruction models (Lin et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2026) efficiently and generally recover scene geometry from visual observations by predicting pixel-aligned 3D Gaus-sians. In addition, some methods (Jiang et al., 2025; Asim et al., 2026; Jin et al., 2025) reconstruct observations from arbitrary viewpoints by constructing implicit representations of the scene.

We first control the genera-tion of the future geometric layout via the camera, and then control the generation of texture content via the geometric layout, thereby achieving more accurate camera control. 3.2 GEOMETRY -C ONTENT DECOUPLED DITGiven the 3D latent state Rref = {Pref , Z ref } encoded from the reference frame and a target camera trajectory Ttarget , our goal is to generate the 3D positions ( Ptarget ) and content features (Ztarget ) of the target scene region. Existing methods commonly inject camera poses or rays into viewpoint-aligned video latents through feature addition, positional encoding, or attention. In these formulations, the camera trajectory remains an implicit conditioning signal, and the model must learn to translate it into changes in scene geometry, visibility, and spatial coverage. Because these geometric effects are expressed indirectly through appearance-oriented latents, accurate camera fol-lowing remains challenging. In contrast, our 3D latent representation provides an explicit geometric variable on which camera control can directly operate. We therefore first generate the spatial struc-ture according to the target trajectory and subsequently generate content features upon this geometry, the overall framework is shown in Figure 3. This establishes a structured control path from camera trajectory to scene geometry and then to scene content, enabling more accurate camera control. 

Camera-Controlled Point Diffusion. We build a lightweight point diffusion model that generates future 3D positions from the reference point cloud and target camera poses. We first express all cameras relative to the reference camera and construct a sparse ray map for each target view. Each ray token contains its Pl¨ ucker coordinates, image-plane location, trajectory index, and the depth information projected from the observed scene when available. We then add noise to the input positions. To provide scene-aware information, we train using the flow path from the first-frame to the target point cloud. At timestep t, the noisy input is: 

ˆP t = (1 − t) · Ptarget + t · Pref (7) The input noise is fed into a lightweight Point DiT for prediction. The Point DiT consists of self-attention on the input, cross-attention with the point cloud of the reference frame, and cross-attention with the ray token of the corresponding target camera after projection onto the target plane. As a result, the camera trajectory can directly constrain the spatial structure of the generated latents. Finally, the model predicts the clean target layout ˆPtarget with an MSE loss.

# 4 EXPERIMENTS 

4.1 TRAINING DETAILS 

Datasets. We train SceneLatent from scratch on DL3DV (Ling et al., 2024), RealEstate10K (Zhou et al., 2018)(RE10K), and ScanNet (Dai et al., 2017). For a fair comparison, we construct each sam-ple from nine views (matching the 4N+1 frame requirement of Wan, Gen3R, etc., while approaching the 8-frame design used by GLD), and then train the scene generation task under both the 1-view setting with a given first frame and the 2-view setting with given first and last frames. 

Training Details. We use DepthAnything V3-Base as the backbone to extract feature maps and depth for the input videos. The image resolution is set to 518×376, and the number N of 3D latents is set to 3000. The training optimizer hyperparameters follow GLD, using AdamW with a learning rate of 5e-5 and a batch size of 40. SceneLatent is trained for 100k iterations on 8 A800 GPUs. 4.4 ABLATION STUDY 

Components of the Latent Encoder-Decoder. Table 8 ablates three components of the latent encoder-decoder on RE10K. Here, Jitter denotes perturbing the latent coordinates during the de-coder stage. Low-Res denotes the decoding paradigm that constructs a low-resolution dense fea-ture map via splat-based initialization and then performs feature refinement through a subsequent deep decoder. Rendering at the original resolution as in 3D Gaussian splatting performs worse, because our latents are much sparser than pixel-aligned Gaussians. Splat-CA denotes the splat cross-attention in the decoder: unlike SceneTok, where each pixel interacts with all latents, our design lets each pixel interact only with the nearest few latents splatted onto the plane. 

Different DiT Design. Table 9 evaluates alternative DiT designs on RE10K. In (a), Joint generates positions and content jointly, Content-Only generates content and regresses positions afterward, and Decoupled first generates geometry and then conditions content generation on it. The decoupled design performs best, showing that explicit geometric priors facilitate content synthesis. In (b), injecting the camera condition only into geometry generation outperforms injecting it into both geometry and content, confirming that camera-appearance entanglement harms generation quality. 

# 5 CONCLUSION 

We presented SceneAE, a sparse and scene-centric 3D latent representation for both scene recon-struction and generation. Unlike view-aligned dense grid latents, each latent couples an explicit 3D anchor position with an implicit content feature, so that every physical region is encoded only once and shared across viewpoints. A splat-based decoder renders this compact representation into 9SceneAE: Scene Generation and Reconstruction in Queryable 3D-Anchored Latent Space RGB-D observations from arbitrary cameras without regenerating a video for each trajectory. Our Geometry-Content Decoupled DiT further generates the 3D anchor layout from the camera trajec-tory before synthesizing content, establishing an explicit control path from camera to geometry to content. Experiments show that SceneAE achieves strong generation quality, superior geometric consistency and camera controllability, at about half the computational cost of dense latents. We hope this work encourages the use of 3D representations as the latent space of video world models.

Query Initialization. We use a frozen DA3-Base backbone and extract 768-dimensional feature maps from all (L=12) transformer layers. For each latent pi, we project it into every input view and retain projections that lie in front of the camera and inside the image boundary. To reduce feature contamination caused by occlusion or inaccurate correspondence, we compare the projected anchor depth dproj iv with the DA3 depth sampled at the same image location. We select the view with the smallest depth discrepancy and sample its features at the anchor projec-tion. The features are layer-averaged and added to a Fourier coordinate encoding (Eq. (1)). 

Dual-path Deformable Cross Attention. Since we need to decode RGBD observations from the 3D latent representation, this involves a trade-off in aggregating multi-view features. Specifically, multi-view aggregation requires balancing feature reliability and scene coverage. Using only the views with the highest depth consistency yields accurately aligned local texture appearance, but may lack complementary geometry information visible from other views. Conversely, aggregating a large number of views can expand spatial coverage and enhance geometric understanding, but may also introduce conflicting texture features from occluded regions or incorrect correspondences. We therefore design two complementary deformable cross-attention paths. The Main path reads only the views with the highest depth consistency, prioritizing reliable local texture evidence, while the Multi-View path introduces more visible views layer by layer, supplementing geometric context.

The Content Diffusion takes the 3D layout generated by Point Diffusion as its basis, generates the content features of each latent, and corrects local position errors. This stage no longer receives the target camera trajectory, since camera control has already been converted into explicit 3D geometry in the first stage. Therefore, the second stage mainly addresses geometry-guided content generation. The model adopts a Wan-style Transformer with 22 layers and a hidden dimension of 1536. Each layer sequentially performs self-attention, reference-state cross-attention, and a feed-forward net-work. The model employs continuous 3D RoPE in all attention layers: the target self-attention uses the current geometric positions, while the cross-attention uses the target positions and reference positions, respectively, making content interaction explicitly depend on 3D spatial relationships. For the content features, we add noise according to Eq. (9) and use Eq. (10) as the supervision loss. Although Point Diffusion can generate future 3D positions ˆPtarget , its generation relies only on reference-frame information and future camera poses, which may lead to erroneous predictions. Since clean content helps correct positions, we construct a correction path from ˆPtarget and Ptarget :

ept = (1 − tpos ) · Ptarget + tpos · ˆPtarget (21) and then predict Ptarget − ept based on the content features and positions. Note that the content time step t and the geometry time step tpos are sampled independently. As a result, the model may observe combinations such as ”content highly noisy but geometry accurate” or ”content nearly clean but geometry still erroneous.” This prevents content generation from implicitly judging geometric quality based on content noise, and forces it to read the current 3D positions. At inference time, the geometry is initialized with the positions from ˆP target , and the content is ini-tialized from Gaussian noise. The model first performs content denoising, and geometry correction is carried out synchronously during the last 10 content sampling steps. This is to ensure that position corrections are predicted only when the content is relatively accurate. Furthermore, we additionally predict a confidence score to determine whether the geometric position of the current point requires strong correction. In this way, latent positions that are already relatively accurate are preserved as much as possible, while low-confidence latents receive stronger position corrections. The updated positions continue to be used for the 3D RoPE in the next step, allowing the progressively generated content features to in turn guide local geometry optimization.

# C DISCUSSION 

C.1 DIFFERENCE WITH FEED -F OWARD 3D G AUSSIAN SPLATTING 

Feed-forward 3D Gaussian methods and our approach both construct explicit 3D scene represen-tations without per-scene optimization, but they differ substantially in how scene information is organized and used. Many feed-forward 3DGS methods predict one or multiple Gaussian primi-tives from each input pixel or image patch. Although these primitives are subsequently transformed into a shared 3D coordinate system, their prediction remains aligned with the source observations. Consequently, the same physical surface observed from multiple viewpoints may be represented by several overlapping Gaussians. This redundancy is beneficial for preserving view-specific details, but it also causes the representation size to grow with the number and resolution of input views. Such pixel-aligned representations are well suited to reconstruction. Each Gaussian explicitly stores the attributes required for rendering, including position, spatial extent, opacity, and appearance. Once the Gaussians have been predicted, novel views can be rendered efficiently through Gaussian rasterization. The dense correspondence between image pixels and Gaussian primitives also helps retain fine textures and thin structures, allowing fast and high-fidelity reconstruction. However, the same design is less convenient as a latent space for generative scene modeling. First, redundant primitives require a generative model to repeatedly model the same physical region across different view-aligned Gaussian predictions. Second, the number of primitives is large, increasing the computational cost of diffusion or autoregressive generation. Third, each primitive jointly carries geometry, scale, opacity, and appearance attributes. Generating this high-dimensional and coupled parameter space requires the model to resolve scene layout, visibility, surface extent, and texture. In contrast, we construct a compact, scene-level 3D representation specifically for generative model-ing. By representing shared scene regions only once, our latent space reduces both the representation size and the burden of generative learning. Moreover, the separation between explicit geometry and latent content allows camera-controllable generation to be decomposed into geometry generation followed by content generation, providing a more direct control path. The trade-off is that our representation relies on a learned decoder and therefore does not match the rendering speed or fine-detail fidelity of rasterized 3D Gaussians. Overall, feed-forward 3DGS favors efficient, high-fidelity reconstruction, whereas our representation favors compact and controllable scene generation. C.2 LIMITATIONS

Dense video-VAE latents preserve spatially aligned appearance informa-tion at every view, whereas our method compresses the scene into a limited number of sparse 3D latents and reconstructs each view through a learned decoder. Observations of a dynamic object at different times may correspond to different spatial locations, making direct multi-view aggrega-tion ambiguous and potentially leading to duplicated geometry or inconsistent appearance. More-over, the generative model predicts spatial scene completion without explicitly modeling object motion or temporal evolution.
