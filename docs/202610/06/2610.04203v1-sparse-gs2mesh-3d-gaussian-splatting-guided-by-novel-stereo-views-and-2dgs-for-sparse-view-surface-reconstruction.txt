Abstract 

Surface reconstruction under sparse-view settings remains challenging due to limited geometric cues. Volume rendering methods based on signed distance func-tions often produce over-smoothed surfaces, while 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), though time-efficient, suffers from incomplete geometry due to the lack of reliable depth supervision and the limitation of being optimized only from given input views. In this paper, we present Sparse-GS2Mesh, a stereo-aware framework for surface reconstruction from sparse views. While 3DGS and stereo matching have been leveraged for surface reconstruction under dense view settings, we extend them to operate effectively under sparse view conditions by first initializing 3DGS using epipolar depth priors to mitigate the 3DGS overfitting problem, followed by our three key components: (I) adaptive baseline selection, (II) fine-tuning with a stereo matching network, and (III) 2D/3D co-regularized fine-tuning. Given a warmed-up 3DGS initialized with epipolar depth, the adaptive baseline selection automatically determines a baseline to synthesize for each sparse view. We then fine-tune 3DGS by backpropagating depth-refining gradients from the stereo match-ing network, effectively specializing the 3DGS for stereo matching. The 2D/3D co-regularization further helps obtain stable reconstruction, addressing weak geo-metric cues in close stereo views. Sparse-GS2Mesh achieves a 15% improvement over state-of-the-art methods in little-overlap settings and comparable results in large-overlap settings. 1 Introduction 

3D surface reconstruction is a long-standing problem and plays a crucial role in various domains such as autonomous driving [14, 15], robotics [4, 46, 19], and virtual reality (VR) [35, 5]. Despite recent progress, reconstructing accurate geometry from sparse inputs remains challenging yet practically important, since real-world applications often allow only a limited number of images.

Workshop: . arXiv:2610.04203v1 [cs.CV] 3 Oct 2026 Previous surface reconstruction methods [21, 31, 27] are built upon neural radiance fields (NeRF) [18], representing a scene as continuous radiance/density fields and optimizing them via differentiable volume rendering. Although several studies [17, 22, 20, 9] have explored sparse-view reconstruction (i.e., only a few different view images as input) and achieved promising results, their major bottleneck lies in long training time, which ranges from several hours to days per scene. Recent studies [38, 37, 43] have extended 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) [12] to surface reconstruction. 2DGS [7] directly reconstructs surfaces from 3DGS by flattening Gaussian elements and applying additional geometric regularization. On the other hand, GS2Mesh [29] generates stereo-aligned image pairs through 3DGS rendering and extracts accurate depth using a pre-trained stereo model. In sparse-view settings, pre-trained multi-view stereo (MVS) networks [32] have also been utilized [30] to initialize Gaussian elements and to enforce depth consistency during optimization. Despite these developments, reconstructed surfaces in sparse-view settings still suffer from geometric inaccuracies due to extremely limited information. To address the aforementioned challenges, we propose Sparse-GS2Mesh, a novel framework designed for surface reconstruction under sparse-view settings. While the original GS2Mesh [29] was devel-oped for dense-view settings, our key idea is that leveraging stereo-aligned renderings as additional geometric cues becomes more effective under sparse-view conditions, where geometric information is extremely limited. However, in such sparse-view settings, 3DGS tends to overfit to training views, leading to a rapid degradation in novel-view rendering quality as the viewpoints move away from the training views. Moreover, initializing 3DGS from sparse SfM points further intensifies overfitting, resulting in unstable Gaussians. Therefore, our method initializes the 3DGS using epipolar depth priors following the strategy of NexusGS [45], to obtain more reliable stereo renderings in the early stage. Instead of using a fixed baseline as in prior works, we adaptively determine the baseline for each view using a multi-view feature score, avoiding the manual setup of baseline parameters. Afterwards, we fine-tune the 3DGS using a pre-trained stereo matching network together with a 2D/3D co-regularization scheme. The stereo matching network is leveraged to propagate gradients that directly improve its depth estimation quality, guiding the 3DGS toward stereo-consistent geometry. Meanwhile, 2D/3D co-regularization provides geometrically stable optimization by complementing the strengths of 2D and 3D Gaussian representations. It is particularly important in large-overlap settings where the synthesized stereo images become similar to the existing input images, making them redundant and thereby weakening the effect. The fine-tuned 3DGS is specialized for stereo-view rendering, from which accurate depth maps are extracted using a pre-trained stereo network. High-quality images rendered from the fine-tuned 3DGS offer additional geometric cues under sparse-view settings, improving our method to preserve more accurate geometry compared to prior works [30, 8], which rely solely on information derived from the given sparse views. Experiments were conducted on the DTU dataset [10], and surface reconstruction qualities are evaluated using the Chamfer Distance metric. We also perform qualitative experiments on the BlendedMVS [34] dataset to demonstrate the generalization of our method. Our contributions are summarized as follows: • We propose Sparse-GS2Mesh, the pipeline specializing 3DGS for stereo matching under sparse inputs, with three novel components: (I) adaptive baseline selection, (II) fine-tuning with a stereo matching network, and (III) 2D/3D co-regularized fine-tuning. • We use synthesized stereo images from 3DGS as geometric cues and leverage epipolar depth priors in initialization to enhance reconstruction quality. • Sparse-GS2Mesh achieves Chamfer Distance (mm) of 1.07 on the DTU dataset with three little-overlap images, outperforming the SOTA (1.27), and 0.95 under the large-overlap setting, which is comparable to the SOTA (0.92). 

2 Related Work 

2.1 Dense View Surface Reconstruction 

Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) [18] represent 3D scenes implicitly for novel view synthesis via differentiable volume rendering. Subsequent studies extend this formulation to surface reconstruction; NeuS [26] improves geometric fidelity via weighted volume rendering, while RegSDF [41] regularizes 2geometry using SfM point clouds. However, these radiance-field methods require heavy computation and degrade significantly under sparse-view conditions due to insufficient geometric cues. 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) [12] models scenes using explicit Gaussian primitives optimized by photometric consistency. Recent works extend 3DGS to dense surface reconstruction: 2DGS [7] flattens Gaussians for surface alignment, GS2Mesh [29] predicts depth from rendered stereo pairs using a pre-trained stereo model, and PGSR [3] integrates unbiased depth rendering with cross-view regularization. Despite high quality under dense views, their dependence on dense observations limits performance in sparse scenarios, motivating our approach. 

2.2 Sparse-View 3D Reconstruction Novel View Synthesis. Although both NeRF [18] and 3DGS [12] achieve impressive performance in novel view synthesis under dense-view settings, they tend to overfit to training views when the number of input images is constrained, leading to a significant degradation in novel view rendering quality. To alleviate this issue, DS-NeRF [6] leverages depth priors from SfM points to constrain scene reconstruction, and InfoNeRF [13] reduces reconstruction uncertainty via entropy-based regularization on ray densities. Cor-GS [40] extends 3DGS with a dual-field co-regularization scheme to enhance multi-view geometric consistency. Unlike Cor-GS, our framework enforces co-regularization between 3DGS and 2DGS, leading to a more robust 3DGS representation, particularly well-suited for surface reconstruction. NexusGS [45] exploits geometric initialization by computing epipolar depth from optical flow estimation. Our method follows the same initialization strategy as NexusGS, and further refines the geometry through fine-tuning using the stereo matching model and 2D/3D Co-Regularization. 

Surface Reconstruction. Beyond novel view synthesis, surface reconstruction also faces challenges under sparse-view settings due to limited geometric observations. To address this issue, NeuSurf [9] integrates on-surface priors into neural implicit representations, while SparseNeuS [17] learns generalizable geometric priors from large-scale datasets using signed distance functions (SDF) and image features, though it still requires per-scene fine-tuning. FatesGS [8], a 2DGS [7] based method, improves geometric alignment by enforcing both multi-view feature consistency and intra-view depth consistency. Sparse2DGS [30] further strengthens 2DGS by introducing a geometry-prioritized enhancement strategy that combines the advantages of MVS and 2DGS. Despite these advances, their performance remains limited under extremely sparse observations (i.e., a little overlap setting), often yielding noisy or incomplete geometry. These methods rely solely on the information available from the given views without leveraging additional geometric cues. This motivates our stereo-guided Sparse-GS2Mesh framework, which effectively leverages high-quality stereo cues to achieve robust geometry reconstruction under sparse-view conditions. 

2.3 Deep Learning Based Stereo Matching 

Stereo matching aims to estimate disparity from a pair of stereo images captured from closely spaced viewpoints. Recent deep-learning methods [2, 24, 39, 28], particularly recurrent architectures [16, 33, 44], iteratively refine disparity predictions for high accuracy. Among them, DEFOM-Stereo [11] integrates monocular depth estimation on the reference view with recurrent stereo refinement. To fully exploit the information from the ground-truth image alongside our synthesized 3DGS stereo rendering, we adopt DEFOM-Stereo to iteratively refine the monocular depth within its recurrent framework.

Our framework first goes through a 2D/3D GS [7, 12] warm-up stage, and then adaptively selects stereo baselines without manual tuning to synthesize reliable stereo-aligned images. During the fine-tuning stage, the stereo matching network [11] adapts 3DGS specifically for stereo matching, while the 2D/3D co-regularization improves their robustness by allowing two modules to compensate for each other’s strengths. 3.1 2D/3D Gaussian Splatting Warm-Up Stage 

To enhance the performance of Gaussian Splatting under sparse-view conditions, we follow the NexusGS [45] initialization strategy, which leverages depth estimation based on epipolar geometry and optical flow. This initialization alleviates the overfitting problem under sparse-view settings. During the warm-up training stage, 3DGS [12] is optimized solely with a photometric loss. For the 2DGS [7] warm-up, we use the photometric loss together with the depth distortion loss and normal consistency loss to optimize surface geometry following the original 2DGS. Warmed-up models are subsequently fine-tuned through the pre-trained stereo matching network (Sec 3.3) and 2D/3D co-regularization (Sec 3.4). Hereafter, the term left view refers to the training view that contains the ground-truth image, and the right view refers to the synthesized stereo view.

Previous work [29] uses a fixed baseline (typically 7% of the scene radius) and was empirically applied to all views. However, the optimal baseline varies across views depending on the geometry observed from each viewpoint, particularly in our setting where stereo views are synthesized from the 3DGS [12]. 3.3 Fine-Tuning with the Stereo Matching Network 

Unlike conventional 3DGS [12] approaches that aim to render across arbitrary novel viewpoints, our framework focuses on the stereo views defined in Sec 3.2. The goal is not only to improve rendering quality, but also to produce effective inputs for the stereo matching network [11]. To this end, we fine-tune the 3DGS to align with the objective described above. As illustrated in Fig.2, the stereo view rendering from the 3DGS is fed into the pre-trained stereo matching network, together with the train-view ground truth image. The gradients that refine the network’s depth are backpropagated through the stereo model into the 3DGS, enabling the 3DGS to adapt specifically for stereo matching rather than general rendering. The loss applied to the estimated depth is designed to maximize the multi-view feature consistency defined in Sec 3.2. For the reference train view, we compute the corresponding stereo view using the previously obtained optimal baseline B∗ and Eq.1, and render Ist r . While fine-tuning with the stereo matching network already provides plausible reconstruction results, synthesized stereo images become similar to the other given input images when captured from adjacent viewpoints, especially in a large-overlap setting. This redundancy weakens the effectiveness of our motivation, as the geometric cues generated from the stereo image overlap with the existing information. Under such conditions, the depth predicted by the stereo matching network might be less reliable than the depth rendered from the 2DGS [7]. To address this issue, we strengthen the 3DGS [12] through a co-regularization scheme with the 2DGS branch, which encourages mutual consistency between the two representations. We demonstrate that this 2D/3D co-regularization improves reconstruction quality in both little- and large-overlap settings (see the experiments). While 2DGS has a tendency to attach Gaussian elements tightly to the surface, causing less artifacts, it is less expressive in image rendering than 3DGS. Through the proposed co-regularization, the two branches complement each other’s strengths, improving both geometric alignment and image fidelity. 4.2 Implementation Details 

Both 2DGS [7] and 3DGS [12] are warmed up for 7,000 iterations, followed by 1,000 iterations of fine-tuning before TSDF-based mesh extraction. Candidate baselines are sampled at 1% intervals from 3%–15% of the scene radius for little-overlap, and 4% intervals from 20%–80% for large-overlap settings. We employ Vis-MVSNet [42] for feature extraction fϕ and DEFOM-Stereo [11] as the stereo network fψ . During inference, occluded regions ( (M tr −st occ )c) are completed using epipolar depth priors from initialization. 

4.3 Experiment Results 

We evaluate surface reconstruction on the DTU dataset using Chamfer Distance (CD) under both little-overlap (Tab. 1) and large-overlap (Tab. 2) settings against SfM-, NeRF-, and 3DGS-based baselines. With epipolar depth initialization, our method achieves SOTA performance in the little-overlap setting, outperforming the previous best method SolidGS [25] by 15%, while maintaining competitive performance under the large-overlap setting. Even with standard SfM initialization, our model remains robust and competitive, demonstrating that our gains do not solely rely on a specific prior. Qualitatively, our framework preserves finer geometric details and global consistency on DTU (Fig. 3) and generalizes effectively to challenging real-world scenes in BlendedMVS [34] (Fig. 4). 

4.4 Ablation Studies Adaptive Baseline Selection. To evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed adaptive baseline selection, we compare our method against fixed settings where the baseline is manually set to 3%, 7%, 11%, and 15% of the scene radius across all views. As shown in Tab.3, our adaptive baseline selection automatically determines optimal baseline values for each view without any manual tuning, achieving lower Chamfer Distance compared to fixed baselines. Importantly, the primary contribution is not merely performance improvement, but the elimination of manual hyperparameter search. Manual baseline tuning requires running all candidate configurations, which takes approximately one hour per scene, whereas our adaptive strategy determines suitable baselines within one minute.

5 Conclusions 

In this paper, we present Sparse-GS2Mesh, a framework integrating Gaussian splatting and stereo matching for sparse-view surface reconstruction. We stabilize optimization by leveraging epipolar depth priors for initialization and automatically determining baselines via adaptive baseline selection. Furthermore, fine-tuning with a stereo matching network and 2D/3D co-regularization specializes 3DGS to generate stereo-consistent renderings that provide robust geometric cues. Rather than a naive combination, our components explicitly complement each other to resolve incomplete geometry. Consequently, our method demonstrates strong robustness across diverse sparse-view regimes and achieves state-of-the-art reconstruction quality on DTU and BlendedMVS datasets. 8References
