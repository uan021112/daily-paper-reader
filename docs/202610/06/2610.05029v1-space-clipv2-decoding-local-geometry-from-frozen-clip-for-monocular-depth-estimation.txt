# SPACE-CLIPv2: Decoding Local Geometry from Frozen CLIP for Monocular Depth Estimation 

Hyun Song, Taewan Cho, Kangmin Kim, and Andrew Jaeyong Choi ∗

School of Computing, iRASC Lab., Gachon University 

{songhyun, taewan2002, kgmin2413, andrewjchoi}@gachon.ac.kr 

Abstract — Vision-language foundation models such as CLIP provide strong semantic representations, but their patch tokens are not directly optimized for dense metric geometry. SPACE-CLIP showed that frozen CLIP features can support monocular depth estimation through layer-group feature fusion, yet it leaves open how neighboring CLIP tokens should be combined to recover fine local structure. We present SPACE-CLIPv2, a frozen-backbone depth decoder that aggregates fixed local neighborhoods in CLIP token space. At selected decoder stages, the model samples a fixed token stencil, predicts aggregation weights, and injects the resulting response through a gated residual update. A token-space high-pass branch further pre-serves shallow local contrast. On NYU Depth V2, SPACE-CLIPv2 improves over a matched SPACE-CLIP baseline, while five-seed experiments consistently favor fixed over learned-offset sampling. Zero-shot iBims-1 evaluation further improves boundary and planar-geometry measures. These results support constrained local token aggregation as a practical mechanism for decoding geometry from frozen CLIP representations. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Monocular depth estimation recovers dense 3D structure from a single RGB image. Unlike image recognition, it depends strongly on local spatial cues such as object bound-aries, depth discontinuities, and surface transitions. This makes frozen foundation encoders useful but difficult to adapt: their representations often encode rich scene informa-tion, yet they are not trained to produce dense metric geom-etry. Vision-language models such as CLIP [20] have there-fore been explored for depth prediction through language-guided or foundation-model adaptation strategies [5], [30], [31]. These works show that pretrained features can support geometric prediction, but the decoder must still recover local structure from patch-token features learned for recognition or image–text alignment. SPACE-CLIP [6] shows that frozen CLIP visual features can be used for monocular depth estimation without updating the CLIP backbone or using text prompts at inference time. Its decoder adapts multi-layer CLIP features through layer-level feature selection and fusion, indicating that CLIP pre-serves depth-relevant structural and semantic cues. However, layer-level fusion does not explicitly specify how the decoder should combine nearby CLIP tokens. For dense depth predic-tion, this local interaction is important because many errors arise from nearby-patch relationships around boundaries, thin objects, and changing surface orientations.

SPACE-CLIPv2 aggregates nearby CLIP tokens before dense prediction. At selected decoder stages, the model reads a fixed token-space neighborhood around each location and aggregates the sampled CLIP responses. The resulting response is injected through a gated residual update, allowing the decoder to control how strongly neighborhood evidence modifies its feature map while keeping the CLIP encoder frozen. Instead of learning deformable sampling offsets from the final depth loss, we use a fixed local stencil and learn the aggregation and residual weighting. This gives the decoder a simple locality prior without adding a trainable offset prediction branch. We also study a token-space refinement branch that uses early CLIP token maps to preserve local contrast. Rather than adding a separate RGB structural branch, this refinement operates directly in token space and injects detail-oriented features near the end of the decoder. We evaluate SPACE-CLIPv2 on NYU Depth V2 [23] under a matched frozen-backbone and text-free inference setting. Local aggregation improves over both the repro-duced SPACE-CLIP baseline and a decoder without local aggregation. Across five randomly selected seeds, fixed-local sampling consistently outperforms learned-offset sampling. We further evaluate the NYU-trained checkpoints zero-shot on iBims-1 [14] and DIODE [26]. The iBims-1 results show substantial improvements in boundary errors together with improved planar-geometry measures, while indoor DIODE provides complementary cross-domain evidence. Together, these results support fixed local aggregation as a simple and robust decoder-side locality prior for frozen CLIP features. Our contributions are summarized as follows:

> •

II. R ELATED WORK 

A. Monocular Depth Estimation 

Monocular depth estimation predicts dense scene geom-etry from a single RGB image. Benchmarks such as NYU Depth V2 [23] have driven progress in indoor depth estima-tion by providing paired RGB and metric depth supervision. Early deep models combined global scene prediction with local refinement for single-image depth estimation [10]. Recent depth estimators commonly rely on strong visual backbones, adaptive depth-bin formulations, and multi-scale decoder designs to recover both global scene layout and local object boundaries [2], [15], [21]. Larger metric-depth and foundation-style systems further improve generalization by combining relative and metric depth, mixed camera models, or large-scale unlabeled data [3], [28], [29]. Other recent approaches use robust distillation or generative priors for monocular depth estimation [4], [30]. However, many of these approaches train or fine-tune depth-specific encoders. Our work studies a stricter setting in which the visual encoder remains frozen and all adaptation is pushed to the decoder. 

B. Frozen CLIP Representations for Dense Prediction 

CLIP [20] learns transferable visual representations from large-scale image–text supervision on top of patch-based visual encoders popularized by Vision Transformers [8]. Prior work on CLIP-based dense prediction shows that image–text pre-training can be adapted beyond image-level recognition [22]. However, CLIP is trained with image-level contrastive objectives rather than dense geometric supervi-sion, and its patch-token maps are coarse and semantically biased. Monocular depth estimation, in contrast, requires fine spatial detail and metric structure. This gap makes decoder design especially important when the CLIP backbone is kept frozen. 

C. CLIP-Based Monocular Depth Estimation 

Several recent methods study how CLIP can be used for monocular depth estimation. DepthCLIP [31] shows that CLIP representations can provide useful depth cues through semantic alignment. Follow-up methods improve CLIP-based depth estimation through prompt learning, task-specific adaptation, or stronger decoder designs [1], [12]. Re-lated language-guided VLM approaches such as DepthBLIP-2 [5] also suggest that linguistic context can support depth reasoning, although our setting remains text-free at inference time. PureCLIP-Depth [17] further investigates depth pre-diction under a frozen-CLIP setting, while CaBins [24] and CLIP2Depth [13] report competitive CLIP-based monocular depth results. SPACE-CLIP [6] is the direct predecessor to our work. It demonstrates that frozen CLIP features can support monocular depth estimation under text-free inference and frozen-backbone constraints. Its decoder mainly adapts CLIP through layer-group feature fusion, such as combining struc-tural and semantic layer groups. SPACE-CLIPv2 keeps the same frozen-backbone and text-free setting, but shifts the focus from deciding which layers to fuse to deciding how local token neighborhoods should be decoded once those layers are available. 

D. Local Geometry Decoding from Frozen Tokens 

Dense depth prediction depends on local evidence as well as global scene structure. Useful cues include object boundaries, surface discontinuities, and fine depth transi-tions. A natural strategy is therefore to aggregate nearby token responses. Local windows already serve as a standard locality prior in visual transformers and dense prediction backbones [16]. However, under a frozen backbone, uncon-strained offset learning may be difficult because the feature space cannot adapt to the learned sampling locations. This motivates a simpler design based on fixed local sampling. SPACE-CLIPv2 follows this direction. It reads from a fixed neighborhood in CLIP token space, learns aggregation weights for the sampled tokens, and injects the result through a gated residual update. We also analyze a lightweight token-detail branch near the depth head to test whether shallow CLIP tokens provide complementary high-frequency contrast. III. M ETHOD

A. Datasets and Evaluation Protocol 

NYU Depth V2 [23] is our primary benchmark. Models are trained on the NYU training split and evaluated on the of-ficial test split using valid ground-truth pixels in [0 .001 , 10 .0] 

m. The reproduced SPACE-CLIP baseline, SPACE-CLIPv2, and all ablations use identical preprocessing, checkpoint selection, and evaluation code, with absolute predictions evaluated without median scaling. We additionally evaluate the same NYU-trained check-points zero-shot on iBims-1 [14] and DIODE [26]. For iBims-1, we use all 100 official core images without target-domain adaptation, calibration, direct-metric scale fitting, cropping, or TTA, and report both standard depth and bound-ary/planarity measures. For DIODE, a single evaluation covers all 771 official validation images (325 indoor and 446 outdoor), and we report the indoor subset as a macro-average over per-image metrics. DIODE evaluation likewise uses no target-domain adaptation, median scaling, scale/shift fitting, calibration, cropping, or TTA. 

B. Implementation Details 

SPACE-CLIPv2 is implemented in PyTorch [18] using a pre-trained CLIP ViT-B/16 image encoder [20]. The CLIP image encoder is kept frozen throughout training and infer-ence, and the CLIP text encoder and text prompts are not used. All trainable parameters are placed in the depth decoder and its adaptation modules. The decoder receives grouped CLIP patch-token maps from selected transformer layers and predicts dense metric depth. No external depth teacher or oracle depth is used in the main experiments. Single-scale predictions are evaluated without horizontal-flip ensembling or test-time depth align-ment. For reference, the evaluated fixed-local configuration (K = 4 ) has 103.99M parameters, of which 18.19M are trainable. On an NVIDIA RTX 5090 with batch-1 BF16 inference at 224 × 224 CLIP input resolution, it runs at 

11 .77 ± 0.08 ms/image (84.995 images/s), with 725.21 MiB peak allocated memory and an estimated 172.33 GFLOPs; an eight-epoch training run takes approximately 2.32 h. 

C. Checkpoint Selection and Evaluation 

During training, checkpoints are selected according to RMSE on the configured checkpoint-selection split. The selected checkpoint is then evaluated under the NYU test protocol to obtain the reported metrics. We use the same checkpoint-selection rule for the reproduced SPACE-CLIP baseline, SPACE-CLIPv2, and all internal ablation variants. We report standard monocular depth estimation metrics, including AbsRel, SqRel, RMSE, RMSE-log, SILog, log10 error, and threshold accuracies δ1, δ2, and δ3 [10], [14]. When available, we also report boundary AbsRel to measure depth accuracy near local geometric discontinuities. For iBims-1, we additionally use depth-boundary error accuracy and completeness (DBE-Acc. and DBE-Comp.) together with the official planar flatness and orientation measures [14]. D. Compared Methods 

We compare SPACE-CLIPv2 with two types of CLIP-based baselines. First, we report published numbers from prior CLIP-based monocular depth estimation methods [13], [17], [24], [31]. Second, we include a SPACE-CLIP [6] baseline evaluated under the same setting as SPACE-CLIPv2. This matched comparison is the primary evidence for mea-suring the effect of our local geometry module. 

E. Ablation Study 

The ablation study isolates which components account for the improvement over the reproduced SPACE-CLIP baseline. We first compare a decoder without local aggregation against variants that add fixed or learned-offset aggregation. We then compare learned-offset aggregation with the proposed fixed local stencil. Finally, we evaluate token-space detail variants to test whether shallow CLIP token maps provide complementary local contrast near the final depth prediction head. All ablation variants use the same NYU split and checkpoint-selection rule. To test sensitivity to initialization, we additionally repeat the fixed-versus-learned comparison across five randomly selected NYU seeds. V. R ESULTS 

A. Local Token Aggregation Ablation 

Table I isolates the main architectural component of SPACE-CLIPv2. Removing local aggregation gives an Ab-sRel of 0.123 and an RMSE of 0.428. Adding local ag-gregation improves both metrics: the learned-offset variant reduces RMSE to 0.420 and increases δ1 from 0.853 to 0.860, while the offset-free variant further reduces RMSE to 0.417 and reaches the highest δ1 of 0.863. VI. C ONCLUSION 

We presented SPACE-CLIPv2, a decoder framework for recovering local geometry from frozen CLIP representations through fixed local token aggregation, gated residual in-jection, and complementary token-space detail refinement. Under matched NYU Depth V2 evaluation, local aggre-gation improves both a decoder without local aggregation and the reproduced SPACE-CLIP baseline, while fixed-local sampling consistently outperforms learned offsets across five randomly selected seeds. Zero-shot iBims-1 evaluation further shows substantial improvements in depth boundary errors together with im-proved planar-geometry measures, and indoor DIODE pro-vides complementary cross-domain AbsRel evidence. These results suggest that a constrained locality prior is a practical decoder-side mechanism for frozen CLIP features. The con-tribution lies in this depth-oriented integration rather than in any individual aggregation primitive. SPACE-CLIPv2 retains a frozen, text-free CLIP backbone without an external depth teacher or test-time alignment; broader cross-domain and downstream evaluation remain directions for future work.
