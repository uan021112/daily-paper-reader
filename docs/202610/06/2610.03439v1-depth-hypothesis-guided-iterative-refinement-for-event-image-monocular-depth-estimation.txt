Depth Estimation

Abstract
Event cameras hold excellent dynamic properties, show-
ing great potential for monocular depth estimation (MDE).
However, existing methods mainly improve performance by
optimizing contextual features, but still struggle with the ill-
posed and nonlinear nature of direct full-depth regression.
In this paper, we propose HypoDepth, the first event–image
monocular depth iterative refinement framework.
By in-
troducing a discrete Depth Hypothesis Volume (DHV), we
transform the depth regression problem into a constrained
depth search task. Specifically, we construct a 3D cost vol-
ume between the DHV features and contextual features and
perform a multi-scale correlation search to guide stable
residual optimization. This lightweight cost volume enables
efficient global-to-local refinement across multi-resolution.
Our method outperforms existing approaches on DSEC and
MVSEC with state-of-the-art results and strong zero-shot
generalization. Meanwhile, our tiny model achieves an ex-
cellent balance between accuracy and efficiency, enabling
real-time performance on resource-limited devices.
1. Introduction
Monocular depth estimation (MDE) serves as a founda-
tional task for 3D scene understanding, yet image-based
approaches [29, 30, 39] often struggle under challenging
conditions such as high-speed motion or poor illumination.
Event cameras [7, 18], with their high temporal resolution
and dynamic range, can capture meaningful scene changes
in such scenarios. However, they lack visual information
in static regions, making the recovery of static structures
difficult. Consequently, leveraging the complementarity of
these two modalities holds great promise for robust MDE.
To fully leverage the complementary strengths of events
and images, significant research efforts [8, 13, 22, 28] have
focused on cross-modal alignment and fusion techniques.
Typically, asynchronous event streams are transformed into

their primary focus on improving contextual features. They
fundamentally struggle with the inherent ill-posedness of
MDE, where directly regressing the full depth distribution
remains highly nonlinear and challenging.
To overcome this limitation, we introduce a novel iter-
ative refinement paradigm for event-image MDE that fun-
damentally reformulates the ill-posed depth regression as a
constrained depth search task. The core of our approach is
a Depth Hypothesis Volume (DHV), which discretizes the
continuous depth space into a set of candidate depth values
within adaptive bounds derived from prior estimates. This
discretization provides inherent robustness by constraining
the solution space to a structured set of plausible depth
hypotheses, effectively mitigating the impact of prediction
noise and enabling stable residual optimization. Conceptu-
ally aligned with search-based optical flow methods such as
RAFT [35], which maintains a 4D cost volume built from

Building on this design,
we develop HypoDepth
(Fig. 2), a framework that establishes an iterative refinement
paradigm for event-image monocular depth estimation. To
effectively balance computational efficiency with multi-
scale representation, we adopt resolution-specific cross-
modal fusion strategies for contextual feature extraction.
For each resolution, a globally shared DHV is first estab-
lished using prior depth estimates and projected into a uni-
fied feature space to represent the scene geometry under dif-
ferent depth hypotheses. We then construct the 3D cost vol-
ume by computing similarity metrics between the contex-
tual features and the DHV embeddings, followed by multi-
scale correlation lookups to integrate structures across vary-
ing distances. Since the DHV provides discrete depth, a ge-
ometry encoder is introduced to incorporate previous depth
estimates and contextual features to produce a spatially con-
sistent geometric representation (Fig. 1 (e)). This feature
is then processed by a GRU-based iterative unit to predict
residual depths, achieving progressive refinement through
multiple iterations.
Benefiting from the lightweight 3D
cost volume, this process can be efficiently operated across
resolutions, enabling effective global-to-local depth refine-
ment across various resolutions. Extensive experiments on
DSEC [9] and MVSEC [40] demonstrate our state-of-the-
art performance. Our main contributions are as follows:
• We propose HypoDepth, a novel iterative refinement
framework for event-image monocular depth estimation.
By introducing a DHV, it reformulates ill-posed depth re-
gression into a constrained depth search task, enabling
stable residual depth optimization.
• We construct efficient multi-resolution 3D cost volumes
coupled with multi-scale correlation lookup, which al-
lows comprehensive geometric feature extraction for
depth refinement from global structure to local details.
• Our approach achieves the best performance on DSEC
and MVSEC datasets with superior generalization ability.
2. Related Work
Image-based Monocular Depth Estimation.
Inspired
by the success of deep learning, early CNN-based meth-
ods [5, 15] in the image domain demonstrate that neural
networks can infer monocular scene geometry. DORN [6]
and BTS [16] further improve accuracy through depth dis-
cretization and scale-aware regression. LapDepth [33] in-
troduces a Laplacian residual pyramid to guide depth com-
pletion, achieving coarse-to-fine depth refinement. Lever-
aging strong self-attention, transformer-based models [3,
29, 30, 37, 38] achieve remarkable progress. AdaBins [3]
introduces adaptive and learnable depth bins for fine-
grained and scale-consistent estimation, while works [29,
30, 37, 38] enhance generalization through large-scale pre-
training and self-distillation.
RayFusion [14] fuses ray-
based cost volumes across multiple frames to achieve con-
sistent and accurate sparse depth completion.
Recently,
diffusion-based methods [4, 31] reformulate depth predic-
tion as generative tasks, producing more detailed depth
maps. However, these approaches often fail under motion
blur or poor illumination, where event cameras can still pro-
vide reliable information.
Event-based Monocular Depth Estimation. Inspired by
image-based learning methods, some works convert events
into image-like representations for data-driven prediction.
Early event-based method [12] first introduces recurrent
units into a U-Net to encode temporal consistency, while
HMNet [11] and EReFormer [23] capture spatiotemporal
relations using multi-scale strategies and recursive Trans-
formers, respectively. Self-supervised approaches [27, 42]
aim to reduce depth annotation and model long-term infor-
mation. DepthAnyEvent [2] further distills geometric priors
from image models to improve generalization. To compen-
sate for missing appearance, RAMNet [8] first fuses events’
temporal cues and image semantics for cross-modal depth
estimation. SRFNet [28] and PCDepth [22] perform iter-
ative feature fusion refinement, whereas UniCT [13] and
FUSE [34] employ transformer or hybrid attention for en-
hanced alignment. Although these methods strengthen con-
textual representations for MDE, their direct depth regres-
sion remains highly nonlinear and difficult to converge due
to the ill-posed nature of monocular estimation.
Iterative Refinement Scheme.
In event-based MDE,
PCDepth [22] adopts iterative strategies to optimize
feature-level fusion rather than explicit depth refinement.
RAFT [35] introduces a cost-volume-based iterative re-
finement scheme to optical flow estimation that greatly
improves accuracy by predicting residual flows.
This
paradigm is later extended to stereo matching [19, 36] and
event-based flow estimation [10, 20, 21], achieving state-of-
the-art performance. Unlike these approaches that rely on
explicit viewpoint correspondences, we introduce a discrete
Depth Hypothesis Volume (DHV) to simulate such corre-
spondences between the DHV features and contextual fea-
tures, enabling iterative local search and explicit geometric
refinement through cost-volume construction.

3.4. Multi-resolution Depth Refinement
In our 3D correlation, the depth dimension D ≪H × W
allows efficient refinement at higher resolutions. We adopt
a coarse-to-fine process from 1/16 to 1/4 resolution: low-
resolution stages emphasize global consistency, while high-
resolution stages focus on recovering fine structural details.
To adapt to different refinement granularity, the number of
depth hypotheses D in the DHV is adjusted accordingly, a
smaller one for lower resolutions and a larger one for higher
ones. The initial depth at 1/16 stage is set to zero.

4. Experiments
Datasets and metrics. We conduct experiments on two
real-world datasets, MVSEC [40] and DSEC [9]. MVSEC
provides outdoor sequences under day and night conditions
with a resolution of 260 × 346 and a depth range from
1.97 to 80.0 meters, featuring relatively simple scenes and
sparse events. Following previous works [8, 11, 22], we
pretrain our model on the synthetic dataset EventScape [8],
fine-tune on outdoor day2, and evaluate on outdoor day1
and outdoor night1. DSEC is a more complex and diverse
outdoor dataset with higher resolution (480 × 640), denser
events, and a depth range of 4.53 to 50.1 meters. We con-
vert the provided disparity ground truth to depth maps using
the official code. Since ground truth for the test set is not
publicly available, we follow works [1, 2] to split the train-
ing set into new train/test subsets, and apply a 320 × 640
center crop to reduce misalignment artifacts.
We utilize
absolute relative error (Abs Rel), root mean squared error
(RMSE), logarithmic RMSE (RMSE log), and accuracy δn
(δ < 1.25n, n = 1, 2, 3) to evaluate the predicted depth. For
DSEC, we also report Floating-Point Operations (FLOPs,
G), model size (Param), and inference time (Infer time) un-
der the same computational environment.
Implementation Details. For MVSEC and DSEC, we use
event slices of 50 ms and 20 ms, respectively, with the num-
ber of temporal bins B = 3. The iteration numbers per stage
are set to 8. The lookup radius r is 8, and the depth hypoth-
esis numbers D for different resolutions are (32, 64, 96).
Detailed network configurations are provided in the supple-
mentary material. Our framework is implemented in Py-
Torch, and all experiments are trained with the AdamW op-
timizer [26] and the one-cycle learning rate scheduler [32].
For DSEC, we train for 60k iterations with a maximum
learning rate of 2e-4 and a batch size of 16. For MVSEC,
we fine-tune from a model pretrained on EventScape for
40k iterations using a maximum learning rate of 1e-5 with
a batch size set to 24.

the lack of appearance cues, and the absence of depth-level
refinement. SRFNet [28] and PCDepth [22] enhance details
at higher resolutions through optimizing multimodal fusion,
yet their refinement remains static at the feature level with-
out explicit constraint or optimization in the depth space. In
contrast, our method performs dynamic, depth-level itera-
tive refinement via a cost-volume-based lookup mechanism,
progressively refining depth from low to high resolutions
while mitigating the ill-posedness of monocular estimation.

5. Conclusion
In this work, we propose HypoDepth, a novel event-image-
based MDE iterative refinement framework. By introduc-
ing a Depth Hypothesis Volume (DHV), we reformulate the
ill-posed depth regression problem as a depth search prob-
lem. We construct a 3D cost volume between DHV and
contextual features for iterative lookup–based residual op-
timization, effectively reducing the challenges of full-depth
regression. Benefits from the lightweight cost volume, we
perform refinement across multiple resolutions, achieving
robust global-to-fine depth estimation. Our method shows
the best performance on DSEC and MVSEC, and demon-
strates superior zero-shot generalization.

6. Network Details
HypoDepth. Our model performs depth refinement pro-
gressively from 1/16 to 1/4 resolution. The detailed net-
work configurations are summarized in Tab. 8, including
the backbone, channel dimensions of core modules, the
number of iterative updates, and the parameters related to
cost-volume lookup.
Ours denotes the event–image fu-
sion model, while Ours-E represents the event-only vari-
ant, in which the image encoder and EIFusion module are
removed. Notably, we adopt resolution-dependent lookup
scales (l in Fig. 4 (a)) across different stages in our base
model. At lower resolutions (such as 1/16 resolution), the
lookup radius r already covers most or even all spatial re-
gions of the feature map, making the resulting correlation
effectively global. Further downsampling would cause the
lookup region to span nearly the entire feature map again,
leading to redundant correlations and reducing the effective-
ness of the multi-scale lookup strategy. For the scaled-down
model in the ablation studies, we train it with a batch size
of 3 for 40k iterations.
PCDepth-Ours.
Since PCDepth [22] performs iterative
feature-level fusion refinement at 1/8 resolution for depth
optimization, we also conduct refinement at the same scale
for fair comparison. The contextual features are obtained
from PCDepth’s fused encoder output, while the parame-
ters of our iterative refinement decoder are listed in Tab. 8.
7. More Results
More Comparison on MVSEC and DSEC. In Tab. 5, we
report the results of our event-only (Ours-E-B) and image-
only (Ours-I-B) variants on DSEC and MVSEC to verify
the benefit of event–image fusion.
In addition, we sim-
ulate image motion blur on DSEC to mimic high-speed
scenarios, and directly evaluate the model trained on the
original DSEC under zero-shot transfer to the blurred set-
ting. Tab. 5 compares our image-only variant (w/o events)
with PCDepth in this challenging scenario.
The results
show that incorporating events consistently improves per-
formance, with larger gains in challenging conditions such
as nighttime (MVSEC on1) and high-speed motion (blurred
DSEC). In these cases, frame appearance is severely de-
graded by low illumination or motion blur, whereas events
still provide rich structural and texture cues, leading to more

Duration of Event Slices. We investigate the impact of dif-
ferent event-stream window lengths, including 20 ms, 50

to the temporal window. In contrast, longer windows cause
excessive event accumulation under large motion, increas-
ing misalignment and motion artifacts, blurring the match-
ing distribution, and degrading performance. We therefore
adopt a 20 ms window to reduce latency, better leverage the
strengths of event cameras, and ensure a fair performance
comparison.

Qualitative Results on DSEC. In Fig. 11, we present ad-
ditional qualitative comparisons on the DSEC [9] among
Ours-B, PCDepth [22], and PCDepth-Ours. The results
demonstrate that on this complex-structured dataset, our ap-
proach consistently produces more geometrically plausible
and spatially accurate depth maps under varying illumina-
tion conditions compared to PCDepth.
Moreover, when
equipped with our iterative refinement module, PCDepth-
Ours also benefits from sharper and more accurate object
boundaries. Thanks to the additional 1/4-resolution refine-
ment stage in our model, our depth predictions exhibit finer
structural details than those of PCDepth-Ours.
Qualitative Results on MVSEC. We present a qualita-
tive comparison between our method and PCDepth [22] on
both daytime and nighttime sequences of the MVSEC [40]
in Fig. 12. The visual results demonstrate that our approach
accurately estimates both distant (rows 2 and 3) and nearby
(rows 1 and 4) structures under challenging lighting condi-
tions and sparse event inputs. In particular, PCDepth fails
to recover distant regions, whereas our model produces sta-
ble and continuous estimates, which are crucial for enabling
autonomous systems to perceive distant scenes in advance.
More visualizations of refinement on DSEC. We visual-
ize additional multi-resolution depth refinement processes
and corresponding error in Fig. 13. At the 1/16 resolution,
the model performs globally consistent regression, produc-
ing coarse estimations. When refined at the 1/8 level, the
scene geometry becomes more pronounced. Further refine-
ment at the 1/4 resolution sharpens both distant and nearby
structures, demonstrating the effectiveness of our method in
achieving coarse-to-fine depth reconstruction.
9. Limitations and Future Work
We develop a depth-hypothesis–guided iterative refinement
framework for event–image monocular depth estimation,
achieving high-performance results. To further improve the
method, we analyze several remaining limitations.
First, the superior performance of PCDepth-Ours over
Ours-B indicates that our multimodal fusion strategy still
has space for improvement. A more adaptive fusion mecha-
nism could better leverage the complementary advantages
of the two modalities.

Second, as illustrated in Fig. 10, our method fails un-
der certain challenging conditions. The camera motion in
this scenario is not forward-facing, resulting in weak par-
allax between foreground and background objects. Conse-
quently, the spatiotemporal structure captured by events is
limited, and the geometric boundaries become ambiguous,
e.g., between the window and the transformer box beneath
it, or between the tree and the house behind it. Addition-
ally, the scene contains textureless, homogeneous regions
such as road surfaces and building facades. These areas
exhibit little brightness change and therefore generate few
events, while also lacking discriminative texture in the im-
age modality. As a result, the cost volume becomes ambigu-
ous (as shown in Fig. 10 (e)), offering limited guidance for
reliable matching. In such cases, the iterative optimization
tends to rely more on contextual priors and the depth distri-
bution rather than meaningful correlation evidence obtained
from lookup operations.
In future work, we will focus on exploring more ef-
fective fusion strategies for images and events to lever-
age their complementary characteristics fully. To address
the limitations of depth estimation under non-forward mo-
tion, we will develop multi-view depth estimation models
with either multiple temporal continuous frames or multi-
perspective inputs.
