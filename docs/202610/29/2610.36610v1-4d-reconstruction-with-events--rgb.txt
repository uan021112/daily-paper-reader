Abstract. Reconstructing dynamic 3D scenes over time (4D reconstruc-tion) from a single moving camera is central to robotics and AR/VR ap-plications, yet state-of-the-art feed-forward methods rely solely on RGB frames and therefore inherit the failure modes of conventional cameras, including motion blur and temporal aliasing under fast or non-uniform camera and object motion. # 1 Introduction 

Reconstructing dynamic three-dimensional scenes over time, referred to as 4D reconstruction , is a foundational problem in computer vision with applications in AR/VR content creation, robotics, autonomous driving, and digital twins. Recent advances in vision-language models (VLMs), such as CL4D [6], have also begun exploring dynamic 4D point cloud representations as an alternative to conventional 2D video inputs, enabling richer spatio-temporal and geomet-ric reasoning and further motivating accurate 4D reconstruction directly from videos. Recent feed-forward models, such as MonST3R [21], recover per-frame depth and camera pose directly from RGB video without per-scene optimiza-tion. However, because these models rely solely on RGB frames, they inherit the failure modes of conventional cameras: motion blur and temporal aliasing under fast or non-uniform camera and object motion, precisely the conditions where accurate motion estimation matters most. Event cameras offer a complementary sensing modality. By reporting per-pixel brightness changes asynchronously at microsecond resolution, they capture motion information largely unaffected by blur or frame-rate limitations. Prior work has explored event-based depth and pose estimation as standalone tasks, but whether an event stream can be incorporated into an existing RGB-based 4D reconstruction pipeline without a full architectural redesign remains less explored. In this preliminary work, we present an early exploration of event-augmented 4D reconstruction through a minimal extension to the MonST3R pipeline as shown in Figure 1: we augment MonST3R with an additional event-voxel input branch, leaving the rest of the architecture unchanged, and fuse it with RGB features via concatenation before processing through the standard MonST3R pipeline. We hypothesize that RGB and event data are complementary—RGB provides spatial information for depth, while events provide motion cues for mod-eling dynamic scenes, particularly under fast object and camera motion—and that even this minimalist, concatenation-based fusion, without a more principled fusion mechanism, is sufficient to realize this complementarity. To test this hypothesis, following prior work, we evaluate on benchmark datasets for both camera pose estimation and video depth estimation. We observe that fusing event voxels and RGB achieves higher reconstruction accuracy than using either modality alone, and that this benefit is particularly pronounced under high object motion. 4D Reconstruction with Events & RGB 3

While this work is a preliminary study on 4D reconstruction with RGB and events, we argue that more principled multi-modal fusion mechanisms are needed to achieve substantially higher reconstruction accuracy, given the fun-damental differences between the two modalities: RGB is a constant-frame-rate, synchronous modality, while events are adaptive and asynchronous. We make the following key contributions: 

– We extend MonST3R with a minimal event-voxel input branch, requiring no other architectural changes, and show that fusing RGB and event voxels reduces depth and pose estimation error relative to RGB-only MonST3R, an event-to-RGB translation baseline, and an event-only variant. 

– We conduct a sensitivity study under varying camera and object motion, establishing that RGB-event fusion provides the largest gains under high object motion. 

# 2 Related Work 

Feedforward 4D reconstruction. Prior work on 4D reconstruction primarily used RGB frames as input. Early work such as DUSt3R [17] proposed predict-ing a separate pointmap for each pair of RGB frames. MonST3R [21] extended the DUSt3R [17] pointmap paradigm to dynamic scenes. Both MonST3R and DUSt3R predict pointmaps followed by a postprocessing step that aligns them into a single coordinate system. Works such as CUT3R [16] and Fast3R [20] instead unify pointmap prediction and alignment into a single feed-forward pipeline, directly predicting aligned pointmaps in one coordinate system with-out a separate postprocessing step. More recently, VGGT [15] and PAGE4D [23] scale up training data and model capacity with transformer-based architectures, achieving more accurate 3D/4D scene reconstruction. Despite this large body of work on 4D reconstruction, all prior methods take a single monocular RGB video as input and are feed-forward, and none incorporate an event stream. Robustness to fast motion remains an open challenge across these methods, motivating the present work: fusing event and RGB data for 4D reconstruction. 

Event cameras and RGB event fusion. Event cameras have been stud-ied extensively for their high temporal resolution, negligible motion blur, and high dynamic range [3]. RGB and event fusion has been explored successfully for related but narrower tasks, including video deblurring [8], frame interpo-lation/extrapolation [11, 13], and event-based visual odometry and SLAM [14], where events are typically used to recover information that has already been lost in blurred or dropped RGB frames. Its potential for 4D scene reconstruction, that is, jointly recovering time-varying geometry and appearance rather than a single deblurred or interpolated frame, remains comparatively underexplored. 

Event-augmented 4D reconstruction. More recently, EAG3R [19] presented the first event-augmented 4D reconstruction method, combining RGB and event data to target low-light conditions. Its design and evaluation are organized 4 D. Weerakoon et al. 

around illumination robustness: an SNR-aware fusion module favors RGB fea-tures in well-lit regions and event features in dark regions, and its central ex-periments vary illumination from day to night while holding the motion regime relatively constant. This leaves comparatively less direct evidence for a comple-mentary motivation for event cameras: their ability to capture fast, non-rigid scene dynamics that a fixed-frame-rate RGB camera under-samples or blurs, independent of illumination. The present work targets precisely this gap, eval-uating explicitly under varying camera and object motion rather than varying illumination.

3.1 Model Architecture 

We extend MonST3R to jointly process RGB images and event voxel grids while preserving the original output representations. For each view, the 3-channel RGB image and the 5-channel event voxel grid are concatenated to form an 8-channel input tensor. Since the pretrained ViT encoder expects a 3-channel input, we introduce a lightweight adapter consisting of a single convolution layer that projects the 8-channel input to a 3-channel feature representation. The adapted inputs from the two views are then processed by the shared Siamese ViT encoder, followed by the original MonST3R decoder and prediction heads without further architectural modifications. 4D Reconstruction with Events & RGB 5

3.2 Dataset Preparation 

The datasets used in this work provide only RGB image sequences. To obtain the corresponding event data, we use the V2E [7] simulator to generate asynchronous event streams between consecutive RGB frames. The simulator first interpolates intermediate RGB frames, effectively increasing the temporal resolution of the original video sequence. It then generates events by modeling the brightness changes between the interpolated frames. Finally, the generated event streams are accumulated into voxel grid representations according to Equation 2, which are used together with the corresponding RGB images as inputs to the proposed network. 

3.3 Training Details 

Similar to MonST3R, we train the proposed variants on a number of prior datasets, each augmented with synthetically generated event data as described in Section 3.2: PointOdyssey [22], TartanAir [18], and Spring [9]. Remaining training configurations are identical to the original MonST3R implementation.

# 5 Discussion & Future Work 

This preliminary study shows that a minimal event-camera branch added to MonST3R reduces both depth and pose estimation error across the benchmarks considered, with the largest gains under high camera and object motion. The design is deliberately simple: an event-voxel branch concatenated with RGB features, with no other architectural changes. This leaves several directions open for a more thorough follow-up study. 

– Event-specific encoders. We encode events as dense voxel grids processed by the same backbone used for RGB. Architectures designed for the sparse, asynchronous structure of event data, such as Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) [5] or event transformers [12], may extract more informative mo-tion cues than a standard RGB-based backbones. 

– Adaptive multi-modal fusion. We fuse modalities by concatenation, which treats RGB and event features uniformly regardless of local motion. Events 8 D. Weerakoon et al.

– More Recent 4D base models. We build on MonST3R, which is no longer the most accurate feed-forward 4D reconstruction model. Applying the same event-branch extension to more recent architectures, such as VGGT [15] or PAGE4D [23], would test whether the benefit of event fusion generalizes across base models rather than being specific to MonST3R. 

– Real event data. Sintel, Bonn, KITTI and PointOdyssey do not natively contain event streams, so the event modality used in our evaluation is syn-thetically generated using V2E simulator. Validating these gains on real event-camera captures is an important next step before drawing conclusions about real-world deployment. 

# 6 Conclusion 

We present a preliminary study on 4D reconstruction using a combination of RGB frames and event voxels. To this end, we propose a minimalist design that concatenates event voxels with RGB frames before passing them through MonST3R, retrained to incorporate this additional modality. Across multiple datasets for camera pose estimation and video depth estimation, we observe that combining event voxels and RGB frames achieves lower reconstruction error than either modality alone, indicating that spatial information from RGB and motion cues from event voxels play complementary roles in accurate 4D reconstruction. 4D Reconstruction with Events & RGB 9
