SG2SLAM: Spatial Geometry-Informed 3D Gaussian Splatting SLAM
for Monocular Outdoor Navigation
Ziang Guo†, Didar Seyidov†, Yixiao Zhou§, Xuefeng Zhang‡,
Ali Alridha Abdulkarim†, Chen Min◦∗, Zufeng Zhang‡∗, and Dzmitry Tsetserukou†
Abstract— Monocular outdoor navigation requires robust
camera motion estimation and a dense, navigable map, where
3D Gaussian Splatting SLAM serves this task through an
explicit differentiable parameterization and closed-form pro-
jection. However, typically, the geometry is recovered by asso-
ciating 2D pixels with 3D points, so a modular SLAM system
absorbs tracking error into the map, leading to cascading degra-
dation. To address these gaps, we present SG2SLAM, which
couples streaming spatial geometry with a ray-conditioned
3DGS kernel. Streaming spatial geometry replaces the per-
frame cross-dimensional correspondence with a chunk-level
metric prior that supervises tracking and mapping from one
source. A ray-conditioned kernel extends each primitive with a
viewing-direction subspace that senses the monocular camera
viewpoints. The joint optimization over spatial geometry and
poses ensures a globally consistent map and trajectory out-
come. Experiments on the KITTI and Waymo Open datasets
show that our SG2SLAM surpasses the per-frame association
baselines in the monocular outdoor navigation task. Code will
be made publicly available upon acceptance.
I. INTRODUCTION
Recovering camera motion and creating a dense, navigable
map of the environment from a monocular RGB stream re-
main core capabilities for autonomous navigation. Coupling
3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) with visual Simultaneous
Localization and Mapping (SLAM) has rapidly become a
mainstream research direction, as its explicit, differentiable
parameterization is naturally compatible with online tracking
and mapping [1]. However, in monocular outdoor navigation,
which is the deployment scenario of practical interest, the
3DGS SLAM suffers from an undetermined global scale and
further exposes two concrete gaps that we identify below.
Existing SLAM systems recover geometry through the
2D–3D matching paradigm, in which 2D pixel observations

to 2D projection irreversibly discards depth, and the inverse
recovery is a non-convex problem that is sensitive to outliers,
reprojection noise, and mismatched feature scales. Under the
conditions of outdoor monocular navigation, these sensitivi-
ties compound. Within a modular SLAM architecture, track-
ing errors from 2D–3D correspondences are absorbed into
the 3D map, which then provides a degraded reference for
the next round of matching, producing a slow but unbounded
drift that is particularly hard to localize and correct.
The 3DGS map becomes geometrically ambiguous while
absorbing tracking errors. 3DGS is mainly optimized against
a photometric loss for novel view synthesis, which admits
multiple geometrically distinct Gaussian configurations that
produce indistinguishable renderings. In outdoor sequences,
this manifests as floating Gaussians, broken thin structures,
and inconsistent surface normals [7]. These artifacts are toler-
able for view synthesis but are directly harmful for navigation
due to the pollution of depth information, where downstream
tasks such as collision checking and traversability estimation
consume the map.
To mitigate these gaps in outdoor monocular condi-
tions, we propose SG2SLAM, a 3DGS SLAM built on
streaming spatial geometry and a monocular-depth-aware
ray-conditioned 3DGS kernel, where the frontend streams
poses, point maps, and per-pixel confidence from windows
of frames to form chunking, with a metric depth network
supervising the metric scale. Then, the backend refines the
3DGS map, conditioning each primitive on the viewing ray
with spatial geometric supervision and keeping the mapping
consistent and aware under monocular camera conditions.
Chunks are then fused by a joint global graph optimization
over spatial geometry and poses. Our contribution is as
follows:
• We present a 3DGS SLAM built on streaming spatial
geometry. Compared to the baselines based on pairwise
2D pixel matching and cross-dimensional correspon-
dences, our SG2SLAM improves both frontend tracking
and backend mapping.
• We render 3DGS through a ray-conditioned kernel in
closed form, where the mean and covariance span a
spatial block and a viewing-direction subspace, thus re-
moving the blending at grazing angles that may produce
floating primitives.
• We fuse chunks through joint global graph optimization

II. RELATED WORK
A. Classical and Learning-Based Monocular SLAM
MonoSLAM [8] showed that a single camera is enough
to track and map in real time with an extended Kalman
filter, and PTAM [9] made keyframe-based bundle adjust-
ment practical by running tracking and mapping in two
parallel threads. The ORB-SLAM family [10]–[12] was built
on this design using ORB features, a covisibility graph,
and bag-of-words place recognition [13]. Methods such as
LSD-SLAM [14] skip keypoints and minimize photometric
error. DROID-SLAM [15] iterates dense optical flow up-
dates through a differentiable bundle adjustment layer and
generalizes well across datasets. DPVO [16] replaces dense
flow with sparse patches to cut memory and runtime. All
of these systems recover depth indirectly by associating 2D
observations with 3D points, and the quality of representation
determines both the accuracy and the cost of the association.
B. SLAM with 3D Foundation Models
Another direction uses 3D foundation models that regress
3D structures directly from images. DUSt3R [17] predicts
point maps for an image pair in a shared frame, with-
out known intrinsics, and MASt3R [18] adds a matching
head that provides dense and accurate correspondences.
Spann3R [19] processes images pairwise and maintains a
spatial memory in a recurrent state, allowing the whole video
to be reconstructed online. VGGT [20] goes further and
predicts cameras, depth maps, and point maps for multiple
views in a single feed-forward pass. Several SLAM systems
are built on these models. MASt3R-SLAM uses two-view
MASt3R predictions for tracking and local fusion, and it runs
in real time on uncalibrated video [4], [18]. SLAM3R [21]
reconstructs dense scenes from monocular RGB with a
sliding window of frames that are registered into a global
map. VGGT-SLAM builds submaps with VGGT and aligns
them with a 15 degree of freedom homography on the SL(4)
manifold, since an uncalibrated reconstruction is only defined
up to a projective transformation [20], [22]. VGGT-Long [23]
targets kilometer-scale driving sequences by splitting the
video into overlapping chunks, aligning neighboring chunks,
and correcting the result with loop closure. This direction
replaces correspondence with regression, predicting the 3D
structures directly by the network rather than by the camera
model. But such systems recover geometric similarity only
after submap registration, sliding windows, or explicit align-
ment in a pairwise manner.
C. 3D Gaussian Splatting SLAM
3D Gaussian Splatting [24] represents the scene with
explicit anisotropic Gaussians that are rasterized at high
frame rates, and it quickly became the preferred map for
dense SLAM. MonoGS [25] optimizes camera poses directly
against the Gaussian map and works with monocular or
RGB-D input. SplaTAM [26] and GS-SLAM [27] rely on
depth sensors, while Photo-SLAM [28] and CaRtGS [29]
leave tracking to ORB-SLAM3 and spend the GPU budget
on mapping. Splat-SLAM [30] and HI-SLAM2 [31] pair a
learned dense tracker with monocular depth priors, and they
deform the 3D Gaussians when the trajectory is corrected by
global optimization. LoopSplat [32] closes loops by register-
ing submaps made of 3D Gaussians directly. S3PO-GS [6]
anchors MASt3R point maps to the Gaussian map, which
keeps the scale consistent across frames and limits scale drift.
GigaSLAM [2] stores 3D Gaussians in a hierarchical sparse
voxel map, tracks with a metric depth network together with
epipolar geometry, and corrects drift with bag-of-words loop
closure. DiskChunGS [3] boosts performance by optimizing
the I/O: it splits the scene into spatial chunks, keeps only the
active ones on the GPU, and moves the rest to disk. KiloGS-
SLAM [5] combines a multi-tier tracker with chunk-based
densification and pruning of 3D Gaussians. Across these
systems, the 3DGS map is dominated by image evidence
rather than spatial geometry, so the cross-dimensional cost
of 2D–3D matching persists beneath the recent progress.
III. METHODOLOGY
Our SG2SLAM couples streaming spatial geometry with
monocular-depth-aware 3DGS based on ray-conditioned ker-
nels to deliver globally consistent mapping and tracking
in monocular outdoor navigation. The frontend provides a
spatial geometry prior in the form of poses, point maps,
and per-pixel confidence maps in local level chunks, paired
with a metric depth prior that supplies the metric scale
correction. The backend optimizes a monocular-depth-aware
3DGS built on the frontend geometry prior for mapping
and chunk fusion, where a ray-conditioned 3DGS kernel
keeps the map consistent and aware as the viewing direction
changes. Bundle adjustment combines a photometric loss
with metric depth supervision, constraining scene scale and a
point-consistency term, thus constraining pose drift. Across
chunks, a joint Sim(3) optimization solves all chunk fusions
in the global pose graph to mitigate the scale-induced drift
accumulation that monocular SLAM suffers from along a
sequence. The system is shown in Fig. 1.

D. Ablation Study
In Tab. IV, we show the ablation results of our design
for the main modules and the effects of different setups on
tracking and rendering performance on the KITTI dataset,
where we report the average metrics across Sequence 04 and
Sequence 07. Our ray-conditioned 3DGS kernel improves
mapping performance under monocular SLAM, as demon-
strated by the improvements in PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS.
For the pair-wise frontend experiment, we feed the images in
pairs to MASt3R [18] to obtain the estimated relative poses,
while different frames in chunks determine the noise level
of the fusion anchor and the initialization of 3DGS training.
The ablations show that more tracking errors may accumulate
frame-wise along the run, and the design of the chunk size
affects both the frontend and backend due to how much prior
noise propagates throughout the fusion and mapping.
V. CONCLUSION
This paper presents SG2SLAM, a 3DGS SLAM system
built on streaming spatial geometry, which dispenses with
the per-frame 2D–3D association and pairwise image match-
ing. Our system introduces a novel paradigm that boosts
monocular 3DGS SLAM by enabling joint 3D–3D associ-
ation for both mapping and tracking in a streaming manner.
Experiments on the KITTI, our own real-world driving, and
Waymo Open Datasets show that our SG2SLAM exceeds
existing paradigms in monocular outdoor navigation. Future
work will focus on the robustness of perception quality under
unstructured conditions, e.g., rainy nights and wild forests,
to further improve the stability of our system.
