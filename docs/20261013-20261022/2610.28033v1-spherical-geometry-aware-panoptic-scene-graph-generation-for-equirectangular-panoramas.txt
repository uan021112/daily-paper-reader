ABSTRACT 

Panoptic scene graph generation (PSGG) represents scene entities with segmentation masks and directed relations. Equirectangular projection (ERP) panoramas challenge PSGG through distortion, latitude-dependent pixel areas, and an ar-tificial seam. We propose a two-stage spherical geometry-aware framework that fuses overlapping perspective-view predictions using observation reliability, spherical over-lap, and angular proximity. Relation reasoning combines spherical-area-aware aggregation, weighted no-relation su-pervision, and dual-longitude score fusion. We also con-struct a dataset of 340 high-resolution panoramas with 10,571 nodes and 6,632 directed relations. Against corresponding baselines, spherical fusion improves PQ by 36.03% under matched views, while relation reasoning improves mR@50 by 11.20% under PredCls with ground-truth masks and object labels. The full framework improves mR@50 by 52.99% un-der SGDet with predicted panoptic segments. These results demonstrate the value of spherical geometry for panoramic PSGG. 

Index Terms — panoptic scene graph generation, panop-tic segmentation, spherical geometry 

1. INTRODUCTION 

Panoptic scene graph generation (PSGG) represents scene entities with segmentation masks and directed semantic re-lations [1]. Panoramas capture full-scene entities and inter-actions in one observation. Prior work exploits panoramic context for holistic 3D understanding [2, 3] and studies omni-range context [4], projection distortion [5, 6], and spherical geometry [7, 8] in panoramic segmentation. PSGG mod-els address predicate imbalance [9], pair selection [10], and mask-aware relation modeling and ranking through DSFormer [11], but largely assume perspective images. Panoramic PSGG must therefore model spherical geome-try in both entity representation and relation prediction. 2. METHOD 

Panoramic PSGG maps an RGB ERP image I ∈ RH×W ×3 to a directed graph G = ( V, E), where H and W are the image height and width. Each node vi = ( Mi, c i, b i) ∈ V contains a 

thing or stuff mask Mi, object label ci, and bounding box bi.Each directed edge assigns a semantic predicate to an ordered subject–object pair. Spherical geometry-aware panoptic seg-mentation (SGPS) constructs panoramic nodes, and spherical geometry-aware relation reasoning (SGRR) predicts their di-rected relations. 3. EXPERIMENTS 3.1. PanoPSG Dataset and Experimental Setup 

We introduce PanoPSG, a high-resolution panoramic panop-tic scene graph dataset comprising 340 ERP panoramas, each with full 360 ◦ coverage and a resolution of 11968 × 5984 

pixels (Table 1). Reviewer-verified masks were delineated in CVAT, while a dedicated platform supported high-resolution relation annotation, persistent node identities, and multi-ple predicates per ordered pair. A second review checked endpoint validity, duplicate triplets, and mask–relation align-ment. Predicate classifica-tion (PredCls) uses ground-truth masks and object labels, iso-lating relation reasoning from segmentation errors. Although the annotations permit multiple predicates for each ordered pair, graph-constrained evaluation retains only the highest-scoring predicate [1, 11]. Recall at K (R@ K) measures the recovery of ground-truth triplets among the top-K predictions per image. Mean recall (mR@ K) macro-averages predicate-specific recall over the 21 predicates, providing a predicate-balanced complement to aggregate retrieval [14, 11]. We report K ∈ { 20 , 50 } over 1,325 validation relations. Under scene graph detection (SGDet), node matching requires iden-tical object labels and original-resolution mask IoU > 0.5.Unmatched predictions remain in the top-K ranking. To-gether, PredCls and SGDet distinguish relation-reasoning gains from improvements inherited from panoramic node construction while retaining the same recall-based evalua-tion. 

3.2. Spherical Geometry-Aware Panoptic Segmentation 

Table 2 compares first-stage panoptic segmentation methods on PanoPSG. Direct ERP, Cubemap-6, Basic Fusion, and our method use the same frozen MaskDINO segmentation model. Basic Fusion applies basic fusion to the best-performing single-FoV configuration. Our method leads all five metrics in Table 2. Compared with Basic Fusion under the same view configuration, it im-proves PQ by 10.41 percentage points, corresponding to a relative improvement of 36.03%. It also gains 10.07 points in Sphere-mIoU, 12.30 points in Seam-mIoU, and 5.98 points in mIR@0.5. With the segmentation model and view sam-pling held fixed, these results demonstrate the contribution of reliability-weighted spherical association and fusion to panoptic quality, spherical semantic accuracy, and seam con-tinuity.

4. CONCLUSION 

We presented a two-stage spherical geometry-aware panoramic PSGG framework combining SGPS and SGRR, and intro-duced PanoPSG with 340 panoramas, 10,571 nodes, and 6,632 directed relations. Against corresponding baselines, SGPS improves PQ by 36.03% under matched views, SGRR improves mR@50 by 11.20% under PredCls, and the full framework improves mR@50 by 52.99% under SGDet. SGPS improves graph-node consistency, while SGRR re-duces sensitivity to latitude-dependent area and seam place-ment. These complementary gains validate spherical ge-ometry for panoramic PSGG.
