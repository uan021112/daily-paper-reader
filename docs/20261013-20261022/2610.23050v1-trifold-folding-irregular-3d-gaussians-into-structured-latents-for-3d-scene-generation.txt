Abstract
Recent progress in 3D generation has motivated growing interest in compact generative
representations of complex 3D scenes. For native 3D Gaussian scenes, however, learning
such representations remains challenging due to widely varying primitive counts, nonuni-
form spatial density, and differences in geometry and appearance even among spatially
neighboring primitives. In this work, we introduce TriFold, a native 3D Gaussian VAE
that encodes Gaussian scenes into a fixed-layout triplane latent and jointly reconstructs
their geometry and appearance. The key insight is to impose structural regularity on the
latent space while preserving the spatial adaptivity of the underlying Gaussians. Dur-
ing encoding, we use cell-wise learned queries to aggregate projected Gaussians into
multiple slots per cell, reducing the information loss caused by single-feature pooling.
During decoding, occupancy prediction identifies active regions, where query-based set
prediction and candidate selection reconstruct local Gaussian sets with spatially varying
primitive counts. Building on this representation, we train a latent flow-matching model
for conditional scene-level 3DGS generation, enabling point-cloud-conditioned synthesis
and single-image-conditioned amodal generation. Experiments demonstrate that TriFold
achieves high-fidelity reconstruction and generation from a single image that provides
only a partial observation of the scene, validating its effectiveness for native 3D Gaussian
representation learning. https://fantasy-amap.github.io/TriFold
1 Introduction
Amodal 3D scene generation aims to synthesize complete environments from partial observations, en-
abling exploration and embodied interaction beyond captured views [1–9]. This requires a representation
that captures complex 3D structure while remaining tractable for generative modeling. Recent scene-level
systems use compositional neural fields, progressive expansion, sparse volumes, or latent triplanes to
model room-scale and unbounded environments [10–16]. Recent advances increasingly rely on structured
latent representations that reorganize irregular 3D signals into formats compatible with generative mod-
els [17–21]. However, extending this paradigm to native scene-level 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) [22]
poses distinct representation challenges. Scene-level Gaussian distributions are heterogeneous: globally,
scenes can differ by orders of magnitude in primitive count and exhibit highly nonuniform spatial density;
locally, spatially adjacent Gaussians suffer from severe attribute heterogeneity: neighboring primitives
often differ sharply in geometry and appearance [23]. These variations motivate a latent representation
that is structured for generative modeling while preserving scene-specific Gaussian cardinality, spatial
distributions, and fine-grained local attributes.

tural paradigms. Quantized-grid methods, such as L3DG [24] and GaussianGPT [25], discretize Gaussians

token methods instead regularize globally. Can3Tok [26] resamples each scene into a fixed-cardinality

While highly effective for bounded, object-centric assets with comprehensive visual coverage, extend-
ing this paradigm to expansive scene-level 3DGS is non-trivial: complex occlusions across broad scenes
complicate exhaustive multiview feature anchoring, while scaling sparse voxel grids and multi-stage
pipelines over unconstrained volumes introduces notable computational and representation overhead.
Finally, continuous-field methods, such as DiffGS [27], reparameterize Gaussians as continuous spa-
tial fields via feature interpolation; yet, this bias toward spatial smoothness can conflict with the high-
frequency variations in attributes such as scale and rotation across neighboring primitives. Consequently,
an open challenge remains: how can we provide generative backbones with a regular, fixed-layout latent
space, while preserving the unconstrained, variable-length nature of native Gaussian representations at
both the input and output?
To bridge this gap, we present TriFold, a variational autoencoder that operates directly on native 3D
Gaussian primitives for scene-level generative modeling. Our key design principle is to impose structural
regularity in the latent space that jointly represents geometry and appearance, and preserve the flexible,
content-adaptive organization of native Gaussian primitives. Specifically, the encoder maps variable-
cardinality, non-uniformly distributed Gaussian sets into fixed-layout triplane latents. It first extracts
context-aware primitive features with a point transformer. Since multiple features may project to the same
triplane cell, we use multiple learned queries per cell to aggregate local information. Then, we use Cross-
plane attention to integrate information across planes through shared Gaussian projections, followed by a
variational bottleneck that produces triplane latents.
To recover native 3DGS from this structured latent, our decoder adopts a set-based decoding paradigm
without rigid 3D discretization. An occupancy predictor first localizes active regions, while an optimal-
transport-guided set decoder dynamically predicts and filters Gaussian candidates within each region.

Because the predefined query count serves only as a local capacity upper bound rather than a fixed al-
location, the spatial allocation and primitive cardinality remain adaptive to scene content. We supervise
Gaussian centers through optimal-transport matching, while the remaining geometric and appearance at-
tributes are constrained by a full-scene rendering loss. Together, these objectives enable accurate Gaussian
reconstruction with high rendering fidelity, providing a regular tensor interface for generative modeling
while retaining the flexible organization of native 3DGS.
Building on this structured latent space, we train a latent flow-matching model for conditional scene-
level 3DGS generation. We consider two conditioning settings: point-cloud-conditioned synthesis from
complete spatial observations and single-image-conditioned amodal generation. In the latter, a single
image provides only a partial observation of the scene, requiring the learned scene priors to preserve
observed content while inferring plausible geometry and appearance in unobserved regions. Experiments
demonstrate high-fidelity reconstruction and generation in both settings, demonstrating the effectiveness
of structured triplane latents for native 3D Gaussian scene generation.
• A native scene-level 3D Gaussian variational autoencoder. We introduce TriFold, which maps
variable-cardinality Gaussian scenes into a fixed-layout triplane latent and decodes them back into
content-adaptive native 3DGS.
• Unified geometry-appearance modeling. Our unified representation jointly encodes scene geometry
and appearance, and we reconstruct both from the same representation through an occupancy predictor
and a query-based set decoder.
• Conditional scene-level 3DGS generation based on TriFold. We train a latent flow-matching model
on the learned triplane latents, enabling both point-cloud-conditioned synthesis and single-image-
conditioned amodal generation with plausible geometry and appearance in unobserved regions.
2 Related Work
3D Representations and Generative Models.
The choice of scene representation shapes the fidelity
and scalability of 3D generative models. Meshes represent explicit surfaces through vertices and faces,
while voxel grids discretize space into volumetric cells storing occupancy or other attributes, with dense
grids incurring cubic memory growth with resolution [28–32]. Neural radiance fields represent scenes as
continuous functions and support high-quality novel-view synthesis, but require repeated neural evalua-
tions during rendering [33–35]. 3DGS instead uses adaptive explicit primitives for differentiable real-time
rendering [22]. Representative generative approaches include π-GAN for implicit radiance fields [36],
SDFusion for volumetric SDF grids [37], GET3D and MeshGPT for explicit meshes [38, 39], and
3DShape2VecSet, Michelangelo, and TripoSG for generation in learned 3D latent spaces [18, 40, 41].
These properties make 3DGS attractive for generative modeling. Native 3DGS, however, represents each
scene as an unordered set whose cardinality and spatial density adapt to scene content. This organization
is mismatched with latent diffusion and flow-matching backbones, which typically operate on fixed-shape
tensors [42, 43]. Triplanes offer a regular layout with a practical balance between spatial expressiveness
and computational efficiency and have been widely used for 3D-aware generation [44, 45]. The central
challenge is therefore to map adaptive Gaussian sets into such fixed-layout latents while retaining their
scene-dependent cardinality and spatial density during reconstruction.
Latent Representations for 3D Gaussians.
We focus on methods that regularize Gaussian represen-
tations or construct generative 3D latents with Gaussian decoders. This differs from image-conditioned
3DGS methods, which directly predict Gaussian representations from one or more images for recon-
struction or content creation [46–53]. Within this generative latent setting, existing approaches differ
primarily in how they impose structural regularity. Grid-based methods impose explicit spatial organiza-
tion: GaussianCube rearranges a fixed number of Gaussians onto voxel grids through optimal transport,

Amodal reconstruction and generative 3D representations. Recovering complete 3D structure from
partial visual observations is an important setting for generative scene modeling. NOVA3R [4] uses
non-pixel-aligned scene tokens and a generative point-cloud decoder to reconstruct visible and occluded
geometry, while VolFill [5] learns a volumetric generative prior for single-view amodal scene reconstruc-
tion. GaussianAnything [56] explores point-cloud-structured latent generation for 3D objects. In contrast,
TriFold targets a direct Gaussian-to-latent-to-Gaussian interface for native scene-level 3DGS, jointly rep-
resenting geometry and appearance while recovering content-adaptive primitive distributions and counts.

4.2 Scene Reconstruction
In this section, we evaluate how well the Gaussian VAE preserves scene appearance and geometry. We
compare against Can3Tok [26], DiffGS [27], and GaussianGPT [25], which respectively use canonical
tokens, continuous feature fields, and voxel-quantized representations. Table 1 reports rendering fidelity
(PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS) and geometric reconstruction (CD). Our method achieves the best results across

provided in Appendix Figure 8, and Appendix A.9 evaluates our VAE’s latent smoothness and robust-
ness. Using official implementations under the same scene-level training and evaluation protocol, adapted

similar coarse structures (Appendix Figure 9).

4.3 Scene Generation
To evaluate the generative utility of the learned TriFold latent, we train latent flow-matching models under
full-point-cloud and single-image conditioning. Our latent supports generation from complete spatial ob-
servations and amodal completion from a single view, while decoding to content-adaptive native 3DGS.
As existing 3DGS generators typically adopt different conditioning protocols, direct comparisons require
method-specific adaptation; we focus on evaluating generation within our conditional setup rather than
benchmarking across generative methods. Qualitatively, Figures 5 and 6 show plausible scene geometry
and appearance across widely separated views. In particular, the single-image model synthesizes plau-
sible room structure and content beyond the input view. Additional samples are provided in Figures 13
and 14. Quantitatively, Table 2 shows better observed-region appearance under full-point-cloud condi-
tioning, while the single-image model achieves an unobserved-region FID of 67.81 and a COV-CD of
85.24%. Since a single image only partially observes the scene, plausible completions may differ from
the reference in unobserved regions.

5 Conclusion
We introduced TriFold, a native scene-level 3D Gaussian VAE that maps Gaussian sets with adaptive
cardinality and density into fixed-layout triplane latents. Rather than regularizing the Gaussian repre-
sentation itself, the encoder captures fine-grained structure using cell-wise learned queries and primitive-
anchored cross-plane attention, while the decoder predicts local Gaussian sets with scene-dependent allo-
cations. The resulting latent jointly represents geometry and appearance and provides a regular interface
for flow-based generative modeling. Experiments demonstrate high-fidelity reconstruction across diverse
and complex scenes and show that the learned latent supports conditional scene generation, including
amodal completion from partial observations.

Challenges of Native Scene-level 3DGS Modeling.
As illustrated in Fig. 7, scene-level 3DGS exhibits
substantial variations in both global primitive cardinality and local spatial density. Across scene crops,
total primitive counts vary by more than a factor of four, while the number of Gaussians within occupied
voxels follows a pronounced heavy-tailed distribution. Compounded by severe clutter and complex occlu-
sions inherent to indoor environments, these structural irregularities are inherently mismatched with the
fixed-dimensional tensor structures required by standard latent generative models. A useful latent repre-
sentation should therefore regularize the representation layout while preserving content-adaptive Gaussian
allocation in the decoded scene.
By native 3DGS modeling, we specifically refer to a Gaussian-to-latent-to-Gaussian representation inter-
face: our autoencoder directly ingests pre-fitted Gaussian sets and reconstructs content-adaptive Gaussian

A.1 Data Preparation
Gaussian Scene Normalization.
Each input scene is represented as an unordered set of fitted Gaussian
primitives. A primitive consists of 59 values: a 3D center, 48 spherical-harmonic coefficients for degree-3
appearance, one opacity logit, three anisotropic log-scales, and a four-dimensional rotation quaternion.
We use dataset-specific spatial normalization while maintaining a common canonical domain for the Gaus-
sian VAE. For InteriorGS, each sample is constructed from a predefined 3D crop with a fixed spatial extent
of B = 8. We translate the Gaussian centers by the corresponding crop center and apply the fixed trans-
formation TB that maps this crop to the canonical VAE domain. Because B is fixed, the spatial scale of
this transformation is identical across all InteriorGS scenes. For Aria, which is used for Gaussian VAE
TriFold
Page 17 of 30


AMAP CV Lab | Alibaba Group
evaluation but not for conditional diffusion training, no fixed B = 8 crop is applied; we instead determine
a per-scene normalization scale from robust statistics of the Gaussian centers to map the valid scene extent
into the same canonical domain.
In both cases, the model operates on a padded cubic domain of side length 1.1, discretized at a resolution of
2563. The corresponding spatial transformation is applied consistently to Gaussian centers and occupancy-
sample coordinates, and Gaussian log-scales are adjusted by the logarithm of its scale factor. Opacity,
appearance, and rotation remain unchanged.
For set-level matching during Gaussian VAE training, an over-populated voxel retains at most 24 target
primitives using an opacity- and scale-aware selection heuristic. This local selection is used only to con-
struct the bounded set-level target; rendering supervision is computed from the complete fitted Gaussian
scene before this selection.
Occupancy Targets Caching.
The occupancy predictor is supervised by sampled 3D locations rather
than by dense enumeration of all 2563 voxels. For each scene, we construct a nearest-center distance field
from the preprocessed Gaussian centers and draw samples across four distinct regions: exact Gaussian
centers, a thin inner shell, a near-surface transition shell, and far-field points. Center and inner-shell sam-
ples represent occupied space, the transition shell decays linearly toward zero, and far-field samples serve
as negatives. In our experimental configuration, the two shell radii correspond to 0.47 and 1.19 voxels at
resolution 256, and the four groups account for 15%, 20%, 35%, and 30% of the cached samples, respec-
tively. Samples outside the padded model domain are removed after applying the corresponding scene

DETR-style set decoder.
Since the decoder must decode a regular triplane into an irregular point set

fixed-index regression. First, an occupancy predictor identifies candidate voxels. For each candidate,
24 learned queries cross-attend to the three sampled plane contexts to explain the local Gaussian set.
Separate task heads predict the primitive’s offset, scale, rotation, spherical-harmonic appearance, opacity,
and objectness. Sinkhorn matching establishes an order-independent correspondence to the target set
(Sec. A.3); unmatched queries are routed to a dummy target. At inference time, inactive candidates are
dynamically pruned based on their predicted objectness and opacity.

algorithm and is supervised via a balanced binary cross-entropy loss. During rendering and inference,

training, the renderer utilizes the union of OT-matched queries and those queries whose predicted scores

the rendering objective, rather than being shielded by the target assignment.
Because candidate enumeration inherently involves a discrete thresholding step, we do not differentiate
through the set membership operation itself. Instead, the occupancy, objectness, opacity, and activation
scores governing this set are jointly optimized using differentiable surrogate losses aligned with the infer-
ence rule. As a result, the threshold functions as a deployment decision based on learned scores, rather
than a manually tuned, post-hoc pruning heuristic.

Phantom Voxels Regularization.
If the decoder were trained exclusively on ground-truth occupied vox-
els, it would never encounter false positives, even though the learned occupancy proposer will inevitably
produce them during inference. This train-test domain gap would cause queries at erroneous locations
to decode “ghost” Gaussians with high objectness or opacity. We bridge this gap by deliberately feeding
selected false-positive proposals to the decoder as phantom voxels. Because they contain no target Gaus-
sians, all queries within a phantom voxel receive negative objectness supervision, forcing their activation
and opacity to be heavily suppressed. Phantom training commences after the initial occupancy warm-up
phase; we subsequently alternate between ground-truth and predicted occupancy supports, enabling the
decoder to master both accurate local reconstruction and robust recovery from proposer errors. Although
sampling the phantom set is non-differentiable, all losses evaluated on the selected queries are strictly
differentiable with respect to the decoder and its prediction heads. This mechanism operates analogously
to hard-negative mining, directly optimizing the network against failure cases it will realistically face at
test time.

Geometric evaluation of degenerate predictions.
We evaluate center-wise Chamfer Distance (CD) for
all methods using the same preprocessing and the common normalized scene coordinate frame, without
method-specific post-hoc alignment. Under our scene-level adaptation, the lower-fidelity baseline solu-

reference geometry. We retain these errors in the geometric evaluation rather than realigning the predic-

Accordingly, the large CD values of the affected baselines reflect their substantial geometric degradation
rather than differences in the evaluation protocol. Rendering-based metrics (PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS) are eval-

metrics are computed under a common evaluation protocol, and degraded baseline predictions are reported
directly rather than removed or assigned special-case values.

A.6 Training and Evaluation Details
Gaussian VAE training.
We jointly train the PTv3 encoder, the variational triplane autoencoder, the
occupancy predictor, and the local Gaussian set decoder end to end. The latent representation comprises
four slots, each with 16 channels per plane, at a spatial resolution of 2562. The decoder predicts K = 24
candidate Gaussians per selected voxel, with the active subset determined by predicted objectness. The
overall objective combines occupancy, center reconstruction, objectness, rendering, and phantom-voxel
suppression losses, together with KL regularization. As detailed in the main paper, optimal-transport
matching operates solely on Gaussian centers and provides both the correspondence for center supervision
and the soft objectness targets. Among the Gaussian attributes, only the normalized center coordinates are
directly supervised under this correspondence, with a loss weight of 5. Scale, rotation, appearance, and
opacity receive no direct matched-attribute supervision and are instead constrained through differentiable
rendering, with additional opacity suppression on phantom voxels.
For the remaining objectives, the point-sampled and voxel-level occupancy losses use weights of 1 and
0.5, respectively; the objectness BCE and threshold-aligned activation losses use weights of 1 and 0.3; and

gradient norms are clipped to 1.

We train a Wan-style latent transformer [71] consisting of 30 blocks,
a hidden width of 1536, a feed-forward width of 8960, and 12 attention heads. The four-slot latent is

0.9999, and clip gradients to unit norm. The reported generator is trained for 100k steps.

performance gap between K = 24 and K = 32 empirically suggests that this bounded local capacity is
not a major bottleneck for reconstruction fidelity under our data distribution.

A.9 Latent Representation Analysis
To evaluate the structural integrity and generative suitability of the learned VAE latent space, we analyze
its smoothness and robustness through interpolation and perturbation. For interpolation, we linearly inter-
polate between two distinct scene latents, zα = (1 −α)zA + αzB. As shown in Fig. 12(a), the decoded
triplane features exhibit smooth, continuous semantic transitions along the interpolation path, confirming
a well-regularized latent manifold.
To assess robustness, we systematically inject Gaussian noise, apply element-wise dropout, and enforce
structured masking on the latent codes, subsequently quantifying the resulting degradation relative to the
unperturbed reconstruction. We further visualize the decoded triplane features under increasing mask-
ing ratios, where selected latent components are zeroed out. As shown in Fig. 12(b), the representation
remains highly resilient under moderate perturbations: the decoded scene is largely preserved under Gaus-
sian noise up to σ = 0.5, gracefully degrading only at extreme noise levels (σ = 1.0).

Limitations.
Our current framework is primarily limited by the scale and diversity of available scene-
level 3DGS training data. In contrast to image and video VAEs and generative models, which can benefit
from substantially larger and more diverse training corpora, our Gaussian VAE and latent flow model are
trained on a relatively limited collection of 3D scenes. This restricts the diversity of the learned scene
prior and may limit generalization to scene distributions that differ substantially from the training data.

We expect larger and more diverse 3DGS datasets to further improve both representation learning and
generative modeling. A second limitation concerns physical scene scale. To obtain a consistent spatial
coordinate system, we construct all training samples using the same fixed-size 3D crop and map them
to a canonical triplane domain. While this fixed-scale normalization stabilizes representation learning, it
also ties the learned representation to the physical scale used during training, and scenes at substantially
different spatial scales may not be represented optimally without additional normalization. Extending
TriFold to scale-conditioned or multi-scale representations, together with training on larger 3DGS corpora,
is therefore an important direction for improving its generalization to more diverse real-world scenes.
