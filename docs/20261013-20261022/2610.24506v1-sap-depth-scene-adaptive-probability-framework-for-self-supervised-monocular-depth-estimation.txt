Abstract
Self-supervised monocular depth estimation remains challenging due to the loss
of fine-grained details and limited adaptability to varying scene structures. In
this paper, we propose SAP-Depth, a novel architecture based on Scene-Adaptive
Probabilistic. Unlike traditional methods, we introduce a Dynamic Bins mecha-
nism powered by a Learnable Parameter Predictor, which adaptively estimates
scene-specific depth distributions conditioned on the input image context. Fur-
thermore, a Bin-wise Probability Distribution Decoder is designed to effectively
fuse multi-scale representations to predict dense depth probability distributions,
significantly enhancing boundary sharpness and structural recovery. Through the
collaborative coupling of these proposed modules, our model achieves dynamic
prediction of scene-specific depth distributions. Extensive experiments on the
KITTI dataset show that SAP-Depth outperforms existing state-of-the-art meth-
ods in accuracy, particularly in its ability to better preserve complex edge details.
Further, tests on the Make3D dataset validate the model’s excellent generalization
capability and robustness.

1 Introduction
Monocular depth estimation is a fundamental and critical task in computer vision, aim-
ing to recover 3D scene depth information from a single RGB image. This technology
is core to numerous applications, including autonomous driving, 3D reconstruction [2],
Augmented Reality (AR) [3], and robotic navigation [4]. However, inferring 3D depth
from 2D imagery is an inherently ill-posed problem, plagued by scale ambiguity and
geometric challenges.
Although the advent of deep learning has significantly advanced monocular
depth estimation, the early supervised learning paradigm heavily relies on dense
ground-truth depth data [8, 9, 19]. To alleviate the dependence on annotated data,
self-supervised learning has emerged as an attractive alternative. These methods for-
mulate depth estimation as a novel view synthesis problem, training by minimizing
photometric reconstruction loss without requiring dense ground truth [5, 10]. Pioneer-
ing works [5, 10] established a robust framework that jointly trains depth and pose
networks, leveraging geometric constraints from adjacent video frames or stereo pairs
and adopting photometric reprojection error as the sole supervision signal. Mainstream
self-supervised methods typically treat the task as a pixel-wise continuous regression
problem, which often leads to over-smoothed depth predictions and fails to preserve
object boundaries [10, 13]. Designing a model that concurrently achieves the data effi-
ciency of self-supervision and the high accuracy of supervised paradigms remains an
open challenge.
To address these limitations, we propose a scene-adaptive probability framework
based on dynamic depth bins, aiming to enrich existing frameworks. Unlike traditional
regression methods, we formulate depth estimation as a ”classification-regression”
task, enabling effective end-to-end training without depth ground truth to generate
more accurate depth maps. To enable self-supervised monocular depth estimation to
learn from more accurate reprojected images as supervisory signals, we meticulously
design a Bin-wise Probability Distribution (BPD) decoder. Combined with depth bins,
this design allows our framework to adaptively generate appropriate depth intervals
and their corresponding probability distributions for diverse scenes.
The main contributions of this paper are as follows:
• We propose SAP-Depth, a multi-scale perception framework that leverages low-
resolution deep semantic features to guide depth binning prediction. This approach

• We design a Bin-wise Probability Distribution (BPD) decoder, which integrates

2 Related Work
2.1 Supervised Monocular Depth Estimation
Supervised monocular depth estimation typically uses an encoder-decoder architecture
to directly regress depth values [8, 9, 19]. While recent advancements in Vision Trans-
formers for dense prediction (ViT) [15], diffusion models (e.g., Marigold [17]), and
large-scale data-driven models (e.g., Depth Anything [34]) have achieved state-of-the-
art results by enhancing global context modeling and scaling up training distributions,
they still fundamentally rely on extensive depth ground-truth priors and exhibit
relatively high computational complexity.
2.2 Self-Supervised Monocular Depth Estimation
To alleviate the need for dense annotations, self-supervised methods formulate depth
estimation as a novel view synthesis problem, optimizing photometric reprojection
loss from stereo pairs or monocular videos [12, 16, 22]. Recent studies have further
improved performance by designing detail-enhancing modules [21] or incorporating
diffusion models to refine boundaries [18]. However, without explicit ground-truth
constraints, mainstream self-supervised models that treat the task as continuous
regression still struggle with over-smoothed outputs and blurred object boundaries.

3 Proposed Method
In this section, we elaborate on the details of our proposed self-supervised monocular
depth estimation framework. Our proposed Bin-wise Probability Distribution (BPD)
decoder integrates multi-scale feature fusion with an adaptive probabilistic binning
strategy to adaptively predict depth information across diverse scenes. This effectively
addresses the issues of fine-grained detail degradation and the insufficient adaptability
of models to varied scene structures. We first present the overall pipeline of the system
in Section 3.1. Subsequently, we detail the architecture of the Depth Network in Section
3.2. Finally, we describe the Pose Network and the self-supervised training in Section
3.3.
3.1 Design Motivation
Existing self-supervised monocular depth estimation methods are plagued by two key
limitations: they often lose fine-grained structural details (e.g., object edges and tex-
ture boundaries) during depth regression, and they lack adaptive modeling capabilities
to handle the diversity of real-world scenes with varying layouts, depth ranges, and
object scales. To address these issues, we propose SAP-Depth, which integrates a Hier-
archical Adaptive Probabilistic Feature Aggregation Module (HAP-FAM) with a core
Learnable Parameter Predictor. This predictor dynamically captures scene-specific
semantic and geometric features from input images, generating adaptive depth model-
ing parameters tailored to each scene, and thus enables the network to predict denser,
more accurate depth maps with well-preserved structural details.

Depth decoder. We design a Bin-wise Probability Distribution (BPD) decoder
through the integration of multi-scale feature fusion and an adaptive probabilistic bin-
ning strategy. This architecture is composed of three synergistic components, namely
the Learnable Parameter Predictor (LP Predictor), the Hierarchical Adaptive Proba-
bilistic Feature Aggregation Module (HAP-FAM), and the Depth Probabilistic Volume
(DPV). The decoder takes multi-scale features from the HRNet encoder as input to
dynamically aggregate global and local contextual information. Finally, it outputs
dense depth maps via a linear combination of probability distributions and the cen-
ters of depth bins, which effectively recovers continuous depth values from the discrete
probability volumes.
Theoretical Advantage of Probabilistic Bins. Mathematically, formulating
depth estimation as a classification-regression task via dynamic bins presents pro-
found optimization advantages over direct continuous regression. Direct regression
relies on minimizing photometric error over depth D, a landscape that is highly non-
convex due to local texture ambiguities and occlusions. By evaluating probabilities
across discrete depth bins and aggregating them via expectation, the BPD decoder
essentially performs a continuous relaxation of the non-convex objective. This mech-
anism softens local minima, leading to a much smoother optimization trajectory.
Furthermore, standard continuous regression inherently assumes a unimodal distri-
bution of depth uncertainty, causing severe ”flying pixel” artifacts around object
boundaries where depth jumps abruptly. Conversely, our probability volume explicitly
models multimodal uncertainty, assigning sharp probabilities at discontinuous edges
without averaging foreground and background. This leads to the remarkable boundary-
preserving capability of our method. The detailed forward process is formalized in
Algorithm 1.
LP Predictor. The LP Predictor fuses multi-scale features to estimate dynamic
depth ranges and bin distributions. It first standardizes input channels via 1×1 convo-
lutions, upsamples and concatenates them to the finest resolution, and then compresses
the fused features into a global vector via adaptive pooling. Finally, two parallel
heads generate normalized bin widths and Sigmoid-activated depth range parameters,
respectively. Specifically, to filter out redundant low-level details and focus on scene
semantics, it extracts the top three high-level multi-scale features from the HRNet
encoder (with channel dimensions of 36, 72, and 144). The initial 1×1 convolutions
standardize these features to a uniform 64 channels, which are then concatenated into
a 192-channel fused tensor before being compressed back to 64 channels and pooled.
HAP-FAM. First, Hierarchical Adaptive Probabilistic Feature Aggregation Mod-
ule (HAP-FAM) processes encoder-derived multi-scale features with parallel con-
volutional blocks and 1×1 convolutional layers to unify channel dimensions, then
upsamples/downsamples features to align resolutions, and aggregates features across
scales through additive fusion at multiple hierarchical levels. Specifically, it operates
on all five multi-scale features (with original channels of 64, 18, 36, 72, and 144).
By executing progressive additive fusion across four hierarchical stages, the module
effectively embeds high-frequency structural details from shallow layers (e.g., 64 and
18 channels) into the semantic representations of deeper layers, ultimately project-

3.3 Pose Network and Self-Supervised Training
Following standard practices in self-supervised depth estimation [10], we use a ResNet-
18 based architecture for the Pose Network. It accepts a pair of images (It, It′)
concatenated along the channel dimension. The network predicts the 6-DoF relative
camera pose Tt→t′, consisting of a 3D translation vector and a 3D rotation vector
(represented by axis-angle). This pose is used to project the source image pixels onto
the target image plane. Our model is trained in a self-supervised manner using a
combination of photometric reprojection loss and edge-aware smoothness loss.
Photometric Loss. Based on the predicted depth Dt and the estimated pose
Tt→t′, we synthesize the target image It′→t by warping the source image It′. The
photometric loss Lp measures the similarity between the original target image It and
the synthesized image It′→t. As introduced in [10], we use a combination of the L1

4 Experiments
4.1 Implementation Details
Our framework is implemented in PyTorch and trained on an NVIDIA RTX 4090
GPU for 20 epochs using AdamW. The learning rate uses a 3-epoch linear warmup
to 1 × 10−4, followed by a cosine decay to 1 × 10−6. We set the number of adaptive
depth bins to 256. The disparity smoothness weight is 10−3, and the photometric loss
combines SSIM and L1 with weights of 0.85 and 0.15, respectively.
4.2 Datasets and Evaluation Metrics
KITTI. The KITTI dataset [1] is the standard benchmark for outdoor depth estima-
tion. We adopt the data split of Eigen et al. [31], which contains 39,180 monocular
triplets for training, 4,424 for validation, and 697 for testing.
Make3D. To evaluate the generalization ability of our model, we use the Make3D
dataset [20] for zero-shot testing. It consists of 134 test images representing outdoor
scenes.
Metrics. We report the results using the standard metrics used in prior works [10]:
absolute relative error (Abs Rel), squared relative error (Sq Rel), root mean squared
error (RMSE), root mean squared logarithmic error (RMSE log), and threshold
accuracy (δ < 1.25, 1.252, 1.253).
Baselines. Due to variations in evaluation protocols across existing literature, we
carefully select representative self-supervised approaches to benchmark our model’s
performance. These baselines span a wide spectrum of architectures, ranging from
computationally intensive frameworks built upon Vision Transformers for dense pre-
diction (ViT) [15] backbones to highly efficient lightweight networks. Furthermore, we
extend our comparison to include recent state-of-the-art foundation models, such as
Depth Anything [34], to explicitly demonstrate the necessity and efficacy of in-domain
self-supervised training.

The benefit of BPD decoder. To verify the necessity of our discretization
design, the proposed binning strategy is substituted with a standard continuous

optimization difficulties and yielding sharper geometric structures.
The benefit of LP Predictor. Removing the dynamic binning mechanism causes
a substantial performance degradation, suggesting that standard static inference fails
to capture intricate depth variations across diverse environments. In contrast, the
LP Predictor acts as a learnable perceiver, actively aligning the depth probability
distribution with the perceived scene context for better adaptability.
The benefit of HAP-FAM. Substituting HAP-FAM with basic upsampling
and concatenation leads to inferior performance across all metrics (Table 3). This
underscores the limitation of simple linear aggregation in bridging the semantic gap.
Conversely, HAP-FAM effectively captures complex cross-scale correlations, selec-

inference.

4.6 Ablation Study on Number of Bins
To rigorously evaluate the impact of the discretization resolution, we investigate
how the number of dynamic bins (N) affects the depth estimation performance. As
presented in Table 5, we conduct experiments ranging from 16 to 256 bins.
Increasing the number of bins generally enhances the resolution of the depth prob-
ability distribution. When scaling from 16 to 64 bins, the metrics show a consistent
improvement, with RMSE reaching a local minimum of 4.452 at N = 64. This indi-
cates that a finer discretization initially allows the network to capture more precise
depth transitions. Interestingly, at 128 bins, the model experiences a slight perfor-
mance fluctuation. This phenomenon suggests that naively increasing bin density can
temporarily introduce optimization ambiguities, where the network struggles to confi-
dently assign probabilities to overly narrow, adjacent bins without sufficient contextual
constraints.
However, when the resolution is further scaled to N = 256, the model success-
fully overcomes this bottleneck and achieves the best overall performance. Notably,
the 256-bin configuration significantly minimizes the Sq Rel metric to a remarkable
0.733 and achieves the highest threshold accuracy (δ < 1.25 of 0.898). Since Sq Rel
strongly penalizes large outlier errors, this substantial improvement indicates that 256
bins are exceptionally effective at resolving complex boundary ambiguities and dis-
tant structures. Although the 64-bin setting yields a marginally lower RMSE (4.452
vs. 4.453), the 256-bin model dominates in logarithmic depth errors (RMSElog) and
accuracy, demonstrating a much more robust and holistic structural preservation.
Finally, formulating the output as a dense probability volume P ∈RH×W ×N
introduces linear growth in the spatial-channel footprint. Scaling beyond 256 bins
inherently exceeds our current available computational resources and GPU memory
constraints during training. Therefore, configuring N = 256 yields the optimal balance,
maximizing structural detail preservation while remaining computationally feasible.

exhibit lower parameter counts and computational costs through customized Lite-

capacity, leading to sub-optimal structural accuracies (e.g., an Abs Rel of 0.107). Con-

computational burdens. In contrast, our SAP-Depth strikes an optimal trade-off: it
effectively prioritizes the rigorous preservation of high-resolution structural details by
adopting the HRNet-18 encoder, achieving a superior Abs Rel of 0.101 while requiring
merely 10.16M parameters and 15.92G FLOPs. Notably, SAP-Depth operates with
fewer FLOPs than the ResNet-50 based Monodepth2 (16.70G) and requires less than
half the computational cost of R-MSFM6 (34.5G).
As shown in the architectural analysis, despite incorporating the sophisticated
dynamic bins mechanism, SAP-Depth maintains an elegant and highly efficient
design through 1 × 1 feature compressions and global pooling operations. Out of
the 10.16M total parameters, the shared HRNet-18 encoder accounts for the vast
majority of the model capacity (approximately 9.57M parameters). In stark con-
trast, our customized Bin-wise Probability Distribution (BPD) decoder requires only
around 0.45M parameters, and the LP Predictor introduces a negligible overhead
of merely 0.14M parameters. This explicit parameter distribution evidently demon-
strates that our method achieves significant performance gains through an intelligently
formulated probabilistic framework rather than relying on a heavily parameterized,
computationally expensive decoder.

5 Conclusion
In this paper, we proposed SAP-Depth, a novel self-supervised monocular depth esti-
mation architecture designed to enhance object details and scene adaptation. By
synergistically integrating dynamic Bins with a HAP-FAM within a BPD decoder,
our method achieves adaptive depth interval fitting and refined cross-scale feature
representation. Extensive experiments on the KITTI dataset and zero-shot testing
on Make3D validate the superiority of our approach, demonstrating that SAP-Depth
effectively captures fine-grained details and maintains stable accuracy in complex
scenarios, thereby offering a reliable solution for real-world applications.
