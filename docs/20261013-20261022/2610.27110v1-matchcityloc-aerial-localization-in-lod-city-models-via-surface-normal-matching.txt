Abstract
Autonomous Unmanned Aerial Vehicle (UAV) navigation
in urban environments requires precise pose estimation.
Because Global Navigation Satellite System (GNSS) sig-
nals are often unreliable in cities, visual localization serves
as a crucial complement. Within this domain, textureless
Level-of-Detail (LoD) city models provide a lightweight
and privacy-preserving substitute for heavy 3D textured
meshes.
However, localizing against abstract geometry
is challenging. Existing pipelines typically score 2D sil-
houette overlaps, which can suffer from severe geomet-
ric ambiguities. We present MatchCityLoc, a framework
that shifts this paradigm to direct feature matching from
a noisy 6-DoF pose prior. By extracting surface normals
from the query image using a zero-shot foundation model
and matching them against rendered LoD normals, we es-
tablish explicit 3D-to-2D correspondences. This enables
a Perspective-n-Point (PnP) pose solve, which we itera-
tively refine through a render-and-match loop.
Experi-
ments demonstrate strong pose recall and accurate trans-
lation and rotation among the evaluated off-the-shelf LoD-
based methods with very few renderings. By bridging the
modality gap between aerial imagery and texture-free ge-
ometry, MatchCityLoc enables accurate localization using
lightweight, privacy-preserving city models.

Operating in these complex environments of-

While drones are equipped with Inertial Measurement Units
(IMUs), Global Navigation Satellite System (GNSS) does
not provide full pose information and its signals are often

Traditional visual localization methods depend heavily
on dense 3D maps or photogrammetry models [20, 25, 30].
While accurate, these representations are exceedingly
memory-intensive and expensive to acquire. They also pose
significant privacy concerns due to their high-fidelity tex-
tures [19, 34]. Alternatively, orthographic geodata provides
a more storage-efficient option [3, 4]. However, using or-
thophotos introduces a massive view gap. Orthophotos cap-
ture the scene from a strict nadir perspective, whereas UAVs
navigate with highly diverse viewpoints. This perspective
discrepancy, coupled with the lack of detailed 3D facade
geometry and vulnerability to radiometric shifts like cast
shadows, makes orthophoto-based matching challenging.
To overcome the heavy memory footprint of dense
meshes, recent approaches [18, 21, 34, 35] have turned
to Level-of-Detail (LoD) city models. These models are
globally available, lightweight, and inherently privacy-
preserving since they discard visual textures [10, 31, 34].

Despite these advantages, localization remains geometri-
cally ambiguous.
LoD-Loc [34] and LoD-Loc v2 [35]
align predicted wireframes or silhouettes with 2D render-
ings (Fig. 1a).
LoD-Loc v3 [21] adds instance segmen-
tation, while SemCityLoc [18] incorporates semantic sur-
faces and depth-based 3D cues to improve the pose-scoring
landscape. Concurrent PlanarCityLoc [5] goes further by
grounding matched planes in 3D for pose refinement, but
first establishes coarse alignment through render-and-score
search. We instead match surface normals directly from the
supplied pose prior and lift the matches to 3D, bypassing
this initial alignment search.
We address this ambiguity geometrically, introducing an
iterative “render-and-match” approach (Fig. 1b).
Rather
than relying on exhaustive silhouette alignment, MatchCity-
Loc solves the localization problem geometrically by per-
forming direct 2D-2D feature matching between the UAV
image and the LoD city model. At first glance, direct feature
matching might seem intractable because LoD models lack
visual textures. Our core insight is that surface orientation
bridges this modality gap. By extracting normal predictions
from the UAV image and matching them against rendered
surface normal maps from the LoD models, we can estab-
lish highly accurate correspondences. Surface normals in-
trinsically encode robust geometric cues that are invariant
to lighting and texture. This allows us to leverage off-the-
shelf foundation feature matching models [6, 7, 14, 23] to
find dense matches without any domain-specific training.
Building on this insight, MatchCityLoc introduces an it-
erative pose refinement framework. Given a noisy position-
and-orientation prior and known intrinsics, we render the
LoD normals. We then perform dense matching between
the predicted image normals and the rendered LoD nor-
mals. Next, we lift these 2D matches into explicit 3D cor-
respondences and solve for the updated camera pose via a
Perspective-n-Point (PnP) solver [8]. This loop iteratively
refines the pose, with most gains in two to three updates. By
capitalizing on the structural details of LoD2 and LoD3 city
models, our approach effectively overcomes the severe ge-
ometric ambiguities that plague silhouette-based methods.
In summary, our main contributions are:
• We introduce MatchCityLoc, an iterative render-and-
match framework for aerial localization that explicitly
solves the 3D pose, avoiding exhaustive silhouette search.
• We identify surface normals as an ideal geometric bridge
between real images and textureless LoD models, en-
abling zero-shot dense matching.
• We achieve strong zero-shot pose recall and accurate
translation and rotation on three benchmarks against off-
the-shelf LoD-based methods.
2. Related Work
Map-based aerial localization. Visual localization esti-
mates camera pose relative to a known map.
Classical
methods rely on memory-intensive Structure-from-Motion
(SfM) point clouds [25]. OrthoLoC [3] and OrthoTrack [4]
instead use lightweight 2.5D orthophotos and elevation
models.
Along this direction, AnyVisLoc [33] bench-
marks low-altitude, multi-view UAV localization against
2.5D aerial and satellite maps.
Such maps introduce a
substantial view gap between nadir reference imagery and
oblique queries.
MatchCityLoc instead localizes against
textureless LoD city models, retaining 3D facade geometry
without requiring map textures.
Mesh-based localization. Several methods align images
directly against 3D meshes. MeshLoc [20] demonstrated lo-
calization using raw, uncolored dense geometry, while oth-
ers formulated iterative render-and-compare pipelines over
textured meshes [32]. NormalLoc [1] learns retrieval and
matching between RGB queries and a database of rendered
normal images, then lifts matches to 3D for pose estima-
tion.
MatchCityLoc instead predicts query normals and
performs normal-to-normal matching from a supplied pose
prior, without a rendered-view database or training new re-
trieval and matching networks.
LoD-based localization.
Because LoD models lack vi-
sual texture, recent methods typically align abstract geo-
metric cues in 2D. LoD-Loc [34] aligns predicted wire-
frames with rendered edges, while LoD-Loc v2 [35] aligns
building silhouettes. Distinct poses can nevertheless yield
similar masks. LoD-Loc v3 [21] adds instance segmenta-
tion, and SemCityLoc [18] incorporates semantic surfaces
and depth-based 3D cues into pose scoring.
Concurrent
PlanarCityLoc [5] establishes explicit 2D-to-3D plane cor-
respondences for PnP refinement after coarse render-and-
score alignment and particle filtering. MatchCityLoc in-
stead matches normal maps directly from the pose prior and
lifts correspondences through rendered depth. This removes
the initial hypothesis search and requires only one rendered
view per refinement step.
Monocular geometry foundation models. Recent foun-
dation models like Metric3D v2 [15], GeoWizard [9], and
Lotus [13] can directly predict monocular depth and nor-
mals.
Monocular depth can suffer from scale errors in
aerial views [12]. Because surface orientation does not suf-
fer from scaling ambiguities, we utilize the MoGe-2 [29]
foundation model to extract normal maps zero-shot, re-
covering metric 3D coordinates from the LoD geometry
via feature matching.
Dense feature matching.
Traditional sparse matchers
like SuperGlue [24] and LightGlue [17] excel on tex-
tured imagery but can struggle in textureless environments.
Detector-free dense matchers like LoFTR [26] remove re-
liance on explicit keypoints.

4.1. Experimental Setup
Datasets. We benchmark on three challenging aerial local-
ization datasets:
• UAVD4L-LoD [34]: Mixed urban scenarios with LoD3
models. We evaluate all 1,604 in-Traj and 2,192 out-of-
Traj queries.
• Swiss-EPFL [34]: High-altitude sequences over dense
residential areas with complex topographies, evaluated on
in-Place and out-of-Place splits using LoD2 models. This
dataset contains systematic georeferencing errors [3, 18]
and temporal discrepancies between the aerial imagery
and the LoD models.
• SemCityLockeD [18]: Close-range urban canyon se-
quences. We evaluate 937 rectified queries with LoD2
and LoD3 models.
Evaluation metrics.
Following the established proto-
col [21, 34, 35], we report median translation (m) and ro-
tation (◦) errors, along with pose recall at three joint thresh-
olds: 2m-2◦, 3m-3◦, and 5m-5◦. All queries are evaluated,
including those for which refinement terminates early, using
the returned pose defined in Sec. 3.
Baselines. We compare against MC-Loc (with RoMa and
DINOv2 features), LoD-Loc [34], LoD-Loc v2 [35], LoD-
Loc v3 [21], and SemCityLoc [18].
We also include
the reported full-pipeline results of concurrent PlanarCity-
Loc [5], using MoGe-2 ViT-S and SAM3, in Tab. 1. Our re-
runs (†) use the publicly available nvdiffrast renderer [16]
for efficient, reproducible evaluation. Alongside off-the-
shelf segmentation, we evaluate oracle variants that replace
predicted masks with masks rendered at the ground-truth
pose. This isolates the effect of segmentation errors and
assesses each adapted method under idealized mask obser-
vations. These controlled re-runs are reported separately
from published results.

degrading matching.

5. Conclusion
We presented MatchCityLoc, a geometric approach for
aerial localization in textureless LoD city models.
By
framing localization as normal-to-normal feature match-
ing rather than pure 2D silhouette alignment, our method
reduces pose ambiguities in dense urban environments.
Leveraging foundation models, MatchCityLoc extracts sur-
face normals zero-shot, eliminating the need for dataset-
specific training.
Extensive evaluations demonstrate that
our PnP formulation achieves strong pose recall and
accurate translation and rotation among evaluated off-
the-shelf LoD-based methods and converges with very
few rendering iterations.

Supplementary Material
A. Overview
As demonstrated in the main paper, MatchCityLoc achieves
strong pose recall and accurate translation and rotation
without dataset-specific fine-tuning. This supplement pro-
vides implementation and evaluation details in Sec. B, fol-
lowed by runtime and memory analysis in Sec. C. We ex-
pand the main-paper modality and matcher ablations with
qualitative comparisons in Sec. D, then present additional
analyses of pose priors, map completeness, and normal pre-
diction quality in Sec. E. Finally, we extend the main-paper
limitations and discuss future work in Sec. F, followed by
ethical considerations in Sec. G.
B. Implementation and Evaluation Details
We specify the correspondence filtering, pose refinement,
and evaluation settings used in the experiments.
Pre-PnP match filtering. RoMa [6] with MINIMA [23]
weights samples up to 2,000 correspondences.
We dis-
card matches outside the rendered image or with invalid
depth. A robust homography fit with an 8-pixel threshold
requires at least ten consistent matches as a validity check.
All depth-valid matches are then lifted to 3D. Both normal
maps use the same camera-frame encoding, and rendered
depth is metric camera-z.
Pose solver parameters. We estimate pose with iterative
PnP within RANSAC, using an 8-pixel reprojection thresh-
old and at most 1,000 RANSAC iterations.
Early stopping. Refinement stops for insufficient corre-
spondences or an update exceeding 45 m or 25◦from the
initial pose prior. A failed PnP solve leaves the current pose
unchanged for the next iteration. Evaluation retains the last
accepted pose, or the prior if no update succeeds.
Evaluation protocol. We use the position-and-orientation
priors distributed with LoD-Loc [34] for UAVD4L-LoD
and Swiss-EPFL, and the perturbed SemCityLoc [18] pri-
ors for SemCityLockeD. Our method and adapted baselines
share these per-query priors without additional perturba-
tions. Their initial accuracy appears in the main tables. Syn-
thetic perturbations are introduced only in Sec. E.
Main evaluations allow three updates on UAVD4L-LoD
and five on Swiss-EPFL and SemCityLockeD. Modality
and matcher ablations use three updates and prior-noise
analysis two. The normal-predictor ablation uses four up-
dates for every predictor on both complete UAVD4L-LoD
splits. Budgets are fixed within each comparison. Unless a
subset is stated, all queries are evaluated at the last accepted
pose or the prior if no update succeeds.
Main and ablation configurations. The main compari-
son resizes query images to a 320-pixel longest side be-
fore MoGe-2 normal prediction. Component ablations use
standard predictor inference without this preliminary down-
sampling and matcher-specific input preprocessing. Both
resample normal maps to 240 × 320 pixels before match-
ing. Their reference rows therefore represent distinct infer-
ence configurations, not repeated measurements of the main
setup.
Oracle evaluation setup.
For LoD-Loc v2 [35], LoD-
Loc v3 [21], and SemCityLoc [18], oracle evaluations re-
place predicted segmentation with the masks required by
each method, rendered from the city model at the ground-
truth pose.
This removes segmentation prediction errors
and assesses pose estimation under idealized mask observa-
tions. Task-trained segmentation weights for LoD-Loc v2
and LoD-Loc v3, and the full InsLoD-Loc training set used
by LoD-Loc v3, were unavailable for our evaluation. The
public LoD-Loc v3 release provides example inference as-
sets rather than this full training set [22].
The public implementations of LoD-Loc v2 and LoD-
Loc v3 replace the original OpenSceneGraph-based ren-
derer with Blender because of project-specific intellectual
property restrictions [22, 36]. To make the repeated ren-
dering of pose hypotheses practical across the full evalua-
tion sets, we replace this Blender-based pipeline with GPU-
batched nvdiffrast [16]. We therefore report these evalua-
tions as adapted implementations, separately from the pub-
lished results. The oracle isolates mask prediction errors
within these adapted pipelines, rather than reproducing ev-
ery component of the original methods.

pose accuracy over the noisy prior on UAVD4L-LoD, where

whereas repetitive close-range views require tighter priors
or stronger geometric cues to overcome the lack of texture.

F. Limitations and Future Work
We expand on the limitations identified in the main paper,
with representative failure cases in Fig. A6.
Pose initialization and visibility. Refinement requires a
sufficiently close position-and-orientation prior with over-
lap between the query and rendered geometry. Large offsets
can place the initial rendering on a neighboring facade or
remove shared structures. Repetitive windows and nearly
identical buildings can then yield plausible but incorrect
correspondences, even with MINIMA [23] weights. In the
recall curves of Fig. A3, performance drops more sharply
on SemCityLockeD as the prior perturbations increase, in-
dicating a narrower recovery range on this dataset.
Geometric coverage and detail. Flat terrain and forests
lacking mapped buildings provide little discriminative LoD
geometry. Close-range views also require sufficient model
detail. LoD1 omits roof and facade structure, while LoD2
may lack the window recesses and wall articulation cap-
tured by LoD3. As illustrated in Fig. 7, coarse geometry
can therefore weaken matching even when its large-scale
alignment is correct.
Map accuracy and temporal change.
Georeferencing
errors or changes in the built environment can invalidate
the assumed image-to-map correspondence.
For exam-
ple, a construction site may differ substantially from the
completed building represented in the map.
The map-
completeness analysis shows tolerance to removing individ-
ual buildings when surrounding structures remain visible,
but does not imply robustness to widespread map changes.
Normal prediction quality. Localization depends on the
geometric consistency of zero-shot normal predictions. Se-
vere weather, challenging illumination, or motion blur can
distort these predictions and undermine matching.
Al-
though Fig. A5 shows tolerance to moderate angular errors,
biased predictions on the few visible structures can prevent
reliable pose refinement.
Computational cost.
Despite requiring few render-
ings, normal prediction and dense matching remain too
costly for real-time inference in the evaluated configura-
tion. Deployment on resource-constrained UAV platforms
requires further acceleration.
Future work.
Temporal filtering and visual-inertial pri-
ors could disambiguate repetitive structures and support
continuous tracking, using LoD geometry as an alterna-
tive to the orthophotos and elevation models in Ortho-
Track [4]. Updating city geometry from new observations
could reduce temporal mismatch. Improving normal pre-
diction under adverse imaging conditions and accelerat-
ing matching and rendering are further directions toward
reliable real-time deployment.
G. Ethical Considerations
Privacy and data usage. Our evaluation uses UAVD4L-
LoD [30, 34], Swiss-EPFL [34], and SemCityLockeD [18],
subject to their licenses. Although textureless maps omit
photographic detail and our method requires no dataset-
specific training, query imagery may remain sensitive and
requires appropriate retention and access safeguards.
Geographic bias and reliability. Although our approach
generalizes well in a zero-shot manner, our current eval-
15


uation remains geographically constrained to Chinese and
European cities. As a result, performance may vary when
deployed in urban environments that feature fundamen-
tally different architectural styles. Moreover, because we
cannot guarantee hard accuracy bounds for every predic-
tion, the system should not be used in safety-critical au-
tonomous flight applications unless rigorous fallback mech-
anisms are in place.
Dual-use potential. We developed this project with strictly
civilian applications in mind, such as infrastructure inspec-
tion and search-and-rescue missions. Nevertheless, we rec-
ognize that precise aerial localization technologies could
theoretically be repurposed for surveillance or military tar-
geting. Consequently, we strongly advocate for the respon-
sible deployment of these systems and urge practitioners to
enforce strict regulatory safeguards in real-world scenarios.
