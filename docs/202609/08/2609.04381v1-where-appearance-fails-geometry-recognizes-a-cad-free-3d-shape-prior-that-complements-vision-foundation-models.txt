A B S T R A C T 

Recognizing specific objects onboarded without a labeled training set recurs across manufac-turing and service robotics, yet the conventional renderable prior, a computer-aided-design (CAD) model, is often unavailable. Two-dimensional capture supplies no shape prior, and frozen foundation features fail on geometrically similar, low-texture industrial parts. We ask what a short object-centric scan buys for recognition beyond the captured images themselves: each object is reconstructed with 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), summarized into a per-class shape prototype, and fused with frozen DINOv2 image features. First, the scan recovers the recognition value of CAD without CAD: geometry from RGB-D depth (on T-LESS), 3DGS, and CAD gives comparable recognition (tied on HOPE, within 1.6 points on T-LESS); 3DGS is only a convenient route to a point cloud. Second, the payoff is governed by how recognizable the shape is: on shape-distinctive household objects (HOPE) geometry alone reaches 0.920 versus image-only 0.832 , a ceiling below which fixed-weight fusion ( 0.872 ) sits. On shape-confusable textureless industrial parts (T-LESS) the gain is modest but consistent ( 0.560 to 0.591 fused, above both single signals). Third, the prior is complementary, not uniformly additive: it rescues far more image failures than it breaks successes, and its benefit grows under partial occlusion. Finally, the worth lies in geometry, not rendered pixels: 3DGS renderings do not help the image side, and frozen-feature recognition is nearly lighting-invariant (within 2.5 points). The study is scoped to recognition, not the BOP pose benchmark. 

## 1. Introduction 

Industrial and service robots increasingly must recognize specific objects that were never part of a labeled training set, onboarded instead from a quick object-centric capture. CAD models, the conventional renderable prior, are frequently unavailable in practice: supplier-restricted, outdated relative to the physical part, or never authored at all. That absence splits industrial visual sim-to-real into CAD-guided and CAD-unavailable regimes (Tao and Choi, 2026). Without CAD, practitioners fall back on two-dimensional cut-and-paste of captured images (Dwibedi, Misra and Hebert, 2017), which can vary background and pose but cannot supply a three-dimensional shape prior. Frozen foundation features (Oquab, Darcet, Moutakanni et al., 2024) are a powerful alternative, but they can fail on the geometrically similar, low-texture parts common in manufacturing, where appearance alone is weakly discriminative. Recent work fuses geometric and vision-foundation features (Caraffa, Boscaini, Hamza and Poiesi, 2024) or reconstructs objects with Gaussian Splatting for model-free detection (Liu, Wang, Li, Li, Zhang, Huang and Ji, 2024). Template-matching methods built on foundation features have advanced unseen-object detection and pose (Nguyen, Groueix, Ponimatkin, Lepetit and Hodan, 2023; Lin, Liu, Lu and Jia, 2024; Örnek, Labbé, Tekin, Ma, Keskin, Forster and Hodan, 2024). These methods target six-degree-of-freedom pose under the BOP benchmark (Nguyen, Tyree, Guo, Fourmy, Gouda, Lee et al., 2025). How much a scan-derived three-dimensional prior helps recognition , how it compares to CAD, and when it helps remain uncharacterized. We treat a scan-derived 3DGS reconstruction as a geometry prior, a per-class shape prototype, and fuse it with frozen foundation image features for CAD-unavailable recognition. Prior CAD-free Gaussian-Splatting pipelines reuse the reconstructed per-object model for detection under BOP (Liu et al., 2024), and model-based methods match frozen features against a provided mesh for pose (Örnek et al., 2024; Caraffa et al., 2024). We characterize the upstream 

recognition question instead: on a textureless-industrial dataset (T-LESS) and a textured-household dataset (HOPE), we measure how much the prior helps and what governs its value. We make four contributions. First, the prior is

## 2. Related Work 

CAD-free and model-free object recognition and pose The BOP benchmark has steadily shifted from model-based, seen-object tasks toward unseen and model-free settings (Hodaň, Sundermeyer, Drost et al., 2020; Nguyen et al., 2025). A dominant recipe renders views of the object and matches them against the query: with frozen foundation descriptors in CNOS (Nguyen et al., 2023) and SAM-6D (Lin et al., 2024) for unseen-object detection and segmentation and in FoundPose (Örnek et al., 2024) for pose, and with learned template matching or render-and-compare in GigaPose (Nguyen, Groueix, Salzmann and Lepetit, 2024), MegaPose (Labbé, Manuelli, Mousavian, Tyree, Birchfield, Tremblay, Carpentier, Aubry, Fox and Sivic, 2022), OSOP (Shugurov, Li, Busam and Ilic, 2022), OVE6D (Cai, Heikkilä and Rahtu, 2022), and FoundationPose (Wen, Yang, Kautz and Birchfield, 2024) for pose and tracking. This unseen-object line departs from earlier seen-object, per-object-trained regressors such as GDR-Net (Wang, Manhardt, Tombari and Ji, 2021a). OnePose (Sun, Wang, Zhang, He, Zhao, Zhang and Zhou, 2022) onboards objects without CAD from a video sweep, and GFreeDet (Liu et al., 2024) reconstructs objects with Gaussian Splatting for model-free detection. These works target 6D pose or detection under BOP, whose model-free track onboards each object from captured video under a fixed budget, not from a CAD model. Our scan-based onboarding instantiates the same regime, the CAD-unavailable end of the prior-availability spectrum (Tao and Choi, 2026). We reuse their building blocks but ask what the reconstructed geometry contributes to recognition, and how it compares to CAD. 

Geometry and vision-foundation fusion Closest to our mechanism, FreeZe (Caraffa et al., 2024) fuses a frozen point-cloud encoder with frozen vision features for training-free pose. Cross-modal methods learn a joint image–point-cloud embedding, including ULIP (Xue, Gao, Xing, Martín-Martín, Wu, Xiong, Xu, Niebles and Savarese, 2023) and OpenShape (Liu, Shi, Kuang, Zhu, Li, Han, Cai, Porikli and Su, 2023). We share the premise that geometry complements vision-foundation features, but we target classification and obtain the geometry from a scan, with no CAD model. Frozen foundation features are extracted per image and are not inherently view-consistent, which is why template-matching pipelines render and match many views (Nguyen et al., 2023; Lin et al., 2024; Örnek et al., 2024). The per-class geometry prototype supplies the view-consistent 3D structure that the image features alone lack. 

Radiance fields and Gaussian Splatting as object representations Neural radiance fields (Mildenhall, Srini-vasan, Tancik, Barron, Ramamoorthi and Ng, 2020) and their accelerated, unbounded-scene, and surface vari-ants (Müller, Evans, Schied and Keller, 2022; Barron, Mildenhall, Verbin, Srinivasan and Hedman, 2022; Wang, Liu, Liu, Theobalt, Komura and Wang, 2021b) reconstruct objects from posed images. 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl, Kopanas, Leimkühler and Drettakis, 2023) enables real-time, high-fidelity reconstruction, and surface-oriented variants such as 2D Gaussian Splatting (Huang, Yu, Chen, Geiger and Gao, 2024) and SuGaR (Guédon and Lepetit, 2024) extract meshes. 3DGS has also been used directly for 6DoF pose: novel-object pose (GS-Pose (Cai, Heikkilä and Rahtu, 2025)), online pose tracking (6DOPE-GS (Jin, Prasad, Jauhri, Franzius and Chalvatzaki, 2025)), and camera pose from a single image against a 3DGS model (6DGS (Bortolon, Tsesmelis, James, Poiesi and Del Bue, 2024)). Reconstructed per-object twins underpin model-free onboarding: FoundationPose builds a neural twin from reference images (Wen et al., 2024), and GFreeDet drives the BOP model-free detection track with a 3DGS twin (Liu et al., 2024). Our question sits upstream of these pose tasks: we use 3DGS only to obtain a CAD-free object point cloud, from which we derive a shape prototype. Reusing the same twin for pose is left to future work.

Foundation visual features Self-supervised vision transformers (Caron, Touvron, Misra, Jégou, Mairal, Bojanowski and Joulin, 2021; Oquab et al., 2024; Siméoni et al., 2025) learn strong dense features, aided by register tokens (Darcet, Oquab, Mairal and Bojanowski, 2024); alternative objectives include masked autoencoding (He, Chen, Xie, Li, Dollár and Girshick, 2022), joint-embedding prediction (Assran, Duval, Misra, Bojanowski, Vincent, Rabbat, LeCun and Ballas, 2023), and multimodal autoregression (Fini et al., 2025). Image–text contrastive encoders (Radford, Kim, Hallacy et al., 2021; Zhai, Mustafa, Kolesnikov and Beyer, 2023; Tschannen et al., 2025; Xu, Xie, Tan, Huang, Howes, Sharma, Li, Ghosh, Zettlemoyer and Feichtenhofer, 2024) and promptable segmentation (Kirillov, Mintun, Ravi et al., 2023) are also widely used as frozen backbones. We benchmark 14 such encoders as the frozen image side and find self-supervised vision transformers strongest for textureless industrial parts. A complementary line instead induces a shape bias for textureless and metallic parts, for example by randomizing surface texture during training (Hönig, Thalhammer, Weibel, Hirschmanner and Vincze, 2025). 

3D shape descriptors and point-cloud encoders Classical global shape signatures such as shape distributions (Os-ada, Funkhouser, Chazelle and Dobkin, 2002) and learned encoders (Qi, Su, Mo and Guibas, 2017a; Qi, Yi, Su and Guibas, 2017b) summarize a point cloud into a vector. Our geometry prototype uses a compact handbuilt descriptor; we also evaluate a learned encoder. 

Synthetic data for CAD-free training, and prototype recognition Without CAD, training data is commonly synthesized by cut-and-paste of captured crops (Dwibedi et al., 2017), domain randomization (Tobin, Fong, Ray, Schneider, Zaremba and Abbeel, 2017), physically based rendering (Denninger, Winkelbauer, Sundermeyer, Boerdijk, Knauer, Strobl, Humt and Triebel, 2023), and copy-paste augmentation (Ghiasi, Cui, Srinivas, Qian, Lin, Cubuk, Le and Zoph, 2021); all supply appearance but no 3D shape prior. Our scoring builds on prototype-based few-shot recognition (Snell, Swersky and Zemel, 2017). 

Classical CAD-free shape priors Before learned features, CAD-free shape cues were recovered from multi-view geometry: the visual hull and shape-from-silhouette reconstruct a coarse 3D shape from object silhouettes (Laurentini, 1994). Separately, edge- and template-based matching recognized textureless objects from many rendered or captured views (Hinterstoisser, Lepetit, Ilic, Holzer, Bradski, Konolige and Navab, 2012). Edge-based cues remain a staple for recognizing textureless parts (Clement, Shah, Pancholi, Lugo Bustillo and Cheng, 2024). We revisit this idea of geometry-from-views without CAD. Instead of matching hand-engineered templates, we pair a modern reconstruction (3DGS or depth) and a compact shape descriptor with frozen foundation image features. 

Benchmarks We evaluate on the textureless-industrial T-LESS (Hodaň, Haluza, Obdržálek, Matas, Lourakis and Zabulis, 2017) and the textured-household HOPE (Tyree, Tremblay, To, Cheng, Mosier, Smith and Birchfield, 2022). Related object datasets include YCB-Video (Xiang, Schmidt, Narayanan and Fox, 2018), LineMOD (Hinterstoisser et al., 2012), the industrial ITODD (Drost, Ulrich, Bergmann, Härtinger and Steger, 2017), and IMD, a recent benchmark for industrial metallic objects (Ma, Zudaire, Li and Zhang, 2025). 

## 3. Method 

Task and overview We address CAD-free instance recognition: a query crop must be assigned to one of 𝐶 object classes that were onboarded only from a short object-centric scan, with no labeled training images and no CAD model. Per class, the pipeline (Fig. 1) builds two prototypes from the onboarding scan: a frozen-foundation image prototype and a scan-derived geometry prototype. A query is then scored by a weighted sum of its similarities to the two. The backbone is never fine-tuned; the only trainable component is a small head that maps image features into the geometry-descriptor space. 

Reconstruction Each object is reconstructed CAD-free from its onboarding scan with 3DGS (Kerbl et al., 2023). We train the splatfacto-big model in Nerfstudio for 20 ,000 iterations on T-LESS and 15 ,000 on HOPE, with a white background and point-cloud initialization, from the known object-centric camera poses given by the BOP scene_gt 

annotations (object rotation 𝑅 𝑚 2𝑐 and translation 𝑡 𝑚 2𝑐 , converted to camera-to-world). We export the Gaussian centers as a point cloud and crop it to the object core (points within 0.15 m of the origin), followed by statistical outlier removal (Open3D, 20 neighbors, standard-deviation ratio 2.0). Datasets and splits We evaluate on T-LESS (Hodaň et al., 2017) ( 28 of its 30 objects; objects 19 and 20 are excluded because their scans did not yield usable reconstructions, an exclusion fixed at reconstruction time, before any recognition evaluation; 160 onboarding frames per class) and HOPE (Tyree et al., 2022) ( 28 objects; 60 onboarding frames per class). Recognition queries are real, labeled crops drawn from scenes disjoint from the onboarding scan, which prevents onboarding-to-query leakage: 6,395 queries for T-LESS and 915 for HOPE. Concretely, T-LESS onboarding uses the object-centric train_primesense turntable scans (one object per scene) and queries are crops from the multi-object test_primesense scenes; HOPE onboarding uses the BOP onboarding_static scans and queries are crops from the validation scenes. All query crops are taken at the ground-truth annotation boxes, isolating recognition from detection. Accuracies are top-1 throughout, averaged over 5 random seeds unless noted ( 3 seeds for the RGB-D depth source in R2). 

## 4. Experiments 

Setup Datasets, onboarding scans, and the scene-disjoint query protocol are those of Section 3 (“Datasets and splits”). Unless stated otherwise the backbone is a frozen DINOv2-giant, the fusion weight is fixed at 𝜆 = 0 .35 , and we report top-1 accuracy averaged over 5 seeds. Prototypes are built only from the onboarding scan, and the only trained component, the fusion head, is trained only on onboarding features; no labeled query images are used anywhere. 

R1. A CAD-free 3DGS geometry prior improves recognition On T-LESS, fusing the 3DGS-derived geometry prototype with frozen image prototypes raised accuracy from 0.560 (image-only) to 0.591 ± 0 .004 (fused, 5 seeds). On HOPE the geometry prototype alone reached 0.920 ± 0 .006 , far exceeding image-only 0.832 ; geometry is so dominant here that geometry-only is the strongest single configuration, while fixed-𝜆 fusion ( 0.872 ± 0 .002 ) sits between image and geometry (Fig. 3, Table 1). The prior helps in both regimes: as a complement on the shape-confusable industrial parts, and as the primary signal on the shape-distinctive household objects. This yields a simple operational rule, made precise in the weight-robustness analysis below: fuse the two signals where appearance is competitive (T-LESS), and let geometry lead where it already dominates (HOPE). Either way, the prior is best read as a geometry signal, used in fusion or, where geometry suffices, on its own. In practice the regime can be identified at onboarding time, without labeled queries: held-out onboarding frames, labeled by construction, give a proxy estimate of each arm’s accuracy, and the class separability (silhouette) of the frozen image features over the onboarding crops signals whether appearance is competitive (cf. the feature-manifold analysis below).

R3. The prior is complementary, helping where appearance fails We stratified each query into image-wrong and image-right cases. A feature-manifold view of when fusion helps The harm-rate asymmetry ( 5.6% on T-LESS vs 0.4% on HOPE) follows from how separable the frozen image features are. A t-SNE projection and silhouette analysis of the DINOv2 features (Fig. 8) reveal a far more collapsed manifold on textureless T-LESS (silhouette 0.02 ) than on textured HOPE (silhouette 0.17 ): on T-LESS many classes alias into overlapping clusters. Where the appearance manifold is collapsed, even correct image predictions are marginal, so fusing geometry both rescues many failures and occasionally overturns a borderline-correct one (higher harm). Where it is well separated (HOPE), correct predictions are confident and geometry almost never harms them. The geometry prior acts orthogonally to appearance, separating classes where the appearance manifold has collapsed.

Robustness to the fusion weight The preceding results all use a single fixed fusion weight; we now check that this choice is not a tuned artifact. Accuracy rises smoothly and monotonically with the fusion weight 𝜆 across the swept range 𝜆 ∈ [0 , 1] on both datasets, with no instability (Fig. 10); the reported 𝜆 = 0 .35 is a single conservative value shared across both datasets, not a per-dataset tuned optimum. The best swept weight ( 𝜆 = 1 ) bounds what fusion reaches within the sweep: 0.598 on T-LESS (versus 0.591 at the fixed weight) and 0.897 on HOPE (versus 0.872 ). On T-LESS, fusion at any reasonable weight exceeds both image-only ( 0.560 ) and geometry-only ( 0.566 ), confirming genuine complementarity. On HOPE, geometry-only ( 0.920 ) is instead the ceiling: fusion approaches it as 𝜆 grows but cannot exceed it, because geometry strictly dominates appearance for these objects. R5. The worth lies in geometry, not rendered pixels Having established when and how much the prior helps, we ask what about the reconstruction carries the signal. Using 3DGS rendered views as the image source did not help. Replacing the real onboarding crops with renders dropped image-only accuracy from 0.560 to 0.392 , and adding renders on top of the real crops slightly hurt it ( 0.560 to 0.554 ), consistent with the domain gap between clean renders and cluttered real queries that is visible in Fig. 2; Fig. 11a quantifies the drop. We also probed lighting sensitivity in a controlled way, rendering the 28 T-LESS CAD models under matched, shifted, and extreme lighting conditions that vary key-light direction, color temperature, intensity, and ambient level ( 24 views per object) and evaluating them by few-shot prototype recognition. The probe left frozen-feature recognition essentially unchanged, within 2.5 points even under extreme illumination (Fig. 11b), so the relighting capability of a renderable asset is inert for this task. What the reconstruction contributes, then, is geometry, not pixels a renderer can synthesize.

R7. Handbuilt vs learned geometry encoders R6 varied the image side; we now vary the geometry side and ask whether the training-free descriptor is a limitation. The two learned alternatives are an in-domain PointNet++ trained on the same reconstructed clouds (with heavy SO(3)-rotation, jitter, scale, and partial-view augmentation to compensate for having only 28 shapes), and a frozen pretrained 3D foundation encoder, OpenShape (Liu et al., 2023) (a PointBERT (Yu, Tang, Rao, Huang, Zhou and Lu, 2022) point encoder aligned to an OpenCLIP ViT-bigG-14 embedding space, trained largely on Objaverse), used zero-shot exactly as we use frozen DINOv2 on the image side. respectively (DINOv2-giant, 𝜆 = 0 .35 ; all differences within seed standard deviation). The handbuilt-versus-learned tie reproduces on HOPE: PointNet++ reaches geometry-only 0.929±0 .007 and fused 0.867±0 .004 against the handbuilt descriptor’s 0.920±0 .006 and 0.872±0 .002 , differences below one point in both arms. That a large-scale-pretrained 3D foundation encoder only matches a 45 -dimensional handbuilt descriptor indicates that encoder sophistication is not what drives the result; geometry quality is (consistent with R2). We retain the training-free descriptor, which is lightweight and needs no point-cloud corpus; a frozen 3D foundation encoder remains a drop-in alternative at equal accuracy.

## 5. Discussion 

What this study establishes, and what it does not We state our claims only at the level the evidence supports. Three findings are each corroborated by several independent experiments. The prior is reconstruction-agnostic , so a short scan recovers the recognition value of CAD without CAD: RGB-D depth, 3DGS, and CAD geometry give comparable recognition, within seed-level variability (R2), and rendered pixels, unlike the geometry, add nothing (R5). The effect does not depend on any rendering-specific property of 3DGS. A second finding is complementarity : the prior helps most where appearance is least reliable, as shown by the occlusion stratification on both datasets (R4), the 14 -backbone sweep in which the fusion gain grows as the backbone weakens (R6), the image-difficulty-controlled partial correlation (R3), and the feature-manifold analysis (Fig. 8). And the result is not driven by descriptor sophistication :a 45 -dimensional handbuilt descriptor, an in-domain PointNet++, and a frozen Objaverse-pretrained 3D foundation encoder all perform within seed noise (R7). We deliberately make no stronger claims than these. We do not claim that fixed-weight fusion is the optimal configuration in every regime: on HOPE, where geometry dominates appearance, geometry alone is the ceiling and the untuned fusion sits below it (Fig. 10). We do not claim end-to-end detection-and-recognition, only recognition on labeled crops. Nor do we claim validation at large scale: our evidence spans 56 objects across two domains. The contribution is a carefully bounded mechanism, not a universal recipe; the rest of this section examines its boundaries. We initially expected the prior’s value to scale with raw shape distinctiveness, but within either dataset a purely geometric distinctiveness metric did not predict the per-class gain. The supported statement is the weaker, controlled one: geometry helps in proportion to its own per-class recognizability once image difficulty is held fixed (R3). The cross-dataset contrast (geometry matters more on HOPE than on T-LESS) is confounded by texture, shape, and overall difficulty: HOPE is easier overall, so it should be read as a trend, not a single-variable result. The fusion weight is fixed and shared across datasets by design; on HOPE a geometry-heavier weight would recover the geometry-only accuracy, but we prefer to avoid per-dataset tuning. We use 3DGS only as a practical way to obtain an object point cloud without CAD. Because the resulting geometry matches gold-standard CAD geometry (R2), any reconstruction that yields a comparable point cloud (e.g., multi-view stereo) would be expected to serve equally well; a systematic comparison of reconstruction methods remains open. Relatedly, our onboarding reconstructions use the calibrated object-centric poses supplied with each scan. Recovering such poses CAD-free in the wild, for example via structure-from-motion or video onboarding as in OnePose (Sun et al., 2022), is an orthogonal onboarding step; recognition depends on the resulting geometry, not its source (R2), so a comparable point cloud recovered that way should serve just as well. We also report recognition accuracy, not BOP detection or pose average precision; the work complements pose-benchmark methods but is not directly comparable with them. Our queries are cropped, labeled boxes, so the study isolates recognition from end-to-end detection-and-recognition. In deployment the prior would sit behind a CAD-free detector or class-agnostic

Confirming the prior at the scale of thousands of objects (e.g., large open-vocabulary or synthetic-object corpora), well beyond the 56 studied here, is an important next step. Finally, we match on a single global per-class feature prototype; intermediate patch-level descriptors, shown to carry positional information valuable under symmetry and texture loss (Örnek et al., 2024), are a complementary representation we leave to future work. 

## 6. Conclusion 

A short object-centric scan recovers the recognition value of CAD without CAD. The geometry prior it yields nearly matches CAD geometry and pays off in proportion to how recognizable the object’s shape is. Fused with frozen foundation image features, it helps most where appearance fails: on geometrically recognizable but visually confusable objects and under partial occlusion. Onboarding needs nothing beyond the scan, and the prior is a compact per-class shape descriptor, so the approach offers a practical route to enrolling new parts on production lines where CAD is unavailable. Integrating it into a full detection-and-pose pipeline is left to future work.
