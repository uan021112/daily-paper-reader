ABSTRACT
Gaussian splatting has emerged as a flexible representation for 3D reconstruc-
tion from posed images. However, existing methods are optimized primarily us-
ing rasterization-based losses, which supervise a splat only when it contributes to
sampled camera rays. Gaussians that are occluded or contribute little to the sam-
pled view therefore receive weak or no geometric gradients and may drift away
from the underlying surface, producing undesired floaters. We introduce PCAs-
plat, a geometry-aware regularization framework for Gaussian splatting based
on differentiable local principal component analysis (PCA). Our PCA regular-
izer acts directly on neighborhoods of Gaussian centers and can therefore update
Gaussians that do not contribute to the current training view. We regularize the
PCA eigenvalues to encourage Gaussians to move to the underlying surface with
isotropic tangent-plane coverage. We also align each Gaussian normal with the
PCA-estimated neighborhood normal to enforce consistent orientation. Experi-
ments on DTU, Tanks and Temples, and NeRF Synthetic show that the splats pro-
duced by PCAsplat better approximate samples of the reference surface while sub-
stantially reducing undesired floaters. These surface-aligned splats enable down-
stream geometry-processing tasks, including point cloud segmentation, and direct
Poisson reconstruction. Additionally, PCAsplat remains competitive under con-
ventional novel view synthesis and mesh extraction tasks. Code will be released.

INTRODUCTION
Gaussian splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) is becoming the standard framework for novel view synthesis
(NVS) and 3D reconstruction from images, offering a favorable trade-off between rendering quality,
optimization speed, and interactive visualization. It represents a scene as a collection of anisotropic
Gaussians with appearance attributes optimized via differentiable rendering. While Gaussian splat-
ting is highly effective for NVS, Gaussians can be located far from the underlying surfaces, forming
floaters both outside and inside objects (see Fig. 1, top-left). These floaters remain a challenge
for using the learned representation directly in geometry-processing tasks such as segmentation and
Poisson surface reconstruction.
Methods such as 2DGS (Huang et al., 2024), GOF (Yu et al., 2024b), PGSR (Chen et al., 2025),
and RaDe-GS (Zhang et al., 2026) use losses based on rendered depth and normals to improve ge-
ometric reconstruction. However, these rendering-based losses supervise Gaussians through their
contributions to sampled views. Gaussians that are occluded or contribute little to the view therefore
receive weak or no geometric gradients from these losses and may drift away from the underlying
surface, producing exterior and interior floaters. The learned representation may also contain over-
sized, diffuse, or poorly localized Gaussians. Although floaters may have little impact on training
views, exterior ones can appear in novel views while interior ones may remain occluded, with both
limiting the representation’s use in downstream applications. Other approaches couple Gaussians to
an auxiliary surface representation. GS-Pull (Zhang et al., 2024) pulls Gaussians onto the zero-level
set of a learned neural signed distance function (SDF), while MILo (Gu´edon et al., 2025) differen-
tiably extracts a mesh from Gaussian parameters during training and enforces consistency between
the mesh and the Gaussians. However, these approaches rely on an auxiliary representation that
must also be optimized, and undesired floaters remain an issue.

Overall, learning surface-aligned Gaussians suitable for downstream geometry processing remains a
challenge. To address this problem, we introduce PCAsplat, a geometry-aware regularization frame-
work for Gaussian splatting based on differentiable local principal component analysis (PCA). Our
key idea is to regularize Gaussian neighborhoods directly in scene space through PCA statistics. This
provides geometric supervision even to Gaussians that do not contribute to the current training view.
For each Gaussian, we estimate a local 3D neighborhood and compute its covariance eigenspectrum
to characterize local thickness and tangent-plane anisotropy. We use these statistics to encourage
Gaussian centers to move toward the surface by reducing local thickness, while promoting isotropic
tangent-plane coverage and aligning Gaussian normals with PCA-derived normals up to sign.
Experiments on DTU, Tanks and Temples, and NeRF Synthetic show that PCAsplat achieves strong
surface reconstruction results compared to current methods. It produces surface-aligned splats with
fewer exterior and interior floaters (Fig. 1, bottom-left and center), allowing for more accurate down-
stream geometry processing. PCAsplat yields substantially more accurate instance segmentation
and more coherent object masks than current methods using the same segmentation pipeline (Fig. 1,
right). PCAsplat also remains competitive under conventional novel view synthesis and TSDF-based
mesh extraction. In summary, our main contributions are:
• A differentiable local PCA framework for regularizing Gaussian neighborhoods directly in
scene space, including Gaussians that do not contribute to the current training view.
• Geometric losses for learning surface-aligned Gaussians by moving them toward the sur-
face, promoting isotropic coverage, and aligning their normals with PCA-normals.
• An evaluation of splats as surface samples, showing improved geometric accuracy and their
use in downstream applications, including segmentation and Poisson reconstruction.

RELATED WORK
Surface reconstruction from images is a long-standing problem in vision. Classical methods such
as COLMAP (Schonberger & Frahm, 2016) combine structure from motion and multi-view stereo
to recover camera poses and point clouds. Recently, feed-forward models such as VGGT (Wang
et al., 2025) and VGGT-Ω(Wang et al., 2026) predict cameras, depth maps, and point clouds di-
rectly from unposed images. However, these methods lack a view-dependent appearance model
2


for novel-view synthesis. In contrast, neural implicit methods jointly learn geometry and view-
dependent appearance: VolSDF (Yariv et al., 2021), NeuS (Wang et al., 2021), and Neuralangelo (Li
et al., 2023) optimize neural SDFs for each scene through differentiable rendering. However, re-
peated neural-field evaluations along each ray make volume rendering computationally expensive.
Gaussian splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) enabled fast rasterization of explicit primitives but lacked
geometry constraints. 2DGS (Huang et al., 2024), Gaussian Surfels (Dai et al., 2024), and Quadratic
Gaussian Splatting (Zhang et al., 2025) made splats surface-like through modified primitives or ren-
dering formulations together with rendering losses to regularize geometric properties of the scene.
SuGaR (Gu´edon & Lepetit, 2024), GOF (Yu et al., 2024b), and RaDe-GS (Zhang et al., 2026)
improve mesh extraction via surface alignment, opacity fields, or better depth rasterization. And,
PGSR (Chen et al., 2025) introduced multi-view consistency losses to further improve geometry.
For mesh extraction, methods such as 2DGS, RaDe-GS, and PGSR fuse rendered depth maps us-
ing TSDF (Curless & Levoy, 1996), which are based on ray-gaussian contributions. Consequently,
accurate meshes do not necessarily imply surface-aligned Gaussian centers.
Rendering-based geometric regularization augments photometric supervision with losses on ren-
dered depth and normals, aiming to reduce undesired floaters. Mip-NeRF 360 (Barron et al., 2022)
introduces a distortion loss that encourages rendering contributions to concentrate within a narrow
interval along each ray, discouraging diffuse density and floaters. 2DGS and GOF adapt this loss to
encourage surface alignment by reducing depth differences between contributing Gaussians along
viewing rays. These differences are weighted by rendering contributions, which depend on opac-
ity and accumulated transmittance. As such, Gaussians that are occluded or contribute little to a
view may receive weak or no geometric gradients from these losses, allowing exterior and interior
floaters to persist. PCAsplat complements this supervision with local PCA losses that act directly
on 3D Gaussian neighborhoods, so Gaussians that do not contribute to the current training view can
still receive geometric gradients.
Hybrid Gaussian–surface representations couple Gaussians with auxiliary surface representa-
tions. GS-Pull (Zhang et al., 2024) pulls Gaussian centers onto the zero-level set of a neural SDF,
at the additional computational cost of jointly optimizing this field. Its Gaussian-disk supervi-
sion addresses sparse, nonuniform support but remains sensitive to extreme sparsity. GSDF (Yu
et al., 2024a) couples Gaussians and SDFs through depth and normal consistency, using Gaussian-
rendered depth to guide SDF sampling and SDF values to control Gaussian growth and pruning.
Gaussian-UDF (Li et al., 2025) jointly learns 2D Gaussians and an unsigned distance field for open
surfaces, using Gaussian centers and local planes to supervise distances. MILo (Gu´edon et al., 2025)
and GaussianWrapping (GW) (Gomez et al., 2026) differentiably extracts a mesh from Gaussian-
derived pivots and learned signed distances, enforcing Gaussian–mesh rendering consistency.
In contrast, we adapt local PCA, a classical method for estimating tangent planes and normals from
point clouds (Hoppe et al., 1992; Pauly et al., 2002), to directly regularize Gaussian neighborhoods.
We add losses for neighborhood thickness, tangent-plane coverage, and normal alignment to existing
splatting objectives without learning auxiliary SDFs or extracting meshes during training.
3
METHOD
Our goal is to learn surface-aligned Gaussians by regularizing their local geometry directly in scene
space, reducing floaters and supporting downstream geometry processing. We formulate local PCA-
based regularization as differentiable losses on Gaussian geometry, optimized jointly with the ren-
dering objective. For each Gaussian, we compute the covariance eigenspectrum and principal di-
rections of a local neighborhood of Gaussian centers. These quantities define losses that reduce
neighborhood thickness, enforce isotropic tangent-plane coverage, and align Gaussian normals with
PCA-derived normals. Fig. 2 gives an overview of our framework.

EXPERIMENTS
We evaluate PCAsplat on the surface alignment of its learned Gaussians and their performance in
downstream geometry processing. We compare against Gaussian-based reconstruction methods on
point-cloud accuracy, 3D instance segmentation (Schult et al., 2023), and Poisson surface recon-
struction (Kazhdan & Hoppe, 2013).
Datasets and metrics. We evaluate surface accuracy on DTU (Jensen et al., 2014) and Tanks
and Temples (Knapitsch et al., 2017) using standard Chamfer distance (CD) and F1-score. Both
metrics compare reconstructed point clouds (Gaussian centers for splatting methods) with ground-
truth point clouds. For 3D semantic instance segmentation, we use ScanNet++ (Yeshwanth et al.,
2023) and report Top-1 and Top-3 mean intersection over union (mIoU). We also evaluate F1-score
on a visible-surface benchmark constructed from the NeRF Synthetic (Mildenhall et al., 2021) scene
assets. Implementation details are provided in Appendix A, and the visible-surface reference meshes
we introduce are described in Appendix B.
Baselines. We compare with standard 3DGS and leading Gaussian splatting methods for surface
reconstruction, including 2DGS (Huang et al., 2024), PGSR (Chen et al., 2025), GS-Pull (Zhang
et al., 2024), MILo (Gu´edon et al., 2025), RaDe-GS (Zhang et al., 2026), and GaussianWrapping
(GW) (Gomez et al., 2026). The latter use depth distortion, geometric consistency, or surface con-
straints to improve reconstruction. VGGT-Ω(Wang et al., 2026) and COLMAP-D (Schonberger &
Frahm, 2016) provide additional point-cloud references; “-D” denotes dense output.

CONCLUSION
We presented PCAsplat, a geometry-aware Gaussian Splatting framework that aligns Gaussians to
the surface by integrating local PCA regularization into training. Our method achieves surface-
aligned Gaussians, which enables downstream tasks while still performing competitively in Novel
View Synthesis and Mesh Extraction. This refined representation helps bridge classical point-set
surface analysis and modern novel view synthesis, enabling downstream geometry-processing tasks
such as 3D segmentation and Poisson surface reconstruction to be applied directly to the optimized
Gaussian means. By encouraging Gaussians to lie on the underlying surface, PCAsplat also provides
a natural basis for estimating local geometric quantities, such as curvature and density, opening
promising directions for heat propagation (Zhou & L¨ahner, 2026) and geometry-aware pruning and
densification (see Appendix D material). While PCAsplat introduces modest training overhead and
requires a depth-based step for distant floaters and sign disambiguation for normals, it substantially
improves local surface fidelity

APPENDIX
A
IMPLEMENTATION DETAILS.
Our method is implemented in PyTorch on top of the RaDe-GS (Zhang et al., 2026) codebase. We
first train the 3D Gaussians with LRGB for 7K iterations, and then enable PCA losses, LPCA and
Lnormal, for the remaining iterations. We train bounded scenes for 20K iterations and unbounded
scenes for 30K iterations. To accelerate PCAsplat, we implemented a custom CUDA kernel for
batched 3 × 3 eigendecomposition, since torch.linalg.eigh relies on general-purpose rou-
tines that are not optimized for this setting. All experiments are conducted on an NVIDIA RTX
A5500 with 24GB of VRAM.
Results Reproducibility
We ran all methods using their most up-to-date GitHub repositories on
all datasets using their specified hyperparameters for each dataset; however, for NeRF Synthetic we
used the DTU hyperparameters for all methods, this is due to the original hyperparameters used for
this dataset being PSNR-focused and lacking surface regularizations. For VGGT-Ω, we unproject the
predicted depth maps through the predicted camera poses to obtain a metric-scaled point cloud. We
then estimate a similarity that aligns the predicted cameras to the dataset COLMAP poses and apply
the same transform to the point cloud, placing it in the ScanNet++ coordinate frame. COLMAP-S
stands for a sparse point output, while COLMAP-D stands for a dense output. Tab. 1 and Tab. 3
have all results generated with our runs of each method with their specified hyperparameters for
each dataset.
B
SYNTHETIC MESH DATASET
NeRF Synthetic (Mildenhall et al., 2021) provides synthetic Blender scenes with posed views but
no official surface mesh for 3D reconstruction metrics. We extract only the visible mesh from each
scene’s original 3D assets downloaded from Blendswap. First, all components are merged via self-
intersection so shared seams become explicit edges, then hidden-surface removal is run for every
train, validation, and test camera, where we raycast each view and keep a triangle if and only if it is
the first hit for at least one pixel, yielding a watertight surface that matches what a 3D reconstruction
method can actually observe. The resulting visible meshes are the exclusive reference for the F-score
measured from 106 Farthest Point Sampling (FPS) samples. This dataset will be made available.
See Fig. 9. Additionally, our pipeline for creating such a dataset is useful for evaluating future and
previous works on mesh extraction, and creating newer, more precise datasets.

Asset-Ready Splats
Unlike standard 3DGS pipelines, PCAsplat directly generate asset-ready
splats suitable for downstream pipelines. In conventional workflows, isolating a reconstructed ob-

floaters and artifacts. Crucially, such manual pruning often removes important primitives: for in-

and used out of the box, eliminating manual post-processing while strictly preserving opaque surface
integrity. Fig. 10 showcases a comparison between PCAsplat and previous works that have surface
