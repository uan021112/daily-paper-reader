# GUARD: Geometric Uncertainty-Aware Point Cloud Denoising and Segmentation for Robotic Hard Disk Drive Disassembly 

Zuoxu Wang, Xiao Liang ∗

Abstract —Reliable robotic disassembly requires part-level rep-resentations that distinguish genuine component geometry from scanning and reconstruction artifacts. In point clouds of hard disk drives (HDDs), structured ghost artifacts can resemble valid components locally while remaining inconsistent with the overall geometry, allowing erroneous measurements to receive plausible semantic labels. This creates an engineering infor-mation problem: semantic prediction confidence alone does not establish whether the underlying geometry is reliable. We propose GUARD, a geometric uncertainty-aware framework that performs point filtering and segmentation within a single forward pass by modeling the reliability of learned geomet-ric representations. GUARD combines a multi-scale geometric transformer with a multi-bandwidth random Fourier feature Gaussian Process to estimate per-point geometric uncertainty, complemented by predictive entropy to suppress unreliable mea-surements while preserving informative structures. Evaluation on 2,745 real HDD point clouds shows that GUARD improves PointNet++ segmentation mean intersection over union from 0.7739 to 0.8318. Additional experiments on ShapeNetPart and ScanNet examine robustness across corruption types, point-cloud domains, and segmentation backbones. On manually annotated ScanNet samples, geometric uncertainty achieves a corrupted-point detection F1 score of 0.7931, compared with 0.2212 for predictive entropy. The results demonstrate the value of dis-tinguishing geometric reliability from semantic confidence and reveal a tradeoff between artifact suppression and preservation of informative structures. GUARD contributes a reliability-aware approach to interpreting imperfect 3D measurements for com-ponent identification and subsequent robotic handling. I. I NTRODUCTION 

The rapid replacement and upgrading of electronic devices has led to a continuously increasing volume of electronic waste (e-waste), creating growing demands for efficient material recovery, component handling, and recycling. Hard disk drives (HDDs), for example, contain multiple reusable or valuable components and materials that require accurate identification, handling, sorting, and disassembly during automated pro-cessing [1], [2]. However, these operations still rely heavily on manual labor, which is time-consuming, repetitive, and difficult to scale. With recent advances in industrial robotics, robotic systems have shown increasing potential for automat-ing component handling and disassembly, thereby improving

Before a robot can grasp, remove, or ma-nipulate an individual component, it must accurately identify the corresponding part and understand its three-dimensional geometry. This requirement is particularly important for elec-tronic devices such as HDDs, where multiple small compo-nents are densely arranged within a limited workspace [6]. Point clouds provide a suitable representation for this purpose because they preserve explicit 3D geometric information and allow the robot to reason about fine-grained part structures and spatial relationships. Consequently, accurate part-level point cloud segmentation is an important perception capability for automated disassembly and manipulation [7]. In real-world deployment, however, point clouds obtained from depth sensors or multi-view reconstruction are rarely free from corruption. Sensor noise, partial observations, occlusion, reflective surfaces, and reconstruction errors can introduce inaccurate or redundant measurements [8]–[10]. More im-portantly, multi-frame scanning and 3D reconstruction can produce structured ghost artifacts, where duplicated, shifted, or partially reconstructed surfaces appear around the true object geometry [11]–[13]. Unlike isolated floating outliers, these ghost structures may preserve locally coherent shapes that closely resemble portions of real components. As a result, they can be particularly difficult to distinguish from valid points and may cause segmentation networks to assign plausible but incorrect semantic labels. Although point cloud denoising has been extensively stud-ied, most existing methods primarily focus on random pertur-bations, sparse outliers, or general surface noise. For example, supervised denoising methods such as PointCleanNet [14] learn to remove corrupted points from paired clean and noisy data, while more recent robustness-oriented approaches at-tempt to suppress high-risk or unreliable samples. However, structured ghost artifacts receive considerably less attention. This type of corruption is fundamentally more challenging because the erroneous points are not necessarily isolated or geometrically irregular; instead, they may form locally realistic structures similar to genuine object parts. Moreover, obtain-ing point-level annotations of such artifacts from real scans is expensive and impractical. Therefore, reliably identifying geometrically unreliable regions while preserving valid fine-grained structures remains an important challenge for robust point cloud segmentation in real robotic perception. Motivated by these limitations, this study investigates how geometric consistency and uncertainty can be represented and used to improve part-level interpretation of artifact-corrupted engineering scans. In particular, local geometric plausibility arXiv:2610.09068v1 [cs.CV] 6 Oct 2026 2

and confident semantic prediction do not necessarily imply that a measured structure is valid. A useful perception method must therefore evaluate the reliability of geometric evidence while preserving the fine structures needed for component recognition. We address this question through GUARD, a framework that combines multi-scale geometric representations, geometry-conditioned uncertainty estimation, and semantic prediction entropy. Rather than relying solely on classification confi-dence, GUARD evaluates how well a point’s geometric repre-sentation is supported by the learned feature distribution and uses this information to guide filtering. The resulting single-pass framework couples part segmentation with an explicit assessment of measurement reliability. We first evaluate GUARD on a real-world HDD point cloud dataset collected from practical scanning and reconstruction pipelines, where sensor noise and reconstruction-induced ghost artifacts directly affect part-level perception for automated component handling and disassembly. This dataset serves as our primary engineering evaluation and reflects the types of geometric corruption encountered in realistic robotic deploy-ment. To further assess robustness beyond the HDD setting, we conduct controlled experiments on ShapeNetPart [15] with synthetic sensor noise and ghost-cluster corruption, and extend the evaluation to ScanNet [16] to examine the behavior of the proposed uncertainty modeling in large-scale indoor scenes. Across these settings, GUARD consistently improves robust-ness to corrupted geometry while maintaining competitive segmentation accuracy on cleaner inputs, demonstrating its potential as a practical perception component for robotic systems operating under imperfect 3D sensing. II. R ELATED WORK 

Engineering information for robotic disassembly Recent re-search in engineering informatics has investigated robotic dis-assembly from the perspectives of human–robot collaboration, sequence planning, and manufacturing-system optimization. For example, Guo et al. [4] studied human–robot collaborative disassembly sequence planning, Liang et al. [2] addressed disassembly line balancing, and Zhang et al. [3] investigated responsive collaborative disassembly optimization. These stud-ies primarily focus on planning and coordination within re-manufacturing and disassembly systems. Complementary to these efforts, the present study focuses on the reliability of the geometric information used to interpret components during robotic disassembly. 

Point Cloud Segmentation. Deep learning on point clouds has evolved rapidly since the introduction of PointNet++ [17] and DGCNN [18], which establish hierarchical and graph-based paradigms for local feature aggregation. Most subsequent ad-vances, including attention-based frameworks and robustness-oriented methods, build upon these backbones. For example, PointNeXt [19] revisits PointNet++ with improved training and scaling strategies, while attention-based variants such as UPA [20], SOE-Net [21], and PointCloud-At [22] intro-duce self-attention to enhance local feature modeling. Recent models such as Point Transformer [23], although attention-driven, still follow the hierarchical neighborhood aggregation paradigm introduced by PointNet++ and DGCNN, and can be viewed as an attention-based extension of these backbones. As a result, PointNet++ and DGCNN remain representative foundations for modern point cloud segmentation networks and plug-in robust segmentation methods. We therefore adopt them as benchmarks for fair comparison, and further evaluate our method on the most advanced Point Transformer backbone to verify its generalization. 

Robust and Noise-Aware Learning. Several approaches have been proposed to improve robustness under noisy or corrupted point clouds. PointCleanNet [14] learns to restore clean shapes through supervised noise–clean correspondences, while PointCVaR [24] leverages conditional value-at-risk op-timization to emphasize hard or corrupted samples. REAL3D-AD [25] and PO3AD [26] further explore uncertainty-guided 3D anomaly detection by reconstructing clean geometry and identifying abnormal regions through residual-based discrep-ancies. Although conceptually related, their reconstruction-driven formulation targets anomaly localization rather than general noise suppression, and struggles to handle fine-grained geometric perturbations or ghost-cluster misalignment. Over-all, these methods either require explicit noise annotations or involve multiple forward passes and resampling, making them computationally expensive and less suitable for real-time applications. 

Uncertainty Estimation. Uncertainty modeling provides a principled way to handle distributional shifts and ambigu-ous inputs. Bayesian deep learning [27], [28] and ensemble methods [29] offer reliable uncertainty estimates but are com-putationally expensive. Spectral-normalized neural Gaussian processes (SNGP) [30] introduce scalable uncertainty esti-3

mation by combining random Fourier features with spectral normalization, enabling efficient, single-pass predictive vari-ance computation. Recent works integrate similar uncertainty modules into 2D vision tasks [31], but their application to large-scale 3D segmentation remains underexplored. 

Summary. Prior research has established complementary ad-vances in engineering informatics for robotic disassembly, point-cloud segmentation and denoising, and scalable uncer-tainty estimation. The remaining challenge addressed in this work is how to integrate these capabilities when corrupted measurements form locally plausible structures that may still receive confident semantic predictions. GUARD addresses this issue by modeling uncertainty in learned geometric repre-sentations and combining it with semantic entropy to guide reliability-aware filtering during segmentation. This formula-tion provides a reliability-aware representation of imperfect geometric information, supporting more robust part-level in-terpretation of engineering point clouds affected by structured reconstruction artifacts. III. M ETHODOLOGY 

A. Overview 

Our goal is to achieve robust and uncertainty-aware point cloud segmentation under noisy perturbations and structured reconstruction artifacts. To this end, we propose a hybrid architecture that integrates a multi-scale geometric encoder with a Gaussian process–based uncertainty head designed for geometric feature-space modeling. Unlike prior SNGP implementations that attach the Gaussian process module to classifier logits, our module operates on intermediate geomet-ric embeddings, allowing it to assign higher uncertainty to geometrically inconsistent structures such as ghost clusters or misaligned fragments. However, geometric uncertainty alone may not fully capture fine-grained ambiguity near object boundaries. Therefore, we further introduce a lightweight logit-entropy measure at the final prediction layer to character-ize residual semantic uncertainty. This dual-uncertainty design enables the model to jointly reason about geometric reliability and semantic ambiguity, thereby improving robustness across different corruption conditions. The overall framework is il-lustrated in Fig. 1. Within the proposed framework, relative offsets and dis-tances characterize local geometric relationships, while the geometry-aware transformer captures broader spatial context in the learned feature representation. The Gaussian process– based uncertainty head estimates the reliability of these ge-ometric representations, whereas predictive entropy reflects ambiguity in the corresponding semantic predictions. Their combination provides a point-wise reliability measure that is used to filter unreliable measurements, while the segmentation backbone produces the associated part labels. In this way, GUARD links geometric representation, reliability assessment, and semantic interpretation, transforming imperfect point-cloud measurements into more reliable part-level information for downstream robotic disassembly. This information-quality perspective is particularly important for engineering scans in which structured artifacts may appear geometrically plausible and can otherwise be retained as valid components.

D. Hybrid Uncertainty Fusion for Robust Segmentation 

The variance estimated by the MultiRFF-GP head effec-tively identifies major outliers that lie far from the learned geometric manifold. These large ghost clusters , often caused by repeated scans or global misalignments, exhibit clearly inconsistent structures and are well separated from valid parts in the feature space. However, not all ghost clusters are distant. Some minor clusters appear very close to real object surfaces—spatially overlapping with valid geometry and sharing similar local patterns. Even for humans, such near-surface outliers are difficult to judge: they may or may not be manually removed across datasets, resulting in inconsistent data quality. Consequently, the GP head, which focuses on large-scale geometric deviations, becomes less sensitive to these small but semantically confusing artifacts. In contrast, the predictive entropy of the softmax logits reflects the model’s uncertainty about semantic consistency. When a point lies close to the true surface but carries mixed or duplicated local features, its neighborhood generates fluc-tuating logits across multiple parts, producing higher entropy than normal points. Therefore, entropy naturally captures these boundary-level or label-like ambiguities that variance alone cannot highlight. As illustrated in Fig. 3, the ghost clusters manifest as glob-ally inconsistent duplicate structures, whereas closed ghost cluster corresponds to locally confusing regions near the part surface. E. Implementation Details 

We implement GUARD in PyTorch, integrating the MultiRFF-GP head into both PointNet++ and DGCNN back-bones. During training, the precision matrix is accumulated online to capture the evolving feature distribution and is frozen during testing for efficient inference. A. Experiment Settings 

Dataset We evaluate our method on three datasets covering real-world scans, standard object-level benchmarks, and large-scale indoor scenes. First, we construct a real-scan dataset of hard disk drives (HDDs), which contains 2,745 point clouds collected from different brands and models. The diversity in brand and design introduces substantial shape variations, making the dataset challenging. We randomly split the dataset into 80% for training and 20% for testing. In addition, we adopt the widely used ShapeNetPart bench-mark [15] for part segmentation, which consists of 12,128 training samples and 2,874 testing samples across 16 object categories. This benchmark enables direct comparison with existing works in point cloud segmentation. Our primary benchmark focuses on part segmentation rather than large-scale semantic segmentation benchmarks such as S3DIS, since our target application emphasizes fine-grained object under-standing for robotic manipulation tasks (e.g., desktop disas-sembly). In these scenarios, recognizing detailed object parts is more critical than scene-level semantics, making ShapeNetPart a more suitable benchmark. Together with our real-scan HDD dataset, which captures domain-specific challenges such as brand variations and scanning noise, ShapeNetPart provides a complementary evaluation: the former reflects real-world robotic use cases, while the latter offers a controlled and widely recognized benchmark for fair comparison. To further validate robustness and generality, we evaluate our method on ScanNet [16], a large-scale indoor scene dataset with complex layouts and reconstruction artifacts. This experiment examines whether our uncertainty-guided filtering remains effective under challenging real-world conditions. 

Noise Injection. To evaluate robustness, we further inject synthetic noise into ShapeNetPart following the protocol of PointCloud-C [32](local and global noise), which system-atically introduces outliers under five severity levels (5%, 10%, 20%, 30%, and 40%). These severity ratios are not arbitrarily chosen: our analysis of the HDD dataset shows that the proportion of noisy points in real scans typically ranges from about 5% in the cleanest cases to nearly 40% in the noisiest ones. Unlike the original setup that replaces a fraction of points with outliers while keeping the total count fixed, we found from real HDD scans that noise more commonly appears as extra points rather than missing ones—since data acquisition often favors capturing more points to avoid losing information. Therefore, we revise the PointCloud-C procedure by appending additional noisy points instead of replacement, yielding a noise distribution closer to real-world scans. In our experiments, we particularly highlight the 5% (least) and 40% (most) cases, which directly reflect the realistic noise levels observed in practice. Beyond random local or global perturbations, we further introduce a ghost-cluster noise that replicates the duplicated structures frequently observed in real HDD scans. Each ghost cluster is generated by cloning a subset of points from a dominant object part and applying a small rigid transformation consisting of random rotation and translation. The severity level controls the number and spatial deviation of these clusters: higher levels produce more duplicated parts with larger displacements. This process yields globally inconsistent but locally coherent structures, closely matching the “double-scanned” artifacts commonly seen in practical industrial 3D sensing.

B. Main Results 1) Noise sensitivity of backbones: We first evaluate two widely used segmentation backbones, PointNet++ and DGCNN, under both clean and noisy inputs. As shown in Table I, both models undergo substantial performance degra-dation when noise is present. For instance, under ghost-cluster noise, PointNet++ drops from 0.8449 mIoU (clean) to 0.6082, and DGCNN from 0.8501 to 0.6200. This confirms that existing architectures are highly vulnerable to outliers, motivating the need for plug-in modules to enhance robustness without redesigning the backbone. 

2) Comparison with plug-in modules: We compare our approach with PointCVaR [24], the most recent plug-in mod-ule for outlier and noise removal that can be inserted into PointNet++ and DGCNN. We evaluate on both the HDD scan data and ShapeNetPart with synthetic corruptions. As shown in Table II, GUARD achieves stronger overall robustness than the vanilla backbones and their PointCVaR-augmented counterparts. On HDD, it improves mIoU by 7.5% over the vanilla PointNet++, highlighting strong generalization to real-world scans. On ShapeNetPart, our approach achieves up to 6–8% higher average mIoU under GhostCluster and Local noise, maintaining robustness even at 40% corruption severity. These results confirm that our method not only enhances segmentation accuracy but also produces more stable and trustworthy predictions, which are critical for robotic applications. For brevity, we report the overall results here, while the per-category performance is analyzed in the later experiment subsection. 

3) Reliability and Efficiency: In addition to robustness, practical deployment in robotic systems demands reliability and efficiency. As shown in Table III, GUARD retains a smaller percentage of noisy points across most corruption types and severity levels. The improvement is most pro-nounced for GhostCluster noise, reducing residual noise from 26.9% to 22.3% at S5, while also achieving steady gains under Local corruptions. These results indicate that our uncertainty-guided filtering effectively suppresses spurious points while preserving structural integrity, providing a strong foundation for downstream manipulation and segmentation tasks. Meanwhile, we also compare inference speed. As illustrated in Fig. 4a, our approach achieves efficient single-pass infer-ence, with an average latency of 169 ms per sample on PointNet++ and 20 ms on DGCNN when tested on an RTX 4080 GPU. This is significantly faster than PointCVaR, which requires two forward passes (average 623 ms on PointNet++ and 26 ms on DGCNN) and exhibits higher tail latency (95th percentile 43.5 ms vs. ours 27.7 ms). These results highlight that our single-pass design achieves a favorable balance be-tween robustness and computational efficiency, making it well-suited for real-time robotic applications. As shown in Fig. 4b, our method consistently surpasses PointCVaR across all noise types and severity levels. For both local and global random noise, it maintains notably higher mIoU under increasing corruption, showing strong resilience to spatial distortions and large-scale perturbations. More importantly, our approach excels under ghost-cluster noise, where duplicated or ambiguous structures often cause severe misclassification. While PointCVaR rapidly degrades, our uncertainty-guided filtering effectively distinguishes gen-uine surface points from mirrored clusters.

A key observation from the experiments is that geometric reliability and semantic confidence capture different aspects of point-cloud quality. A corrupted structure may remain locally plausible and therefore receive a confident semantic prediction, even though its geometry is inconsistent with the underlying object. The uncertainty analyses indicate that explicitly modeling geometric uncertainty provides comple-mentary information to predictive entropy for identifying such measurements. This distinction is particularly important for structured reconstruction artifacts, where semantic confidence alone may not adequately reflect the reliability of the observed geometry. The results also highlight a tradeoff between artifact re-moval and preservation of information useful for segmentation. More aggressive filtering can suppress additional corrupted measurements, but may also remove informative points that contribute to part recognition. Conversely, retaining more points may preserve discriminative geometric details while allowing some unreliable measurements to remain. Therefore, segmentation accuracy alone does not fully characterize de-noising quality. For engineering perception, both the reliability of the retained geometry and its usefulness for identifying components should be considered when selecting the filtering strategy. For robotic disassembly, GUARD serves as a perception-stage mechanism for converting imperfect 3D measurements into more reliable part-level information. By associating ge-ometric representations with uncertainty and combining this information with semantic predictions, the framework can reduce the influence of duplicated or inconsistent structures before the resulting representation is used by downstream manipulation or planning modules. The present experiments evaluate this capability through point filtering, part segmen-tation, and uncertainty-based corruption detection across the 12 

HDD, ShapeNetPart, and ScanNet datasets. The present study focuses on perception-level reliability, while future work will examine how improvements in geometric reliability translate into downstream robotic manipulation and disassembly perfor-mance. VI. C ONCLUSION 

This study presented GUARD, a geometric uncertainty-aware framework for unreliable-point filtering and segmen-tation of engineering point clouds. By combining multi-scale geometric representations, Gaussian Process-based un-certainty estimation, and semantic entropy, GUARD distin-guishes geometric reliability from confidence in part identifica-tion within a single-pass architecture. Evaluation on real HDD scans demonstrates improved PointNet++ part segmentation, while complementary benchmarks and ablations character-ize corrupted-point detection, computational efficiency, and backbone-dependent filtering behavior. The results show that artifact suppression and preservation of informative geometry must be considered jointly rather than treating segmentation accuracy as the sole indicator of perception quality. GUARD therefore contributes both a computational method and empir-ical understanding of reliability-aware 3D interpretation, pro-viding a perception-level foundation for component handling and robotic disassembly under imperfect sensing conditions.
