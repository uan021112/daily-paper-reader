Abstract
Feed-forward 3D reconstruction models have achieved remarkable
progress through large-scale training and strong learned priors.
However, they remain fragile when applied to dynamic in-the-
wild videos, where non-rigid motion, occlusions, and illumination
changes are prevalent. A key limitation lies in their reliance on ex-
pensive depth and camera pose annotations, which hinders scaling
to diverse real-world dynamic data and restricts generalization. To
address this issue, we propose PURE, an unlabeled post-training
refinement framework that improves depth and camera estima-
tion without requiring ground-truth supervision. Instead of relying

Introduction
Recovering accurate 3D geometry and camera motion from videos
is a fundamental problem in computer vision, with applications [2,
11, 13, 50, 51] ranging from robotics and autonomous driving to
augmented reality and digital content creation. Classical Structure-
from-Motion (SfM) and visual SLAM systems achieve high accuracy
through iterative optimization over sparse correspondences and
bundle adjustment, but rely on rigid-scene assumptions, sufficient
parallax, and carefully engineered pipelines. These limitations com-
promise the robustness of such methods in dynamic scenes and
impede their scalability to unconstrained real-world videos.
Recent advances in feed-forward 3D reconstruction models have
significantly shifted this paradigm. By leveraging large-scale datasets
such as CO3Dv2 [29] and powerful Transformer-based [37] neu-
ral architectures, these models directly regress depth and camera
parameters from images or videos, enabling fast and scalable recon-
struction. Large video-based models such as VGGT [38], Pi3 [43],
and DepthAnything3 [21] demonstrate impressive zero-shot per-
formance on long sequences, highlighting the potential of data-
driven priors for 3D understanding. Despite this progress, most
feed-forward models are trained with strong supervision, including
ground-truth depth, camera poses, or synthetic renderings, which
are costly to acquire and limit generalization to real-world dynamic
scenes.
Adapting pre-trained 3D reconstruction models to in-the-wild
videos without annotations remains a challenging problem. Existing
unsupervised approaches (e.g., [10, 54]) typically rely on photomet-
ric consistency, which fundamentally degrades in the presence of il-
lumination changes, non-Lambertian surfaces, and highly dynamic
objects. Conversely, methods relying on test-time optimization
or per-scene adaptation [12, 20, 25, 48] incur prohibitive compu-
tational overhead, rendering them impractical for efficient video
processing. Consequently, an effective annotation-free strategy
that refines feed-forward models at the dataset level—preserving
inference efficiency—remains largely underexplored.
In this work, we propose a novel post-training framework, termed
PURE (Post-training Unlabeled REconstruction), for annotation-
free refinement of feed-forward 3D reconstruction models. PURE
adapts a pre-trained base model using unconstrained video se-
quences, without requiring ground-truth supervision or slow test-
time optimization. Instead of relying on photometric signals, PURE
distills robust structural knowledge from state-of-the-art 2D monoc-
ular priors into the 4D feed-forward architecture. To enable stable
refinement despite the absolute scale and shift errors inherent in
teacher models, we introduce relative depth constraints that pre-
serve ordinal depth, local affine-invariant geometry distillation that
maintains fine-grained surface structure, and correspondence-based
pose refinement that enforces multi-view consistency.
We validate our approach through extensive experiments on
both synthetic and real-world dynamic datasets, including video
depth estimation, monocular depth prediction, and camera pose
evaluation. Across multiple state-of-the-art architectures, our post-
training strategy consistently improves performance and robust-
ness, particularly in challenging dynamic scenes. These results
demonstrate that annotation-free post-training offers a practical
and scalable pathway for advancing feed-forward 3D reconstruc-
tion in real-world settings.
In summary, the contributions of this work are as follows:
• A Novel Post-Training Paradigm: We propose PURE,
a unified, model-agnostic post-training framework that
adapts pre-trained feed-forward 3D reconstruction models
to in-the-wild videos using purely unlabeled data, elimi-
nating the need for ground-truth annotations or test-time
optimization.
• Robust Geometry Distillation: We introduce a noise-
tolerant distillation strategy utilizing relative depth order-
ing and local affine-invariant constraints. This allows the
base model to absorb rich structural priors from 2D teacher
models while actively filtering out their absolute scale and
shift errors.
• Dynamic-Aware Pose Refinement: We design a correspondence-
based camera pose refinement mechanism featuring a rigor-
ous filtering and sampling pipeline, ensuring reliable multi-
view consistency even in scenes dominated by non-rigid
dynamic motion.

Related Work
2.1
Optimization-based 3D Reconstruction
Classical SfM and visual SLAM systems recover camera poses and
scene geometry through iterative optimization using 2D correspon-
dences and bundle adjustment. While accurate in controlled settings
(e.g., COLMAP [32, 33]), they rely on rigid scenes, sufficient parallax,
and reliable feature matches.
Recent optimization-based methods [6, 12, 20, 25, 36, 44, 45, 52]
extend this paradigm to handle dense geometry and dynamic con-
tent. Systems such as MegaSAM [20] and ViPE [12] combine dense
depth estimation, motion reasoning, and iterative refinement to re-
construct long video sequences. Despite improved robustness, these
approaches remain computationally expensive and require care-
ful system design, limiting their scalability to diverse in-the-wild
videos.
2.2
Feed-forward 3D Reconstruction
To overcome the limitations of iterative optimization, recent works [5,
7, 14, 17, 18, 21, 24, 26, 38, 40, 42, 43, 46, 47, 49, 56] have explored
feed-forward 3D reconstruction models leveraging large-scale data-
driven priors. These methods can be broadly divided into pair-wise
and video-based approaches.
Pair-wise methods originate from DUSt3R [42], which formu-
lates multi-view reconstruction as dense point-map regression from
image pairs. Subsequent works [14, 18, 24, 49] extend this paradigm
to dynamic videos by incorporating temporal fine-tuning or monoc-
ular depth priors, but remain limited by pair-wise processing and
poor scalability to long sequences.
Video-based methods extend feed-forward reconstruction to
full video sequences. Models such as CUT3R [40], StreamVGGT [56],
Point3R [46], and TTT3R [7] capture long-range temporal depen-
dencies via memory or streaming mechanisms, while VGGT [38],
Pi3 [43], and DepthAnything3 [21] learn strong spatio-temporal
priors from large-scale video data. Despite strong performance,
these models remain brittle on dynamic, in-the-wild videos without

training of feed-forward 3D reconstruction models (Fig. 3). Given
a sequence of 𝐾images 𝐼= {𝐼1, . . . , 𝐼𝐾}, the base model F𝜃pre-

These predictions are refined via local geometry optimization using
ordinal depth, affine-invariant constraints, and intrinsic distilla-
tion, and through dense correspondences from GIM [34] filtered
by confidence, cycle consistency, and inverse density sampling. By
combining these mechanisms, our framework improves both ge-
ometry and camera pose estimation, yielding more accurate and
temporally consistent 3D reconstructions from unlabeled video
sequences.
3.1
Relative Local Geometry Optimization
Monocular depth estimators [41] produce depth maps with more
accurate relative ordering and local geometric structures compared
to feed-forward 3D reconstruction models [21, 38, 43], as illustrated
in Fig. 2. However, their predictions often exhibit temporal scale in-
consistencies. Directly supervising feed-forward 3D reconstruction
models with monocular depth can lead to pronounced “layering”
artifacts, since monocular object-wise depth scales may vary sig-
nificantly across frames and deviate from the 3D reconstruction
model’s learned geometric prior. To address this issue, we avoid
absolute depth supervision and instead introduce two relative con-
straints: an ordinal depth ranking loss and a local affine-invariant
loss. These losses transfer the reliable structural and local geomet-
ric cues from the monocular teacher while remaining invariant to
global scale and shift, enabling stable refinement without collapsing
the underlying geometry of the 3D reconstruction models.

3.2
Pose Estimation via Correspondences
Accurate camera pose estimation is essential for coherent multi-
view reconstruction. Traditional Structure-from-Motion (SfM) pipelines
rely on sparse 2D feature correspondences to recover epipolar ge-
ometry. To approximate this process in an unsupervised manner, we
leverage GIM [34] for robust correspondence estimation between
image pairs. These correspondences serve as powerful constraints,
ensuring that matched 2D pixels correspond to the same 3D point
in space. To guarantee the reliability of these constraints, we im-
plement a rigorous filtering pipeline:
• Dynamic and Confidence Filtering: Matches located in
dynamic regions or with confidence scores below a pre-
defined threshold 𝜏are discarded.
• Cycle Consistency Check: We enforce forward-backward
cycle consistency (𝑝𝐴→𝑝𝐵→𝑝′
𝐴) to remove ambiguous
or erroneous matches.
• Inverse Density Sampling (IDS): High-confidence corre-
spondences often cluster in texture-rich regions, potentially
biasing the global pose estimation. To address this, we em-
ploy IDS, which prioritizes sampling matches from sparser,
lower-confidence regions. This ensures a spatially uniform
distribution of constraints and improves global robustness.

Experiments
We present implementation details in Sec. 4.1 and primarily evaluate
our approach on dynamic-scene benchmarks, focusing on video
depth estimation (Sec. 4.2) and camera pose estimation (Sec. 4.3).
To further validate the effectiveness of our annotation-free post-
training strategy, we conduct additional experiments in which post-
training is performed directly on the test sets (Sec. 4.4). This setting
isolates the impact of the proposed post-training objectives from


Zhu et al.
data scale and distribution effects, demonstrating that our method
consistently improves geometric predictions even when adaptation
is carried out on the evaluation data itself. Finally, we present
detailed ablation studies in Sec. 4.5 to analyze the contributions of
individual framework components, alongside a scaling-up analysis
that investigates the impact of post-training data volume on overall
performance.
4.1
Implementation Details
We fine-tune three baseline models: VGGT [38], Pi3 [43], and DepthAny-
thing3 (DA3) [21]. For VGGT and DA3, optimization is applied to
the intermediate global attention layers. For Pi3, in addition to these
layers, we also train a newly replaced point prediction head.
To support video inputs with varying resolutions, we constrain
image aspect ratios to the range [0.5, 2.0] and limit the total number
of pixels to between 160k and 250k. Our training setup includes both
multi-view and single-view inputs, where the number of views is
uniformly sampled from [1, 24]. To ensure stable training efficiency,
the batch size is dynamically adjusted to maintain an approximately
constant number of tokens per iteration.
All experiments are conducted on 4× H800 GPUs, using dy-
namic batch sizing and a gradient accumulation step of 2. We adopt
AdamW optimizer with a learning rate of 1 × 10−5, weight decay of
5 × 10−2, and 𝛽= (0.9, 0.95). The learning rate is scheduled using
OneCycleLR with cosine annealing and a maximum learning rate
of 1 × 10−5. Gradient clipping is applied with a maximum norm of
1.0.
In terms of training efficiency, the PURE post-training stage
takes approximately 20 hours for VGGT (12K iterations) and 8
hours for DA3 (5K iterations). For Pi3, training is divided into two
stages: a depth head initialization phase (10K iterations, approxi-
mately 12 hours), followed by a post-training phase (5K iterations,
approximately 8 hours).
Notably, PURE is a post-training framework that introduces
negligible architectural modifications (except for a minor head
replacement in Pi3). As a result, the inference computational cost
remains identical to that of the original base models.
4.2
Video Depth Estimation
We evaluate our method on video depth estimation in dynamic
scenes, which is particularly challenging due to non-rigid mo-
tion, moving objects, and temporal inconsistencies. Following prior
work [38, 40, 43], we conduct experiments on a diverse set of
real-world and synthetic datasets, including Bonn [27], Sintel [3],
KITTI [9], and Dycheck [8], and additionally present qualitative
results on in-the-wild videos.
We report Absolute Relative Error (Abs Rel) and threshold ac-
curacy (𝛿< 1.25) under two alignment settings: (i) scale-only
alignment and (ii) joint scale and 3D translation alignment. As
shown in Tab. 2, our proposed post-training consistently improves
performance across nearly all datasets and backbone architectures,
demonstrating the robustness of our approach. Notably, even for
Pi3—where the original pixel-shuffle point head is replaced and
retrained with fewer parameters—our method yields clear gains.
VGGT and DepthAnything3 also benefit consistently from post-
training across all evaluated datasets, indicating effective geometric
refinement.
As illustrated in Fig. 4, all three models exhibit visibly improved
reconstruction quality after post-training, especially in regions with
strong object motion. Post-training enhances temporal consistency
and reduces dynamic artifacts, leading to more stable and accu-
rate depth predictions. These qualitative results corroborate the
quantitative improvements and highlight the effectiveness of our
annotation-free post-training strategy for dynamic scene recon-
struction.
4.3
Camera Pose Estimation
We evaluate predicted camera poses using two complementary sets
of metrics: angular accuracy [38, 43] and distance error [40, 49].
Specifically, we measure performance using the Absolute Trajec-
tory Error (ATE), Relative Pose Error for translation (RPEtrans), and
Relative Pose Error for rotation (RPErot) on both synthetic and
real-world datasets.
Experiments are conducted on the synthetic outdoor Sintel [3]
dataset, as well as the real-world indoor Bonn [27] and DyCheck [8]
datasets. To account for scale ambiguity, all predicted camera tra-
jectories are first aligned to the corresponding ground-truth trajec-
tories using a Sim(3) transformation before computing errors. As
shown in Tab. 3, post-training with our annotation-free strategy
consistently improves camera pose estimation accuracy across all
datasets, demonstrating that our method enhances robustness to
non-rigid motion and complex scene dynamics.
4.4
Post-Training with Test-Set Data
To evaluate the effectiveness of our annotation-free post-training
strategy under limited data regimes, we conduct additional experi-
ments on VGGT [38] using images from the Bonn [27], Sintel [3],
and KITTI [9] test sets. These images are processed and used for
post-training with our proposed framework.
For the Sintel dataset, we post-train on a selected subset, exclud-
ing scenes with insufficient static regions for reliable correspon-
dence estimation. Following standard protocols, we report Abs Rel
for depth under joint scale and 3D translation alignment, and ATE
for camera pose estimation.
As shown in Tab. 4, post-training on test-set data consistently
improves performance across all evaluated benchmarks. However,
the gains are smaller than those obtained when post-training is
conducted on large-scale unlabeled video datasets. This indicates
that, while the proposed method remains effective even with lim-
ited data, its benefits scale more substantially with increased data
diversity and volume. These results underscore the importance of
scaling up annotation-free post-training on rich, large-scale unla-
beled video collections to fully unlock the potential of feed-forward
3D reconstruction models.

Ablation Study
To thoroughly analyze the contribution of each component in our
annotation-free post-training framework, we conduct ablation stud-
ies on the overall framework effectiveness and its scaling behavior
with increasing data.

Component Ablation. We first evaluate the impact of each
component on the DyCheck dataset using VGGT as the base model.
All variants are tested under the same protocol, reporting both
depth estimation accuracy and camera trajectory errors.
Specifically, w/o all denotes a variant trained only on datasets
with ground-truth annotations (Tab. 1), without leveraging our
large-scale unlabeled video data. This results in clear performance
degradation, demonstrating the critical importance of annotation-
free data for both depth and pose estimation.
We further ablate individual loss terms. Removing the ordinal
depth loss (L𝑜𝑟𝑑𝑒𝑟) degrades both depth accuracy and camera stabil-
ity, indicating its importance in preserving relative depth ordering
in unconstrained scenes. Similarly, removing the local geometry
distillation loss (L𝑙𝑜𝑐𝑎𝑙) leads to worse performance, particularly in
camera trajectory metrics, highlighting its role in enforcing cross-
view geometric consistency.
Overall, the full model achieves the best performance across all
metrics, confirming that both large-scale unlabeled data and the
proposed geometric supervision losses are essential and comple-
mentary.

5
Conclusion
We present PURE, an annotation-free post-training framework for
adapting feed-forward 3D reconstruction models to unconstrained
dynamic videos. By distilling relative geometric priors and enforc-
ing correspondence-based pose consistency, PURE significantly
enhances depth and camera estimation across state-of-the-art ar-
chitectures. Ultimately, this work demonstrates that unsupervised
post-training is a highly effective, scalable pathway for unlocking
robust 3D reconstruction in the wild.
