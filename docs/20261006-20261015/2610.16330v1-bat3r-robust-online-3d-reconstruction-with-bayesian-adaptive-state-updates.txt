## Abstract 

Feed-forward transformer models have driven remarkable progress in 3D vision, yet their quadratic complexity renders them impractical for long video sequences. Streaming 3D reconstruction addresses this bottleneck by processing frames sequen-tially with constant memory. However, existing recurrent architectures inevitably suffer from progressive degradation over long sequences, as they fail to account for the intrinsic reliability of observations and the accumulated confidence of the historical state, leading to severe error accumulation. To address these issues, we introduce BAT3R, a training-free Bayesian adaptation framework comprising two synergistic modules: State Innovation Gating (SIG) and Bayesian-guided Adaptive State Updating (BASU). SIG acts as a distributional regularizer that anchors state evolution to its geometric initialization, effectively arresting representational drift during long-term inference. Building on this, BASU reformulates state update as a recursive Bayesian inference process within a Kalman filtering framework. By jointly modeling the temporal reliability of observations via information-theoretic gain and spatial relevance via cross-attention maps, BASU achieves an asymmetric spatio-temporal state fusion: well-consolidated historical regions are protected from redundant updates, while genuinely novel scene content is aggressively incor-porated. Extensive experiments on standard benchmarks (7-Scenes, TUM, ScanNet, KITTI, etc.) demonstrate that BAT3R consistently outperforms state-of-the-art online 3D reconstruction methods, with the most significant gains observed on long sequences and complex scenes. 

## 1 Introduction 

Real-time 3D scene understanding from monocular video streams, as illustrated in Fig. 1, is a founda-tional challenge in computer vision, with direct applications in robotic navigation and augmented reality . Recent feed-forward transformer models [45, 42, 57], such as DUSt3R [45] and VGGT [42], have achieved remarkable reconstruction accuracy through dense global attention [38] that jointly reasons across all input frames; however, the computational and memory cost of these methods grows quadratically with sequence length, rendering them impractical for continuous perception over arbitrarily long videos. Test-Time Training (TTT) has recently emerged as a compelling paradigm for efficient, linear-complexity sequence modeling. In the context of spatial perception, TTT3R [10] pioneers this formulation by conceptualizing the persistent scene state as dynamic “fast weights” [32, 33] that are recursively updated during inference. However, while TTT3R employs an attention-based soft gating mechanism to modulate these updates, this heuristic approach lacks strict probabilistic bounds. Consequently, over long-horizon sequences, these unconstrained recursive updates inevitably suffer from progressive representational drift. Mitigating such cumulative distortion requires a principled rethinking of state integration. On one hand, as evidenced by classical visual odometry [24, 47] and recent video generation frameworks [21, 30, 59], sequential drift can be explicitly bounded by anchoring recurrent updates to a reliable global reference (e.g., the initial frame), which prevents the latent state from deviating outside the valid geometric manifold. On the other hand, from a probabilistic standpoint, heuristic gating fundamentally deviates from the rigorous principles of Bayesian state estimation [2]. This uncertainty-aware paradigm has recently demonstrated profound success in suppressing drift across diverse vision tasks, including dynamic NeRFs [56], streaming 3D Gaussians [50], and video diffusion [52]. By reformulating the update process within a Bayesian framework, the integration of novel information is no longer arbitrary; rather, it is strictly governed by an optimal balance between the prior covariance, which encodes the accumulated confidence of the historical state, and the measurement noise, which quantifies the intrinsic uncertainty of incoming observations. While reformulating the recurrent update as Bayesian inference offers a principled solution to sequen-tial drift, calibrating the requisite noise models conventionally demands domain-specific training. However, we observe that spatial and temporal uncertainty signals are already inherently encoded within the pretrained transformer. Specifically, cross-attention alignment implicitly captures spatial visibility, while the prediction head’s confidence maps provide per-pixel proxies for observation reliability. Building upon this insight, we propose BAT3R, a training-free Bayesian adaptation framework for uncertainty-aware state updating in streaming 3D reconstruction, which comprises two synergistic modules (see Fig. 2). First, State Innovation Gating (SIG) acts as a distributional regularizer that explicitly anchors the recurrent state evolution to its geometric initialization, bound-ing spatial divergence by suppressing updates that fall outside the reliable latent manifold. Second, Bayesian-guided Adaptive State Updating (BASU) addresses optimal observation fusion by reformu-lating state integration as recursive Bayesian inference. By jointly modeling temporal observation reliability through information-theoretic gain and spatial relevance via cross-attention aggregation, BASU achieves an asymmetric spatio-temporal fusion, protecting well-consolidated memory from redundant updates while preserving full plasticity for novel scene content. Extensive experiments across seven standard benchmarks demonstrate that BAT3R substantially improves sequence length 2generalization. BAT3R reduces camera pose ATE by up to 67.8% and 32.3% relative to CUT3R and TTT3R, respectively, while maintaining the exact similar computational cost as the baselines. 

## 2 Related Works 

2.1 Streaming Online Reconstruction Foundation Models. 

To improve scalability, streaming methods progressively register local geometries onto a canonical 3D space, mitigating computational overhead from redundant multi-view observations. StreamVGGT [64] introduces a causal transformer for streaming 4D spatio-temporal reconstruction, employing temporal causal attention and KV-caching [28] as implicit memory for incremental historical context integration. However, it inherits the scaling bottlenecks of VGGT [42], incurring excessive resource overhead as frame count increases. STream3R [55] reformulates 3D perception as a decoder-only task, leveraging causal attention for scalable reconstruction in both static and dynamic environments. Spann3R [41] extends the DUSt3R paradigm with an external spatial memory to track 3D context, enabling direct regression of pointmaps into a unified global coordinate system. Point3R [48] uses hierarchical 3D position embeddings and an explicit pointer-based memory to consolidate streaming visual information into a persistent scene representation. Recurrent architectures [8, 14, 41], exemplified by CUT3R, maintain a persistent latent memory to reconcile historical context with new observations recursively. While they stabilize inference memory usage, the inherent forgetting problem [11, 22] in recurrent units compromises long-term consistency, causing significant performance degradation as input views accumulate. More relevant models are provided in A.1. 

2.2 Test-Time Training for 3D Reconstruction. 

The O(N 2) complexity of standard Transformers [40] has motivated the development of linear-complexity architectures. Modern RNNs and Linear Transformers [25], such as Mamba [20, 16] and DeltaNet [32], maintain a finite-sized hidden state as an efficient alternative. To counter infor-mation decay, these models incorporate advanced gating mechanisms, including input-dependent selection [20] and delta-rule updates [53], which regulate information flow across extended sequences. Paradigms such as Titans [4] and TTT [37, 60] reframe state updates as test-time optimization, en-abling hidden states to function as fast weights [37, 43] for rapid online adaptation [29]. Recent works have ported the TTT paradigm to 3D reconstruction, achieving linear-time inference by compressing historical context [17] into fixed-capacity states. TTT3R [10] reinterprets recurrent state update as an online learning problem, using test-time optimization to iteratively align historical geometric memory with incoming observations. ZipMap [23] employs a stateful architecture driven by test-time training to continuously compress dense pointmap priors, enabling scalable multi-view association across extended video streams. To mitigate catastrophic forgetting in recurrent frameworks, subsequent research has introduced training-free adaptive mechanisms. TTSA3R [62] dynamically modulates update intensity based on temporal scene evolution, while MeMix [18] partitions the recurrent state into independent patches to prevent transient noise from corrupting stable structural components. In contrast, BAT3R replaces heuristic updates with a principled Bayesian filter that mathematically models the confidence of spatial observations, achieving robust probabilistic state updates.

3.3 Bayesian-guided Adaptive State Updating 

Given the drift-regularized candidate eSt, determining the optimal update magnitude for each state token becomes the central challenge. The heuristic gate βt in TTT3R relies exclusively on attention alignment, overlooking crucial factors: it neither assesses the intrinsic reliability of the prediction nor accounts for the accumulated confidence of the existing state token, and fails to distinguish whether a given token is even visible in the current frame. We address these two concerns jointly. We augment the deterministic state with a per-token uncertainty Σt ∈ RN , establishing a Gaussian belief qt = N (μt, diag( Σt)) over the N state tokens. Here S0 ∈ RN ×D denotes the scene state obtained by encoding the first frame through the image encoder and projecting into the state space, and Σ0 = σ20 1N with σ20 = 10 3, reflecting complete prior ignorance. Σt functions as a historical ledger of observation quality, progressively decreasing as reliable frames are accumulated over time. 4.1 Experimental Setup Datasets. We evaluate BAT3R on three tasks across Seven benchmarks. For camera pose estimation, we evaluate BAT3R on TUM dynamics [36], ScanNet [15], and Sintel [7] datasets. TUM dynamics contains indoor RGB-D sequences captured with a hand-held camera, including both static scenes and dynamic scenes with moving people. ScanNet contains large-scale indoor RGB-D sequences captured across diverse room types with significant viewpoint variation. Sintel contains long synthetic sequences rendered from a 3D animated film, featuring complex motions, dynamic objects, and challenging lighting conditions. For video depth estimation, we evaluate BAT3R on Bonn [31], KITTI [19], and Sintel [7] datasets. Bonn contains highly dynamic indoor RGB-D sequences, featuring complex human activities, fast-moving objects, and varying camera motions. KITTI contains large-scale outdoor driving sequences captured from a moving vehicle, including real-world street views with dynamic entities such as pedestrians and cars. For 3D reconstruction, we evaluate on 7-Scenes [35] and NRGBD [3], which contains indoor RGB-D sequences of seven distinct room categories captured with a Kinect sensor, commonly used to assess geometric reconstruction accuracy. 

Baselines . We evaluate BAT3R against state-of-the-art online 3D reconstruction baselines, including the recurrent CUT3R [44] and its long-sequence extensions, Point3R [48] and StreamVGGT [64], which rely on explicit spatial memory and KV-caching mechanisms. Furthermore, we compare against recent training-free Test-Time Training (TTT) frameworks , such as TTT3R [10] and TTSA3R [62], which utilize heuristic gating and spatio-temporal alignment for adaptive state updates. 

4.2 Camera Pose Estimation 

Consistent with established evaluation protocols [58], we assess camera pose estimation on the TUM dynamics [36] and ScanNet [15] datasets. Performance is measured using the standard Absolute Translation Error (ATE) following Sim(3) trajectory alignment [39] between the estimated and ground-truth trajectories. The long-sequence evaluation results are presented in Fig. 4. For a fair comparison, we adopt the experimental settings and baseline data established by TTT3R [10]. Empirical evaluations demonstrate that BAT3R consistently bounds representational drift, sustaining high-accuracy pose estimation across widely varying sequence lengths ranging from 50 to 1000 frames. When scaling to 1000-frame sequences, BAT3R reduces ATE by 67.8% on ScanNet and 66.1% on TUM dynamics compared to the CUT3R baseline. Even when compared to the competitive TTT3R, BAT3R achieves a further error reduction of 32.3% and 44.4% on ScanNet and TUM, respectively. Besides, we also conduct comprehensive evaluations of GPU memory consumption and inference speed on the ScanNet dataset in Fig. 3. As is shown in Fig. 3a, 3b, BAT3R achieves competitive GPU memory and inference speed compared with previous methods. 4.4 3D Reconstruction 

Following established protocols [41, 44, 12], we evaluate 3D reconstruction on the 7-Scenes [35] dataset. Geometric accuracy is quantified through Chamfer Distance which denotes the average of accuracy and completeness, and Normal Consistency (NC). In contrast to the sparse sampling, we conduct evaluations on extended image sequences to rigorously assess the models’ capacity for long-term spatial memorization. As illustrated in the long-sequence evaluations in Fig. 6, our BAT3R method consistently outperforms existing recurrent baselines. On the 7-Scenes benchmark, BAT3R achieves a substantial improvement, reducing the Chamfer Distance (from 0.134 to 0.024) by approximately 82.1% compared to the CUT3R baseline at a sequence length of 400 frames. 4.5 Ablation Studies Effect of Each Component. To evaluate the individual components of BAT3R, we conduct com-prehensive ablation studies on camera pose and depth estimation (Table 1). Removing the Bayesian Recursive Update (BRU) causes the most severe degradation (ScanNet ATE surging from 0.058 to 0.099), validating its necessity in optimally balancing historical confidence against observation noise. Omitting the State Innovation Gating (SIG) increases Abs Rel from 0.061 to 0.065 on Bonn, confirming its critical role as a distributional regularizer to bound representational drift. Furthermore, excluding cross-attention compromises spatial awareness, dropping the KITTI δ < 1.25 metric from 89.2 to 88.1. Finally, the absence of adaptive process noise ( Qt) degrades performance (ATE increasing to 0.077), verifying that motion-aware uncertainty dynamically prevents the Kalman filter from becoming overly restrictive.

## 5 Conclusion 

In this work, we present BAT3R, a training-free Bayesian adaptation framework that mitigates catastrophic forgetting and representational drift in streaming 3D reconstruction. BAT3R comprises two synergistic modules: State Innovation Gating (SIG), which acts as a distributional regularizer to anchor state evolution to its geometric initialization, and Bayesian-guided Adaptive State Updating (BASU), which reformulates state integration as a recursive Bayesian inference process within a Kalman filtering framework. By dynamically balancing the reliability of incoming observations against the accumulated confidence of historical memory, BAT3R achieves an asymmetric spatio-temporal state fusion that protects well-consolidated regions from redundant updates while preserving plasticity for genuinely novel content. Extensive experiments across seven standard benchmarks demonstrate consistent improvements over state-of-the-art online methods, with particularly strong gains on long sequences. BAT3R achieves these results without any training or fine-tuning, making it immediately applicable to any pretrained streaming reconstruction model.

A.1 Offline Reconstruction Foundation Models. 

Recent advances in 3D vision have been driven by feed-forward foundation models that streamline the traditional reconstruction pipeline. DUSt3R [45] pioneers this shift with an end-to-end framework that directly regresses pixel-aligned pointmaps [5, 6, 35] from image pairs, bypassing explicit feature matching [1, 9] and camera calibration. MASt3R [27] extends this with cross-view correspondences for 3D matching, though it remains limited to pairwise inputs and requires heavy global optimization for multi-view integration. Fast3R [51] leverages global self-attention to jointly reason across all input frames in a single forward pass, achieving strong accuracy in camera pose and 3D reconstruction without iterative alignment. VGGT [42] introduces a feed-forward framework that reconstructs 3D attributes from an arbitrary number of views in one pass. π3 [46] employs a permutation-equivariant architecture to predict camera poses and point maps in a reference-free manner, ensuring robustness to input ordering. Despite inference acceleration through token merging or sparse attention [34], the 

O(N 2) complexity of full attention remains a fundamental bottleneck: it routinely causes out-of-memory errors on consumer GPUs, limiting the maximum sequence length that can be processed. 

## B Additional Results 

B.1 Experimental Settings Evaluation Settings. To thoroughly evaluate our model, we establish two primary evaluation setups: (1) Long-sequence evaluation (results in the main paper), which challenges the recurrent state’s ability to maintain long-term memory and geometric consistency over hundreds of frames against other online methods. (2) Short-sequence evaluation (detailed in this supplement), which allows for benchmarking against offline or memory-intensive baselines that would otherwise fail due to out-of-memory (OOM) errors on longer videos. 

Implementation Details. In all experiments, inference is conducted on a single NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU (24 GB). BAT3R is built upon the pretrained CUT3R checkpoint and operates strictly without any fine-tuning or additional training. The underlying architecture consists of a ViT-Large encoder (24 layers, embedding dimension of 1024, 16 attention heads) coupled with a 12-layer recurrent decoder (embedding dimension of 768, 12 attention heads). At the beginning of each sequence, the Bayesian state variance is initialized to 1e3 per patch, reflecting a state of high initial uncertainty. Consistent with prior training-free adaptation frameworks [10, 62], we do not perform any test-time gradient updates; instead, continuous state adaptation is achieved entirely through our proposed Bayesian state update rules. 

B.2 Baselines 

We first compare BAT3R against pairwise 3D reconstruction foundation models (e.g., DUSt3R [45] , MASt3R [27], MonST3R [58], and Easi3R [9]), which take view pairs as input and require an extra global alignment stage to integrate their predictions. We also evaluate against AETHER [63] and VGGT [42], which simultaneously predict all pointmaps to achieve state-of-the-art 3D point and camera pose reconstruction. However, constrained by their reliance on global alignment or full-attention mechanisms, these methods are restricted to offline processing of short sequences, necessitating the re-inference of the entire sequence whenever a new frame arrives. For online methods, we compare BAT3R with Spann3R [41] and CUT3R [44], which leverage RNN-based architectures to process streaming images on the fly. Additionally, we compare against the most relevant concurrent works, Point3R [48] and StreamVGGT [64]. To handle long sequences, these approaches fine-tune CUT3R and VGGT by introducing explicit pointmap memory and KV caching as their respective state representations. In contrast to these methods, TTT3R reformulate CUT3R from a Test-Time Training (TTT) perspective. Build on TTT3R, BAT3R achieves robust online perception by integrating State Innovation Gating (SIG) to constrain representational drift and Bayesian-guided Adaptive State Updating (BASU) for optimal, uncertainty-aware fusion , entirely eliminating the need to fine-tune CUT3R or train additional parameterized components. 16 Table 2: Evaluation on Camera Pose Estimation- Short Sequence on Sintel [7], TUM-dynamic [36], and ScanNet [15] datasets. TTT3R achieves the best overall performance among online methods, while its accuracy has not yet matched strong offline methods (e.g., VGGT), where full attention, despite being slower and more memory-demanding, preserves the entire history context.
