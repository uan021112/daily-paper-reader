## ABSTRACT 

Feed-forward reconstruction recovers camera poses and dense geometry from im-age sets whose views are assumed to come from a single scene. In practice, un-curated image collections often contain views from disjoint scenes, and recon-structing these views jointly can corrupt camera poses and geometry. Existing pipelines typically resolve view associations in separate stages before final re-construction, leaving view graph initialization and reconstruction inference only loosely coupled. We introduce Graph3R , which unifies view graph initialization with feed-forward reconstruction through a shared geometric representation .A lightweight verification head predicts pairwise covisibility from the multi-view features of π3 to construct a view graph. This graph classifies the input views into scene groups. For each group, we mask the shared features before decod-ing camera poses and dense geometry. Reusing the multi-view features allows 

Graph3R to reconstruct disjoint scenes separately with a single backbone pass. Across three benchmarks, Graph3R improves average maximum recall at 100% precision by upto 8.99 percentage points over the baseline, while improving re-construction quality on mixed-scene inputs. Regarding the multi-session mapping task, Graph3R improves cross-session localization and achieves competitive pose estimation accuracy, while delivering a runtime speedup of approximately 35 .9×

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

Feed-forward 3D reconstruction methods such as MASt3R (Leroy et al., 2024), VGGT (Wang et al., 2025a), and π3 (Wang et al., 2025b) enable efficient recovery of camera poses and dense geometry from unordered images. These methods typically assume that the input views form a geometri-cally connected scene. Uncurated image collections, including Internet photos and images gathered across robot sessions, can violate this assumption by mixing views from disjoint scenes. Processing these views jointly can corrupt both camera poses and scene geometry (Han et al., 2025), as illus-trated in Fig. 1. Reliable reconstruction therefore depends on identifying which views should be reconstructed together. Classical structure-from-motion (SfM) (Sch¨ onberger et al., 2016; Sch¨ onberger & Frahm, 2016) and simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM) (Campos et al., 2021; Mur-Artal et al., 2015) sys-tems establish view associations through image retrieval and geometric verification. These associ-ations define a view graph , whose nodes represent images and whose edges indicate covisibility. Visual place recognition (VPR) methods (Berton & Masone, 2025) propose candidate pairs, and geometric verification checks the spatial consistency of local feature correspondences using robust estimation such as RANSAC. However, correspondence consistency alone does not guarantee co-visibility. GV-Bench (Yu et al., 2024) and Doppelgangers (Cai et al., 2023) document verification failures under long-term appearance changes and perceptual aliasing, including false associations between visually similar but distinct places. Fig. 2 contrasts how representative pipelines connect view association to reconstruction. GLUEMAP (Pan et al., 2026) builds a view graph through image retrieval and pairwise verifica-tion before applying a feed-forward model to local view neighborhoods. RobustVGGT (Han et al., 2025) identifies outlier views from the reconstruction model’s internal representations, then reruns the backbone on the retained inputs. These pipelines require separate feature computation for verifi-cation or a second backbone pass for reconstruction. Doppelgangers++ (Xiangli et al., 2025) already shares MASt3R features for graph construction and pruning within an SfM pipeline, demonstrating the value of feature reuse. This motivates a further question: Can view graph initialization and feed-forward reconstruction be unified through one shared backbone pass? 

We introduce Graph3R , a π3-based model that unifies learned view graph initialization and feed-forward reconstruction through a shared geometric representation. A lightweight verification head predicts pairwise covisibility from intermediate multi-view features. The resulting view graph par-titions the input into scene groups, whose masks condition pose and geometry decoding. The back-2Preprint bone features are computed once and reused to reconstruct each group, allowing disjoint scenes to be recovered separately without a second backbone pass. Our contributions are threefold: (a) We unify learned view graph initialization and feed-forward reconstruction through shared backbone features, avoiding repeated feature extraction between the two tasks. (b) We develop graph-conditioned decoding that masks latent embeddings by inferred scene group to recover camera poses and dense geometry separately for disjoint scenes. (c) We evaluate verification accuracy, reconstruction quality, and inference efficiency on uncurated image collections, and demonstrate Graph3R in a real-world multi-session mapping system. 

## 2 RELATED WORK 

2.1 FEED -FORWARD RECONSTRUCTION AND STRUCTURE -FROM -M OTION 

Feed-forward reconstruction models recover scene geometry through learned multi-view representa-tions. MASt3R (Leroy et al., 2024) combines pairwise pointmap prediction with learned descriptors for dense matching, while VGGT (Wang et al., 2025a) jointly predicts camera parameters and dense geometry from multiple views. π3 (Wang et al., 2025b) introduces a permutation-equivariant archi-tecture that removes the need for a designated reference view. These advances improve geometric inference, but reconstruction remains sensitive to input connectivity: unrelated views can corrupt both camera poses and scene geometry (Han et al., 2025). GLUEMAP (Pan et al., 2026) combines feed-forward local reconstruction with global SfM opti-mization. It retrieves and verifies image pairs to construct a sparse view graph, applies π3 to local graph neighborhoods, and aligns and refines the resulting reconstructions globally. The graph re-stricts local inference to selected views, but its initialization requires verification features computed separately from the reconstruction backbone. Graph3R integrates graph prediction into the recon-struction model and reuses the same backbone features for decoding each inferred scene group. 2.2 VIEW GRAPH INITIALIZATION 

View graph initialization depends on distinguishing covisible views from visually similar distractors. VPR methods (Keetha et al., 2023; Ye et al., 2023; Berton & Masone, 2025) retrieve candidate views using global descriptors, while local matchers (Edstedt et al., 2025) provide correspondences for geometric verification. GV-Bench (Yu et al., 2024) evaluates the reliability of this verification step under long-term appearance changes and perceptual aliasing, exposing limitations of decisions based on geometric inliers. Learned verification methods address visual ambiguity explicitly. Doppelgangers (Cai et al., 2023) classifies image pairs using appearance and correspondence cues, and Doppelgangers++ (Xiangli et al., 2025) builds a classifier on MASt3R’s geometric features. Its integration with MASt3R-SfM shares features for graph construction and pruning, with the verified graph guiding subsequent SfM reconstruction. OpenNavMap (Jiao et al., 2026) leverages the learned confidence map of MASt3R to reject outlier matches while it only focuses on pixel-level verification and does not provide a global view association. RobustVGGT (Han et al., 2025) provides a training-free alternative: it selects relevant views using internal attention scores or feature similarities, then reruns the backbone on the retained inputs. 

Graph3R predicts pairwise covisibility with a learned verification head operating on shared multi-view features. The predicted graph conditions group-wise pose and geometry decoding from these retained features, coupling view association and reconstruction within one backbone pass. Table 8 in Appendix A.2 compares the association features, decision rules, and reconstruction stages of representative methods. 2.3 MULTI -SESSION MAPPING 

Multi-session mapping relies on reliable associations across sessions to align and merge maps. A typical pipeline retrieves candidate views from existing sessions and filters outliers using a learned verifier such as Doppelgangers++ (Xiangli et al., 2025) or geometric verification (Yu et al., 2024), before estimating relative poses. MASt3R-SLAM (Murai et al., 2025) and VGGT-SLAM (Maggio 3Preprint et al., 2026) combine feed-forward geometry with global map optimization for single-session map-ping, while FLOCC-SLAM (Lajoie et al., 2025) and OpenNavMap (Jiao et al., 2026) apply feed-forward model for relative pose estimation across sessions. Specifically, FLOCC-SLAM uses these models for inter-robot loop closing. OpenNavMap (Jiao et al., 2026) utilizes the calibrated con-fidence map to reject outlier matches. MR.ScaleMaster (Ju & Kim, 2026) addresses inter-session scale ambiguity through a Sim(3) pose-graph backend. Graph3R complements these systems by integrating view association into local reconstruction. Given images from disjoint scenes, it predicts a view graph and recovers camera poses and geometry separately for each inferred scene group. The resulting reconstructions support downstream alignment and map refinement. 

## 3 METHOD 

3.1 PROBLEM FORMULATION AND SYSTEM OVERVIEW        

> Figure 3: System overview of Graph3R .(a) The π3baseline (Wang et al., 2025b). (b) Graph3R introduces a verification head that predicts pairwise covisibility before geometry decoding. These predictions are used to construct a view graph, which guides the graph-conditioned reconstruction as described in Sec. 3.3, enabling each scene to be reconstructed individually.

Problem Formulation. Given an image collection I = {Ii}Ni=1 , our goal is to identify groups of views that should be reconstructed together and recover the corresponding camera poses and dense geometry. The collection may contain views from several disjoint scenes, and the number of scenes is unknown. We follow the classical SfM pipeline (Pan et al., 2024) and formulate the problem as a two-stage process: (a) view graph initialization , which predicts pairwise covisibility between images and constructs a view graph G = ( V, E), where each vertex corresponds to an image and an edge indicates predicted geometric overlap, and (b) graph-conditioned reconstruction , which selects shared latent features by scene group and reconstructs each group separately. 

System Overview. As illustrated in Fig. 3, Graph3R builds on π3 (Wang et al., 2025b) and unifies the view verification and reconstruction with the geometric representation of the shared feature aggreation backbone. We denote the shared feature aggregation backbone by Bθ , which takes the input image collection I and produces shared features Z = Bθ (I) ∈ RN ×P ×D , where N is the number of views, P is the number of tokens per view, and D is the concatenated feature dimension. A verification head (described in Sec. 3.2) predicts pairwise covisibility from Z and constructs a view graph G, whose connected components define the scene groups. For each group, we gather the corresponding view features from Z and pass them together to the geometry decoder, achieving graph-conditioned reconstruction (Sec. 3.3). 3.2 LEARNED VIEW GRAPH PREDICTION 

Pairwise covisibility prediction. We adpot a similar design of the π3’s camera decoder for the verification head, with an additional prediction head to produce covisibility scores as illustrated in Fig. 4. In specific, the verification head takes the shared feature representation Z as input and processes it with a stack of transformer decoder blocks, followed by an MLP and pooling layer to generate a per-view latent descriptor vi ∈ RC . The pairwise prediction head hϕ maps each descriptor pair to a scalar logit, which is converted to a covisibility probability by a sigmoid:

## 4 EXPERIMENTS 

We evaluate Graph3R through four complementary experiments: (a) controlled feature reuse to as-sess the effect of shared features on graph-conditioned reconstruction; (b) view graph initialization to measure association accuracy; (c) mixed-scene reconstruction to evaluate end-to-end geometric prediction; and (d) real-world multi-session mapping to assess cross-session localization and com-putational efficiency. 

Datasets. We use GSV-Cities (Ali-bey et al., 2022) and 360Loc (Huang et al., 2024) to train the verification head and evaluate view graph initialization. ETH3D (Schops et al., 2017) provides high-fidelity ground-truth depth for controlled feature-reuse evaluation and supports multi-view re-construction benchmarks under Easy and Hard overlap splits. OpenNavMap (Jiao et al., 2026) provides crowdsourced visual mapping data from two geographically distinct regions, with up to 55 sessions per region, for evaluating pose recovery and cross-session localization. The selection of these datasets and split details are provided in Appendix A.1. 

Baselines. We use π3 (Wang et al., 2025b) and VGGT (Wang et al., 2025a) as standard feed-forward reconstruction baselines. Neither explicitly verifies view associations, limiting their applica-bility to uncurated image collections. We also compare with GLUEMAP (Pan et al., 2026), a closely related system that constructs a view graph using Doppelgangers++ (Xiangli et al., 2025) and uses it to guide π3 reconstruction. We denote its view graph initialization component by GLUEMAP †.For the reconstruction and pose estimation benchmarks, we omit GLUEMAP’s post-optimization module for fair comparison with the direct feed-forward outputs. 

Implementation details. We construct view graphs by retaining edges with Sij > τ s and par-titioning the resulting graph into connected components, with τs = 0 .5 across all benchmarks. Following the ablation study in Sec. 4.2, we use full-layer LoRA, a GSV-Cities and 360Loc training mixture, and a contrastive loss weight of λ = 0 .25 . The training is conducted on 16 NVIDIA H200 GPUs, and evaluations are conducted on a single NVIDIA A800 GPU. 4.1 CONTROLLED FEATURE REUSE 

Graph3R aims to unify view graph initialization and feed-forward reconstruction through a shared feature representation. However, as shown in Fig. 3, views from unrelated scenes are processed together by Bθ , whose global attention aggregates across all input views. This aggregation may introduce cross-scene noise into the Z passed to the decoders, even when each scene is decoded 6Preprint separately with masks. In principle, jointly training the backbone and geometry decoder with the verification head could help the model learn representations that are robust to such interference. This would require substantial training data and computational resources, which are beyond the scope of the question that Graph3R aims to answer. We therefore conduct a controlled experiment to assess how shared features affect graph-conditioned reconstruction. We compare Individual and Graph3R on ETH3D (Schops et al., 2017), using identical π3 weights and ground-truth scene groups to isolate the effect of feature sharing. Individual feeds each scene to the backbone and decoder independently, so no information is mixed across scenes in Bθ . In con-trast, Graph3R processes the entire mixed-scene collection in a single backbone pass, then decodes each scene separately. We vary the number of scenes K from 2 to 4, more details are provided in Appendix A.3. As shown in Table 1, the degradation remains modest as K increases. This suggests that shared features retain sufficient geometric information for separate scene reconstruction despite cross scene interactions in the Bθ . Feature reuse also avoids a separate backbone pass for each scene, contributing to the efficiency of Graph3R as reported in Table 7, which can achieve nearly 30% speedup.

## 5 CONCLUSION 

We presented Graph3R , which unifies view graph initialization and feed-forward reconstruction through shared geometric features to reconstruct image collections containing disjoint scenes. A learned verification head predicts pairwise covisibility, and the resulting graph guides separate pose and geometry decoding for each scene group using features from a single backbone pass. Controlled experiments show that feature reuse incurs only a modest loss in depth accuracy, while verification and reconstruction benchmarks demonstrate the benefits of learned grouping for uncurated inputs. On OpenNavMap, Graph3R improves cross-session localization and achieves competitive pose esti-mation accuracy with GLUEMAP, with approximately 35 .9× runtime speedup when decoding only the query group. It achieves the lowest latency among the compared methods under the evaluated configurations, supporting its use as a front end for multi-session mapping. Grouping errors can still affect reconstruction by merging unrelated views or separating connected ones. Future work will explore grouping strategies that account for prediction uncertainty and more diverse training data to improve generalization across scenes and capture conditions. With sufficient computational resources, we also plan to jointly train the backbone and geometry decoder alongside the verification head on mixed-scene image collections, aiming to preserve reconstruction accuracy despite feature mixing across unrelated scenes. 9Preprint

## A SUPPLEMENTARY MATERIAL 

A.1 DATASETS 

We distinguish the datasets used to train the multi-view verifier from those used for geometric eval-uation and the supplementary pairwise study. The descriptions below specify their roles and the evaluation subsets reported in the paper; task-specific metrics are defined in the corresponding ex-perimental sections. 

GSV-Cities. GSV-Cities (Ali-bey et al., 2022) provides place-labeled imagery for supervised vi-sual place recognition. We use it for multi-view verification training and validation. Table 2 reports the Full validation split, while Table 9 additionally reports Easy, Medium, and Hard. The selected training configuration mixes GSV-Cities and 360Loc at a 4:1 ratio; the data-composition ablation compares this mixture with GSV-Cities alone. 

360Loc. 360Loc (Huang et al., 2024) is an omnidirectional visual-localization dataset with cross-device queries. It supplies the second source of multi-view verification training data and a separate verification evaluation reported alongside GSV-Cities. The pose experiment uses the atrium subset, with results in Table 13. The verification and atrium pose evaluations are distinct tasks and should not be interpreted as using an identical sample set. 

ETH3D. ETH3D (Schops et al., 2017) is used to evaluate controlled feature reuse, view-graph prediction, depth consistency, and pose recovery. For the controlled depth and graph experiments, each collection contains K ∈ { 2, 3, 4} scenes with four views per scene, giving 4K input images. Each setting uses 20 fixed episodes. Easy (clique) requires pairwise within-scene overlap; Hard (connected) requires a connected within-scene overlap graph without requiring all pairs to overlap. Inputs are evaluated at 224 × 224 resolution. Appendix A.3 describes the oracle-group GT-depth comparison, and Appendix A.5 describes predicted-group cross-view consistency. 

OpenNavMap. OpenNavMap (Jiao et al., 2026) supplies data from Region A and Region B (Aria) for pose estimation evaluation, cross-session localization, and runtime measurement. For pose es-timation and cross-session localization, positive matches require a ground-truth camera-center dis-tance of at most 25 m and relative orientation of at most 40 ◦. Cross-session localization reranks the same eight CosPlace candidates for every method. Region-specific results and their equal-weight macro-average are reported in Table 14. The runtime protocol uses nine-image batches, as detailed in Appendix A.8. 

VisymScenes and Doppelgangers. These datasets are used for the supplementary two-image ver-ification study, not the GSV-Cities/360Loc multi-view training mixture. Doppelgangers++ and our pairwise variants are trained on a combined 210K pairs from VisymScenes (Xiangli et al., 2025) and Doppelgangers (Cai et al., 2023). VisymScenes provides the in-domain evaluation in Table 17. 

GV-Bench. GV-Bench (Yu et al., 2024) is used for zero-shot evaluation of the pairwise models trained above. Table 18 reports Day, Night, Weather, Season, Nordland, and Uacampus conditions, together with their unweighted mean. This experiment tests transfer beyond the pairwise training data; it is separate from the main multi-view verification benchmark. A.2 COMPARISON OF DIFFERENT SFM P ARADIGMS
