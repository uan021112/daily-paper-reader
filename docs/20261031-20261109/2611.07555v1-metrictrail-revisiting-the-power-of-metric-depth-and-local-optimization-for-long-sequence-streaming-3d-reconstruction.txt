Abstract
We present MetricTrail, a streaming system for accurate camera motion estimation and dense
reconstruction from long monocular videos. To keep inference efficient over long sequences, we revisit
DPVO’s sparse local estimator. We find that single-image metric depth substantially improves its trajectory
accuracy, without retraining the correspondence network or enlarging the optimization window. Metric
predictions initialize patch depths and remain as soft constraints during optimization. We further
improve correspondence estimation through learned adapters that augment matching features and
recurrent context with frozen DINOv3 representations. The correspondence estimator and adapters are
trained on diverse short clips without additional long-sequence training. This modular design also allows
the depth provider to be independently upgraded and supports supplied camera intrinsics and calibrated
stereo observations without retraining. Under the reported RGB-only protocols, MetricTrail achieves
the lowest average trajectory error among evaluated methods on KITTI, Oxford Spires, and VBR. Pairing
the estimated poses with single-image depth predictions also produces strong dense reconstruction
on indoor and outdoor benchmarks and coherent reconstructions of in-the-wild videos. With supplied
intrinsics, the system processes KITTI-02 at 12.70 FPS with 3.21 GB peak GPU memory.

1. Introduction
Streaming 3D reconstruction turns thousands of monocular observations into a coherent geometric model of
the world. For robotic navigation and large-scale mapping, this model must remain consistent as the camera
moves through new environments and revisits familiar places. Over long trajectories, camera-motion and scale
errors can accumulate, misaligning observations of the same scene. Maintaining consistency with manageable
computation and memory is a central challenge for long-video reconstruction.
Recent systems seek long-sequence consistency through learned temporal context and explicit geometric
refinement. CUT3R [1] maintains a persistent recurrent state, but accumulated drift remains a challenge on
very long sequences. LingBot-Map [2] structures attention memory to retain geometric information across time;
for extremely long inputs, its windowed mode introduces alignment errors that can accumulate across window
boundaries. Optimization-based camera estimation provides a complementary way to enforce multi-view
consistency. ViPE [3] combines learned correspondences and metric-depth regularization with local tracking
and global bundle adjustment (BA). However, its original pipeline performs global BA over all retained frames,
including a final refinement after processing the full video, increasing memory and computation as the sequence
grows.
These costs motivate strengthening local estimation with more accurate depth and metric scale cues, which
modern single-image depth models can now provide. We therefore revisit DPVO [4], which alternates sparse
patch matching with local BA within a bounded active window. Although efficient, the original estimator falls
substantially behind recent reconstruction systems on our long-sequence benchmarks. This raises a question:
How can we improve long-sequence reconstruction without enlarging the optimization window?
We introduce MetricTrail, a streaming reconstruction system that strengthens DPVO with single-image
metric depth and further improves correspondence estimation with visual foundation representations. In the
original estimator, new patch depths are initialized heuristically and subsequently refined through reprojection
constraints alone. Building on metric-depth regularization in learned odometry [3, 5], we initialize patch
depths with MoGe-2 [6] predictions and retain them as robust soft constraints during optimization. These
predictions provide both geometry specific to each image location and a learned reference for physical scale,
rather than simply propagating the estimator’s current scale as the camera advances. We find that these
single-image metric priors alone substantially improve the long-sequence accuracy of the pretrained sparse
estimator, without retraining its correspondence network or enlarging its optimization window.
Metric depth helps predict where a patch should appear, but repeated structures, weak texture, and
appearance changes can still make its correspondence ambiguous. We therefore augment native matching
features and recurrent context with frozen DINOv3 representations [7] through learned adapters. These
representations provide complementary appearance and contextual cues for predicting correspondence targets
and confidence weights. The correspondence estimator and adapters are trained on diverse public datasets
using short clips, preserving the sparse patch graph and local solver without additional long-sequence training.
Metric predictions and constraints are not used during training and enter only at inference.
MetricTrail achieves the lowest average full-sequence absolute trajectory error (ATE) among the eval-
uated methods on KITTI, Oxford Spires, and VBR under the reported RGB-only protocols, while retaining
efficient local inference. Its dense reconstructions also achieve the lowest Chamfer distance on 7Scenes and
Oxford Spires using single-image predictions without dense depth optimization. On challenging in-the-wild
video (Fig. 1), it preserves coherent layout and fine local detail across large indoor–outdoor depth changes.
Beyond monocular RGB, it supports supplied intrinsics and calibrated stereo without retraining, and allows
independent upgrades to its depth provider.

2. Related Work
Optimization-based 3D Reconstruction. Optimization-based reconstruction optimizes explicit camera and
scene variables for multi-view consistency. Classical systems solve offline SfM [8, 9] or maintain online es-
timates [10, 11]. Local windows bound update costs; global refinement and loop closure address errors
accumulated beyond them. Learning augments these solvers with geometric priors and feature-metric residu-
als [12, 13]. DROID-SLAM [14] and DPVO learn recurrent correspondence updates within differentiable BA;
other approaches [15, 16, 17, 18] incorporate pretrained features, learned matches, or neural scene represen-
tations. DINO-VO [19] combines pretrained features and adaptive patch selection with sparse BA, initializing
patch depths with Depth Anything V2 predictions rescaled to the odometry scale. Such initialization differs
from retaining a metric reference during subsequent optimization. RoMeO [5] and ViPE retain metric-depth
constraints in local BA; DROID-W [20] also regularizes depth and uses DINOv2 [21] features to estimate
dynamic uncertainty.
Feed-Forward 3D Reconstruction. Feed-forward models [22, 23, 24, 25] learn to predict geometry directly.
Subsequent work advances unified and metric prediction [26, 27, 28, 29, 30] and model architecture [31, 32,
33, 34]. MoGe [35] and MoGe-2 provide single-image priors; MoGe-2 recovers metric depth while preserving
fine relative geometry. Long videos require consistent geometry without retaining the full observation history.
Persistent or adapted neural states [1, 36, 37, 38, 39] and structured temporal aggregation [40, 41, 42, 43, 44,
45] preserve context across frames or chunks while managing memory. LingBot-Map combines anchor context
for coordinate grounding, local pose references, and compact trajectory tokens. HorizonStream [46] couples
local geometric attention to bounded recurrence and learned pose/scale readouts, with optional loop-closure
optimization. Explicit geometric refinement complements retained temporal context by correcting accumulated
pose and structure errors.
Hybrid 3D Reconstruction. Hybrid systems refine learned predictions through geometric backends. VG-
GSfM [47] and MASt3R-SfM [48] optimize learned tracks or matches globally, while MASt3R-SLAM [49] builds
an online estimator from point-map priors. VGGT-SLAM [50] and VGGT-Long [51] align local submaps or
overlapping reconstructions; VGGT-SLAM 2.0 [52] additionally corrects keyframes within and across submaps.
Marginalized Bundle Adjustment [53] refines cameras and predicted depth maps through per-frame scale
and shift adjustments, while BLASt3R [54] combines learned matching and monocular depth regularization
with BA and periodic global refinement. We focus on long-sequence accuracy within a fixed local window:
persistent metric-depth constraints help stabilize scale across updates, while richer visual representations
improve correspondences for joint pose and structure refinement.

Training the Local Estimator.
The two priors play different roles during training and inference. MoGe-2 enters only through inference-time
initialization and BA factors, so the correspondence network never sees metric predictions during training.
DINOv3, by contrast, changes the matcher inputs during adaptation. Its encoder remains frozen; starting from
the official DPVO checkpoint, we optimize the DPVO modules and residual adapters with the original pose and
induced patch-motion supervision.
The network learns local correspondence corrections, while repeated geometric optimization composes these
measurements over time. This division allows us to train on diverse short clips without requiring long-trajectory
supervision. Each sample contains 15 frames drawn from TartanAir [55], TartanGround [56], CarlaOcc [57],
LightwheelOcc [58], or OmniWorld-Game [59]. No evaluation sequence or dedicated long-trajectory pose
corpus is used.
3.4. Complete Long-Video System
Compared with learned streaming reconstructors such as HorizonStream and LingBot-Map, our optimization-
based estimator can directly incorporate additional observations and geometric constraints at inference time,
without retraining.
Camera calibration. Camera intrinsics K are supplied or estimated once from an RGB prefix. Following
ViPE [3], MoGe-2 initializes a shared camera model, and a DROID-style backend [14] jointly refines its focal
correction, auxiliary poses, and depths under metric-depth constraints. We then fix K, discard the auxiliary
state, and replay the prefix through the local estimator before continuing online. Revisit-based focal refinement
remains an optional retrospective step.
Stereo input. For synchronized, rectified stereo, each left-image patch is additionally matched to the right
image [14]. The known rig transform provides a same-time reprojection constraint on patch geometry, without
introducing right-camera pose variables or requiring stereo-specific training.
Loop closure. Following VGGT-Long [51], Scal3R [39], and HorizonStream [46], we use similar loop closure
based on retrieval and pose-graph optimization. The graph connects camera-pose nodes with sequential
odometry edges. SALAD [60] retrieves distant near-duplicate frames; each pair adds a weak identity relative-
pose edge, softly encouraging the poses to coincide. Optimizing poses against both edge types corrects drift
while retaining metric scale.
Dense reconstruction. We back-project per-frame metric depth maps into the world frame using T−1
𝑖. BA
refines camera poses and sparse patch depths, while the dense predictions remain fixed. We can therefore
reassemble the dense reconstruction using updated poses, including those from optional pose-graph correction,
without rerunning local estimation.

Runtime analysis. On KITTI-02 with an NVIDIA A100 GPU, MetricTrail achieves a steady-state throughput

increases start-up latency to approximately 175 s and peak memory to 6.81 GB, while subsequent throughput
remains 12.70 FPS.
5. Conclusion
We presented MetricTrail, a streaming reconstruction system centered on the finding that single-image
metric depth substantially improves sparse local odometry over long sequences. These gains do not require
retraining the correspondence network or enlarging the optimization window, while pretrained visual repre-
sentations further improve correspondence estimation. The resulting system supports accurate trajectories
and strong dense reconstruction across benchmark and in-the-wild videos without additional long-sequence
training. Its modular design accommodates improved depth models, supplied camera intrinsics, and calibrated
stereo observations. Together, these results demonstrate that strong image-level priors, coupled with sparse
local optimization, offer an accurate, efficient, and flexible approach to long-video reconstruction.

A.1. Additional Reconstruction Results
Reconstruction across scale changes.
Fig. 6 shows a handheld sequence that traverses indoor and outdoor spaces before returning indoors. The
resulting changes in depth range and scene extent test whether local estimates can remain in a common scale
and coordinate frame. Both offline/chunk-based [39, 51, 72, 73] and streaming baselines [2, 38, 46] exhibit
fragmentation, duplicated structure, or scale-dependent misalignment. MetricTrail instead maintains a
coherent scene and camera trajectory throughout the sequence, demonstrating stable geometric composition
beyond a fixed network context.
In-the-wild comparison with ViPE.
ViPE may exhaust GPU memory on long sequences, whereas MetricTrail processes them with bounded
active memory. We therefore complement the long-sequence results with a comparison on three shorter but
challenging videos for which both systems can run end to end. To separate the contribution of the metric
prior from that of the sequential estimator, we additionally replace ViPE’s original prior with the same MoGe-2
prior used by MetricTrail while leaving the remainder of its pipeline unchanged [3, 6]. Fig. 7 considers a
strong scale transition, backward motion followed by a turn, and rapid forward motion. The two ViPE variants
retain visible scene deformation and near–far misalignment even after sharing the metric prior. MetricTrail
consistently preserves sharper structural boundaries and better aligned foreground and background geometry
with substantially less drift. This comparison shows that reliable reconstruction depends not only on the
feed-forward prior, but also on how its geometry is composed and refined over time.
Additional long-sequence reconstructions.
Fig. 8 presents further qualitative results on KITTI and VBR. The full-sequence views show road networks
reconstructed over extended trajectories, while the selected local views retain buildings, parked vehicles, and
street furniture. Reconstructions of additional sequences show this behavior across different route layouts.
Together, the global and local views illustrate how predicted metric depth and geometrically estimated camera
motion contribute to reconstruction at both scene and object scales.

Metric global bundle adjustment.
For the 7Scenes Global BA variant, we preserve the RGB-estimated shared camera model and the same metric
local estimator. Every 15 retained keyframes, the estimator proposes long-range patch factors from predicted-
flow proximity and retains factors that have left the active window. Whenever this search inserts long-range
factors, MetricTrail invokes the global BA solver once. Within this single invocation, the solver performs
two optimization iterations over camera poses and patch depths using the union of active and retained inactive
factors. Poses preceding the earliest active source remain fixed as gauge anchors. Crucially, the global update
preserves the robust metric inverse-depth prior instead of normalizing away the recovered scale. Enabling
Global BA increases the measured inference time by only 1.6 seconds per sequence on average over the 18 test
sequences of 7Scenes.
TUM RGB-D camera trajectory comparison.
We next evaluate all nine Freiburg1 sequences of TUM RGB-D [65], retaining every associated frame at stride 1
for a total of 8,694 frames. We report full-trajectory ATE RMSE in centimeters after a proper per-sequence

contrast, ViPE inherits DROID-SLAM’s dense-flow formulation and aggregates correspondence evidence over
the full image rather than a sparse set of patches. This broader spatial support makes its pose updates more
resilient when low parallax, rotation, or blur degrades part of the matching field [3, 14].

A.5. Focal Refinement from Revisit Geometry
Motivation.
The base calibration fits a shared focal with the principal point fixed at the image center. This matches
the processed Oxford Spires imagery but leaves a 17.3-pixel horizontal offset (3.4% of image width) on
VBR. Under predominantly forward motion, focal and principal-point offsets can partially compensate in the
bearing ((𝑢−𝑐𝑥)/𝑓, (𝑣−𝑐𝑦)/𝑓, 1), allowing biased depth and translation to produce small reprojection errors.
Retrospective evaluation shows a focal error of 0.1–0.5% on Oxford Spires but a 7–9% overestimate on six of
the seven VBR sequences. We therefore refine the focal using revisits with more varied viewing directions. The
refinement was motivated by our analysis of VBR, but can also benefit other sequences whose revisits provide
sufficiently diverse viewing geometry to constrain the focal length.

Optimization and precision.
We use a frozen DINOv3-S/16 encoder pretrained on LVD-1689M [7]. Its 384-dimensional features are
projected to 64 channels and split into two 32-channel branches, which augment the 128-dimensional matching
stream and the 384-dimensional recurrent context stream, respectively. The final residual projections are zero
initialized. In joint training, the learning rates are 8 × 10−6 for the odometry network and 8 × 10−5 for the
fusion branches. We use AdamW with weight decay 10−6, linear OneCycle annealing with a 1% warm-up,
gradient clipping at 10, and seed 1234. For each clip, we sample 80 3 × 3 patches per frame and unroll 18
recurrent refinements. Images are resized while preserving aspect ratio and center-cropped to 480 × 640. Only
the frozen DINO forward pass uses BF16, evaluated in three-frame chunks; the adapters, odometry network,
differentiable bundle adjustment, and losses remain in FP32. MoGe-2 is not used during adaptation and is kept
frozen when invoked at inference. End-to-end training takes approximately seven hours.

Loop closure.
Following VGGT-Long, Scal3R, and HorizonStream, we use a similar loop-closure strategy that combines revisit
retrieval with graph refinement. We use SALAD to retrieve temporally separated near-duplicate views and
suppress redundant matches. Each selected pair contributes a weak identity relative-pose factor, treating
the views as approximately coincident without pairwise geometric verification. These factors are combined
with sequential odometry constraints in an SE(3) pose graph, which refines camera poses with the first pose
fixed. This correction preserves metric scale and leaves patch depths unchanged; dense reconstructions can be
reassembled from the corrected poses.
Stereo inference.
For each newly created left-image patch, we add a same-time edge to the synchronized right image under the
fixed left-to-right rig transform. The right image supplies only a target matching map, including its DINOv3
matching augmentation; no right-image patch, recurrent context, metric-depth variable, or camera-pose
variable is created. The recurrent matcher predicts the right correspondence and confidence, and the resulting
reprojection residual is added to the local BA objective. No stereo-specific data or loss is used during training.
