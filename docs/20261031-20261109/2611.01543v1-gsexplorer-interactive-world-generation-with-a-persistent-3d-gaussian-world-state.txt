ABSTRACT
Interactive world generation requires both accurate local control and stable
scene consistency over long rollouts. Existing methods improve local genera-
tion through camera or geometry conditioning and extend historical context with
memory mechanisms. However, long-horizon exploration remains challenging as
target-view guidance becomes incomplete and inconsistencies accumulate across
repeated scene updates. We present GSExplorer, a generation–reconstruction
framework built around a persistent 3D Gaussian world state. At each step, the
current 3DGS scene provides multimodal rendering conditions for a condition-
driven video generator, while camera geometry is injected through a separate Rel-
ative Ray Encoding branch. Generated videos are then fed back to a SLAM-based
reconstruction backend, where inconsistency filtering prevents conflicting content
from corrupting the scene and cross-chunk bundle adjustment improves spatial
alignment across generation steps. We further design a data-construction pipeline
that reproduces realistic 3DGS degradation during scene extrapolation, yielding
293K training pairs of degraded multimodal renderings and real target videos.
GSExplorer achieves the best overall performance among the compared meth-
ods on both single-step and three-step WorldScore evaluation, with strong camera
control and consistency across both settings, including a Camera Control score
of 99.07 in single-step extrapolation. On 481-frame leave-and-return rollouts, it
also shows strong revisit consistency, demonstrating the value of maintaining a
reliable 3D world state for continued exploration. The project page is available at
Project Page.

INTRODUCTION
Starting from a single image, interactive world generation aims to continuously create explorable
scenes or videos following user-specified camera trajectories and text prompts (Yu et al., 2024a;
2025a; Duan et al., 2025). This requires two key capabilities: (1) precise camera control with high
visual and spatial consistency for each local generation, and (2) stable scene evolution over long roll-
outs, including revisiting previously explored regions. The former enables reliable scene expansion,
while the latter determines whether such capability can persist over continuous exploration.
Existing methods improve local controllability by injecting camera poses, rays, or relative positional
encodings into video generators (He et al., 2024; Dai et al., 2026; Zhang et al., 2026), but these sig-
nals provide limited information about the scene structure at the target view. Geometry-guided
approaches (Yu et al., 2024b; Ren et al., 2025; Cao et al., 2025; Huang et al., 2025; Zhou et al.,
2026; Yang et al., 2026b;a) address this limitation by rendering explicit 3D representations along
planned trajectories, providing spatially aligned conditions for generation. Meanwhile, memory-
based methods extend temporal context through historical frames, compressed states, or retrieved
references to improve long-term consistency (Yu et al., 2025b; Li et al., 2025b; Sun et al., 2025;
Team et al., 2026). However, long-horizon generation remains challenging: incomplete 3D ren-
derings provide imperfect guidance (Wu et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2025), while pose misalignment

and conflicting generated content can accumulate as the scene is repeatedly updated (Zhao et al.,
2026c; Wang et al., 2026). This motivates a more fundamental question: how can we maintain a
reliable and queryable 3D world state that continuously provides useful target-view conditions
throughout exploration?
To this end, we introduce GSExplorer, a generation–reconstruction loop built around a persistent
3D Gaussian world state. Our key insight is to separate long-term spatial memory from short-
horizon video generation: the persistent 3D scene carries accumulated spatial information, while
the video model repeatedly solves local generation problems conditioned on the current world state.
Generated videos are selectively integrated back into the same scene, allowing the world state to
evolve together with exploration.
Specifically, GSExplorer renders degraded RGB, depth, accumulated opacity, and hole masks from
the current 3DGS state as multimodal scene conditions, while camera geometry is injected sepa-
rately through Relative Ray Encoding to provide complementary cross-view guidance. The gener-
ated videos are then fed into a SLAM-based 3DGS reconstruction backend. Inconsistency filtering
prevents conflicting generated content from corrupting established regions, while cross-chunk bun-
dle adjustment aligns current and historical views for coherent scene updates. To train the genera-
tor on realistic incomplete scene observations, we construct 293K degraded-condition–target video
pairs from real-world scene datasets.
GSExplorer achieves the best overall performance among the compared methods on both single-step
and three-step WorldScore evaluation (Duan et al., 2025). In single-step extrapolation, it achieves
a Camera Control score of 99.07 while also attaining leading performance in 3D and Style Con-
sistency. We further evaluate long-horizon revisitation on 50 leave-and-return cases from R2M-
Bench (Gu et al., 2026) with 481-frame rollouts, where GSExplorer achieves the best results among
the compared methods across PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, and Match Ratio. These results demonstrate
strong controllability and consistency across both local generation and long-horizon exploration.
Our main contributions are summarized as follows:
1. We introduce a generation–reconstruction framework centered on a persistent 3DGS world
state, where inconsistency filtering and cross-chunk bundle adjustment enable selective and
spatially coherent scene updates across generation steps.
2. We develop a condition-driven video generator that combines multimodal 3DGS renderings
with a separate camera-geometry branch, and construct 293K degraded-condition–target
video pairs for accurate camera control and scene completion.
3. GSExplorer achieves leading overall performance on single-step and three-step WorldScore
evaluation and shows strong long-horizon revisit consistency after 481-frame rollouts.

RELATED WORK
2.1
CAMERA-CONTROLLABLE WORLD GENERATION
Camera-controllable video generation commonly injects camera poses (Wang et al., 2024), Pl¨ucker
rays (He et al., 2024; Dai et al., 2026), or relative positional encodings into DiT backbones to guide
viewpoint changes (Li et al., 2026; Zhang et al., 2026). While these signals improve camera control,
they provide limited information about the scene structure at the target view. Recent geometry-
guided approaches therefore reconstruct explicit 3D representations and render them along planned
camera trajectories as spatially aligned conditions (Ren et al., 2025; Cao et al., 2025). However,
such conditions can become unreliable under large viewpoint changes.
Recent methods improve geometry conditioning from different perspectives. UniWorld-View (Zhou
et al., 2026) addresses visibility and occlusion ambiguity in point-cloud rendering, while Neo-
Verse (Yang et al., 2026b) combines feed-forward 4D Gaussian reconstruction with online degrada-
tion simulation for geometry-conditioned generation. DreamWorld (Yang et al., 2026a) further com-
pletes geometry-aware features as an intermediate scaffold before appearance synthesis. GSExplorer
combines complementary cues from multimodal 3DGS renderings with a separate camera-geometry
encoding, enabling precise camera control and robust scene completion.

where inconsistency filtering enables selective scene updates and cross-chunk bundle adjustment
aligns current and historical views. The updated 3DGS world state then provides reliable conditions
for the next step, forming a generation–reconstruction loop.
2.2
MEMORY FOR WORLD GENERATION
Memory-based video models reuse historical observations to maintain consistency beyond the recent
temporal context. Existing methods extend long-term memory by conditioning generation on stored
historical frames (Yu et al., 2025b) or retrieving geometry-indexed reference views (Li et al., 2025b).
HY-WorldPlay (Sun et al., 2025) and DreamX-World (Team et al., 2026) similarly reconstruct or
retrieve historical context during autoregressive generation. These mechanisms extend access to
long-term visual information without processing the full history at every step.
Explicit 3D representations provide a more spatially grounded form of memory. Early methods
progressively construct and expand explicit 3D scenes (Chung et al., 2023; Yu et al., 2024a; 2025a;
2024a), while later approaches couple generation with scene reconstruction or refinement (Wu et al.,
2025; Zhang et al., 2025). Voyager (Huang et al., 2025) and Spatia (Zhao et al., 2026c) maintain
updatable point-cloud memories for subsequent generation, with Spatia additionally retrieving his-
torical frames. LSM-World (Wang et al., 2026) stores diffusion features directly in a persistent 3D
cache to avoid repeated RGB rendering and re-encoding. In contrast, GSExplorer maintains a per-
sistent 3DGS world state through cross-chunk BA and inconsistency filtering, allowing the updated
scene to provide reliable target-view conditions for subsequent short-horizon generation.

METHODOLOGY
Figure 1 gives an overview of GSExplorer. We present the persistent 3DGS world update in Sec. 3.1,
the condition-driven generation model in Sec. 3.2, and the training-pair construction in Sec. 3.3.
3.1
PERSISTENT 3DGS WORLD STATE
A persistent world state should not only retain past information, but also remain reliable and
queryable as new generated videos are incorporated. We maintain a 3DGS scene with a SLAM-
based reconstruction backend that carries camera estimates and historical mapping views across
generation steps. Instead of blindly integrating generated videos, we selectively update the world
state according to their consistency with the existing scene. This enables the scene to expand into
unseen regions while preserving previously reconstructed content during exploration and revisit.

3.2
CONDITION-DRIVEN VIDEO GENERATION
We build our generator on Wan2.2-TI2V-5B (Wan et al., 2025) and condition it on multimodal
3DGS renderings, camera geometry, and text prompts. As shown in Fig. 2, rendered observations
provide the main scene guidance, while camera geometry is injected through a separate branch
as complementary guidance. This keeps camera geometry separate from rendered content while
allowing the two condition streams to cooperate during generation.
Multimodal conditioning.
Given a planned camera trajectory, we render degraded RGB, depth,
and accumulated opacity from the current 3DGS state, and derive a binary hole mask from the
opacity. RGB provides observed appearance, depth provides explicit geometry, accumulated opacity
describes continuous rendering coverage, and the mask indicates hole regions to be completed.
RGB and normalized depth are encoded separately with the same pretrained Wan VAE, while opacity
and the hole mask are mapped to the same latent grid using a lightweight 3D convolutional encoder.
Their features are concatenated and projected to the DiT hidden space. The fused condition is
injected through a zero-initialized input projection and five independent zero-initialized residual
projections after selected DiT blocks, yielding six content-conditioning sites in total.
Unlike prior designs that inject camera cues together with rendered modalities (Yang et al.,
2026b), we encode camera geometry through a separate Relative Ray Encoding (RRE) branch from
UCPE (Zhang et al., 2026). In this branch, camera intrinsics and poses define token-wise ray ge-
ometry, which is used to transform the queries, keys, and values of a lightweight attention module,
followed by a geometry-aware output transform before projection back to the backbone features.
The resulting RRE attention output is added in parallel to the original self-attention in every DiT
block. This design allows rendered conditions to dominate scene completion, while camera geom-
etry provides complementary cross-view guidance. For geometric consistency, depth and camera
translation are normalized using the same clip-level scale, while text prompts follow the original
Wan cross-attention pathway.

3.3
TRAINING PAIR CONSTRUCTION
To train our model to complete videos from multimodal conditions and text prompts, we construct
degraded conditions paired with target videos from diverse real-world scene datasets, including
RealEstate10K (Zhou et al., 2018), DL3DV (Ling et al., 2024), ACID (Liu et al., 2021), and Scan-
Net (Dai et al., 2017). As shown in Fig. 3, the key is to reproduce realistic 3DGS degradation while
maintaining pixel alignment with the real targets.
Data preprocessing.
We segment long sequences into 57-frame clips after quality filtering and
use Qwen3.5 (Qwen Team, 2026) to annotate a scene description for each clip. The first 8 frames
serve as context and the remaining 49 as targets. More details are provided in the Appendix.
Degraded condition construction.
Reconstructing only the 8 context frames provides insufficient
geometric constraints, causing rendered conditions to become severely misaligned with real targets
at farther extrapolated views. We therefore run the same 3DGS-SLAM system in our extrapolation
pipeline over the full 57-frame sequence and record the birth frame bg of each Gaussian primitive.
Full-sequence optimization, however, may cause context Gaussians to adapt to future observations
and weaken the degradation we aim to simulate. We therefore force the first target frame (frame 9) to
be a keyframe and explicitly insert new Gaussians, providing dedicated capacity for newly revealed
content. After reconstruction, we retain only Gaussians born within the context:

EXPERIMENTS
4.1
EXPERIMENTAL SETUP
Benchmarks and metrics.
We evaluate both single-step and three-step world extrapolation on the
photorealistic subset of the WorldScore static benchmark (Duan et al., 2025). Following its proto-
col, we report seven metrics covering controllability and generation quality: Camera Control, Object
Control, Content Alignment, 3D Consistency, Photometric Consistency, Style Consistency, and Sub-
jective Quality. For long-horizon revisit evaluation, we select 50 cases from R2M-Bench (Gu et al.,
2026) and use its leave-and-return trajectories. Each method generates a 10-step, 481-frame rollout
that returns to the initial viewpoint. We compare the final frame with the input image using PSNR,
SSIM (Wang et al., 2004), LPIPS (Zhang et al., 2018), and Match Ratio. Following R2M-Bench,
Match Ratio is computed using SuperPoint (DeTone et al., 2018) and LightGlue (Lindenberger et al.,
2023), normalized by the smaller number of detected keypoints in the image pair.

Implementation details.
We initialize the generator from Wan2.2-TI2V-5B and train it for 30K
iterations on 32 NVIDIA H20 GPUs with a global batch size of 32. Training clips contain 49 frames
at 640×352 resolution, while ScanNet clips use 640×480. For the first 10K iterations, we freeze the
DiT backbone and train the multimodal adapter and pose branch with a learning rate of 10−4. We
then jointly fine-tune the DiT, multimodal adapter, and pose branch for 20K iterations with learning
rates of 1 × 10−7, 1 × 10−5, and 1 × 10−5, respectively. We apply clean-reference and text-prompt
dropout with probabilities of 0.5 and 0.1. Additional training details are provided in the Appendix.

MAIN RESULTS
World generation on WorldScore.
Tables 1 and 2 report the single-step and three-step extrapola-
tion results on WorldScore, respectively. In the single-step setting, GSExplorer achieves the highest
average score of 82.85, outperforming the strongest baseline by 6.24 points. It also reaches 99.07
Camera Control and ranks first in 3D Consistency, Style Consistency, and Object Control, demon-
strating precise local control together with scene consistency. The improvement is not limited to
one metric category, but spans camera following, geometric consistency, and generation quality,
suggesting that the multimodal scene conditions and explicit camera guidance are complementary.
The advantage remains under three-step extrapolation, where GSExplorer achieves the best average
score of 71.56, exceeding the strongest baseline by 7.48 points. It ranks first in Style Consistency and
Object Control and second in 3D Consistency, Photometric Consistency, and Camera Control. The
margin over the strongest baseline increases from 6.24 points in the single-step setting to 7.48 points
after three steps, indicating that the advantage is maintained as errors accumulate over successive
generation chunks. Together with the qualitative results in Fig. 4, these results show that GSExplorer
preserves strong controllability and generation quality throughout multi-step scene extrapolation.

ABLATION STUDY
Conditioning design.
As shown in Table 4, each conditioning modality contributes to the final
performance. Removing depth mainly degrades geometric consistency, reducing 3D Consistency
from 90.83 to 87.13, while removing opacity also lowers the overall score by 0.69 points. Pose

design provides the best combination of controllability, consistency, and generation quality.
Persistent world-state update.
Table 5 evaluates the two key components of our persistent world-

5
CONCLUSION
GSExplorer shows that interactive world generation can benefit from separating long-term spatial
state from local video synthesis. Rather than asking the generator to carry an increasingly long
visual history, we maintain an explicit 3DGS world that is repeatedly queried, selectively updated,
and geometrically aligned as exploration proceeds. This design keeps generation local while allow-
ing spatial information to accumulate in a reusable scene representation. Our results on multi-step
extrapolation and long-horizon revisit suggest that reliable world-state maintenance is a useful com-
9


plement to increasingly capable video generators. A natural next step is to make the world update
more robust to dynamic content and ambiguous observations, while improving the efficiency and
scalability of persistent scene maintenance.

A.1.1
PERSISTENT 3DGS WORLD UPDATE
3DGS-SLAM backend.
Our world state must support both viewpoint-dependent rendering and
incremental updates as new generated videos arrive. A 3DGS-SLAM system (Yu et al., 2025c;
Cheng et al., 2025b) naturally provides these capabilities by maintaining a continuously renderable
Gaussian scene (Zhao et al., 2026b;a) while estimating and refining camera states from streaming
observations (Cheng et al., 2025a; Yu et al., 2026). We therefore build our world update on an
ARTDECO-based (Li et al., 2025a) 3DGS reconstruction backend, using MASt3R (Leroy et al.,
2024) correspondences and a Depth Anything 3 (DA3) (Lin et al., 2025) geometry prior.
The same Gaussian scene, historical mapping views, camera graph, and retrieval database are re-
tained throughout a rollout. Each 49-frame generation chunk shares its first frame with the preced-
ing chunk and contributes 48 new observations. DA3 jointly processes the shared boundary frame
and the new frames to initialize depth and cameras, with depth aligned to the existing reconstruction
using a scale-only transformation. Image correspondences and retrieved historical matches then
13


connect current and previous keyframes for cross-chunk camera refinement. The refined camera
states are propagated to Gaussian reconstruction before the updated world state is rendered for the
next generation step.
Natural tracking keyframes are selected with a match-fraction threshold of 0.45 and a minimum
separation of four global frames. Up to 24 ordinary mapping frames are retained per chunk. Frames
with insufficient geometric support are discarded before entering the mapping stage.

Multimodal condition adapter.

encoder with 64 output channels. The resulting

The projected condition is injected once at the DiT input and after five selected blocks. Using zero-

{5, 10, 15, 20, 25}.
The six conditioning projections are zero-initialized, allowing the pretrained backbone to begin from
its original behavior while gradually learning the additional scene conditions.
Relative Ray Encoding.
We use the Relative Ray Encoding (RRE) component of UCPE (Zhang
et al., 2026) without its Absolute Orientation Encoding. Camera intrinsics and camera-to-world
poses define a local viewing-ray frame for each latent token. Each RRE module projects the 3072-
dimensional hidden state to three attention heads of width 128. Geometry-aware transformations
are applied to the query, key, and value features in their corresponding ray frames, followed by the
inverse geometric transform on the attention output.
The RRE branch is instantiated in every DiT block and operates in parallel with the original
self-attention. Its output is projected back to the 3072-dimensional hidden space through a zero-
initialized projection and combined with the original attention output before the remaining cross-
attention and feed-forward operations.

A.3
LIMITATIONS
GSExplorer currently introduces additional runtime compared with purely feed-forward video gen-
eration because each generation chunk is followed by camera estimation and 3DGS scene refine-
ment. The current formulation is also primarily designed for static or predominantly static envi-
ronments, where persistent geometric conditions remain meaningful across exploration. Extending
the world state to explicitly model dynamic objects would broaden its applicability to more interac-
tive scenes. Finally, repeated generation and reconstruction may soften very fine textures and small
indoor details over long rollouts. Improving high-frequency appearance preservation and recon-
struction efficiency are promising directions for future work.
