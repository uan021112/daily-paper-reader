# OctreeSplat: Hierarchical Octree-Guided Feed-Forward Gaussian Splatting 

Yibin Zhao *, Yihan Pan *, Wenli Yang, Jun Nan, and Jianjun Yi †

Fig. 1. Given a variable number of images and optional camera parameters, OctreeSplat reconstructs a geometrically accurate 3DGS scene through feed-forward inference. Predicted densification scores guide the spatial allocation of Gaussian primitives. Compared with pixel-aligned [1] and voxel-aligned [2] methods, OctreeSplat improves RGB synthesis, depth synthesis, and surface reconstruction while using fewer primitives. Optional camera-conditioned refinement is performed through test-time optimization. 

Abstract —3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has shown consider-able potential for novel view synthesis (NVS) and 3D reconstruc-tion. Feed-forward methods avoid costly per-scene optimization, but fixed image or spatial resolutions limit their ability to allocate primitives according to local complexity. We present OctreeSplat, a hierarchical octree-guided feed-forward Gaussian splatting. OctreeSplat extracts multi-view features and geometric priors from a frozen visual foundation model (VFM), constructs a hierarchical octree, and predicts densification scores. The resulting representation allocates Gaussian primitives at spatially varying densities according to local complexity. Stochastic solids volume rendering improves depth rendering, strengthens geo-metric supervision, and supports surface reconstruction. When camera parameters are available, an optional test-time opti-mization (TTO) module improves agreement between predicted and ground-truth parameters. Training updates only 76 million additional parameters and requires 35 GPU-hours. Experiments on Replica, TUM, Tanks and Temples, and DTU demonstrate competitive RGB view synthesis, depth synthesis, and surface reconstruction. OctreeSplat reduces the number of Gaussian primitives while maintaining high reconstruction quality. 

Index Terms —3D Gaussian splatting, visual foundation model, hierarchical octree, novel view synthesis.

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Image-based 3D reconstruction has traditionally relied on structure-from-motion (SfM) pipelines, with iterative tech-niques represented by bundle adjustment (BA) [3] for multi-view pose estimation and sparse reconstruction. Common scene representations include point clouds, voxels, and signed distance fields. More recently, 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) [4] has combined photorealistic novel view synthesis with high rendering efficiency. Given images and camera parameters, 3DGS iteratively renders the scene and optimizes the param-eters of Gaussian primitives. Adaptive density control (ADC) periodically adds and removes primitives during optimization, which typically takes tens of minutes to an hour. Visual foundation models (VFMs) [5]–[7] learn strong pri-ors from large-scale datasets and directly regress 3D infor-mation from image collections. Outputs include depth maps, 3D point maps, and camera parameters. These models reduce accumulated errors and iterative computation in conventional reconstruction pipelines while maintaining strong general-ization. Feed-forward 3DGS methods [1], [2], [8] similarly predict Gaussian scenes in a single feed-forward pass. How-ever, commonly used pixel-aligned representations [8], [9] can introduce overlapping primitives and floating artifacts. Voxel-aligned methods [2], [10] and methods that regress primitives from fixed token sets [11], [12] produce more compact rep-resentations. Nevertheless, their primitive allocation does not arXiv:2611.02658v1 [cs.CV] 2 Nov 2026 2

explicitly adapt to local scene complexity, limiting density flexibility and geometric accuracy. To address the above problems, we propose OctreeSplat ,a hierarchical octree-guided feed-forward Gaussian splatting. Given uncalibrated images and optional camera parameters, OctreeSplat predicts a geometrically accurate 3DGS scene with spatially adaptive primitive allocation. Depth maps and camera parameters predicted by a VFM backbone support voxel aggregation at multiple resolutions. A dense prediction transformer (DPT) upsamples the backbone’s patch tokens to construct aligned multi-resolution voxel features. A sparse-convolutional U-Net (SPUNet) processes these features and predicts Gaussian parameters at each level, together with densification scores between adjacent levels. During inference, subdivision starts from the coarsest voxels and selects a fixed fraction for refinement according to the predicted scores. Gaussian primitives associated with the selected octree nodes form the final octree-aligned scene. We initialize the backbone with pretrained VFM weights and introduce an optional test-time optimization (TTO) mod-ule. When camera parameters are supplied, TTO improves alignment between the predicted parameters and the ground-truth. Stochastic-solid volume rendering provides more accu-rate geometric supervision by representing Gaussian primitives as equivalent stochastic solids and volume rendering along viewing rays. This formulation improves depth rendering and geometric accuracy while supporting surface reconstruc-tion. The backbone remains frozen during training. Only the feature-upsampling DPT, SPUNet, densification-score heads, and Gaussian prediction heads are optimized, totaling 76 million parameters. This design allows OctreeSplat to be trained in 35 GPU-hours on RTX PRO 6000. Our main contributions are summarized as follows:  

> •

We introduce OctreeSplat, a hierarchical octree-guided method that reconstructs geometrically accurate Gaussian scenes from uncalibrated images and optional camera parameters.  

> •

Predicted densification scores guide adaptive octree sub-division and allocate Gaussian primitives according to local scene complexity.  

> •

A frozen VFM backbone limits training to 76 million additional parameters and 35 GPU-hours. Optional TTO aligns camera predictions with ground-truth without up-dating backbone weights.  

> •

Extensive experiments demonstrate competitive RGB view synthesis and geometric reconstruction across mul-tiple datasets and settings. II. R ELATED WORK 

A. 3D Scene Representations 

Conventional reconstruction methods represent scenes us-ing point clouds [13], voxels [14], signed distance fields (SDFs) [15], or triangle meshes [16]. Neural radiance fields (NeRFs) [17] instead use multilayer perceptrons (MLPs) to represent scenes as continuous implicit radiance fields, which map 3D positions and viewing directions to volume densities and colors. Differentiable image rendering optimizes radiance fields’ parameters to produce photorealistic NVS. Subsequent work has improved reconstruction speed [18], [19], rendering quality [20], [21], and surface reconstruction [22], [23]. De-spite NeRF’s reconstruction accuracy, implicit representations require repeated field inferences along each ray during volume rendering. This computational cost limits rendering efficiency and transferability. 3DGS [4] addresses this bottleneck by explicitly storing scenes as 3D Gaussian primitives. These primitives are ini-tialized from sparse point clouds, typically obtained through SfM [13], and optimized by iteratively rendering the input images. ADC splits, clones, and prunes primitives using pho-tometric gradients [4] or alternative criteria [24], [25]. The optimization process typically takes tens of minutes to an hour. Later methods accelerate optimization [26]–[28] by controlling the primitive count. However, they still require precomputed camera poses and sparse point clouds, and optimization gen-erally takes at least several minutes. ADC strategies have also received sustained attention [29], [30]. Vanilla 3DGS primarily optimizes RGB rendering and lacks explicit surface supervision. Subsequent studies improve geometric accuracy through surface-alignment regularization [31]. Depth-distortion, normal-consistency, and multi-view-consistency constraints further reduce geometric ambiguities arising from photometric supervision [32], [33]. GOF [34] constructs an opacity field from Gaussian primitives and extracts its isosurface for surface reconstruction. GGGS [35] and GW [36] further improve surface reconstruction by rep-resenting Gaussian primitives as equivalent stochastic solids. 

B. Feed-Forward Reconstruction 

Another line of research predicts Gaussian scenes through a single feed-forward based on neural-network. Feed-forward methods such as pixelSplat [37] take images and camera parameters as input. They aggregate multi-view features, es-timate per-pixel depth, back-project pixels into world coordi-nates, and regress Gaussian parameters. Multi-view cost vol-umes [9] and pretrained depth priors [38] improve reconstruc-tion quality in challenging scenes. VolSplat [10] replaces pixel alignment with voxel alignment, reducing the dependence of primitives on individual input views. VFMs extend reconstruction to uncalibrated images without requiring camera parameters as input. DUSt3R [39] formulates image-pair reconstruction as 3D point-map regression, allow-ing dense geometry prediction without camera parameters. MUSt3R [40] extends DUSt3R to multiple views and predicts geometry directly in a common coordinate system. VGGT [5] alternates single-view and multi-view self-attention to aggregate information across a variable number of views. A shared backbone and separate prediction heads jointly estimate camera parameters, depth maps, 3D point maps, and point tracks. Subsequent work improves robustness [6], long-sequence processing [41], and model scalability [7]. VFMs have also advanced feed-forward 3DGS.Some ap-proaches build directly upon VFM backbones by adding prediction heads for pixel-aligned Gaussian attributes [1], [42]. B. Visual Foundation Model Backbone 

OctreeSplat’s VFM backbone follows the DA3 architec-ture [1]. A pretrained DINOv2 encoder [44] extracts patch tokens tpatch ,i from each input image Ii. These tokens are concatenated with learnable camera tokens tcam and register tokens treg , then processed by 40 attention layers. The first 12 layers apply single-view self-attention; subsequent layers alternate single-view self-attention with global multi-view self-attention. A DPT decoder [45] upsamples the resulting patch tokens into pixel-aligned depth maps. An MLP decodes the camera tokens into camera parameters, including poses and fields of view. When camera parameters are available, lightweight low-rank adaptation (LoRA) modules support TTO to align predic-tions with ground-truth. LoRA matrices are inserted into the alternating attention layers, starting from layer 13, and applied 4

to the query, key, and value projections. Within each attention layer, these projections share a down-projection matrix A and use separate up-projection matrices BQ, BK , and BV , as shown in Equation 3.

The original projection parameters are W ∈ Rd×d and b ∈ Rd.The low-rank dimension is r = 4 by default, and the scaling factor α/r is set to 4. The LoRA modules are activated when camera parameters corresponding to the input images are supplied. A TTO loss compares the backbone’s camera predictions with ground-truth. Back-propagation updates only the LoRA parameters for a fixed number of iterations. VGGT-style VFMs typically anchor the world coordinate system to the first frame, while image-based reconstruction retains an inherent scale ambigu-ity. We therefore align predicted and reference poses before computing the TTO loss. All poses are expressed relative to the first frame, and scale is normalized using the mean distance of the cameras. D. Octree-Aligned Reconstruction 

The predicted Gaussian scenes {G (ℓ)}Lℓ=1 and densification scores d(ℓ) 

> v

∈ [0 , 1] guide octree construction and primitive allocation. Reconstruction starts from the coarsest Gaussian scene. Previous work [47] measures voxel complexity using the maximum multi-view sampling rate, defined as the largest number of pixels covered by a voxel across views. However, this sampling rate reflects projected coverage rather than local 5 

> Fig. 4. Comparison of the maximum multi-view sampling rate and predicted densification scores.

detail. Nearby regions with simple textures can therefore receive high sampling rates, which may not indicate a need for Gaussian densification. We instead predict densification scores that are higher near edges and complex textures, as shown in Figure 4. These scores guide subdivision toward regions requiring finer representations. At each of the L − 1 non-finest levels, we rank voxels by their predicted scores and subdivide a fixed fraction into eight children. The corresponding finer voxels are retrieved from the next level. Children absent from that level are skipped, preserving spatial sparsity and reducing computational cost. Repeating this procedure (2 subdivision rounds by default) constructs a sparse octree that allocates finer voxels to more complex regions. Each selected octree node is associated with a previously predicted Gaussian primitive at this level. The final scene is thus assembled from a single feed-forward prediction.

2) Training Strategy: OctreeSplat’s training pipeline is dif-ferentiable and trained end-to-end. We use pretrained DA3-GIANT weights [1] for the VFM backbone. Excluding the camera-parameter encoder and Gaussian head, the backbone contains 1.190 billion parameters. OctreeSplat adds 76 million parameters: 36 million in the feature DPT and 40 million in the sparse U-Net and decoding MLPs. The backbone remains fully frozen, and only the additional parameters are optimized, substantially reducing training time. 

a) Training datasets: We train on a mixture of Infinigen [49], ScanNet++ [50], ARKitScenes [51], and DL3DV [52]. Infinigen provides synthetic RGB-D data, ScanNet++ and ARKitScenes provide real-captured RGB-D data, and DL3DV provides real-captured RGB data. The combined dataset con-tains 6,810,309 images from 12,419 scenes, covering indoor and outdoor environments as well as synthetic and real-captured images. 

b) Implementation details: We train the additional pa-rameters for 100,000 iterations using AdamW. The learning rate starts at 3 × 10 −6 and decays to 10 −8 with cosine annealing. We use L = 3 voxel levels, with a coarsest voxel size of 8 mm. Training images have a resolution of 244 × 448 .Each iteration randomly samples 3–18 images per scene, with at most 18 images and four scenes in a batch. Training takes 35 hours on a single NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 GPU.

A. RGB View Synthesis 

We evaluate NVS on Replica [53], TUM [54], and Tanks and Temples [55]. These datasets provide synthetic indoor, real-captured indoor, and real-captured outdoor scenes, respec-tively. We conducted qualitative and quantitative experiments between OctreeSplat and several SOTA baselines, which can be categorized into two groups: the first follows pixelSplat [37] and requires camera parameters, including MVSplat [9] and VolSplat [10]; the second uses VGGT-style [5] reconstruction, including AnySplat [2], YoNoSplat [8], DA3 [1], and QueryS-plat [11]. AnySplat and VolSplat use voxel alignment, whereas QuerySplat uses implicit query tokens. MVSplat, YoNoSplat, and DA3 use pixel alignment. We measure RGB synthesis quality using PSNR, SSIM [56], and LPIPS [57]. These metrics characterize pixel-level fidelity, structural similarity, and perceptual similarity between synthesized and ground-truth views. As shown in Table I and Figure 6, OctreeSplat produces clear RGB renderings and suppresses overlap artifacts. Octree-Splat (Finest) directly uses Gaussian primitives predicted from the finest voxel level. OctreeSplat efficiently controls the primitive count, which reduces the count by approximately half or more in most settings, with a quality–compactness trade-off relative to the finest-level variant.

E. Efficiency 

The inference time of OctreeSplat is shown in Figure 9. The total inference time is 2.66 s, compared with 2.33 s for DA3 using the same backbone, an increase of 14.2%. Most additional computation comes from the multi-scale SPUNet. When TTO is enabled, OctreeSplat runs the VFM backbone in bf16 to improve efficiency, with little effect on accuracy. A backbone forward pass takes 287.9 ms, and back-propagation takes 426.0 ms. The total runtime with TTO remains below 15 s.

V. C ONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK 

We presented OctreeSplat, a hierarchical octree-guided method for feed-forward 3DGS reconstruction. Given images and optional camera parameters, OctreeSplat constructs hierar-chical voxel features from multi-view features and geometric priors provided by a frozen VFM. A sparse-convolutional U-Net and separate heads jointly predict Gaussian parameters and inter-level densification scores. These scores guide octree subdivision and allocate primitives according to local scene complexity. Training updates only 76 million parameters in the feature DPT, SPUNet, densification-score heads, and Gaussian prediction heads, and takes 35 GPU-hours. Stochastic-solid volume rendering improves depth rendering and geometric supervision. Optional TTO optimizes LoRA modules inserted into the frozen backbone to align predicted and ground-truth camera parameters. Experiments across multiple datasets demonstrate strong RGB synthesis and geometric accuracy, while octree-guided allocation produces more compact scene representations. When camera parameters are supplied, TTO improves pose accuracy and alignment between reconstructed and ground-truth surfaces. Future work will investigate more flexible subdivision strategies and dynamic scene reconstruction. The current octree uses predefined levels and fixed subdivision ratios. Learning adaptive subdivision and merging from local geo-metric complexity may offer greater flexibility in balancing compactness and reconstruction quality. The present method primarily targets static scenes and does not explicitly model object motion or nonrigid deformation. Incorporating Gaussian motion models could extend feed-forward reconstruction to dynamic scenes while preserving geometric consistency across frames. ACKNOWLEDGMENT
