# DrawSplat: Atlanta World-Guided Implicit Gaussian SLAM for Indoor Reconstruction 

Fan Zhu, Zhisong Xu, Ziyu Chen, Zhenjun Zhao, Takayuki Okatani 

Abstract —RGB-D SLAM requires accurate online tracking, high-quality rendering, and geometrically consistent reconstruc-tion. Existing Gaussian SLAM methods mainly optimize Gaus-sian primitives with photometric, depth, or local frame-to-model constraints, which are often insufficient in indoor scenes with weak textures, large planar structures, noisy depth, and long trajectories. The resulting maps may suffer from pose drift, surface thickness artifacts, noisy normals, and blurred structural boundaries. We propose DrawSplat, an Atlanta World-guided implicit Gaussian SLAM framework for indoor reconstruction. DrawSplat follows a drawing-like process: explicit point-line geometry first sketches the structural layout, and implicit neural Gaussians then fill in high-fidelity appearance and continuous geometric details. It estimates camera poses, sparse geometry, and gravity direction through point-line joint tracking, infers struc-tural planes from tracked lines using the shared-gravity compo-nent of the Atlanta World prior, and converts them into differen-tiable regularization terms on Gaussian centers and normals. To improve compactness and efficiency, DrawSplat further combines an anchor-based implicit neural Gaussian representation with adaptive structural sampling, which allocates Gaussian capacity to structurally informative regions. Experiments on multiple datasets show that DrawSplat achieves robust tracking, high-fidelity rendering, and strong geometric reconstruction among Gaussian-based SLAM systems, while maintaining a practical balance among quality, efficiency, and model size. 

Index Terms —Gaussian Splatting, Visual SLAM, Reconstruc-tion, RGB-D, Atlanta World.

A. Neural Implicit SLAM 

Unlike traditional visual SLAM systems [4], [37], [38] that represent scenes with point clouds, voxels, or TSDF volumes [39], [40], neural implicit SLAM methods use contin-uous scene representations for dense reconstruction [16]–[20]. Starting from iMAP [41] and NICE-SLAM [16], later methods improve efficiency and geometry with multi-scale feature grids, tri-plane features, hash grids, or neural point clouds [17], [18], [42], [43]. These representations often recover smooth surfaces, but dense field queries, volumetric rendering, and iterative optimization limit real-time performance and interac-tive rendering [26]. DrawSplat instead keeps the efficient dif-ferentiable rasterization of 3D Gaussian Splatting [22], while using anchor-based implicit Gaussian decoding to introduce continuity and structural compatibility for online mapping. 

B. Gaussian-based SLAM 

3D Gaussian Splatting [22] has been widely used in SLAM for online mapping and photorealistic rendering [44]–[48]. SplaTAM [27] and MonoGS [28] directly optimize Gaus-sian primitives for tracking and reconstruction, while Photo-SLAM [49] and RTG-SLAM [30] combine feature-based tracking with Gaussian mapping for efficient pose estimation. Splat-SLAM [50] and LoopSplat [2] further address global optimization, loop constraints, and long-sequence consistency, with extensions to dynamic [51]–[53] and outdoor scenes [54], [55]. Despite these advances, accurate geometry remains difficult because explicit Gaussian primitives are discrete and are often optimized from local photometric or depth cues. For Gaussian reconstruction, GP-GS [25] uses Gaussian-process regression and uncertainty filtering to densify sparse SfM point clouds for initialization. DrawSplat instead allocates Gaussian capacity during incremental mapping using line, depth-gradient, and photometric responses. Methods based on 3DGS variants [36], [56]–[59] improve surface quality, and some systems intro-duce monocular estimation modules to avoid noisy captured depth [36], [50], [60]. Other methods [61]–[63] use multi-view stereo (MVS) [64], [65] or 3D foundation models [66], [67] to obtain geometric information from monocular input. These designs may increase complexity or underuse the structural in-formation available in RGB-D SLAM [68]. DrawSplat adopts a hybrid Atlanta World-guided Gaussian SLAM design that uses explicit geometry for stable tracking and structural cues, and implicit Gaussians for compact, high-quality mapping. 

C. Structural Priors for Gaussian SLAM 

Man-made indoor scenes contain structural regularities that have long been used to improve SLAM stability. Traditional structured SLAM methods exploit lines, planes, and Manhat-tan World constraints for pose estimation and map consistency in weak-texture scenes [69], [70]. Atlanta World relaxes the single Manhattan frame by allowing multiple horizontal dom-inant directions while preserving a shared gravity direction, making it better suited to multi-room indoor layouts [35]. MG-SLAM [31] is a representative Manhattan World-based Gaussian SLAM method. It uses line and planar constraints for tracking and mesh completion [71], but its structural priors are not fully integrated into differentiable Gaussian map optimization. DenseSplat [34] instead uses a NeRF prior to densify Gaussian maps under sparse keyframes and occlu-sions, improving continuity at the cost of an additional prior model. AtlasGS [33] studies Atlanta World-guided implicit Gaussian reconstruction with precomputed poses, whereas DrawSplat derives structural planes from online point-line tracking and updates them during mapping. Compared with SEGS-SLAM [1], DrawSplat introduces line-induced plane regularization and adaptive structural sampling into incremen-tal neural Gaussian mapping. III. M ETHODS

The key idea is to use sparse anchor points as the spatial backbone and decode local neural Gaussian primitives around each anchor. This design preserves the rendering efficiency of Gaussian Splatting while introducing the spatial continuity and structural compatibility needed for downstream regularization.

B. Explicit Geometry-Guided Tracking 

Relying only on an implicit scene representation is insuf-ficient for stable pose estimation in low-texture indoor envi-ronments. Inaccurate poses would, in turn, degrade Gaussian initialization and subsequent map optimization. We therefore introduce an explicit geometry-guided tracking front-end that provides reliable camera poses, sparse scene geometry, and gravity cues for the implicit Gaussian back-end. 

1) Point-Line Feature Extraction and Association: Our tracking front-end is built upon the well-established point-based SLAM method [4], which provides reliable local cor-respondences and efficient camera pose estimation. Unlike Gaussian SLAM systems that mainly rely on point features or feature-based tracking for pose estimation [1], [49], DrawSplat further extracts line features from each RGB-D frame to expose structural cues that are important for indoor recon-struction and subsequent Gaussian regularization. Specifically, for each RGB-D frame, we extract ORB point features [4] and detect line segments using EDLines [72] over an image pyramid. Inter-frame point associations are established using ORB descriptors, while line associations are established using LBD descriptors. Point features provide con-ventional local correspondence constraints in textured regions, whereas line features remain stable along wall boundaries, door frames, floor boundaries, and ceiling contours. Their joint use improves tracking robustness in weak-texture indoor regions and provides explicit structural observations for grav-ity estimation, plane inference, and structure-aware Gaussian optimization.

C. Atlanta World-Guided Structural Regularization

In low-texture planar regions, photometric and depth supervision alone often lead to thickness artifacts, noisy normals, and local geometric drift. To address this issue, we introduce an Atlanta World-guided planar formulation that uses the gravity direction to regularize horizontal and vertical structural surfaces. We integrate this prior with tracked line-induced planes to guide online implicit Gaussian mapping. D. Ablation on Semantic Source 

We further evaluate robustness to different semantic sources. Since semantic cues identify structural regions and modulate plane-to-Gaussian associations, directly removing semantics would change the intended weighting mechanism. We there-fore compare ground-truth semantic labels with 2D predicted semantic maps. As shown in Table XIII, predicted semantics achieves nearly identical PSNR and F1-score to ground-truth semantics [79], with high IoU scores for the major struc-tural classes. The results indicate that DrawSplat does not rely on ground-truth semantic labels and remains compatible with practical 2D semantic predictions. We also report the preprocessing speed for the predicted semantic maps.

C ONCLUSION 

We presented DrawSplat, an Atlanta World-guided implicit Gaussian SLAM framework for RGB-D indoor reconstruction. DrawSplat uses explicit point-line geometry to estimate stable poses, gravity cues, and structural planes, and converts these cues into differentiable regularization terms for an anchor-based implicit Gaussian map. Together with adaptive structural sampling, this design improves the balance among tracking accuracy, rendering quality, geometric reconstruction, and model compactness. Experiments on synthetic and real-world indoor datasets validate the complementary roles of explicit geometric tracking, structural regularization, implicit Gaussian representation, and adaptive sampling. 14 

Limitation and Future Work DrawSplat is mainly designed for RGB-D indoor scenes with dominant architectural struc-tures. Its performance may be less stable in highly dynamic environments, outdoor scenes, or spaces that weakly satisfy the Atlanta World assumption. Severe depth errors or unre-liable lines may impair pose, gravity, and plane estimation. DrawSplat also cannot predict all the unobserved holes in the same way as the generation method [80] does.
