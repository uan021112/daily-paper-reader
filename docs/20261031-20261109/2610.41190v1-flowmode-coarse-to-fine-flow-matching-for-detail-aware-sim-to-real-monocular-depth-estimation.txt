# FlowMoDE: Coarse-to-fine Flow Matching for Detail-aware Sim-to-Real Monocular Depth Estimation 

Liangjing Shao 1,2, Wanhao Liu 2, Jinsong Lin 1, Zhiwei Fang 1, Hongliang Ren 1,2,#

Abstract — Monocular depth estimation is a fundamental capability for 3D spatial perception in robotic and intelli-gent systems. While data-driven discriminative methods have achieved compelling performance for monocular depth esti-mation, generative approaches can reconstruct fine structural details with stronger generalization. However, most existing generative diffusion models suffer from low inference effi-ciency caused by multi-step sampling, whereas single-step diffusion models struggle to recover high-fidelity depth maps. To mitigate these drawbacks, this paper presents Flow-MoDE, a coarse-to-fine flow-matching-based framework for efficient, structure-aware monocular depth estimation sup-porting sim-to-real and cross-scene generalization. The core architecture first conducts coarse-level decoding conditioned on diffused features extracted from a pre-trained semantic encoder, and subsequently restores fine-grained structural patterns via expanded decoding layers. Trained exclusively on a single syn-thetic indoor dataset, our proposed method outperforms prior methods across five real-world indoor and outdoor datasets, yielding an average 14.1% reduction in relative error against state-of-the-art works as well as 9.9% reduction in chamfer distance for point cloud reconstruction. We further validate the generalization capability of FlowMoDE on realistic robotic scenes, with qualitative comparisons against state-of-the-art methods. The project page is https://flowmode-shao.netlify.app/. 

I. INTRODUCTION Monocular Depth Estimation (MDE) is a core 3D per-ception technique that underpins a wide range of robotic downstream tasks, including scene reconstruction, robotic manipulation, and autonomous navigation. [1], [2] Due to its practical significance, numerous MDE methods have been developed, which are generally divided into discriminative and generative paradigms. Despite delivering promising over-all depth prediction performance, discriminative methods inherently suffer from inaccurate depth estimation at object boundaries and fine-grained scene details, which severely degrades the geometric consistency of predicted depth maps. In contrast, generative models alleviate such local detail degradation issues yet introduce inevitable limitations: high computational cost and poor global scene contextual infor-mation, resulting in constrained overall prediction accuracy and robustness in complex scenarios. Current generative models [10], [11] commonly fine-tune Stable Diffusion to perform depth estimation. These ap-proaches rely on a Variational Autoencoder (VAE) to project depth maps into a latent space. Such compression inherently sacrifices edge sharpness and structural fidelity. Therefore, recent studies have attempted to conduct diffusion directly in the pixel space for better recovery of structural details.[7] Nevertheless, the multi-step pixel-wise diffusion process in-troduces high computational overhead. While several existing approaches [12], [13] adopt single-step Stable Diffusion for depth estimation, the heavy VAE encoder and decoder still create significant computation bottlenecks. Meanwhile, single-step denoising can limit the performance of depth estimation. In this work, we propose FlowMoDE, a coarse-to-fine flow matching model for computationally efficient sim-to-real pixel-accurate depth estimation. A lightweight decoder named as Semantic Decoder extracts global semantic features from a pre-trained discriminative encoder. Guided by global features, our model performs efficient decoding to establish a coarse depth distribution through Coarse Decoder. A sub-sequent Fine-grained Decoder with few wide Transformers further recovers delicate structural details to produce the final pixel-perfect depth map. Our main contributions can be summarized as the following: 1) We propose a novel flow matching framework for sim-to-real monocular depth estimation, which can provide structure-aware depth maps with lower computational cost. 2) We introduce coarse-to-fine semantic-guided velocity prediction which efficiently extracts coarse features based on semantic prior and further recover delicate details. 3) The proposed model is only trained on synthetic in-door dataset, but achieve state-of-the-art performance across five diverse datasets including both indoor and outdoor scenes . Further application of the proposed model in robotic scenes demonstrate the generalization and potential ability for spatial perception of robots. II. RELATED WORK 

A. Discriminative Monocular Depth Estimation 

Early monocular depth estimation relied on hand-crafted visual priors and graphical models to infer scene layouts from single images. Deep learning redefined MDE as a supervised dense prediction task, giving rise to various CNN-based solu-tions such as [14]. Later relative-depth studies such as MiDaS [15] prioritized cross-dataset generalization, proving that mixing heterogeneous datasets improves zero-shot transfer. Meanwhile, DPT [16] firstly established a high-performance transformer-driven dense prediction framework, leading to a series of excellent ViT-based methods. Recent founda-tion models such as Depth Anything [8], [9] enhance open-domain robustness via large-scale training. In paral-lel, metric-depth approaches such as Metric3D [17] and UniDepth [5], [6] aim to reconstruct depth with absolute scale and have achieved steady improvements in accuracy and generalization. Despite these advances, modern dis-criminative models commonly adopt large-patch ViT en-coders together with convolutional upsampling decoders. Many discriminative methods strengthen detail reconstruc-tion through local refinement, high-resolution inference or multi-scale decoding. Depth-Pro [18] produces sharp metric depth via high-resolution architectures and boundary-aware training. MoGe [3] improves local geometry by predicting affine-invariant point maps under combined global and lo-cal geometric supervision, while MoGe-2 [4] further pre-serves fine details using refined training data. MDA [19] models per-pixel depth ambiguity with mixture-density rep-resentations to generate point maps without flying-point noise. InfiniDepth [20] formulates depth as a neural im-plicit field and employs local implicit decoders to support arbitrary-resolution queries and clearer depth details. Nev-ertheless, early tokenization in such architectures damages pixel-level cues and high-frequency components, making fine geometric structures hard to recover in decoding. 

B. Generative Monocular Depth Estimation 

Diffusion generative models have been applied in monoc-ular depth estimation models such as Marigold [10] and MonoDiffusion [11], benefiting from rapid convergence and strong representation priors. PPD [7] introduces the injection of semantic features into late diffusion process to maintain global information and perform pixel-level denoise to pro-vide pixel-perfect depth maps. However, the computational cost acutely increase due to multiple diffusion transformer layers. Recently, DepthMaster [13] utilize single-step UNet-based diffusion to perform generative depth estimation. How-ever, heavy VAE still limit computational efficiency, while single-step denoise degrades the performance of the proposed model. DepthFM [21] firstly introduces flow matching into monocular depth estimation. However, lack of semantic information limits its performance, while VAE-based ar-chitecture still cause high computational cost and loss of structural details. To this end, FlowMoDE proposed in this work injects adjusted semantic features into few-step flow matching to efficiently perform structure-aware monocular depth estimation. III. METHOD

A. Implementation Details 

We utilize frozen DINOv2-B from MoGe as our semantic encoder. The dimensions of semantic decoder, coarse decoder and fine-grained decoder are respectively set to 384, 384 and 768. We train the proposed model on single dataset Hypersim [28], which consists of 54K high-quality synthetic indoor frames and corresponding depth maps. The proposed model is trained for 200 epochs (2000 sampling steps for each epoch) with a batch size of 4 and a fixed learning rate of 1e-4, while all training samples are resized to 640 ×480. All experiments are implemented on a NVIDIA H100 GPU. 

B. Comparison Results: Monocular Depth Estimation 

All methods are evaluated on five diverse real-world datasets NYUv2 [29], KITTI [30], ETH3D [31], ScanNet [32] and DIODE [33] following Marigold [10], resizing all samples to a resolution with a long edge of 768. We compared our model with existing discriminative models and generative models. For DIODE, we directly use masks provided officially, while some works such as PPD [7] use Sobel to filter areas with high gradient and RANSAC to align pixels in their papers. D. Comparison Results: 3D Perception in Robotic Scenes

Therefore, we choose diverse cross-embodiment robotic scenes which respectively consist of Baxter, Franka, Kuka and Da Vinci Robot, to implement the proposed model for zero-shot monocular depth estimation. V. CONCLUSIONS In this paper, we present FlowMoDE, a flow-matching generative model for sim-to-real monocular depth estimation to recover fine structural details. Trained solely on synthetic data, our model outperforms prior methods on five diverse benchmarks. We further validate its strong ability to re-construct fine-grained geometry in various robotic scenarios against state-of-the-art approaches. With fewer Transformer blocks and minimal inference steps, FlowMoDE is lighter and more efficient than existing generative depth models. Still, patch embedding in our flow-matching pipeline may produce occasional flying pixels.
