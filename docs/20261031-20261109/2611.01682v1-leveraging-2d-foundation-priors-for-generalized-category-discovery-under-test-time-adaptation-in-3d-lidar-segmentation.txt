Abstract
In autonomous driving, 3D LiDAR semantic segmentation is ex-
pected to adapt to evolving environments and recognize previously
unseen categories, making generalized category discovery under
test-time adaptation (GCD-TTA) an important problem. However,
existing works treat GCD and TTA separately, while the limited
semantic information in 3D point clouds makes GCD-TTA under
domain shift more prone to feature collapse when combining GCD
with TTA. To address this issue, we propose a two-stage framework
for GCD-TTA in 3D LiDAR semantic segmentation. Specifically, in
the first stage, we adaptively aggregate local 3D features according
to geometric density variations and obtain reliable pseudo labels
under a global class-wise prototype consistency constraint. In the
second stage, we cast category discovery as an optimal transport
assignment process for globally consistent grouping of novel cate-
gories. Meanwhile, semantic features from 2D vision foundation
models are fused with 3D point cloud features to enrich 3D se-
mantic information and promote a more separable feature space.
Extensive experiments on Synth4D to SemanticKITTI, SynLiDAR
to SemanticKITTI, and Synth4D to nuScenes demonstrate the effec-
tiveness of our framework. Under a unified evaluation protocol, our
method achieves the best overall mIoU on 9 out of 10 benchmark
splits and improves the overall mIoU by up to 12.21 points over the
strongest re-implemented baseline.

Introduction
The semantic segmentation of LiDAR point clouds is fundamental
to autonomous driving, providing fine-grained scene understand-
ing for downstream tasks such as planning and safe navigation [29].
Beyond safety requirements [38], real-world deployment faces two
coupled challenges: domain shifts caused by changing data distri-
butions [44] and the emergence of unseen semantic categories in

open-world scenarios [15]. Therefore, practical 3D LiDAR segmen-
tation models should generalize across environments and discover
categories absent from the source domain, corresponding to test-
time adaptation (TTA) [19] and generalized category discovery
(GCD) [36], respectively.
Existing 3D LiDAR TTA methods [12, 30, 45–47] mainly follow
a synthetic-to-real paradigm, adapting models pretrained on la-
beled synthetic domains (e.g., SynLiDAR [39], Synth4D [30]) to
unlabeled real-world data (e.g., SemanticKITTI [2], nuScenes [10]).
Meanwhile, although GCD has been extensively studied in 2D vi-
sion [21, 24, 26, 34–37], most 3D approaches [13, 18, 28, 41] focus on
novel class discovery (NCD), assuming that only unseen categories
require grouping. Recent work [16] extends 3D LiDAR segmenta-
tion toward GCD by considering disjoint labeled and unlabeled sets
containing both known and novel categories. However, existing
studies still treat TTA and GCD as separate problems, leaving their
joint challenge unexplored. Fig. 1 illustrates their distinct pipelines.
Jointly addressing TTA and GCD is particularly challenging due
to domain shift, online unsupervised adaptation, and the sparse,
ambiguous nature of 3D point clouds. These factors can destabilize
feature representations and cause feature collapse during adapta-
tion. Consequently, noisy pseudo labels induce confirmation bias,
where repeated self-supervision reinforces incorrect alignment and
category assignments, ultimately degrading class separability and
representation capacity.
To address these challenges, we introduce generalized category
discovery under test-time adaptation (GCD-TTA) for 3D LiDAR
semantic segmentation, aiming at unified adaptation and discov-
ery. We adopt a teacher-student framework for online adaptation,
as illustrated in Fig. 2. For source-known categories, we refine
teacher-generated pseudo labels using geometry priors to reduce
domain-induced errors. For source-unknown categories, we employ
Sinkhorn-based balanced assignments [9] to discover emerging
categories from unlabeled target points. Furthermore, we lever-
age transferable semantic priors from 2D foundation models (e.g.,
DINO [5, 23, 33]) to enhance feature discrimination and allevi-
ate confirmation bias caused by noisy supervision. The proposed
framework enables simultaneous target-domain adaptation and
novel-category discovery.
Our contributions are summarized as follows:
• We introduce generalized category discovery under test-time
adaptation (GCD-TTA) for 3D LiDAR semantic segmentation
and present the first unified framework for this setting.
• We propose a geometry-aware pseudo-label refinement strat-
egy that integrates distance-aware filtering and global pro-
totype consistency to suppress noisy supervision.
• We incorporate 2D foundation model features as transfer-
able semantic priors, improving ambiguous-point discrimina-
tion and novel-category discovery while preserving shared-
category performance.
2
Related Work
2.1
Test-Time Adaptation
Test-time adaptation (TTA) adapts a source-pretrained model to
unlabeled target data at inference time, without accessing source-
domain samples during deployment [19]. In existing 3D LiDAR
semantic segmentation studies, GIPSO [30] formulates source-free
online adaptation through adaptive self-training and geometric-
feature propagation. MM-TTA [32] extends this setting to multi-
modal segmentation with intra-modal pseudo-label generation and
inter-modal refinement. HGL [46] exploits hierarchical geometric
structure through local pseudo-label generation, global prototype
refinement, and temporal consistency. More recent methods move
toward continual and deployment-oriented adaptation, such as
D3CTTA [45] and APCoTTA [11]. GOOD [47] further highlights
the importance of handling semantic shifts beyond the standard
closed-set setting. Annotator [40] and ATTA [12] incorporate ac-
tive learning into TTA, leading to markedly improved adaptation
efficiency.
2.2
Generalized Category Discovery
Generalized category discovery (GCD) considers a realistic mixed
setting with both known and novel categories [36]. In 2D vision,
SimGCD [37] and PIM [6] show that parametric classification can
be effective for this task, while CMS [7] improves clustering quality
with mean-shift updates and contrastive learning. More practical
variants have also been explored, including active GCD [21], GCD
for semantic segmentation [24], and related settings under dis-
tribution shifts [42]. In 3D LiDAR semantic segmentation, recent
works [18, 27, 28, 41] focus on novel category discovery, assuming
coexisting labeled and unlabeled points in each scan, with unla-
beled points belonging only to novel categories. Study [16] further
considers a generalized setting, where unlabeled data contain both
known and novel categories and are disjoint from labeled data.
This setting is more general and better reflects realistic open-world
scenarios.

4
Experiments
4.1
Experimental Setup
Source and target datasets. We use SynLiDAR [39] and Synth4D [30]
as the source datasets for pre-training, and adapt the pre-trained
models to the real-world target datasets SemanticKITTI [2] and
nuScenes [10]. For both target datasets, we follow the official vali-
dation split. For SemanticKITTI, this corresponds to Sequence 08,
which contains 4,071 LiDAR scans at 10 Hz. For nuScenes, we use
the official validation split with 150 sequences and 6,000 LiDAR
scans, where 40 scans are sampled from each scene at 2 Hz.
Baselines. We select several novel category discovery methods,
including NOPS [27] and SNOPS [28], and adapt them as baselines
for the GCD-TTA setting. To make these methods compatible with
our setting, we apply the required modifications, in particular using
the pretrained source model to generate pseudo labels for adap-
tation. For SNOPS, the auxiliary network alignment stage does
not converge with the original SGD optimizer in our experiments,
whereas AdamW [20] yields stable results, and is therefore used
in our implementation. Other methods, including DASL [41], Li
et al. [18], and Kim et al. [16], are excluded due to their code is
unavailable or incomplete in core parts.
Implementation details. We use MinkowskiNet [8] as the back-
bone for LiDAR segmentation. In total, we pre-train 10 source
models across three settings, namely four splits for SynLiDAR →
SemanticKITTI, three splits for Synth4D →SemanticKITTI, and
three splits for Synth4D →nuScenes. The models are trained for
200 epochs on SynLiDAR →SemanticKITTI and 100 epochs on the
other two settings. We use SGD with momentum 0.9, weight decay
0.0001, and batch size 16. All source pre-training experiments are
conducted on 8 NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPUs. During adaptation, we
perform online test-time adaptation by traversing the unlabeled
target validation set only once with a batch size of 1. The optimizer
and other hyper-parameters are kept the same as in pre-training.
For the GCD procedure, we follow [4, 30] and adopt the Sinkhorn-
Knopp [9] algorithm with 3 Sinkhorn iterations. All adaptation
experiments are performed on a single NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU.
Evaluation metrics. For evaluation, we adopt the strict Hungarian
matching algorithm [17] following GCD [36]. Hungarian matching
is applied only to category discovery, since their predicted indices
are ambiguous under clustering. We report the final performance
of the mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) for both known and
novel categories.

Different numbers of 2D image views. Tab. 5 shows that mIoUall
and mIoUnovel exhibit different responses to the number of 2D views.

views are introduced, complementary image priors improve the
representation, with all six views achieving the best mIoUall across
all splits. For mIoUnovel, additional 2D priors provide larger im-
provements, suggesting that semantic cues from foundation models
help distinguish ambiguous target points beyond geometric evi-
dence. However, the three front-facing views achieve the highest
mIoUnovel, while adding rear views slightly reduces performance on
some splits. This may be because novel class discovery relies more
on semantic consistency than coverage: extra views can introduce
unreliable cues caused by occlusion, sparsity, and domain gaps be-
tween Synth4D and nuScenes. Overall, multi-view 2D priors are
beneficial, but high-quality semantic guidance is more important
than simply increasing the number of views.

5
Conclusion
In this paper, we investigate generalized category discovery under
test-time adaptation for 3D point cloud segmentation. To improve
both domain adaptation and novel category discovery, we combine
3D geometric priors with 2D-3D multimodal semantic priors. Exper-
iments on three cross-domain benchmarks show that the proposed
method achieves consistent improvements, obtaining the best over-
all mIoU on 9 of the 10 splits. The ablation results further highlight
the value of multi-view semantic priors, while the additional analy-
ses suggest that our method learns features with better separation
and less collapse. Although the current framework still assumes
that the number of novel categories is known in advance and cannot
fully avoid partial degradation on known classes compared with
source-only training, the results indicate that integrating geometric
and semantic priors is an effective direction for GCD-TTA in 3D
LiDAR segmentation.
Limitations. Our method still has some limitations. In particular, it
assumes prior knowledge of the number of novel categories, which
may not be available in real-world applications. Moreover, although
it alleviates the performance drop on known categories, partial
degradation still exists. An important direction for future work
is to develop category-number-free discovery strategies, further
improve the preservation of known-category performance, and
extend the proposed framework toward continual and incremental
learning scenarios.
