ABSTRACT 

LLM-based robotic agent systems can generate programs for robot control, yet their generalization to new tasks often depends on elaborate scaffolding. Expanding their skill repertoires alone offers limited guidance on how to assess new capabilities or identify what remains beyond their reach. We introduce META-Robot, a framework that couples self cognition with capability scaling by expanding the criteria through which an agent evaluates and develops its skills. At its core, action-level predicates express explicit conditions for assessing execution and provide a structured basis for LLM reasoning. Using the resulting assessments, the agent identifies capability gaps, extends relevant evaluation criteria, and guides the structured expansion of its skills. Proprioceptive feedback further grounds this self-scaling process in the robot’s physical state. Experiments in simulation and on real robots demonstrate improved overall task performance, generalization to new tasks, continued expan-sion of skills and evaluation criteria, and effective use of non-visual proprioceptive signals. 

1 INTRODUCTION 

Robotic agents have emerged as a promising paradigm for general-purpose robotic manipulation recently with generated code as control policy Liang et al. (2023); Xiao et al. (2026). While these systems Kumar (2026) can adapt to new tasks without training to some extent, their scalability remains limited. When tasks require evaluation criteria, feedback modalities, or skills beyond predefined scaffolds, agents cannot revise these system-level limitations, leading to repeated strategy switching and compounding ineffective modifications that make meta-level task generalization or reliable recovery nearly impossible. Predicate scaling offers a natural solution by allowing agents to retain and reuse predicates acquired from previous tasks. This accumulation can improve transfer and reduce the need for additional predefined scaffolding. However, scaling predicates alone cannot overcome fixed system scaffolding, as systems cannot have explicit cognition of task and its own capability limits. Recent efforts to improve scalability follow a primary strategy: replacing accumulation of fixed predicates with validated, agent-discovered skills distilled from execution processes Zhang et al. (2026); Lu et al. (2026). This reduces manual engineering and allows past repairs to guide future tasks. However, these skills are accumulated without structured links to their expected effects and required evidence. Consequently, the skill library may continue to grow while the system’s understanding of the task and its own capabilities remains unchanged, limiting substantive capability scaling. The emergence of Neuro-Symbolic Predicates (NSPs) Liang et al. (2025); Athalye et al. (2026) presents a compelling foundation for scaling robot agent system cognition beyond fixed mechanisms. By combining neural perceptual queries with programmatic logic, NSPs can construct abstractions for evaluation, offering a path toward adapting to new evaluation criteria. Nevertheless, extend-ing predicate invention to self cognition and capability scaling in robotic agent systems remains challenging. Predicates must assess not only whether task goals are satisfied, but also whether the 1arXiv:2610.41868v2 [cs.RO] 4 Nov 2026 Preprint. 

current system has sufficient observations, evaluation criteria, and executable skills. We argue that grounding predicates with physical evidence at the action level is crucial, as it allows the agent to exploit available proprioceptive signals when vision is insufficient, construct corresponding skills and evaluation criteria, and integrate them into control and verification, effectively coupling cognitive and capability scaling with evidence-grounded assessment. To this end, we propose META-Robot, as illustrated in Fig. 1, a system for self cognition and capability scaling that grounds robotic meta-generalization in evidence-based, action-level predicates for both system self-assessment and task evaluation. By recording predicate transitions around skill executions, coupling validated predicates with reusable skills, and orchestrating proprioceptive feedback, META-Robot creates a scalable system for jointly scaling robotic cognition and capabilities. Its framework consists of three components: (1) Loop Execution Agent, which generates programs and predicates, evaluates execution evidence, and repairs failures; (2) Proprioceptive Signal Orchestration, which supplements insufficient visual observations with physical feedback; and (3) coupled Predicate and Skill Libraries, which consolidate validated criteria and repairs for cognitive and capability scaling. 

META-Robot incorporates several key innovations. First, to mitigate the ambiguity between coarse execution logs and actual task-state changes, we introduce a closed-loop execution agent that evaluates each action execution through action-level predicate transitions. The agent records observations, inputs, outputs, and pre- and post-execution predicate states, associating program behavior with changes in robot action. When an error is detected, it performs targeted local repair and re-execution, followed by full-task validation of the revised program. Second, fixed evaluation criteria make failures beyond the coverage of the existing system difficult to diagnose and can lead to unstructured skill accumulation. To address this, META-Robot maintains coupled predicate and skill libraries that expand through validated execution experience. Although this design supports continual knowledge scaling, it also requires precise alignment between the two libraries. META-Robot establishes explicit predicate–skill associations to support grounded skill retrieval, composition, and expansion. We conduct extensive experiments in both simulation environments [23] and real-world environments to validate the effectiveness of META-Robot. (i) META-Robot achieves stronger overall performance than prior methods, reaching a success rate of 95.8% on the LIBERO-Pro Spatial-Task, compared with 60.0% for other baselines. Crucially, we observe (ii) emergent cognition and capabilities including the scaling of reusable skills and their corresponding evaluation criteria from validated execution experience. We further demonstrate (iii) robust generalization through zero-shot transfer to 2Preprint. 

held-out long-horizon tasks without additional debugging and successful deployment across diverse real-robot tasks. Together with the ablation results, these findings establish META-Robot as a scalable framework for jointly expanding robotic cognition and capability. This paper makes the following contributions: • We introduce META-Robot, which scales its cognition and capability by jointly improving task policies and the supporting system itself through predicate-guided self-assessment. • We develop coupled action-level predicate and skill libraries that support continual refine-ment of evaluation criteria and policies, as well as the generation of new structured criteria and skills beyond the existing repertoire. • We propose a proprioceptive signal orchestration mechanism that expands the system’s capacity for perception, evaluation, and control. 

2 RELATED WORK 

Agentic Robotic Systems. Recent work increasingly formulates robot control as a closed-loop agentic process rather than a one-shot mapping from language to actions. Systems such as Hi Robot and Being-0 combine high-level vision-language reasoning with lower-level VLA policies or modular skills Shi et al. (2025); Yuan et al. (2025). Robot-use agents further expose capabilities through executable interfaces and stateful harnesses: CaP-X systematically studies Code-as-Policy agents across different abstraction levels and introduces structured execution feedback and visual differencing; Guava introduces semantic tools and multimodal observations; Thea adds persistent scene context and execution evaluation; and Show-Harness enables VLM-driven manipulation through semantic motion primitives, like Cap-X, Guava Fu et al. (2026); Liu et al. (2026). These works establish perception, planning, execution, verification, and recovery as core components of agentic robotics. However, most still rely on predefined tools, observation channels, and evaluators provided by the surrounding harness. 

Skill-Scaling Robotic Agents. Beyond closed-loop execution, skill-scaling agents retain and adapt experience across tasks. One line of work reuses prior programs or reflections as memory, including Memory Transfer Planning, Growing with Your Embodied Agent, DAHLIA, and EEAgent Ka-gaya et al. (2025); Meng et al. (2025a;b); Wang et al. (2026a). Another expands the executable repertoire itself: Playful discovers reusable skills, ASPIRE distills validated repairs, RHO searches repository-level policies, and Embodied Skills, ALRM, and AOR broaden reusable tools or controller implementations Zhang et al. (2026); Lu et al. (2026); Elmaaroufi et al. (2026); Wang et al. (2026b); Santos et al. (2026); Kumar (2026). Yet capability growth is typically decoupled from the evolution of evaluation criteria and evidence requirements. META-Robot instead couples hierarchical skills with action-level predicates and additional feedback channels, allowing capabilities and the mechanisms used to assess them to scale together. 

3 METHOD 

We introduce META-Robot, a novel framework for meta-level generalization to new tasks. The META-Robot framework is grounded in the scaling of cognition and capability, where evaluation criteria, feedback acquisition, available signals, and executable skills are jointly expanded, thereby reducing dependence on predefined scaffolding. META-Robot operates directly on action-level predicates throughout the process, instead of coarse execution outcomes, which better connects program behavior with changes in task conditions. Specifically, the closed-loop execution agent records observations, inputs, outputs, and pre- and post-execution predicate states for every skill invocation and subtask end. The predicate-state trace enables the system to consistently assess progress, attribute failures, and verify targeted repairs through local re-execution before full-task validation. We also introduce coupled action-level predicate and Skill Libraries and a proprioceptive signal orchestration mechanism to overcome the limitations of unstructured skill accumulation and vision-only verification. With these components, META-Robot can enrich its task evidence and cognition, consolidate validated repairs as reusable skills, and acquire additional physical evidence when existing observations are insufficient, progressively expanding both system cognition and capability. 3.1 LOOP EXECUTION AGENT SYSTEM 

Inspired by software coding agents DeepSeek-AI (2026); Barbaste et al. (2026), we treat evaluation of the execution system itself as a key component of scaling, just as shown in Fig.2. Existing robotic agents often focus on task outcomes or intermediate execution feedback, with limited assessment of the mechanisms responsible for observation and evaluation. When failures arise beyond these mechanisms’ coverage—the system’s cognition and capability boundaries—repeated iteration alone may be insufficient. To solve this problem, META-Robot’s loop execution agent system uses action-level predicates to assess the outcome of each action execution. Given a task description, dense semantic injection Smith et al. (2025) enriches its specification and decomposes it into a subtask trace. For each subtask, the agent generates action-level predicates from the task description and the LLM’s own reasoning, establishing explicit criteria for execution assessment. These predicates are evaluated after each skill invocation and at subtask completion, providing fine-grained evidence of whether the intended action conditions have been satisfied. To recover from an unsatisfied action-level condition, we initiate a bounded local repair loop whenever a required action-level predicate evaluates to False. To restore the expected state transition within a limited number of turns, the repair agent revises the code surrounding the failed action or applies iterative refinement procedures, like Picard iteration Ince (1956) and multi-directional approach search Panic (2026); Ortega and Rheinboldt (2000). To preserve diagnostic evidence when the failure cannot be localized or resolved locally, we record the latest predicate state and continue the remaining execution. To support task-level failure attribution, we consolidate all evaluations into a predicate transition trace. If the overall task fails, a global repair agent analyzes this trace together with execution logs and auxiliary evidence, formulates diagnostic hypotheses, and revises the task program or its supporting observation and verification mechanisms. To validate these system-level modifications, we re-execute the complete task, enabling improvements beyond isolated action-level repairs. Skills specify how to execute or repair an interaction, while associated predicates specify the conditions to be satisfied after execution. Predicates and skills are associated: each skill can be associated with multiple predicates, and different skills may share the same predicate.

To enable the agent to reason about the robot’s own perceptual capabilities, META-Robot introduces a mechanism for the synergistic use of proprioceptive signals. Instead of relying on fixed observation interfaces, the system identifies available proprioceptive signals—such as joint states, motor currents, force/torque measurements, and contact-related feedback—by examining specifications tied to the robot’s embodiment (e.g., simulator configurations, controller parameters, and robot description files like URDF). The system then associates these signals with their physical semantics and utilizes them as actionable sources of evidence during execution. When a task indicates that visual information alone is insufficient to achieve the goal, the system incorporates these identified signals into the execution and verification processes. Validated pro-prioceptive applications are refined and categorized within the skill library (e.g., contact-aware grasping, slip detection, or force-sensitive manipulation), while the predicate library is expanded to include corresponding action-level criteria that define the state conditions required before and after an interaction. For instance, the assessment of a grasping action is no longer limited to a visual check of the gripper’s closed state; it can also be determined based on contact conditions, motor load, or force stability. This is particularly crucial for failure cases that are visually ambiguous (such as "near-miss" grasps) or for tasks involving deformable and thin objects (like folding a towel, just as shown in Fig.3), where image evidence alone often fails to confirm whether stable contact or effective manipulation has been achieved. Through this process, META-Robot moves beyond treating proprioception merely as a static auxiliary input; instead, it continuously explores how embodiment-specific signals can support novel execution strategies and verification criteria, thereby improving the cognition and capabilities of the system. As these signal-based skills and predicate-based criteria accumulate, the system gains access to interaction state information that is difficult to infer visually, leading to significant improvements in meta-level generalization to more complex tasks.

4.4 ZERO -S HOT TRANSFER TO UNSEEN TASKS 

We evaluate transfer to LIBERO-Pro Long using fixed predicate and skill libraries, without additional debugging or library updates, as shown in Fig. 5. We randomly retain {25%, 50%, 75%, 100%} of the skills and their associated predicates. On Task perturbations, success is 0.0%, 31.8%, 40.2%, and 37.0% at the four retention ratios, respectively. The corresponding results on Swap perturbations are 0.0%, 21.2%, 8.0%, and 8.2%. These results show that accumulated predicate–skill knowledge supports partial zero-shot transfer, but its benefit varies substantially with the perturbation type and retained library composition. In particular, the non-monotonic Task trend and the weaker Swap results indicate that retaining more entries does not necessarily improve retrieval or execution. However, because each ratio is evaluated using a randomly retained subset, the experiment cannot determine whether the differences arise from redundant context, missing task-relevant entries, or sampling variation. Repeated subsampling across multiple library masks is therefore required before interpreting 75% retention as an optimal trade-off. Per-task results in Figure. A.2 further show that gains are concentrated in a subset of tasks, suggesting that transfer depends on whether the retained skills and predicates cover each task’s required conditions rather than on library size alone. 8Preprint.

4.5.2 PROPRIOCEPTION ABLATION TEST 

Libero-Pro lacks tasks that heavily rely on proprioceptive signals. Therefore, we specifically designed a new simulation task involving the grasping of a rigid, thin object for ablation testing; the success rate was 50% without proprioceptive signals and 78% with them, as shown in Fig. 6. The whole process can be divided into 4 steps: 1. The gripper positions itself and approaches the sheet; 2. it descends slowly to locate the point of contact; 3. it uses internal feedback to determine whether the gripper has made contact with the sheet or the tabletop and adjusts the grasping position; 4. it closes the gripper, lifts the sheet, and verifies the success of the grasp. Since the sheet lies flat against the 9Preprint.

5 CONCLUSION 

This paper presented META-Robot, a self congnition and capbility scaling robotic agent that jointly improves execution, observation, and evaluation of task and system itself. Predicate state-transition traces support failure diagnosis and targeted repair, while coupled Predicate and Skill Libraries consolidate reusable criteria and procedures. Proprioceptive signal orchestration further incorporates physical evidence beyond vision. Experiments on LIBERO-Pro and the Galaxea R1 Lite demonstrate effective manipulation and transfer to held-out long-horizon tasks without additional debugging. The varying transfer performance also shows that more accumulated knowledge does not necessarily yield better generalization, emphasizing the importance of selecting task-relevant experience. More broadly, META-Robot supports joint cognitive and capability scaling by refining not only executable skills, but also the evidence channels and verification criteria used to assess them. These results motivate robotic self scaling through the coordinated refinement of execution, sensing, and evaluation. Future work will study relevance-aware knowledge selection, automatic signal calibration, and broader continual evaluation across tasks, embodiments, and contact-rich settings.
