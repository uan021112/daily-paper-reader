## Abstract 

We present Queryable Gaussian View Index (QGVI) for querying existing Gaussian scene assets and their asso-ciated calibrated reference data. Text-view retrieval and selective RGB camera localization return poses in the as-set’s coordinate frame. QGVI-T turns Gaussian-rendered views into a language-searchable index of scene loca-tions, binding structured captions to known camera poses and place identifiers. Semantic and lexical retrieval se-lect views from distinct places without online rendering, VLM calls, or a scene-specific pose network. The image route, QGVI-I, filters LoGS-based geometric estimates us-ing distinct inlier query keypoints. It omits photometric refinement and preserves accepted pose coordinates. On nine development scenes, QGVI-T achieves 58.3% Top-1 and 78.3% Top-5 joint relevance-and-validity success, com-pared with 26.1% and 34.6% for our CityLoC reimple-mentation with refinement. QGVI-I achieves 97.3% scene-mean pose success at 1 m/2 ◦, with pooled false-acceptance and false-rejection rates of 0.707% and 1.142%. A paired cached evaluation retains all 1,775 initializer poses cor-rect at this threshold. Recorded median text query latency is 0.020 s. Matched controls show competitive BM25 re-trieval and slightly lower false acceptance for thresholded full LoGS; grid augmentation suggests modest gains on novel-view-derived descriptions. We report model-judged semantic success separately from source-camera recovery, with source-verified or estimated scales for geometric eval-uation. 

1arXiv:2610.41373v1 [cs.CV] 30 Oct 2026 1. Introduction 

We study how to locate viewpoints within an existing Gaus-sian scene asset from a text description or an RGB observa-tion. The reconstructed asset and its associated calibrated reference data are supplied inputs. Both query modalities seek camera poses in the asset’s coordinate frame, with dif-ferent conditions on a valid answer. A description may ad-mit several viewpoints; an RGB observation constrains the camera that captured it. A retrieved view may omit a re-quired attribute, and a localizer may return a pose for an image from another scene (Fig. 1). For semantic scene querying, a description specifies con-ditions on a view without necessarily identifying its source camera. Several distant poses may satisfy the same con-ditions. A continuous pose distribution can represent this ambiguity, but estimating that distribution is not required to return a bounded set of satisfying views. We therefore eval-uate semantic satisfaction separately from source-camera recovery. This formulation retains the need for geometric pose accuracy when the query is an RGB image. Gaussian splatting supports fast rendering of reference images at known poses [18]. CityLoC uses Gaussian scenes for text-conditioned pose localization [26]; LoGS com-bines geometric initialization and render-based refinement for visual localization [8]. Queryable Gaussian View In-dex (QGVI) provides semantic view access and geometric camera localization within the supplied asset. Its routes use separate reference assets and solvers: QGVI-T retrieves Gaussian-rendered views with stored poses, while QGVI-I estimates camera poses from original RGB references and sparse 3D landmarks. Their common scene-grounding for-mulation supports a shared accounting of correct returns, incorrect returns, and coverage, with modality-specific cor-rectness criteria. Our primary contribution, QGVI-T, makes the supplied Gaussian asset searchable through a posed view index. Virtual cameras sample observations beyond the captured training views. Each index entry links a structured de-scription to visible evidence, a camera pose, and an iden-tifier shared by views at the same sampled camera cen-ter. Sentence-level semantic retrieval and lexical retrieval jointly address paraphrases and explicit terms, including visible text. Place-aware selection prevents several views from one indexed location from exhausting the five-result budget. Rendering and captioning are performed offline; a query searches the index without loading the Gaussian model, calling a VLM, or training a scene-specific pose net-work. An original-image-only control isolates view-bank augmentation: confirmed retrieval gains are small and con-centrated in descriptions authored from novel views, with uncertainty limiting a general improvement claim. The complementary image route, QGVI-I, applies an acceptance gate to the LoGS geometric initializer, omit-ting photometric refinement. The gate counts distinct in-lier query keypoints, avoiding repeated support from one feature matched across several references. A threshold se-lected on Block A and SF validation queries is frozen across nine test scenes. Every accepted pose retains its original co-ordinates. We evaluate separately whether the gate rejects any estimate that satisfies the pose-accuracy thresholds. Our contributions are: • A posed view index (QGVI-T) that connects structured language to Gaussian-rendered scene observations, com-bining semantic and lexical retrieval with selection across distinct places. • Selective camera localization (QGVI-I), which filters LoGS-based geometric estimates using distinct inlier query keypoints and preserves accepted pose coordinates. • A nine-scene evaluation with a shared accounting of correct returns, coverage, and accepted-result precision, while distinguishing semantic satisfaction, source-camera recovery, and RGB pose accuracy. On this benchmark, QGVI-T attains 78.3% Top-5 success with a recorded 20 ms median query latency. An optional verification ablation improves Top-1 success but reduces Top-5 success. The RGB gate retains every previously correct pose in the evaluated cohort. These findings con-cern model-judged development data; they do not guarantee correct-pose retention on unseen queries. 

## 2. Related Work 

Text-conditioned localization. Text-conditioned local-ization connects a description to a position or pose in a scene. Text2Pos and Text2Loc localize within point-cloud maps through coarse-to-fine inference [20, 44]; PMSH models uncertainty when descriptions only partially match submaps [12]. CityLoC [26] instead learns a text-conditioned distribution over camera poses using diffusion and rendered candidates for refinement. Such distributions accommodate multiple plausible poses. Our semantic query task instead asks for a bounded set of satisfying views; source-pose error alone cannot credit every valid alterna-tive. This task distinction does not establish that discrete retrieval universally outperforms continuous estimation. Semantic scene representations also support spatial queries. CLIP [30] aligns image and text representations, and OpenScene aligns point features with image and text embeddings [28]. LERF, LangSplat, and Feature 3DGS ex-tend language or semantic features to renderable scene rep-resentations [19, 29, 47]. QGVI-T searches posed Gaus-sian views using BM25 [32] and MiniLM-based sentence embeddings [31, 43], preserving explicit terms alongside semantic similarity and selecting distinct indexed places. Its semantic evaluation measures description satisfaction at returned views; source-camera recovery is evaluated sepa-rately. 2Geometric localization. Structure-based methods esti-mate pose from image-to-map correspondences, with re-trieval and local matching determining the available geo-metric evidence. Active Search prioritizes direct 2D–3D matching [36]; hierarchical localization retrieves candidate places before local matching [33]. InLoc further combines dense matching with synthesized-view verification [40]. Global descriptors, including NetVLAD, MixVPR, and SALAD, retrieve reference images [1, 3, 16]. Local representations range from SIFT and its RootSIFT nor-malization [2, 25] to learned detectors and descriptors such as SuperPoint and D2-Net [10, 11]. SuperGlue and LightGlue assign sparse feature matches [24, 34]; LoFTR establishes detector-free correspondences [39]. For pose recovery, COLMAP supplies sparse landmarks [37], while PnP solvers such as EPnP [21] and robust con-sensus estimation [13] handle noisy matches. Scene-coordinate regression learns image-to-scene associations di-rectly: DSAC makes robust estimation differentiable [6], and ACE accelerates scene-specific training [7]. QGVI-I adopts NetVLAD, SuperPoint, SuperGlue, and SfM land-marks through the LoGS initializer [8]. Its added accep-tance rule counts distinct inlier query keypoints, avoiding repeated support across references, and leaves the accepted pose fixed. 

Rendering and pose refinement. Pose refinement can use rendered appearance or feature alignment. NeRF represents continuous radiance fields [27]; iNeRF re-fines an initial pose using rendered-image residuals [45]. Feature-based alternatives include PixLoc’s learned multi-scale alignment [35] and MCLoc’s pretrained features with particle filtering [42]. 3D Gaussian Splatting supports dif-ferentiable novel-view rendering [18]. GSLoc differenti-ates image-alignment error with coarse-to-fine blurring [5]; LoGS adds affine brightness correction and informative-pixel masks to photometric refinement [8]. GSplatLoc grounds keypoint descriptors in Gaussian maps [38], and SplatHLoc combines rendered and image-derived features at different matching stages [41]. 6DGS estimates pose from image–ray bindings without requiring an initial pose or iterative pose optimization [4]. Rendering supplies QGVI-T’s candidate views. QGVI-I uses original RGB ref-erences and SfM landmarks, applying its acceptance rule af-ter geometric initialization without photometric refinement. 

Selective prediction. Selective classification separates prediction from withholding an output, exposing a risk– coverage trade-off [15]. For localization, accepting an out-of-map image, rejecting an in-map image, and accepting an inaccurate in-map pose are distinct outcomes. QGVI-I selects its threshold subject to retaining validation-correct poses and holds the underlying estimates fixed in the paired gate ablation. The optional QGVI-T verification ablation withholds views whose required visual constraints cannot be verified. These decisions are evaluated separately from view relevance and pose accuracy.

For RGB, CI requires an in-map query and agreement with its source pose within the evalu-ation thresholds. Out-of-map RGB queries have an empty valid-answer set. This set defines the evaluation target; nei-ther route computes it exhaustively. QGVI-T retrieves up to five indexed candidate views with stored poses. We use these assets with the LoGS initializer [8]. Supplementary Sec. B gives imple-mentation settings.

## 4. Experiments 

Across nine scenes, we evaluate text-view retrieval, RGB pose accuracy, rejection errors, and query latency. Supple-mentary Secs. A and C provide historical comparisons and protocol details. 

4.1. Experimental Setup 

Datasets and reference views. The nine-scene bench-mark combines Block Small and Block A from Matrix-City [22], NYC and SF from UC-GS [46], and Citysample, Colosseum, Elvenruin, Park, and Road from the Horizon-GS dataset [17]. Seven scenes are synthetic; Park and Road contain real captures. We use frozen Horizon-GS recon-structions across all nine scenes to generate reference views and novel-view queries. The text bank contains 30,522 ren-dered views. The source split has no exact file overlap, but some evaluation observations contributed to reconstruction. 

Query cohorts. The main text cohort contains 180 DeepSeek-authored descriptions, 20 per scene: 74 from original test images and 106 from novel Gaussian renders; six are unsupported by their source images. Earlier 176-query and 135-query cohorts remain separate. RGB testing uses 1,839 in-map and 1,839 out-of-map queries. Positives comprise 1,029 held-out source im-ages, including synthetic imagery, and 810 novel Gaus-sian renders. Negatives comprise 1,029 training images and 810 renders from disjoint scene worlds within the nine scenes. Query composition varies by scene; Block A sup-plies 32.7% of positives. Methods share frozen queries, splits, calibration, and target scenes. Four scene scales are source-verified and five DA3-estimated [23] (Supplemen-tary Table S8); calibration and interpretation are specified in Supplementary Sec. C. 

Compared methods. Our CityLoC [26] reimplementa-tion uses three fixed seeds and 64 pose proposals. QGVI-T retrieves 64 candidates and returns up to five distinct lo-cations. Optional verification is reported as an ablation. QGVI-I uses the LoGS geometric initializer [8] without photometric refinement; its gate requires successful PnP and at least 40 distinct inlier query keypoints. The gate is calibrated on 490 Block A and SF validation queries and frozen across all nine test scenes (Sec. 3). LoGS and SplatHLoc [41] use their native maps; QGVI-I shares LoGS’s RGB/SfM assets. Native LoGS accepts retrieval fallbacks; SplatHLoc requires four final inliers. We cali-brate count and ratio gates for both LoGS pose variants, and a count gate for SplatHLoc, on the same validation set and correct-pose-retention objective, then freeze thresholds. 

Model judgments. DeepSeek Flash supplies captions, descriptions, verification, and 176-query relevance labels, creating shared author–judge bias in that cohort. His-torical 180- and 135-query scores use blind Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct relevance judgments and separate DeepSeek image-only validity judgments. The completed histori-cal Kimi/GPT reassessment appears in Supplementary Ta-ble S5. New controls use complete GPT judgments; partial Kimi assessments retain missing-label bounds. Judges and rubrics are kept separate (Supplementary Sec. D.2).

4.2. Answer Quality and Localization Results 

First-answer utility. In Table 1, QGVI-T yields 16.67 net reward units per 100 queries; optional verification yields 57.22 with lower coverage. On the separate RGB cohort, QGVI-I yields 46.74 and calibrated full LoGS 46.82, with equal correct-return rates but fewer wrong returns for LoGS (1.44% versus 1.52%). The SplatHLoc calibration retains its native operating point. Cross-modality utility values are not comparable. 

Text-view retrieval. On the 180-query cohort, QGVI-T achieves 58.3% Top-1 and 78.3% Top-5 success, versus 26.1% and 34.6% for refined CityLoC (Supplementary Ta-ble S8(a)); no-result rates are 0.0% and 19.8%. All queries have joint judgments; macro and pooled rates agree after seed averaging. A one-query CityLoC repeatability check produced different refined poses and rankings despite fixed inputs and seed; no independent paired refinement effect has been measured. 

Source poses and semantic judgments. On the same 180 descriptions, QGVI-T achieves 0.6% Top-1 source-pose success and 3.3% Pose Recall@5 at 5 m/5 ◦; full-bank or-acle coverage is 22.2%. Semantic retrieval scores therefore 6do not establish source-camera accuracy. Supplementary Sec. D reports the pose audit and the separate, completed cross-model assessment. 

RGB localization and acceptance. QGVI-I achieves 97.3% and 97.4% scene-macro pose success at 1 m/2 ◦ and 5 m/5 ◦ (Supplementary Table S8(b)). It accepts 13/1,839 out-of-map queries (0.707% pooled; 0.764% scene mean) and rejects 21/1,839 in-map queries (1.142% pooled; 0.765% scene mean). Accepted in-map poses include 43 strict and 40 loose localization failures. LoGS and SplatHLoc achieve 97.3% and 76.0% strict success, with 100.0% and 99.8% scene-mean false acceptance under na-tive output rules. These native rates do not measure the ben-efit over calibrated gates. Every NYC and SF positive lies within 0.59 m of a training camera, with shared reconstruc-tion dependencies; difficulty varies across scenes. Table S16 isolates the acceptance gate by holding cached initializer outputs fixed. All 1,831 retained camera-to-world matrices are numerically unchanged. All 1,775 strict and 1,778 loose correct poses survive, preserving pooled success of 96.52% and 96.68%. The 21 rejected posi-tives fail both thresholds. Strict accepted-pose precision rises from 48.26% to 96.94% and conditional in-map pre-cision from 96.52% to 97.63%. Table 2 applies the same validation-retention objective to alternative rejection rules. Raw initializer inlier count accepts 24 distractors, versus 13 for distinct-keypoint support; both retain all strict-correct poses. Applying distinct support at the full LoGS pose ac-cepts only ten distractors, with the same pose success and false-rejection rate as QGVI-I. Thus QGVI-I does not have the lowest false acceptance among these calibrated alterna-tives. The paired photometric audit finds no strict or loose suc-cess/failure transitions, but all 3,678 pose matrices change. Over in-map queries, median translation/rotation errors increase from 0.012 m/0.023 ◦ to 0.028 m/0.085 ◦. Equal threshold success therefore does not imply identical pose precision. Supplementary Sec. J reports all controls and continuous-error diagnostics.

## 5. Discussion and Limitations 

Pose preservation and acceptance. QGVI-I preserves accepted pose coordinates by construction, but retention of every correct initializer pose is empirical. Accurate poses with fewer than 40 distinct inliers could be rejected; ac-cepted geometric support also does not certify accuracy. The rule provides no distribution-free risk bound. Full LoGS with the same distinct-support criterion rejects three more distractors at unchanged threshold success, while changing continuous pose errors and incurring photomet-ric refinement. The fixed-initializer gate therefore offers a different cost/accuracy trade-off; it does not establish the general ineffectiveness of photometric refinement. 

Text success and abstention. Optional verification im-proves Top-1 but reduces Top-5 success. Its 25.0% no-result rate does not measure correct rejection: failure to verify a shortlist cannot establish absence of a matching view. RGB distractors come from other benchmark scene worlds; their rejection does not establish robustness to arbitrary appear-ance, sensor, or content changes. 

Retrieval and view coverage. BM25-only retrieval re-mains competitive with rank fusion under matched controls. Adding rendered grid views produces modest confirmed gains on novel-view-derived descriptions, but unresolved labels prevent a conclusive paired improvement claim. Can-didate count, camera-domain coverage, and the scene com-position of each description group limit causal interpreta-tion. 

Evaluation scope. Model-judge agreement measures consistency rather than human correctness. DA3 supplies a fixed scale conversion where source metric scales are un-available, not surveyed ground truth. Reconstruction reuse and scale calibration are specified in Supplementary Sec. C. 

## 6. Conclusion 

Queryable Gaussian View Index queries supplied Gaussian scene assets through complementary text-view retrieval and selective camera localization routes. QGVI-T retrieves dis-tinct places from structured descriptions of posed Gaussian views, without online verification. QGVI-I gates LoGS-based geometric estimates using distinct inlier query key-points while preserving accepted poses. On the nine-scene development benchmark, QGVI-T reaches 78.3% Top-5 success; the RGB gate rejects most out-of-map queries while retaining every correct initializer pose. Matched con-trols find competitive BM25 retrieval and small, uncertain gains from view-bank expansion. A calibrated full-LoGS gate slightly improves rejection at the same pose-success thresholds. Shared coverage, precision, and correct-return accounting retains the distinct semantic and geometric def-initions of correctness.

A.1. Historical Gaussian-Depth Pipeline 

The historical Gaussian-depth pipeline (v3) localizes im-ages using Gaussian-rendered RGB-D references and geo-metric verification. It differs from QGVI-I in retrieval, local matching, 3D geometry, and verification. Table S1 com-pares these complete designs; Table S16 isolates the QGVI-I acceptance gate. The historical pipeline uses SALAD [16] retrieval with four diverse starting views, RootSIFT [2] correspondences based on SIFT [25], geometric pose estimation, and verifi-cation against a newly rendered Gaussian RGB-D view. The query is resized to a maximum side of 512 pixels before SALAD’s standard 322-pixel input transform. The refer-ence bank, 512-pixel local features, solver, gates, and ren-der budgets remain fixed. CLIP embeddings are retained for diagnostics and do not determine geometric acceptance. This variant uses the original Horizon-GS [17] checkpoints and frozen native reference banks. Each reference is back-projected with its own intrinsics. Pose estimation and verification renders use the query in-trinsics, so retrieval does not require the reference and query cameras to have matching calibration. The historical pipeline achieves 81.1% strict scene-macro pose success, accepts no out-of-map queries, and rejects 381 in-map queries (20.7% pooled; 16.0% scene mean). It also accepts 45 inaccurate poses; these local-ization failures are distinct from false rejects. QGVI-I has 97.3% strict scene-macro pose success, 0.707% pooled false acceptance, and 1.142% pooled false rejection on the same queries. The historical pipeline rejects more in-map queries while accepting no out-of-map query in this cohort. Neither result establishes a universal rejection guarantee.

## B. Implementation Details 

Posed view bank. The nine-scene text bank contains 30,522 rendered views at 2,330 distinct place identifiers. Camera centers form a deterministic lattice near the train-ing cameras; heights are interpolated from nearby training cameras, and intrinsics are borrowed from training views. Each place identifier combines the scene, camera domain, and camera-center hash. Different orientations at one center therefore share an identifier; a place is not a learned seman-tic landmark or a spatial-radius cluster. This construction extends the candidate observations beyond captured views. Its effect on retrieval accuracy is evaluated using original-only and original-plus-grid controls in Sec. 4.4; density and replacement studies are reported in Supplementary Sec. I. 

QGVI-T and optional verification. Each structured cap-tion contains a summary and fields for objects, attributes, relations, visible text, and uncertainty. Retrieval indexes the descriptive fields and excludes uncertainty to avoid in-dexing that field as positive retrieval evidence. BM25 uses 

k1 = 1 .2 and b = 0 .75 . Sentence scores are pooled over the complete index before the view ranking is truncated; view identifiers break fusion-score ties. Acceptance and answerability. For a confidence score, thresholds should be selected on validation data under a common retention or risk objective, then frozen for test evaluation. Risk–coverage curves require a recorded score and an explicit threshold policy; isolated binary decisions provide operating points, not a continuous curve. The text cohort does not establish whether each description is an-swerable anywhere in the scene. We therefore do not credit an empty text return as correct rejection or report text false-accept/false-reject rates. An unsupported source image does not imply that all other views fail. Likewise, Y is not an-swerability recall, so combining P and Y into a harmonic mean would not produce a conventional F1 score. Full shared risk–coverage curves cannot be recovered from the available operating points: some native abstentions have no returned answer whose correctness can be scored when hy-pothetically relaxing the policy.

## G. Optional Text Verification 

QGVI-T + verification evaluates the 20 shortlisted views using a VLM. The verifier receives the rendered image and query, excluding the caption. It enumerates mandatory con-straints, including conjunctions, counts, attributes, and spa-tial relations, and assigns each a label with supporting vi-sual evidence: supported, contradicted, or unclear. A view qualifies only if the verifier reports a match and every re-ported requirement is supported. Response validation over-rides a match verdict when any requirement is contradicted or unclear. Contradiction produces a non-match decision; insufficient evidence produces an uncertain decision. Eligible views are ranked by verifier relevance, with shortlist order breaking ties. Scores are not calibrated prob-abilities. We return at most five distinct places with un-changed stored poses, or no result when none qualifies. This abstention concerns the shortlist and does not establish ab-sence throughout the scene. Query processing uses cached renders without new rendering calls.

## J. Matched rejection controls and photometric refinement 

We apply alternative acceptance rules to the same saved poses and calibrate each rule using only the 490 Block A/SF validation queries (245 in-map and 245 out-of-map). Count thresholds are {0, 4, 8, 12 , 20 , 40 , 80 }; ratio thresholds are 

{0, . 05 , . 1, . 2, . 35 , . 5, . 7}. A feasible rule must retain every pose in each validation-correct set at 1 m /2◦ and 5 m /5◦.Among feasible rules we maximize out-of-map rejection, then in-map acceptance, and break remaining ties in favor of fewer active gates and a lower threshold. We freeze these rules before scoring the 3,678 queries across nine scenes; the cohort has been used in earlier development and is not a new independent test set. Validation retention does not guarantee retention on these queries. The raw match ratio divides RANSAC inliers by all saved raw matches; the pair ratio instead uses the dedu-plicated query-keypoint/landmark pairs supplied to PnP. A unique-keypoint ratio uses distinct inlier query keypoints over distinct matched query keypoints. For refined LoGS, final support is recomputed at the unchanged refined pose using positive depth and a 12 -pixel reprojection bound. Initializer-score gates in that panel select already-refined poses and do not save refinement computation. SplatHLoc does not save a correspondence denominator, so we evalu-ate its observed final inlier count only. Recalibration selects the current unique-keypoint thresh-old of 40. Relative to a raw inlier-count threshold of 40 on the same initializer, it removes 11 additional out-of-map re-turns and two incorrect in-map returns, without losing a cor-rect pose. Applying the distinct-keypoint rule to full LoGS gives 10 false accepts instead of 13 for QGVI-I, at the same 21 in-map rejections and 1,775 strict-threshold successes.
