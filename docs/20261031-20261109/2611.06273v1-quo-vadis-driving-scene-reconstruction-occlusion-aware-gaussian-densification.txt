Abstract. While recent feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting methods enable efficient driving-scene reconstruction from surround-view images, their reliance on a single observation fundamentally hinders novel-view synthesis. Regions occluded in the input can become visible from novel viewpoints, leaving the reconstructed Gaussians under-constrained and incomplete. Moving beyond image-space inpainting for rendered views, we introduce OccSplat, a feed-forward framework whose core contribu-tion is Occlusion-Aware Gaussian Densification, explicitly targeting miss-ing scene geometry rather than missing pixels. By predicting new Gaus-sian primitives for unsupported regions, OccSplat completes the scene directly in the 3D representation. Specifically, OccSplat first identifies occluded regions that lack Gaussian support through Gaussian Occlusion Mapping. Gaussian Occlusion Completion then generates new primitives for these regions. The predicted primitives are integrated into the origi-nal representation to improve novel-view synthesis while retaining feed-forward inference without per-scene optimization. Experiments on the nuScenes benchmark demonstrate that OccSplat improves on the com-pared methods and yields particularly large gains in occluded regions, leading to more accurate and visually coherent driving-scene reconstruc-tion. 

Keywords: 3D Gaussian Splatting · Feed-forward Reconstruction · Novel View Synthesis 

## 1 Introduction 

Photorealistic reconstruction and novel-view synthesis of driving scenes from vehicle-mounted cameras are foundational capabilities for autonomous driving, underpinning closed-loop simulation, safe evaluation of perception and planning stacks, and large-scale data augmentation for rare or safety-critical events. The recent emergence of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) [13], which represents a scene with an explicit set of anisotropic Gaussians and renders through fast differen-tiable rasterization, enables fast, high-fidelity rendering after scene optimization and has become a widely used representation for this setting.

Building on 3DGS, a growing body of work specializes the representation to driving scenes [6, 14, 28, 38], modeling the static background, dynamic actors, and unbounded street layout that characterize on-road data. These methods achieve impressive rendering quality, but are fundamentally per-scene optimiza-tion approaches: each scene must be fit individually, typically requiring densely overlapping views, accurate camera extrinsics, and minutes to hours of opti-mization. The need to optimize each new scene limits throughput in large-scale autonomous-driving pipelines. To lift these constraints, feed-forward (generalizable) Gaussian Splatting pre-dicts pixel-aligned Gaussian primitives directly from images in a single forward pass, enabling training on large corpora and inference without per-scene opti-mization on unseen scenes [2,3,4,7,11,23,27,33,34]. DrivingForward [24] adapts this paradigm to driving, jointly learning pose, depth, and Gaussian networks in a self-supervised manner so that surround-view scenes can be reconstructed without depth ground truth or camera extrinsics. We focus on its challenging single-frame (SF) setting: the model observes only the surround-view images of the current frame t and must synthesize the surround views of the future frame 

t+1 .As the ego-vehicle drives forward between t and t+1 , the camera rig translates along the direction of travel and the effective viewpoint changes. Consequently, scene regions that are occluded at t, hidden behind trees, poles, fences, or building Occlusion-Aware Gaussian Densification 3

edges, become disoccluded at t+1 (Fig. 1). Feed-forward pixel-aligned methods place a Gaussian only where there is an observed pixel to lift it from, so surfaces with no visible support at t receive no primitives at all. When these surfaces are exposed at t+1 , the renderer has nothing to draw in their place, yielding holes and stretched “rubber-sheet” artifacts. This disocclusion gap is intrinsic to forward-driving SF synthesis: it arises from viewpoint changes that reveal content absent from the source images. 

Quo vadis ? Where, then, is the scene going? The question is literal as well as rhetorical: the model must reason not only about what it currently sees, but about the content that its forward motion is about to uncover. We there-fore recast future-frame driving reconstruction as an Occlusion-Aware Gaus-sian Densification problem and instantiate it in OccSplat , a feed-forward surround-view framework that anticipates and completes soon-to-be-revealed content before the ego-vehicle uncovers it. Starting from a pixel-aligned Gaus-sian representation of the current frame, OccSplat first localizes regions that lack Gaussian support and are therefore prone to disocclusion, then densifies them with new 3D Gaussian primitives that plausibly complete the disoccluded geometry and appearance. Because these primitives are fused into the same rep-resentation as the observed scene, the completed content can be rendered from a shared 3D representation instead of being patched independently in each image. This densification is a single additional feed-forward pass on top of the backbone, closing the disocclusion gap while retaining feed-forward inference without per-scene optimization. Our contributions are summarized as follows: 

– We introduce OccSplat , a feed-forward framework that reframes occlusion handling as Occlusion-Aware Gaussian Densification, targeting missing scene geometry rather than missing pixels by predicting new Gaussian primitives for occluded regions. 

– We design two modules realizing this idea: Gaussian Occlusion Mapping, which identifies regions lacking Gaussian support, and Gaussian Occlusion Completion, which generates and integrates new primitives for them. 

– Extensive experiments on nuScenes show that OccSplat improves on the compared feed-forward methods, with particularly large gains in occluded regions. 

## 2 Related Work 

Feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting. To eliminate the expensive per-scene optimization of conventional 3DGS, numerous feed-forward approaches have been proposed to directly predict Gaussian representations from sparse input images. Early methods such as PixelSplat [2] and Splatter Image [23] generate Gaussians from image features, while MVSplat [3] and DepthSplat [27] improve geometric reconstruction through explicit cross-view matching and geometry-aware representations. Subsequent methods [10, 32, 33, 36], further relax pose re-quirements and enable reconstruction in pose-free or fully uncalibrated settings. 4 Dung Do Tien et al. 

Despite their impressive efficiency and generalization ability, these methods often struggle in sparse-view scenarios with severe occlusions and limited view overlap, resulting in incomplete geometry and inconsistent scene representations. 

Driving Scene Reconstruction. NeRFs [19] and 3DGS [13] have been adapted to driving scene reconstruction [6, 14, 28, 38]. However, most of them focus on high-quality reconstruction of individual scenes, relying on offline per-scene optimization, which limits their applicability to online driving systems. Their reconstruction quality can also degrade under severe occlusions and sparse-view observations. A few recent feed-forward and pose-free approaches, such as [10,22,33,36], explore online reconstruction for driving scenes, but they mainly focus on general scene reconstruction and cross-scene generalization, while lack-ing explicit mechanisms for handling severe occlusions and sparse-view ambi-guities. DistillNeRF [25] proposes a generalizable framework for driving scenes and achieves reasonable novel view synthesis, but it requires an expensive offline NeRF optimization stage per scene and additional LiDAR supervision, while still struggling in highly occluded regions. In contrast, OccSplat targets feed-forward driving scene reconstruction from RGB inputs alone while improving robustness to occlusions and incomplete observations in sparse-view environments. 

Occlusion-aware and Structure-aware 3DGS. Recent efforts have sought to improve structural modeling and visibility reasoning in 3DGS. Scaffold-GS [18] introduces an anchor-based representation to enhance structural control and reconstruction efficiency, while CityGaussian [17] and Octree-GS [20] improve scalability through hierarchical scene representations. OccluGaussian [16] fur-ther enhances reconstruction consistency by leveraging occlusion-aware scene partitioning based on camera co-visibility. In parallel, methods for recovering missing or occluded regions [5, 8, 21, 37] employ depth-guided geometry reason-ing, multi-view consistency constraints, and iterative refinement to recover in-complete scene content. However, these methods still rely on scene-specific op-timization and therefore cannot operate in a fully feed-forward manner.

## 4 Experiments 

4.1 Experimental Setup Datasets. We evaluate on the nuScenes dataset [1], comprising 1,000 driving scenes with six surround-view cameras capturing a 360 ◦ view with ∼10% overlap between adjacent views. Following the official split, we use 700 scenes for training and 150 for validation at 114 × 228 resolution. 

Evaluation Metrics. We evaluate novel-view synthesis quality at time step 

t + 1 given observations at t, reporting standard PSNR ( ↑), SSIM [26] ( ↑), and LPIPS [35] ( ↓). 

Implementation Details. Training has two stages. Stage I jointly optimizes the base network and Pixel Refiner with DrivingForward losses and Lrefine (λref =0.05 , λ MSE = 1 .0, λ LPIPS = 0 .05 ). Stage II freezes Stage I weights and trains Ad

and Af under novel-view depth ( λnd = 1 .0) and feature supervision ( λnf = 0 .5). We set ϵ = 0 .7, depth range [1 .5, 80 .0] m, learning rate 1 × 10 −4, and batch size 1. Both stages run for 10 epochs on an NVIDIA RTX 4090.

4.3 Ablation Studies 

To validate the effectiveness of each component, we perform ablation experi-ments on the nuScenes validation set, progressively adding Gaussian Occlusion Completion, the Pixel Refiner, and Gaussian Occlusion Mapping on top of the base reconstruction. Quantitative results are reported in Table 2. 

Gaussian Densification. Comparing (a) and (b), applying Gaussian Occlu-sion Completion across the entire image lowers PSNR from 21.76 dB to 19.92 dB and SSIM from 0.767 to 0.747, while slightly improving LPIPS from 0.194 to 0.182. Predicting new primitives from the raw, artifact-corrupted future-view renders and inserting them everywhere adds redundant Gaussians in already re-constructed areas, degrading pixel-wise fidelity even though the added geometry recovers some perceptual detail. This result motivates using reliable completion cues and restricting densification to unsupported regions. 

Pixel Refiner. Adding the Pixel Refiner in (c) improves all three metrics over (b), raising PSNR to 20.71 dB and SSIM to 0.753 while achieving the best LPIPS of 0.175. By restoring plausible appearance in the disoccluded areas be-fore completion, the Refiner provides a cleaner intermediate representation, so the depth and feature attention modules infer more accurate geometry for the missing regions. PSNR and SSIM nonetheless remain below those of the base reconstruction, since densification is still applied over the whole image without localization. 

Occlusion Mapping. Gaussian Occlusion Mapping supplies the missing localization. Even without the Refiner, restricting densification to disoccluded regions in (d) already lifts PSNR to 22.27 dB, above the base reconstruction. Combining all components in (e) yields the best overall result, raising PSNR to 23.50 dB and restoring SSIM to 0.767 while retaining strong perceptual qual-ity. By identifying disoccluded regions through dual-background rendering, the mapping confines completion to where the representation truly lacks support, preserving the original reconstruction elsewhere and supporting the complemen-tary roles of densification, the Refiner, and the mapping.

4.4 Qualitative Results 

Figure 3 presents qualitative comparisons among MVSplat, DrivingForward, and OccSplat on representative scenes from the nuScenes validation set. MVSplat suffers from inaccurate geometry caused by erroneous depth estimation at the source frame, leading to distorted structures and inconsistent renderings in the future view. DrivingForward reconstructs the visible scene more accurately, but when the viewpoint moves to frame t+1 , newly disoccluded regions lack Gaussian support, resulting in holes and stretched artifacts around tree trunks, building boundaries, and roadside structures. OccSplat explicitly addresses this failure mode by generating Gaussian prim-itives only in unsupported disoccluded regions while preserving the correctly reconstructed geometry. As highlighted in the red boxes, OccSplat recovers missing content and produces renderings that are visually closer to the ground truth. ## 5 Conclusion 

We presented OccSplat , an occlusion-aware feed-forward framework that ad-dresses disocclusion in single-frame novel-view synthesis for driving scenes. By identifying disoccluded regions and generating complementary primitives to den-sify the 3D Gaussian representation, OccSplat improves scene coverage while re-taining feed-forward inference. reconstruction quality relative to the compared methods, particularly in newly exposed areas. Furthermore, the completion strategy offers a potential extension to other feed-forward 3DGS models. 

Limitations and Future Work. Operating in image space, the Pixel Re-finer cannot explicitly enforce 3D geometric consistency across viewpoints, lead-ing to potential texture over-smoothing under severe disocclusions or large ego-motion that reveals previously unobserved content. Future work will integrate explicit 3D geometric and semantic priors, as well as temporal information from consecutive frames, to improve structural consistency and robustness in dynamic driving environments.
