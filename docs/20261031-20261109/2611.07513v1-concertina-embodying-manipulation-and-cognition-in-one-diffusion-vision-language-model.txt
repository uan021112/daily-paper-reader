ABSTRACT 

Most vision-language-action (VLA) policies decouple manipulation from cog-nition by bolting an external action mechanism onto a vision-language model (VLM)—either quantizing actions into discrete tokens whose errors bear no stable correspondence to physical displacements, or delegating continuous trajectory generation to a separate action expert whose gradients compete with or are blocked from updating the VLM backbone. We argue that manipulation and cognition should instead be embodied in the same model, shifting from a decoupled VLA to an Embodied Vision-Language Model (eVLM ). We introduce Concertina, an eVLM that places continuous trajectory representation, blockwise generation, and physical-coordinate supervision directly on the computation and parameter path of a shared diffusion VLM backbone. Each control timestep is encoded as one contin-uous latent action token in the backbone hidden space, and the shared Transformer reconstructs temporally ordered action blocks via the same corrupt-and-reconstruct operator used for multimodal pretraining. Active action projections account for only 0.21% of parameters in the 4.46B model with zero separate action-generator layers, compared with 16 .5% –35 .1% dedicated action-expert capacity in represen-tative continuous VLAs. Using our proposed Action Interface Audit , we confirm that cross-time trajectory coupling emerges in the middle and late layers of the shared backbone rather than at the action interfaces. On standard benchmarks, Concertina achieves 98 .15% average success on regular LIBERO, 85 .9% on the 10,030-task LIBERO-Plus benchmark, and 4.142 average completed chain length on CALVIN ABC–D. 

1 INTRODUCTION 

The premise of vision-language-action (VLA) policies is to transfer the visual perception, semantic grounding, and generalization of pretrained vision-language models (VLMs) to physical manipulation. Beneath specific design choices lies a foundational question: how can we enable a pretrained VLM to natively output precise continuous actions while preserving its visual-language generalization, its model architecture, and its native training paradigm? Existing VLAs largely compromise on at least one of these requirements by treating action as an external attachment to a VLM—dividing manipulation and cognition across separate representations, modules, or training objectives, where the VLM handles scene and instruction understanding while another mechanism handles action representation, trajectory generation, or coordinate supervision. Our thesis is that manipulation and cognition should not be decoupled across a bolted-on VLA pipeline; they should be embodied in a single Embodied Vision-Language Model (eVLM ). Concretely, in an eVLM, actions are represented directly in the backbone’s continuous hidden space, generated across time by the same parameters and native generative operator that process vision and language, and supervised in physical coordinates end-to-end without degrading the shared VLM.

Three principles motivate this shift from decoupled VLAs to a unified eVLM. First, cognition should participate in manipulation at every layer. Grasping, placement, and obstacle avoidance rely on the same spatial and physical cues as scene understanding; when trajectory generation happens outside the VLM, cognition is reduced to a one-time conditioning handoff and cannot interact with evolving action states across depth. Second, manipulation should shape cognition in return. Robot trajectories are the primary source of physical and spatial grounding for a VLM; only when continuous action gradients directly update the vision-language parameters can manipulation experience consolidate into the backbone’s spatial cognition rather than remaining confined to an external head. Third, 

preserving the backbone’s architecture, training paradigm, and representation stability enables end-to-end scaling. When cognitive and motor modules rely on incompatible paradigms or objectives, their gradients compete and degrade pretrained representations, often forcing practitioners to insulate or freeze the backbone (Driess et al., 2025; Liang et al., 2025) and permanently fixing the separation between perception and control. From this perspective, conventional VLAs fail to form a unified eVLM in two primary ways (Ta-ble 1). The first is representational decoupling . Methods such as RT-2 (Brohan et al., 2023) and OpenVLA (Kim et al., 2024) quantize each action dimension into discrete bins and reuse the lan-guage vocabulary under cross-entropy loss, while FAST (Pertsch et al., 2025) compresses action chunks via a discrete cosine transform and byte-pair encoding. Although these methods preserve the VLM architecture and training paradigm, control is moved from physical space into symbol space: token-level cross-entropy penalizes adjacent-bin and far-bin errors equally, and compressed tokens entangle multiple timesteps and dimensions so that a single token error can corrupt an entire trajectory. The second is computational and parameter decoupling . Continuous policies such as 

π0 (Black et al., 2024), π0.5 (Physical Intelligence et al., 2025), SmolVLA (Shukor et al., 2025), GR00T N1 (Bjorck et al., 2025), and X-VLA (Zheng et al., 2025) preserve physical coordinates via flow matching or diffusion, but alter the model architecture and training paradigm by delegating trajectory generation to a dedicated action expert or 24-layer action Transformer outside the VLM. Because action supervision primarily updates the external expert and its competing gradients can degrade pretrained VLM features, Knowledge Insulation (Driess et al., 2025) blocks expert gradients from entering the backbone—resolving training instability by hard-wiring architectural decoupling. Recent hybrids partially restore coupling along individual axes—sharing a backbone over discrete diffusion tokens (Liang et al., 2025; Yang et al., 2026a; Wang et al., 2026; Lee et al., 2026), combining discrete and continuous branches (Liu et al., 2025), training a separate trajectory codec (Yang et al., 2026b), or switching from discrete pretraining to downstream flow matching (Ye et al., 2025)—yet none realizes a true eVLM that natively generates continuous physical trajectories while preserving its model architecture, its native pretraining paradigm, and its visual-language generalization. We instantiate this eVLM paradigm in Concertina through an end-to-end continuous latent action representation inside a diffusion VLM. To rigorously test whether a policy acts as a decoupled VLA or a true eVLM—and to localize where action representation and trajectory computation reside—we introduce the Action Interface Audit , a checkpoint-level methodology comprising three metrics aligned with the three decoupling axes: (i) Error-Geometry Consistency (EGC = p95 /p 05 ), measuring whether unit training errors map stably to physical action errors; (ii) Trajectory Coupling Attribution (TCA) via the non-local response ratio ( NLR ), using deterministic finite differences to localize where cross-time dependencies emerge; and (iii) Action-Specific Capacity Ratio (ASCR ), quantifying parameters dedicated solely to action generation. The audit reveals that uniform discrete bins induce a 25 .14 × physical-error span per single-token error and FAST-style compressed tokens suffer 77 .9% –82 .2% structural decoding failures under single-token edits; π0 and π0.5 derive all cross-time coupling from external action experts ( ASCR = 16 .51% and 19 .17% , rising to 22 .19% in SmolVLA and 35 .10% in X-VLA); whereas Concertina’s active action interfaces occupy only 0.21% of the 4.46B model, exhibit zero cross-time response on their own ( NLR = 0 ), and delegate all temporal trajectory coupling to 3the middle and late layers of the shared VLM backbone ( NLR = 4 .06% in pretraining, 4.10% 

on CALVIN, and 1.22% on LIBERO). Furthermore, a matched counterfactual intervention shows that attaching a second coordinate objective to the shared hidden space induces opposing gradients (cosine −0.891 ) and collapses CALVIN chain length from 2.970 to 0.237 , confirming that enabling native action generation in an eVLM without degrading the shared VLM requires co-designing the action representation and its supervision objective as a unified whole. On standard closed-loop benchmarks, Concertina reaches 98 .15% average success on regular LIBERO, 85 .9% on the 10,030-task LIBERO-Plus benchmark, and 4.142 average completed chain length on CALVIN ABC–D (Table 2). Our contributions are threefold: • Embodied Vision-Language Model (eVLM) Paradigm. We formulate the eVLM paradigm, embodying manipulation and cognition within a single VLM across representa-tion, supervision, and generation so that a pretrained VLM natively generates continuous action trajectories while preserving its model architecture, native training paradigm, and visual-language generalization. • Continuous Blockwise eVLM Architecture (Concertina). We introduce Concertina, representing a horizon-H trajectory as H continuous latent action tokens with blockwise causal-bidirectional visibility and packed teacher forcing, requiring only 0.21% active interface parameters and no external action expert, tokenizer, or codec. • Action Interface Audit and Empirical Validation. We introduce EGC, TCA ( NLR ), and ASCR to audit representative discrete VLAs, action-expert VLAs, and our eVLM, provide counterfactual evidence on shared-space objective design, and demonstrate state-of-the-art closed-loop performance on LIBERO and LIBERO-Plus alongside strong zero-shot transfer on CALVIN ABC–D. 

2 RELATED WORK 

Latent action representations. Several recent works learn compact latent action variables from video or robot demonstrations before policy learning. LAPA and IGOR extract discrete latent actions from action-free videos and image-goal pairs before grounding them into physical controls in a subsequent stage (Ye et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2024a), while UniVLA (Bu et al., 2025), Moto (Chen et al., 2024b), villa-X (Chen et al., 2025), and CAT (Yang et al., 2026b) introduce task-centric codebooks, motion tokens, proprioceptive latent dynamics, or frozen trajectory codecs. Because these methods freeze the tokenizer or codec prior to policy optimization, the latent space is never shaped directly by closed-loop physical control gradients. By contrast, Concertina embeds continuous actions directly into the hidden space of the diffusion VLM through lightweight position-wise projections co-trained end-to-end with the backbone under physical-coordinate reconstruction loss. 

External action experts versus shared-backbone generation. Building on diffusion and flow-matching policies (Chi et al., 2023), continuous VLA architectures such as π0 (Black et al., 2024), 

π0.5 (Physical Intelligence et al., 2025), SmolVLA (Shukor et al., 2025), GR00T N1 (Bjorck et al., 2025), and X-VLA (Zheng et al., 2025) preserve continuous control coordinates by pairing a VLM with a dedicated flow-matching action expert or a 24-layer external action Transformer, while OpenVLA-OFT attaches a continuous regression head (Kim et al., 2025). Delegating trajectory generation to an external module separates motor computation from visual-language reasoning and introduces cross-module gradient tension; Knowledge Insulation (Driess et al., 2025) addresses the resulting backbone degradation by blocking expert gradients from updating the VLM, permanently fixing the division between perception and control. Concertina eliminates the external action gen-erator altogether, executing trajectory reconstruction inside the shared VLM Transformer so that manipulation and cognition co-adapt along a single parameter path.

3 METHOD 

Concertina instantiates an Embodied Vision-Language Model (eVLM) that embodies manipulation and cognition within a single diffusion vision-language model, enabling the pretrained backbone to natively generate continuous action trajectories without altering its model architecture or training paradigm. Its atomic generation unit is one complete continuous action vector at one control timestep, represented as a continuous latent action token: a lightweight position-wise encoder maps it into the VLM hidden space, and a position-wise decoder maps the contextualized hidden state back to physical control coordinates. All cross-time and cross-modal dependencies are modeled by the shared Transformer Fθ without an external action expert or discrete action tokenizer. This design establishes two direct coupling pathways between cognition and control: in the forward pass , action tokens attend to visual and linguistic representations at every Transformer layer rather than receiving a one-time conditioning summary; in the backward pass , physical-coordinate reconstruction gradients update the shared vision-language parameters end-to-end, consolidating manipulation experience into the backbone’s spatial representations. 53.1 PRELIMINARIES AND CONTINUOUS LATENT ACTION TOKENS 

Given visual observations I, a language instruction l, and proprioceptive state s, the policy predicts a horizon-H continuous action trajectory A = ( a1, . . . , a H ) ∈ RH×D, where ai ∈ RD. The VLM encodes visual, language, and state inputs into a multimodal conditioning prefix C = ( c1, . . . , c P ).Concertina initializes Fθ from BARD-VL (Chen et al., 2026), reusing its masked blockwise corrupt-and-reconstruct operator across continuous latent action tokens so that cognitive pretraining and motor learning share the same generative procedure. A position-wise action encoder Eϕ and action decoder Dψ bridge physical control coordinates and the d-dimensional backbone hidden space: 

zi = Eϕ(ai) ∈ Rd, bai = Dψ (hi) ∈ RD . (1) Both Eϕ and Dψ are lightweight multilayer perceptrons applied independently at each timestep 

i and trained jointly with Fθ. Because they act strictly position-wise, they perform coordinate projection without temporal mixing; all trajectory-level dependencies across the H latent action tokens Z = ( z1, . . . , z H ) are computed inside Fθ :

[HC , H A] = Fθ ([ C, Z ]) , bA = Dψ (HA). (2) Representing the full vector ai as a single token preserves the physical coupling among translation, rotation, and gripper coordinates within each control step while keeping the action sequence length to H tokens; local Jacobian analysis confirms that Eϕ maintains a non-collapsed, full-rank D-dimensional chart inside Rd throughout pretraining (effective rank 18 .63 /20 ; Section 4.5). 

Unified latent space across embodiments. For multi-embodiment training, heterogeneous control spaces are mapped into a shared maximum coordinate width D. A binary dimension-validity mask 

qn,d ∈ { 0, 1} indicates active coordinates for sample n, and a timestep-validity mask vn,i ∈ { 0, 1}

indicates valid trajectory positions. Inactive coordinates are zeroed at the input and excluded from the loss denominator, allowing diverse robot embodiments to share a unified d-dimensional latent space inside Fθ while preserving exact physical supervision on each embodiment’s active degrees of freedom. 3.2 BLOCKWISE TOPOLOGY AND PACKED TEACHER FORCING 

We partition the horizon H into M = ⌈H/K ⌉ temporally ordered action blocks of size at most 

K, with block index b(i) = ⌊(i − 1) /K ⌋ and A = ( A(0) , . . . , A (M −1) ). Generation is causal across blocks and bidirectional within each block: all tokens in block b are reconstructed jointly conditioned on the multimodal prefix C, completed earlier blocks A(<b ), and the corrupted state eA(b)

of the current block. Inter-block causality preserves the temporal directionality of execution, while intra-block bidirectionality allows contiguous control steps (e.g., approaching, closing the gripper, and lifting) to coordinate and update in parallel as a coherent motion segment. During training, Concertina packs the multimodal prefix, a corrupted action copy, and a clean teacher-forced action copy into a single Transformer sequence: 

Xtrain = [ C, eZ, Z ], eZ = Eϕ( eA), Z = Eϕ(A). (3) Both copies share aligned action position identifiers but follow distinct blockwise visibility rules. A corrupted query at timestep i attends to the prefix, its own corrupted block, and strictly earlier clean blocks: 

Vnoisy (i) = C ∪ { ezj : b(j) = b(i)} ∪ { zj : b(j) < b (i)}, (4) whereas a clean query attends to the prefix and clean blocks up to its own block, Vclean (i) = C ∪ { zj :

b(j) ≤ b(i)}. Action predictions are read exclusively from the corrupted branch eZ, while the clean branch Z supplies teacher-forced context to later blocks without leaking current-block targets. Prefix tokens use causal attention within C and do not attend to action branches, so action queries read multimodal features at every layer during the forward pass while action gradients update the shared prefix-processing parameters during backpropagation. This packed layout supervises all M block-conditional distributions simultaneously in a single backbone forward pass over P + 2 H positions, with sequence length scaling linearly in H, providing a compute-efficient and scalable organization for end-to-end pretraining. 63.3 BLOCK -S TRATIFIED CORRUPTION AND COORDINATE RECONSTRUCTION

Counterfactual dual-objective intervention. Enabling a VLM to natively output continuous ac-tions without degrading its pretrained representations requires care: when manipulation and cognition share a single hidden space, that space is not a passive feature buffer onto which incompatible coordinate readouts can be attached in parallel. We test this principle via a matched counterfactual intervention on the 8.35B shared-backbone checkpoint ( H = 25 ) that holds every model and opti-mization setting identical except for the supervision target: the single-objective baseline supervises relative actions alone, whereas the dual-objective variant attaches a frozen auxiliary decoder supervis-ing absolute actions at loss weight 0.5, with all 21/21 main action-interface tensors initialized bitwise identically from pretraining. Attaching the second coordinate target induces severe gradient conflict directly on the shared backbone hidden states (Figure 4a–c). Across eight fixed CALVIN windows, the pooled cosine similarity between the relative main gradient and absolute auxiliary gradient on the shared hidden state is 

−0.891 , with 99% of individual action-token gradients pointing in opposing directions. Even at weight 0.5, the auxiliary gradient norm is 0.881 × the main gradient norm, reducing the cosine alignment between the combined gradient and the main objective to 0.474 . Inspecting the resulting parameter updates across all 26 trainable Transformer layers (Figure 4b) reveals that while both runs make updates of virtually identical magnitude (relative L2 update norm rising from 4.6% in shallow layers to 9.3% in layers 17–22; overall norm ratio 1.003 ×), the parameter update direction cosine drops from 0.604 at Layer 2 down to 0.138 at Layer 22 (pooled cosine 0.225 )—precisely the middle and late layers where trajectory coupling resides. Consequently, teacher-forced main loss increases 

5.12 ×, one-pass position error increases 2.47 ×, normalized MAE increases 1.60 ×, and closed-loop CALVIN chain length drops by 92 .0% from 2.970 to 0.237 across 1,000 sequences (Figure 4c). In a unified model, the action coordinate representation and its supervision objective jointly define the optimization geometry of the shared VLM backbone and must be co-designed as a single coherent interface.

5 CONCLUSION 

We presented Concertina, an Embodied Vision-Language Model (eVLM) built on the principle that manipulation and cognition should be embodied within the same model rather than decoupled across a bolted-on VLA pipeline—enabling a pretrained diffusion VLM to natively output precise continuous actions while preserving its visual-language generalization, model architecture, and native training 13 paradigm. By representing each control timestep as a continuous latent action token inside a shared diffusion VLM backbone and supervising temporally ordered action blocks directly in physical coordinates via packed teacher forcing, Concertina eliminates both discrete action tokenizers and external action experts while retaining the backbone’s native corrupt-and-reconstruct operator. Our Action Interface Audit across representative VLA families and Concertina demonstrates that an eVLM preserves unit error geometry ( EGC = 1 ) and a full-rank 20D action manifold ( 18 .63 /20 

effective rank), uses only 0.21% active action-interface parameters ( ASCR ), and forms cross-time trajectory coupling directly within the middle and late layers of the shared 36-layer VLM backbone (NLR = 1 .22% –4.10% ). Controlled ablations further establish why blockwise bidirectional horizon partitioning ( K = 10 capturing 2.17 × more coherent action mass and yielding +34 .5 points on LIBERO), physical coordinate weighting with state masking ( +1 .431 and +0 .744 CALVIN AvgLen), multi-block pretraining continuation ( +0 .208 AvgLen over single-block continuation), and unified single-objective supervision with probability-valued gripper BCE ( +0 .427 mean AvgLen across 8,000 factorial chains) are essential when manipulation and cognition share one hidden space. On standard closed-loop benchmarks, Concertina achieves 98 .15% average success on regular LIBERO, 

85 .9% on the 10,030-task LIBERO-Plus benchmark, and 4.142 average completed chain length on CALVIN ABC–D.

To ensure high trajectory quality across heterogeneous robot datasets, we apply an offline multi-stage curation pipeline prior to pretraining. The pipeline verifies timestamp continuity, finite coordinate ranges, and frame-to-action alignment across all 146,433 episodes.
